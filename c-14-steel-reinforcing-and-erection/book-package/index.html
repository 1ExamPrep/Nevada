Exam Details

The Nevada C-14 Steel Reinforcing and Erection examination is the trade examination associated with the C-14 specialty contractor classification.

Preparation should reflect the full C-14 scope rather than focusing exclusively on reinforcing bars. Nevada's C-14 classification encompasses work authorized under its steel reinforcing and erection subclassifications, including reinforcing steel, structural steel, and ornamental metal work.

Major preparation areas should include:

  • Reinforcing Steel: reinforcing bars, placement, tying, supports, fabrication, post-tensioning, and related field practices.
  • Structural Steel: structural shapes, plates, erection methods, connections, rigging, welding-related concepts, and safe handling.
  • Steel Joists and Joist Girders: handling, erection, positioning, temporary stability, and installation practices.
  • Steel Deck: deck placement, handling, attachment, support conditions, and coordination with structural framing.
  • Post-Tensioning: tendons, anchors, stressing concepts, field procedures, equipment, and safety.
  • Safety: fall protection, hoisting, rigging, personal protective equipment, access, material handling, and steel erection hazards.

Candidates should approach the exam as a practical trade test that requires knowledge of how steel components are fabricated, handled, placed, erected, connected, supported, and installed safely.

Closed Book Test

The Nevada C-14 Steel Reinforcing and Erection Contractor examination is a closed-book test.

The books included in this package are study references and are not intended for use inside the C-14 trade examination room. This makes memorization alone less effective than developing a practical understanding of trade concepts.

Candidates should study each reference with the goal of being able to explain procedures from memory. After reviewing reinforcing-bar placement, for example, close the book and describe the sequence for receiving, positioning, supporting, tying, and checking reinforcement.

The same approach should be used for post-tensioning, steel joist erection, structural steel handling, steel deck installation, and jobsite safety. Candidates should focus on why a procedure is performed, what can go wrong, and what safe or correct practice looks like.

Active recall is particularly valuable for a closed-book exam. Use practice questions, flash cards, verbal explanations, and short written summaries to reinforce technical vocabulary and field procedures.

State Requirements

Nevada designates C-14 as the specialty classification for Steel Reinforcing and Erection. A contractor holding the C-14 classification may perform work authorized within the C-14 subclassifications.

The C-14A Reinforcing Steel subclassification covers fabrication, placement, and tying of steel reinforcing bars and post-tensioning used to reinforce concrete buildings and structures.

The C-14B Structural Steel subclassification covers fabrication and erection of structural steel shapes and plates used as structural members or tanks, including connected riveting, welding, and rigging.

The broader C-14 classification therefore requires a wider working knowledge than reinforcing-bar placement alone. Candidates should understand the relationship among concrete reinforcement, structural steel members, joists, deck, connections, and safe erection procedures.

The C-14 trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-14 preparation plan should begin with reinforcing steel. Candidates should understand bar identification, placement, spacing concepts, supports, tying methods, fabrication, handling, and coordination with concrete construction.

The Placing Reinforcing Bars reference should be used to develop practical knowledge of how reinforcement moves from drawings and fabrication into the field. Study common bar arrangements, supports, ties, placement practices, and terminology.

The CRSI Manual of Standard Practice 2009 (USED) can reinforce industry terminology, fabrication practices, detailing concepts, placement expectations, and communication among reinforcing-steel fabricators, detailers, and installers.

Rather than memorizing isolated terms, connect each term to a field task. Understand what a bar mark communicates, why reinforcing bars must be properly positioned, and how supports and ties help maintain the required location before and during concrete placement.

Post-tensioning should receive focused study. Candidates should understand the basic purpose of unbonded post-tensioning, the role of tendons and anchors, installation sequencing, stressing operations, and associated safety considerations.

Use the Level 1 Field Fundamentals reference to review component identification and field procedures. After studying each section, practice explaining the installation and stressing process without looking at the book.

Structural steel erection requires understanding how structural members are received, rigged, lifted, positioned, temporarily stabilized, and connected. Candidates should recognize the importance of sequencing and communication during erection.

Rigging should be studied as part of structural steel work. Candidates should understand basic load-handling concepts, lifting practices, sling-related considerations, communication, and the importance of controlling loads during erection.

Steel joists and joist girders require careful handling because stability can be a concern during erection. Use Technical Digest No. 9 to study lifting, positioning, erection sequences, bridging relationships, and safe handling practices.

Candidates should understand that a structural component may not have full stability simply because it has been set in place. Temporary conditions during erection can create hazards that differ from the completed structure.

Steel deck should also receive dedicated preparation. Study how deck is delivered, handled, placed, supported, aligned, and attached to the structural frame.

The SDI Manual of Construction with Steel Deck can help candidates understand practical deck-installation concepts, field conditions, attachment, openings, support relationships, and coordination with framing.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Review how deck sheets are positioned and how installation sequences can affect both productivity and safety. Candidates should understand the relationship between deck and the supporting joists, beams, or other structural members.

Safety should be integrated into every study area rather than treated as a separate subject. Steel reinforcing and erection work can involve elevated work, suspended loads, heavy materials, sharp edges, rigging equipment, welding operations, power tools, and changing site conditions.

Use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 to study fall protection, steel erection safety, material handling, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, tools, hoisting, and other construction hazards.

Because the trade examination is closed book, candidates should use the OSHA reference to develop retained safety knowledge. After reviewing a safety topic, summarize the hazard and appropriate protective practices from memory.

Fall protection deserves particular attention because steel erection frequently involves work at elevation. Candidates should recognize hazardous conditions and understand the role of protective systems and safe access.

Material handling is another important topic. Reinforcing bars, structural members, joists, girders, and deck can all create hazards during unloading, staging, lifting, and installation.

Communication matters during lifting and erection. Candidates should understand why coordinated signaling and clear work procedures are important when heavy steel components are being moved.

Connections should be studied in relation to stability. Whether work involves structural steel, joists, or deck, candidates should understand that temporary and permanent connections affect how safely the structure behaves during erection.

Welding-related concepts should be included because structural steel work under the C-14 classification can include connected welding. Candidates should understand basic field concerns involving preparation, equipment, safety, and connection quality.

Riveting and fastening should also be understood as connection methods associated with structural steel work. Focus on the purpose of the connection and how it contributes to structural assembly.

A useful closed-book study method is to organize preparation by work sequence. Begin with drawings and material identification, then move through fabrication, delivery, staging, rigging, placement, temporary stability, fastening, inspection, and completion.

For reinforcing work, create a separate sequence covering bar identification, placement, support, tying, inspection readiness, and coordination with concrete placement.

For post-tensioning, create a sequence covering component identification, tendon placement, anchors, preparation, stressing, and safety.

More on test Information and Study Materials

For joists and structural steel, review handling, rigging, erection, temporary stability, bridging or bracing, connections, and final positioning.

For steel deck, review delivery, bundling, placement, alignment, attachment, openings, and safe movement across the work area.

Practice questions should initially be grouped by subject so weak areas are easier to identify. Later, move into mixed practice that shifts among reinforcing steel, post-tensioning, joists, structural steel, deck, and safety.

When a practice question is missed, identify the reason. The issue may involve unfamiliar terminology, confusion about installation sequence, weak safety knowledge, or misunderstanding of a structural component.

Correcting the underlying weakness is more useful than memorizing a single answer. Closed-book examinations reward candidates who understand the work well enough to apply principles to unfamiliar situations.

How does the Steel Reinforcing and Erection Contractor (C-14) exam work?

The Steel Reinforcing and Erection Contractor (C-14) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
