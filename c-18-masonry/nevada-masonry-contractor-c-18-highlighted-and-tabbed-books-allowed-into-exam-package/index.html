Exam Details

The Nevada Masonry Contractor (C-18) trade examination evaluates technical knowledge associated with masonry construction and the responsibilities of contractors working within the classification.

Candidates should prepare for subjects involving brick, block, stone, structural masonry, mortar, grout, reinforcement, walls, openings, supports, anchors, flashing, building-code requirements, and related masonry construction principles.

The International Building Code, 2018 supports broader building-code preparation. Candidates should be comfortable locating requirements that affect masonry walls, structural systems, fire-resistance concepts, building components, openings, and other construction conditions.

ACI 530/530.1-13 focuses more specifically on structural masonry. Candidates should understand how to locate requirements involving masonry materials, reinforcement, grout, wall systems, construction specifications, structural provisions, and quality requirements.

Because the examination is open book, candidates should develop the ability to choose the correct reference quickly. A broad building-code issue may lead to the International Building Code, while a technical structural masonry issue may be found more efficiently in ACI 530/530.1.

Preparation should also include understanding how masonry components work together. Reinforcement, grout, mortar, masonry units, anchors, supports, and openings all contribute to the performance of a completed masonry assembly.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-18 Masonry as a specialty contractor classification covering qualifying masonry work performed within the scope of the license.

Masonry work can involve brick, baked clay products, rough-cut or dressed stone, artificial stone, precast blocks, structural glass brick or block, and related masonry materials.

The trade requires more than simply placing masonry units. Contractors must understand layout, structural support, mortar, grout, reinforcement, openings, anchors, wall systems, and the broader building conditions that affect masonry performance.

The International Building Code supports the broader building-code side of this preparation. Masonry walls can interact with structural requirements, fire-resistance concepts, building components, openings, and other general building provisions.

ACI 530/530.1 provides more specialized requirements for masonry structures and related construction specifications. Candidates should understand how these two references complement one another during exam preparation.

The Nevada contractor licensing process also includes requirements beyond the C-18 trade examination. Applicants must complete the applicable application, qualification, examination, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-18 study plan should combine practical masonry knowledge with repeated navigation of the highlighted and tabbed International Building Code and ACI 530/530.1.

Structural Masonry should receive focused review. Candidates should understand how masonry units, reinforcement, grout, mortar, anchors, and supports work together within a complete structural system.

Building-Code Requirements should be practiced through the International Building Code. Use the tabs to move quickly to major sections, but also learn the organization of the code so unfamiliar questions can still be researched efficiently.

Masonry Materials should include the different units and materials used in masonry construction. Candidates should understand how material properties affect wall construction, structural performance, and installation methods.

Mortar and Grout should be understood as separate materials serving different purposes. Candidates should recognize how mortar bonds masonry units and how grout is used within reinforced or filled masonry systems.

Reinforcement should receive dedicated attention. Structural masonry can involve reinforcing steel, placement requirements, interaction with grout, openings, walls, and other structural elements.

Wall Systems should include layout, structural requirements, openings, supports, anchors, and the relationship between individual masonry components and the completed wall.

Openings should be studied for the way doors, windows, and other penetrations affect masonry construction. Candidates should understand how support and reinforcement may be incorporated around openings.

Anchors and Connections should be reviewed for their role in tying masonry to surrounding construction and supporting multi-component wall systems.

Moisture Control should also be part of preparation. Flashing, drainage, detailing, and wall construction methods can affect the long-term durability of masonry systems.

Fire-Resistance Concepts should be reviewed through the International Building Code where they relate to masonry construction and wall assemblies.

The highlighted sections can help selected technical information stand out during repeated review. When a practice question is missed, return to the marked section and study the surrounding language rather than memorizing only the final answer.

The tabs provide useful navigation points, especially for major code subjects. Repeated use of the same tab locations during practice can help candidates become more comfortable with the physical organization of the books.

One of the most important preparation skills is choosing the correct reference. Before opening either book, decide whether the question is primarily a general building-code issue or a structural masonry issue.

How does the Masonry Contractor (C-18) exam work?

The Masonry Contractor (C-18) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
