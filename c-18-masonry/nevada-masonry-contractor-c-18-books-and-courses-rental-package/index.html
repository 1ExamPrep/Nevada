Exam Details

The Nevada Masonry Contractor (C-18) trade examination evaluates technical knowledge associated with masonry construction and the responsibilities of contractors working within the C-18 classification.

Candidates should prepare for subjects involving brick, block, stone, structural masonry, mortar, grout, reinforcement, wall systems, openings, anchors, flashing, layout, tools, building-code requirements, and construction safety.

The International Building Code, 2018 supports preparation involving broader building-code requirements that affect masonry construction. Candidates should become familiar with how masonry walls interact with structural systems, building components, fire-resistance concepts, openings, and other general building requirements.

ACI 530/530.1-13 supports more specialized structural masonry preparation. Candidates should understand how to locate requirements involving masonry materials, reinforcement, grout, structural wall systems, specifications, quality requirements, and related technical provisions.

Modern Masonry supports practical trade preparation involving brick, concrete masonry units, stone, mortar, tools, layout, walls, bonding, reinforcement, and hands-on masonry methods. Because this book is not allowed inside the trade examination room, candidates should use it to build knowledge that can be recalled during testing.

OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports construction-safety preparation. Masonry work may involve scaffolds, ladders, material handling, cutting tools, heavy units, wall construction, personal protective equipment, and other hazards that require strong jobsite safety knowledge.

The Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada supports a different side of the contractor licensing process by helping candidates prepare for business and law topics. Combining technical masonry study with business preparation can create a more balanced licensing study plan.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada C-18 trade examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the masonry examination.

  1. Confirm the C-18 classification. Make sure the masonry work the business intends to perform falls within the Nevada Masonry classification.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor license application must designate the person responsible for providing the applicable trade qualification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable work history involving masonry, brick, block, stone, mortar, grout, reinforcement, structural walls, or related trade responsibilities.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Submit the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. Examination eligibility is established through the contractor licensing process.
  6. Receive examination authorization. Once eligible, the qualifying individual can proceed with scheduling the applicable examinations.
  7. Prepare for the C-18 trade exam. Study masonry materials, building-code requirements, structural masonry, mortar, grout, reinforcement, walls, openings, and safety.
  8. Practice with the highlighted and tabbed IBC and ACI references. Learn when each book is the best starting point for a technical question.
  9. Prepare for Nevada business and law. Use the included Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada and course materials to organize business and law study.
  10. Complete applicable examinations and remaining licensing requirements. Satisfy the requirements associated with the Nevada contractor license application.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-18 Masonry as a specialty contractor classification. The classification applies to qualifying masonry work involving materials such as brick, baked clay products, rough-cut and dressed stone, artificial stone, precast blocks, structural glass brick or block, and related masonry materials.

Masonry work requires an understanding of much more than placing individual units. Contractors must understand layout, mortar, grout, reinforcement, anchors, walls, openings, structural support, building-code requirements, and proper installation methods.

The International Building Code supports the broader building-code side of the classification, while ACI 530/530.1 addresses more specialized structural masonry requirements. Candidates should understand how both references work together when preparing for the trade examination.

Practical masonry knowledge is also important. Brick, block, stone, mortar, reinforcement, tools, layout, joint work, wall construction, and material handling are all parts of the trade that can affect workmanship and system performance.

Safety should remain part of the overall preparation process because masonry can involve elevated work, cutting, lifting, scaffolds, ladders, heavy materials, and other jobsite hazards.

The Nevada contractor licensing process includes requirements beyond the trade examination. Applicants must complete the applicable application, qualification, examination, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-18 preparation plan should combine practical masonry knowledge, structural masonry requirements, building-code navigation, jobsite safety, business and law study, and practice-oriented course preparation.

Brick and Block Construction should include unit selection, layout, coursing, bonding, alignment, joint work, openings, and complete wall construction. Candidates should understand how individual masonry units contribute to the finished assembly.

Mortar and Grout should be reviewed as separate materials serving different purposes. Candidates should understand the role of mortar between masonry units and the role of grout in reinforced or filled masonry construction.

Reinforcement should receive focused study. Candidates should understand reinforcing steel, placement, interaction with grout, wall systems, openings, and the way reinforcement contributes to structural performance.

Stone Masonry should include material selection, placement, cutting, bonding, support, and finishing. Stone work differs from standard brick and block construction and should be studied as a distinct masonry area.

Wall Layout and Construction should include dimensions, corners, lines, levels, plumb conditions, coursing, and proper sequencing. Accurate layout is essential because small deviations can affect the completed wall.

Openings and Supports should include lintels, bearing, reinforcement, anchors, and the way doors, windows, and other penetrations affect a masonry wall.

Flashing and Moisture Control should also receive attention. Candidates should understand how moisture can affect masonry construction and why proper detailing contributes to durability.

Structural Masonry Requirements should be practiced through ACI 530/530.1. Use the highlighted and tabbed reference to locate requirements involving reinforcement, grout, wall systems, specifications, and structural provisions.

Building-Code Requirements should be practiced through the International Building Code. Candidates should learn how broader code provisions interact with masonry construction and when the IBC is the better reference for a particular question.

Masonry Tools and Equipment should include equipment used for cutting, mixing, laying, measuring, aligning, finishing, and handling masonry materials.

Scaffolds and Ladders should receive dedicated OSHA review because masonry work often occurs above ground level. Candidates should understand safe access, working platforms, fall hazards, and proper equipment use.

Material Handling should include safe movement and storage of brick, block, stone, reinforcing steel, mortar materials, and equipment. Masonry materials can be heavy and require careful staging and handling.

How does the Masonry Contractor (C-18) exam work?

The Masonry Contractor (C-18) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
