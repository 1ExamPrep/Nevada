## Exam Details

The Nevada C-18 Masonry Contractor trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who receive examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

- **Exam:** Nevada C-18 Masonry Contractor
- **Number of Questions:** 50
- **Time Allowed:** 2 hours
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 35 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The C-18 examination content outline is divided into five major technical areas:

- **Safety:** 5 questions
- **Concrete Block:** 20 questions
- **Brick:** 11 questions
- **Specialty Masonry:** 6 questions
- **Mortar and Grout:** 8 questions

Concrete Block is the largest individual category with 20 questions. Candidates should make concrete masonry units a major part of their preparation. Study should include layout, materials, wall construction, reinforcement concepts, bond patterns, openings, placement, alignment, cutting, support, and practical block-laying procedures.

Brick contains 11 questions and represents another substantial portion of the examination. Preparation should include brick materials, layout, bonding, joints, coursing, cutting, wall construction, openings, installation techniques, and other practical brick-masonry concepts.

Mortar and Grout accounts for eight questions. Candidates should understand mortar and grout materials, proportions, mixing concepts, placement, handling, joints, application, and the relationship between mortar, grout, masonry units, and reinforcement.

Specialty Masonry contains six questions. Candidates should review stone, artificial stone, structural glass brick or block, precast masonry products, specialty applications, and the methods used to install and finish these materials.

Safety contributes five questions. Candidates should review OSHA construction-safety subjects relevant to masonry work, including scaffolds, ladders, fall hazards, personal protective equipment, material handling, tools, cutting operations, and other jobsite conditions.

## State Requirements

The Nevada C-18 classification is specifically designated for masonry contractors. A license holder may select, cut, and lay brick and other baked-clay products, rough-cut and dressed stone, artificial stone, precast blocks, and structural glass brick or block.

The materials may be installed at random or in courses and may be laid with or without mortar. The classification does not authorize applying tile to existing surfaces, so contractors should understand the boundaries between masonry work and work requiring another Nevada contractor classification.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual generally must have at least four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the specific classification requested.

The qualifying experience must generally have been gained within the 15 years immediately preceding the contractor application. Applicants should maintain organized records describing the masonry work performed and the period during which the experience was gained.

Nevada contractor licensing uses both management and trade qualification roles. One individual may serve as both the Management Qualified Individual and Trade Qualified Individual when the applicable requirements are met, or separate individuals may fulfill those roles.

The Management Qualified Individual generally satisfies the Nevada Business and Law examination requirement. The Trade Qualified Individual establishes the required masonry experience and satisfies the applicable trade examination requirement.

The contractor licensing process also includes financial responsibility, bonding, background requirements, and a monetary limit associated with the license. Passing the C-18 trade exam is one important part of licensing but does not by itself complete the entire process.

## How does the Masonry Contractor (C-18) exam work?

The Masonry Contractor (C-18) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
