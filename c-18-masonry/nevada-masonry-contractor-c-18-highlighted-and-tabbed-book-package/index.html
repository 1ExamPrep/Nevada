Exam Details

The Nevada Masonry Contractor (C-18) trade examination evaluates technical knowledge associated with masonry construction and the responsibilities of contractors working within the classification.

Candidates should prepare for subjects involving brick, concrete masonry units, stone, mortar, grout, reinforcement, structural masonry, wall construction, openings, anchors, flashing, tools, layout, building-code requirements, and construction safety.

The International Building Code, 2018 supports preparation involving broader building requirements that affect masonry construction. Candidates should become familiar with how general building provisions interact with masonry walls, structural systems, fire-resistance concepts, openings, building components, and related construction conditions.

ACI 530/530.1-13 supports more specialized structural masonry preparation. Candidates should understand how to locate requirements involving masonry materials, reinforcement, grout, construction specifications, structural walls, quality requirements, and related technical provisions.

Modern Masonry supports practical trade review involving brick, block, stone, mortar, tools, layout, wall construction, bonding, finishing, reinforcement, and common masonry methods. Since this book cannot be used during the examination, candidates should use it to build knowledge that can be recalled without reference access.

OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports jobsite safety preparation. Masonry work can involve scaffolds, ladders, cutting equipment, material handling, overhead hazards, heavy masonry units, wall construction, and other conditions that require strong safety awareness.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada C-18 examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable qualification and licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the masonry trade test.

  1. Confirm the C-18 classification. Make sure the masonry work the business intends to perform falls within the Nevada Masonry classification.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor license application must designate the individual who will provide the applicable trade qualification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable work history involving masonry construction, brick, block, stone, mortar, grout, reinforcement, wall systems, or related responsibilities.
  4. Complete the contractor license application. Submit the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. Examination eligibility is established through the contractor licensing process.
  6. Receive examination authorization. Once eligible, the qualifying individual can proceed with scheduling the applicable examinations.
  7. Prepare for the C-18 trade examination. Study masonry methods, structural masonry, reinforcement, mortar, grout, walls, building-code requirements, and safety.
  8. Practice with the highlighted and tabbed exam-room references. Become comfortable deciding when to use the IBC and when to use ACI 530/530.1.
  9. Complete applicable examinations. Satisfy the examination requirements associated with the contractor license application.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish the other applicable requirements before the Nevada contractor license is issued.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-18 Masonry as a specialty contractor classification. The classification applies to qualifying masonry work involving materials such as brick, baked clay products, rough-cut and dressed stone, artificial stone, precast blocks, structural glass brick or block, and related masonry materials.

Masonry work requires more than placing individual units. A contractor must understand the relationship between materials, supports, reinforcement, mortar, grout, anchors, layout, openings, wall systems, and overall structural performance.

Structural masonry can also interact with broader building requirements. Walls may be affected by structural provisions, fire-resistance requirements, openings, building components, and other code conditions. This is why both the International Building Code and ACI 530/530.1 are important preparation references.

Candidates should approach the C-18 classification as a complete construction system. Brick, block, stone, mortar, reinforcement, grout, flashing, openings, supports, and safety practices all contribute to the quality and durability of the finished work.

The Nevada contractor licensing process also includes requirements beyond the C-18 trade examination. Applicants must complete the applicable application, qualification, examination, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-18 study plan should combine practical masonry knowledge, structural masonry requirements, building-code navigation, safety preparation, and repeated practice with the highlighted and tabbed references.

Brick and Block Construction should include unit selection, layout, coursing, bonding, alignment, joint work, openings, and overall wall construction. Candidates should understand how individual masonry units function as part of a complete assembly.

Mortar and Grout should be studied as separate materials with different purposes. Understand how mortar is used between masonry units and how grout is used within reinforced or filled masonry construction.

Reinforcement should receive dedicated attention. Candidates should understand reinforcing steel, placement, interaction with grout, wall systems, openings, and the way reinforcement contributes to structural masonry performance.

Stone Masonry should include material selection, cutting, placement, bonding, support, and finishing. Stone construction presents different practical considerations from standard brick or concrete masonry units.

Wall Layout should include dimensions, corners, lines, levels, plumb conditions, coursing, and construction sequencing. Accurate layout is important because small errors can multiply as masonry rises.

Openings and Supports should include lintels, bearing, reinforcement, anchors, and the way doors, windows, and other penetrations affect masonry walls.

Flashing and Moisture Control should also be part of preparation. Candidates should understand the role of moisture management in the durability and performance of masonry walls.

Structural Masonry Requirements should be practiced using ACI 530/530.1. Use the tabs and highlighted sections to return quickly to major subjects, but also become familiar with the overall organization of the reference.

Building-Code Requirements should be practiced using the International Building Code. Candidates should learn how general building requirements connect to masonry systems and how to move efficiently between broader code provisions and masonry-specific requirements.

Masonry Tools and Equipment should include tools used for cutting, mixing, laying, aligning, measuring, striking joints, and handling masonry materials. Understand both the purpose of the equipment and the safety concerns associated with its use.

Scaffolds and Ladders deserve focused OSHA study because masonry work is frequently performed above ground level. Review access, platforms, fall hazards, and safe work conditions.

Material Handling should also receive attention. Brick, block, stone, mortar materials, reinforcing steel, and equipment can be heavy, so candidates should understand safe staging, lifting, movement, and storage practices.

The tabs can help candidates develop a more organized reference-navigation routine. Repeatedly moving to the same major subjects during practice can build familiarity with the books and make exam-room navigation more natural.

Timed practice can also improve preparation. Select a masonry question, determine whether the answer is more likely to be found in the IBC or ACI 530/530.1, locate the applicable section, and then review the full requirement.

If structural masonry questions are difficult, spend additional time with ACI 530/530.1. If broader building-code questions take too long, increase IBC navigation practice. If practical trade questions are weak, return to Modern Masonry. If safety questions are challenging, increase OSHA review.

How does the Masonry Contractor (C-18) exam work?

The Masonry Contractor (C-18) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
