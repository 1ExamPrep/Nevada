Exam Details

The Nevada Masonry Contractor (C-18) trade examination evaluates technical knowledge associated with masonry construction and the responsibilities of contractors working within the C-18 classification.

Candidates should prepare for subjects involving brick, concrete masonry units, stone, structural masonry, mortar, grout, reinforcement, wall systems, openings, supports, anchors, flashing, structural requirements, layout, and construction methods.

The two books included with this package support different parts of that preparation. The International Building Code provides broader building requirements, while ACI 530/530.1 focuses more specifically on structural masonry design and construction requirements.

Structural masonry should receive focused study. Candidates should understand how masonry units, mortar, grout, reinforcement, anchors, and supports work together as a complete structural system.

Reinforcement is especially important in masonry construction. Candidates should understand the role of reinforcing steel and how reinforcement interacts with grout, masonry units, openings, wall systems, and structural loads.

Mortar and grout should be understood as separate materials serving different purposes. Candidates should be able to distinguish their functions and recognize how each contributes to the performance of a masonry assembly.

Wall systems and openings should also be part of preparation. Candidates should study the relationship between masonry walls, lintels, supports, anchors, reinforcement, and the openings created for doors, windows, and other building components.

State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-18 Masonry as a specialty contractor classification. The classification applies to qualifying masonry work involving materials such as brick, baked clay products, stone, artificial stone, precast blocks, structural glass brick or block, and other masonry materials used within the authorized scope.

Masonry construction can involve both aesthetic and structural responsibilities. Contractors must understand material placement, layout, structural requirements, wall systems, reinforcement, mortar, grout, supports, openings, and the way individual masonry components work together.

Building-code requirements are important because masonry does not exist in isolation. Walls can interact with structural systems, fire-resistance requirements, openings, building height, occupancy, and other building components.

ACI masonry requirements provide a more detailed framework for structural masonry. Candidates should understand how the specialized reference complements the broader building-code requirements found in the International Building Code.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-18 preparation plan should combine practical masonry knowledge with repeated navigation of the International Building Code and ACI 530/530.1.

Structural Masonry should be studied through ACI 530/530.1. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of the reference and the way it addresses materials, reinforcement, grout, structural walls, construction specifications, and quality requirements.

Building Code Requirements should be practiced through the International Building Code. Candidates should understand how general building requirements interact with masonry construction and how masonry can be affected by broader structural and building provisions.

Brick and Block Construction should be reviewed for unit layout, coursing, bonding, alignment, openings, supports, and overall wall construction. Even when practical topics are not located directly in the exam-room references, understanding the trade helps candidates interpret code questions more effectively.

Mortar and Grout should be studied for their different purposes within masonry systems. Candidates should understand how mortar bonds units together and how grout is used within reinforced or filled masonry construction.

Reinforcement should receive dedicated attention. Study the role of reinforcing steel, placement, interaction with grout, structural walls, and the way reinforcement contributes to the performance of masonry assemblies.

Anchors and Ties should be reviewed for their role in connecting masonry to surrounding construction or supporting multi-component wall systems.

Wall Openings should include lintels, reinforcement, supports, bearing conditions, and the effect of openings on the continuity of a masonry wall.

Moisture Control should also be part of preparation. Flashing, drainage, detailing, and proper wall construction can affect the durability and performance of masonry systems.

Fire-Resistance Concepts may also interact with masonry construction. Candidates should understand how masonry walls can be part of rated assemblies and how broader building-code requirements affect those systems.

One of the most useful study techniques is to practice choosing the correct book. Read a question and decide whether the answer is more likely to be found in the International Building Code or ACI 530/530.1 before opening either reference.

Once the correct book is chosen, identify the strongest keyword or subject term. Use the index, table of contents, headings, and cross-references to reach the correct section as efficiently as possible.

When an answer is found, read the surrounding language rather than stopping at one sentence. Exceptions, definitions, tables, or cross-references can change how a requirement applies.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Timed code-navigation practice can help candidates become more efficient. Work through groups of masonry questions and track how long it takes to identify the correct reference and locate the applicable section.

If structural masonry questions are taking too long, spend additional time with the organization of ACI 530/530.1. If broader building-code questions are difficult, increase International Building Code navigation practice.

Practice questions should also be used to identify knowledge gaps. A candidate who consistently struggles with reinforcement may need more time studying structural masonry concepts, while difficulty with walls and openings may point to another weak area.

A balanced study schedule can rotate between structural masonry, general building-code navigation, mortar and grout, reinforcement, walls and openings, moisture control, and practical trade review.

How does the Masonry Contractor (C-18) exam work?

The Masonry Contractor (C-18) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
