Exam Details

The Nevada Masonry Contractor (C-18) trade examination evaluates technical knowledge associated with masonry construction and the responsibilities of contractors working within the C-18 classification.

Candidates should prepare for subjects involving brick, block, stone, mortar, grout, reinforcement, structural masonry, walls, openings, anchors, supports, building-code requirements, and related masonry construction principles.

The International Building Code, 2018 supports preparation involving the broader building-code requirements that affect masonry construction. Candidates should become familiar with provisions involving masonry walls, structural systems, building components, fire-resistance concepts, openings, and other general requirements that can influence masonry work.

ACI 530/530.1-13 provides specialized structural masonry requirements and specifications. Candidates should understand how to locate information involving masonry materials, reinforcement, grout, wall systems, construction specifications, structural provisions, and quality requirements.

One of the most important preparation skills is learning which reference to use first. A question involving a broader building requirement may lead to the International Building Code, while a question focused on reinforcement, structural masonry, grout, or masonry specifications may be addressed more directly in ACI 530/530.1.

Trade knowledge is still important during an open-book exam. Candidates who understand masonry materials, wall systems, mortar, grout, reinforcement, and structural relationships can usually identify the correct reference and subject area more efficiently.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada C-18 trade examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable qualification and licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the masonry test.

  1. Confirm the C-18 classification. Make sure the masonry work the business intends to perform falls within the Nevada Masonry classification.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor license application must designate the person responsible for providing the applicable trade qualification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable work history involving brick, block, stone, mortar, grout, reinforcement, structural walls, layout, or related masonry responsibilities.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Submit the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.

  6. Receive examination authorization. Once eligible, the qualifying individual can proceed with scheduling the applicable examinations.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-18 Masonry as a specialty contractor classification covering qualifying masonry work performed within the authorized scope of the license.

Masonry work can involve materials such as brick, baked clay products, rough-cut and dressed stone, artificial stone, precast blocks, structural glass brick or block, and related masonry materials.

The trade requires an understanding of complete masonry systems rather than individual materials alone. Contractors should understand the relationship between masonry units, mortar, grout, reinforcement, anchors, supports, openings, wall layout, and structural performance.

The International Building Code supports preparation for the broader construction requirements that can affect masonry. ACI 530/530.1 addresses more specialized structural masonry requirements and specifications. Using both references together helps candidates prepare for the different layers of technical knowledge involved in C-18 work.

The contractor licensing process also includes requirements beyond the trade examination. Applicants must complete the applicable application, qualification, examination, financial, and other licensing requirements before a Nevada contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-18 preparation plan should combine practical masonry knowledge, building-code navigation, structural masonry review, business and law study, and practice-oriented course preparation.

Structural Masonry should receive focused attention. Candidates should understand how masonry units, reinforcement, grout, mortar, anchors, and supports work together within complete structural systems.

Building-Code Requirements should be practiced through the International Building Code. Candidates should learn where broader requirements affecting masonry are located and how those provisions interact with masonry-specific standards.

Masonry Materials should include the characteristics and uses of brick, concrete masonry units, stone, and other masonry products. Understanding the materials makes it easier to interpret code requirements involving structural systems and construction methods.

Mortar and Grout should be studied as separate materials serving different purposes. Candidates should understand how mortar bonds units and how grout is used within reinforced or filled masonry construction.

Reinforcement should receive dedicated review. Study reinforcing steel, placement, interaction with grout, wall systems, openings, and other structural applications.

Wall Systems should include layout, support, reinforcement, anchors, openings, and the relationship between individual masonry components and the completed wall.

Openings should be reviewed for lintels, supports, bearing, reinforcement, and the way doors, windows, and other penetrations affect masonry walls.

Anchors and Connections should be studied for their role in connecting masonry to surrounding construction and supporting multi-component wall systems.

Moisture Control should also be included in preparation. Flashing, drainage, detailing, and proper wall construction can affect the durability and long-term performance of masonry assemblies.

Fire-Resistance Concepts may also interact with masonry walls and building systems. The International Building Code can help candidates review broader requirements affecting rated construction.

The highlighted and tabbed format can make repeated study more efficient. When a practice question is missed, return to the applicable marked section and review the surrounding requirement rather than memorizing only the correct answer.

Tabs can help create familiar navigation points. Repeatedly locating major sections involving structural masonry, reinforcement, walls, or building requirements can help candidates become more comfortable with both references.

Timed reference-navigation practice is also useful. Select a masonry question, identify the central subject, decide whether the International Building Code or ACI 530/530.1 is the better starting point, locate the section, and confirm the requirement.

How does the Masonry Contractor (C-18) exam work?

The Masonry Contractor (C-18) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
