## Exam Details

The Nevada C-18 Masonry Contractor trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who have received examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

- **Exam:** Nevada C-18 Masonry Contractor
- **Number of Questions:** 50
- **Time Allowed:** 2 hours
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 35 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The C-18 examination content is divided into five major technical areas:

- **Safety:** 5 questions
- **Concrete Block:** 20 questions
- **Brick:** 11 questions
- **Specialty Masonry:** 6 questions
- **Mortar and Grout:** 8 questions

Concrete Block is the largest individual category with 20 questions. Candidates should make concrete masonry units a major focus of preparation. Study should include layout, wall construction, bond patterns, cutting, alignment, openings, reinforcement concepts, placement, tools, materials, and practical concrete block installation methods.

Brick contains 11 questions and represents another substantial part of the examination. Candidates should review brick materials, coursing, bonds, joints, layout, cutting, openings, installation methods, workmanship principles, and the practical techniques used to build brick masonry assemblies.

Mortar and Grout contains eight questions. Candidates should understand the purpose and use of mortar and grout, mixing concepts, placement, joints, handling, application, and the relationship between masonry units, reinforcement, grout, and completed masonry construction.

Specialty Masonry contains six questions. Preparation should include rough-cut and dressed stone, artificial stone, structural glass brick or block, precast masonry products, specialty applications, and installation methods that extend beyond standard block and brick work.

Safety contains five questions. Candidates should review OSHA-related construction safety involving scaffolds, ladders, personal protective equipment, fall hazards, tools, cutting operations, lifting and material handling, and other hazards commonly associated with masonry work.

Because candidates have two hours for 50 questions, preparation should include both trade knowledge and efficient use of the approved references. Strong pacing helps prevent excessive time from being spent on individual code searches.

## Open Book Test

The Nevada C-18 Masonry Contractor trade examination is an **open-book test**. Specific approved code references may be used during testing.

The approved exam-room references used for this preparation are:

- **International Building Code, 2018**
- **ACI 530/530.1-13: Building Code Requirements and Specification for Masonry Structures and Companion Commentaries**

The International Building Code supports preparation involving masonry-related building requirements, walls, materials, structural provisions, fire-resistance requirements, openings, and other code topics relevant to masonry construction.

Candidates should practice navigating the IBC by using the index, chapter organization, definitions, tables, section numbers, exceptions, and cross-references. The goal is to know how to find applicable information without having to search the entire book during the examination.

ACI 530/530.1-13 provides masonry-specific code requirements and specifications. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of this reference and practice locating information involving masonry materials, construction requirements, reinforcement, mortar, grout, and related technical provisions.

The following books are study references and are **not allowed into the C-18 trade examination:**

- **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)**
- **Modern Masonry - Brick, Block, Stone, Clois E. Kicklighter, 10th Edition**

Because OSHA and Modern Masonry cannot be used during the examination, candidates should study those books for understanding and retention. Practical construction methods, safety procedures, tools, masonry materials, brick and block installation, stone work, mortar, and grout should become familiar enough to apply without referring to those books during testing.

An open-book exam still rewards preparation. Candidates who try to research every question can lose valuable time. The more practical masonry knowledge a candidate develops before test day, the easier it becomes to reserve reference searches for questions that truly require code verification.

## State Requirements

The Nevada C-18 classification is a specialty contractor classification focused on masonry construction. Contractors should understand the authorized scope of the classification and perform work that falls within the license held by the business.

The classification covers qualifying work involving brick and other baked-clay products, rough-cut and dressed stone, artificial stone, precast blocks, and structural glass brick or block. These materials may be installed at random or in courses and may be laid with or without mortar.

The C-18 classification does not authorize applying tile to existing surfaces. Candidates should understand the distinction between masonry work covered by C-18 and work that requires another Nevada contractor classification.

The Trade Qualified Individual generally must demonstrate at least four years of experience at the journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor level in the specific classification requested. Nevada generally requires that experience to have been obtained within the 15 years immediately preceding the filing of the application.

Applicants should maintain clear documentation showing the type of masonry work performed and the period during which the experience was gained. Experience documentation is an important part of establishing trade qualification.

Nevada contractor licensing distinguishes between management and trade qualification. A single individual may serve as both the Management Qualified Individual and Trade Qualified Individual when all applicable qualifications are satisfied, or separate people may fulfill those responsibilities.

The Management Qualified Individual generally completes the Business and Law examination requirement, while the Trade Qualified Individual establishes the required experience and applicable trade qualification for C-18.

Financial responsibility, background requirements, contractor bonding, and a monetary limit are also part of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Passing the C-18 examination does not by itself result in license issuance.

## How does the Masonry Contractor (C-18) exam work?

The Masonry Contractor (C-18) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
