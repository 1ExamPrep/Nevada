Exam Details

The Nevada Masonry Contractor (C-18) trade examination evaluates knowledge associated with masonry construction and the responsibilities of contractors performing masonry work within the classification.

Candidates should prepare for questions involving brick, concrete masonry units, stone, structural masonry, mortar, grout, reinforcement, wall construction, openings, anchors, flashing, structural requirements, tools, estimating concepts, and jobsite safety.

The International Building Code supports preparation involving building-code requirements that affect masonry construction. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of the code and understand how general building provisions interact with requirements for masonry walls, structural systems, fire-resistance concepts, building components, and other construction conditions.

ACI 530/530.1 provides more specialized structural masonry information. Candidates should understand how to locate requirements involving masonry materials, reinforcement, grout, structural walls, construction specifications, quality, and related masonry provisions.

Practical masonry knowledge is equally important. Modern Masonry can help candidates review brick and block construction, stone masonry, mortar, bonding patterns, tools, layout, wall construction, reinforcement, and installation procedures. Because this book is not allowed inside the examination room, candidates should use it to build understanding that can be applied from memory.

Safety should be incorporated throughout preparation. Masonry work can involve scaffolds, ladders, material handling, cutting equipment, silica-producing operations, masonry saws, lifting, wall construction, overhead hazards, and other conditions addressed through construction-safety practices.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada C-18 trade examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable Nevada contractor licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the masonry examination.

  1. Confirm the C-18 classification. Make sure the masonry work the business intends to perform falls within the Nevada C-18 Masonry classification.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor license application must identify the individual who will qualify on behalf of the business.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience information. Organize applicable work history involving brick, block, stone, structural masonry, mortar, grout, reinforcement, layout, or related masonry responsibilities.
  4. Submit the contractor license application. Complete the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Wait for application and experience review. Examination eligibility is established after the application is submitted and the qualifying experience is reviewed.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. The qualifying individual can schedule the applicable examinations after authorization is issued.
  7. Prepare for the C-18 trade examination. Study masonry materials, construction methods, structural requirements, code navigation, and safety.
  8. Prepare for Nevada business and law. Contractor applicants are also subject to the applicable business and law examination requirements unless a waiver applies.
  9. Complete the required examinations. Satisfy the examinations associated with the requested classification.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish all other applicable requirements before the Nevada contractor license is issued.

State Requirements

Nevada designates C-18 Masonry as a specialty contractor classification. A contractor holding this classification may perform qualifying masonry work involving the selection, cutting, and laying of materials such as brick, other baked clay products, rough-cut and dressed stone, artificial stone, precast blocks, and structural glass brick or block.

The work may involve materials laid at random or in courses and may be performed with or without mortar, depending on the type of masonry installation.

The C-18 classification requires specialized trade knowledge because masonry systems combine material selection, layout, structural considerations, installation methods, reinforcement, mortar or grout, and finished workmanship. A candidate should understand not only how individual units are placed but also how the completed wall or masonry assembly functions.

Candidates should study masonry construction as a complete system. Foundations or supports, wall layout, coursing, bonds, openings, lintels, reinforcement, anchors, control concepts, flashing, mortar, grout, and weather conditions can all affect the performance and appearance of finished masonry.

Nevada contractor licensing also involves requirements beyond the trade examination. The qualifying individual must complete the applicable examination process and the applicant must satisfy the other licensing requirements associated with the requested classification.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-18 study plan should combine practical masonry knowledge, structural masonry requirements, building-code navigation, and construction-safety preparation.

Brick and Block Construction should include unit selection, layout, coursing, bonding, cutting, placement, joints, alignment, and workmanship. Candidates should understand how individual units become part of a complete wall or structure.

Mortar and Grout should be reviewed for their roles in masonry construction. Candidates should understand the difference between mortar and grout, how each is used, and why proper placement and consistency matter.

Reinforcement should receive dedicated attention. Structural masonry can use reinforcing steel, anchors, ties, and related components to improve performance and connect masonry to surrounding construction.

Stone Masonry should be studied for material selection, cutting, placement, bonding, support, and finishing. Stone work can differ substantially from standard concrete masonry or brick construction, so candidates should understand the basic trade principles involved.

Wall Layout and Construction should include dimensions, corners, openings, lines, levels, plumb conditions, coursing, and proper sequencing. Accurate layout is essential because small errors can compound as wall height increases.

Openings and Structural Features should include lintels, supports, reinforcement around openings, bearing conditions, and the way doors, windows, or other penetrations affect masonry systems.

Flashing and Moisture Control should be reviewed as part of wall performance. Candidates should understand how moisture can enter masonry construction and why proper detailing is important.

Structural Masonry Requirements should be practiced through ACI 530/530.1. Learn how the reference is organized and where requirements involving materials, reinforcement, structural walls, specifications, and construction quality can be found.

Building Code Requirements should be practiced through the International Building Code. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of the code and the relationship between general building requirements and masonry construction.

Masonry Tools and Equipment should include tools used for mixing, cutting, laying, aligning, measuring, striking joints, and handling masonry materials. Candidates should understand both the purpose and safe use of common equipment.

Scaffolds and Ladders are important safety subjects because masonry work frequently takes place above ground level. Review safe access, working platforms, fall hazards, and equipment conditions through OSHA study.

Material Handling deserves attention because masonry units, mortar materials, reinforcing steel, and equipment can be heavy. Safe storage, lifting, movement, and staging contribute to both productivity and jobsite safety.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Code-navigation practice should be timed. Choose a masonry question, identify the central subject, decide whether the International Building Code or ACI 530/530.1 is the better starting point, and then locate the requirement efficiently.

For the books that are not allowed in the exam, use active recall. Review a chapter or topic, close the reference, and explain the procedure or answer practice questions without looking back at the text.

Practice questions should be used to identify weak areas. If structural masonry questions are difficult, spend additional time with ACI 530/530.1. If general building-code questions take too long, improve International Building Code navigation. If practical masonry questions are weak, return to Modern Masonry. If safety questions are challenging, increase OSHA review.

How does the Masonry Contractor (C-18) exam work?

The Masonry Contractor (C-18) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
