Exam Details

The Nevada Masonry Contractor (C-18) trade examination evaluates knowledge associated with masonry construction and the technical responsibilities of contractors working within the C-18 classification. Preparation should combine practical masonry knowledge with familiarity using the approved technical references.

Important areas of study include brick and concrete masonry unit construction, stone, structural masonry, mortar, grout, reinforcement, wall systems, openings, lintels, supports, anchors, ties, flashing, moisture control, building-code requirements, structural requirements, layout, and masonry construction methods.

The International Building Code, 2018 provides broader building-code provisions that interact with masonry construction. Candidates can use it when studying requirements affecting structural systems, walls, building components, fire-resistance concepts, and other building-code subjects that relate to masonry work.

ACI 530/530.1-13 provides more specialized requirements for masonry structures. It supports preparation involving masonry materials, reinforcement, grout, structural wall systems, construction specifications, quality requirements, and related structural masonry provisions.

Candidates should learn the role of each book instead of approaching both references as interchangeable sources. Knowing whether a question involves a broader building-code provision or a specialized masonry requirement can make reference navigation more efficient.

State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-18 Masonry as a specialty contractor classification. The classification applies to masonry work involving materials and systems such as brick, baked clay products, stone, artificial stone, precast blocks, structural glass brick or block, and other masonry materials within the authorized scope of the classification.

Masonry contractors work with systems in which individual components must perform together. Masonry units, mortar, grout, reinforcing steel, anchors, supports, flashing, and structural elements all contribute to the completed assembly.

Because masonry can serve both structural and architectural purposes, candidates should understand more than the placement of individual units. Preparation should address wall construction, structural interaction, reinforcement, supports, openings, building-code requirements, and specialized masonry standards.

The contractor licensing process extends beyond passing the C-18 trade examination. Applicants must complete the applicable application, qualification, examination, financial, and other licensing requirements associated with obtaining a Nevada contractor license.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-18 preparation plan should combine trade knowledge, reference-book navigation, business and law review, and practice-oriented course study. The included 1 year of course access provides a structured period for working through these areas.

Structural masonry deserves focused study because it brings together masonry units, mortar, grout, reinforcing steel, anchors, supports, and structural requirements. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of ACI 530/530.1 and understand when this reference is likely to apply.

Brick and block construction preparation can include unit layout, alignment, coursing, bonding, openings, supports, and wall construction. Understanding practical masonry methods makes it easier to interpret technical code questions.

Mortar and grout should be studied as separate materials with distinct functions. Mortar is associated with bonding masonry units, while grout is used within masonry assemblies in applications such as reinforced or filled construction. Candidates should be comfortable recognizing how each material functions within a masonry system.

Reinforcement should receive dedicated attention. Review how reinforcing steel interacts with masonry units, grout, wall systems, openings, and structural assemblies.

Walls and openings can bring several topics together, including lintels, reinforcement, bearing, supports, anchors, and structural continuity. Studying these relationships can help candidates approach questions as complete construction situations rather than isolated facts.

Anchors and ties are important components in masonry construction because they can connect masonry to surrounding construction or connect parts of wall assemblies. Candidates should understand their function within masonry systems.

Moisture control should also be part of trade review. Flashing, drainage, detailing, and appropriate construction practices can influence the long-term performance of masonry assemblies.

Building-code navigation should be practiced through the International Building Code. Rather than attempting to memorize page numbers, candidates can develop familiarity with chapters, section headings, definitions, tables, and index terms.

Structural masonry navigation should be practiced separately using ACI 530/530.1. This helps candidates develop a clearer sense of which reference is appropriate for different types of questions.

The highlighted and tabbed preparation of the supplied books can support repeated study sessions. Use the tabs as navigation points and the highlighted material as a way to return to important provisions during review.

How does the Masonry Contractor (C-18) exam work?

The Masonry Contractor (C-18) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
