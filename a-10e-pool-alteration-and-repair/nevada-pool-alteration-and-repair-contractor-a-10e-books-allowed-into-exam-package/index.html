Exam Details

The Nevada A-10E Pool Alteration And Repair Contractor examination contains 40 questions. Candidates are allowed 1.5 hours to complete the examination and must answer 28 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Filtration/Circulation Systems: 10 questions
  • Maintenance and Operation: 8 questions
  • Accessories and Associated Trades: 11 questions
  • Pool and Spa Business Laws and Rules: 3 questions
  • Design: 5 questions
  • Soils and Earthwork: 3 questions

Accessories and Associated Trades is the largest exam category with 11 questions. This subject can connect to pumps, filters, heaters, controls, lights, finishes, decks, and other components that may be involved in pool alteration or repair work.

Filtration/Circulation Systems accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should understand how water moves through a pool or spa system, the role of pumps and filters, suction and return arrangements, equipment connections, valves, and circulation performance.

Maintenance and Operation accounts for 8 questions. Preparation should include system operation, equipment care, troubleshooting, water movement, routine maintenance concepts, and recognition of problems that may require adjustment, repair, or replacement.

Design accounts for 5 questions and can involve the relationship between equipment, circulation, safety features, access, and the overall pool or spa system. Pool and Spa Business Laws and Rules and Soils and Earthwork account for 3 questions each.

State Requirements

Nevada identifies A-10E as the Maintenance and Repair of Pools and Spas sub-subclassification under the A-10 Commercial and Residential Pools classification.

The scope includes the alteration and repair of plaster, tile, coping, pumps, filters, heaters, decking, pool painting, timers, controls, and lights on residential and commercial spas, pools, fountains, waterfalls, and ponds.

The scope means candidates should be prepared for both equipment and construction-related questions. Pool alteration and repair can involve circulation systems, finishes, heating equipment, controls, lighting-related components, decks, and the interaction between these systems.

The classification is broader than routine cleaning or basic water care. Candidates should understand how physical repairs and equipment modifications affect the function, safety, and performance of a pool or spa.

The trade examination is one part of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, and other requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada A-10E preparation plan should combine pool alteration and repair knowledge with regular practice using the Uniform Swimming Pool, Spa and Hot Tub Code.

Filtration and Circulation Systems should receive significant study time because they account for 10 of the 40 exam questions. Candidates should understand how water moves through the system and how circulation components work together.

Review pumps, filters, suction and return systems, piping, fittings, valves, equipment placement, and the purpose of each major component. Candidates should also understand how circulation problems can affect system performance.

Maintenance and Operation should include equipment inspection, routine care, system operation, troubleshooting, and recognition of problems that may require repair. Even though this package focuses on the exam-room code, strong practical knowledge remains essential.

Accessories and Associated Trades is the largest category. Candidates should review pumps, filters, heaters, timers, controls, lights, decks, finishes, plaster, tile, coping, coatings, and other components that may intersect with alteration and repair work.

Pool heaters should be studied in relation to equipment operation, water flow, system connections, and installation conditions. Timers and controls should be understood as part of circulation, heating, lighting, and other pool functions.

Finishes are important because A-10E work can include plaster, tile, coping, and pool painting. Candidates should understand basic repair considerations, surface preparation, compatibility, and how finish work relates to the existing pool structure.

Decking can also be part of the classification. Study surface conditions, drainage, repair considerations, and the relationship between the deck, pool shell, and surrounding area.

Design should be approached as a systems topic. Candidates should understand how equipment, circulation, access, safety features, finishes, and repairs interact within the overall pool or spa installation.

Soils and Earthwork should include basic concepts involving soil support, settlement, drainage, excavation, backfill, and the effects that ground conditions can have on pool shells, decks, piping, and equipment areas.

The Uniform Swimming Pool, Spa and Hot Tub Code, 2018 should be used throughout preparation. Start by learning its major sections and how topics are organized.

Practice finding subjects such as circulation, pool equipment, spas, barriers, safety provisions, suction systems, and other code-based requirements. The goal is to know where to begin a lookup without wasting time searching the entire book.

How does the Pool Alteration and Repair Contractor (A-10E) exam work?

The Pool Alteration and Repair Contractor (A-10E) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
