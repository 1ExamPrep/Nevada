## State Requirements

Nevada identifies A-10E as the **Maintenance and Repair of Pools and Spas** sub-subclassification under the A-10 Commercial and Residential Pools classification.

The scope covers the **alteration and repair of plaster, tile, coping, pumps, filters, heaters, decking, pool painting, timers, controls, and lights** on residential and commercial spas, pools, fountains, waterfalls, and ponds.

The scope is broader than basic pool cleaning or routine water care. It involves alteration and repair of the physical finishes, circulation equipment, heating equipment, controls, lighting-related components, decks, and other systems associated with pools and spas.

Pool alteration projects may also require an understanding of existing system conditions. Candidates should be able to recognize how pumps, filters, piping, heaters, finishes, decks, and controls work together and how repairs to one component can affect overall system performance.

The A-10E trade exam is one part of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy the applicable application, qualification, business and law, and other requirements before a contractor license is issued.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive A-10E study plan should begin with **Filtration and Circulation Systems**. Candidates should understand how water moves through a pool or spa, from suction points through pumps and filters and back through the return system.

Review pump operation, filter types, circulation components, piping, valves, suction and return arrangements, equipment placement, and common problems that can reduce circulation performance. Equipment troubleshooting is especially important in an alteration and repair classification.

**Maintenance and Operation** should include equipment inspection, circulation performance, water treatment concepts, routine service, cleaning, troubleshooting, and recognition of conditions that require repair or replacement.

The Pool and Spa Operator Handbook and Ultimate Pool Maintenance Manual can support this portion of preparation. Because these references are not permitted during the exam, candidates should gradually transition from reading to active recall and practice questions.

**Accessories and Associated Trades** deserves substantial study time because it is the largest exam category. Review heaters, timers, controls, lights, decking, coatings, plaster, tile, coping, pumps, filters, and the various trades that may intersect with pool alteration work.

Pool heaters require an understanding of installation conditions, water flow, fuel or energy interfaces, equipment clearances, and safe operation. Controls and timers should be studied in relation to circulation equipment, heaters, lighting, and other pool functions.

**Finishes** are another important part of A-10E work. Candidates should understand basic considerations related to plaster, tile, coping, pool painting, surface preparation, repair, and compatibility with existing pool structures.

**Decking** should be reviewed in connection with pool access, drainage, surface conditions, repair methods, and the relationship between the pool shell and surrounding paved areas.

**Design** preparation should focus on understanding how circulation, equipment, access, safety features, and repair decisions affect the overall pool or spa system. Even when a project involves alteration rather than new construction, candidates should understand the original design intent of the system being modified.

**Soils and Earthwork** can affect decks, pool shells, piping, and equipment areas. Study basic concepts involving soil support, settlement, drainage, excavation, backfill, and how ground movement can contribute to cracking or structural problems.

**Pool and Spa Business Laws and Rules** should be reviewed using the Nevada-specific study references. Candidates should understand contractor responsibilities and regulatory requirements relevant to performing pool alteration and repair work.

## How does the Pool Alteration and Repair Contractor (A-10E) exam work?

The Pool Alteration and Repair Contractor (A-10E) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
