Exam Details

The Nevada C-38 Gas Equipment Contractor examination contains 50 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the exam, and the minimum passing score is 35 correct answers.

The examination content is organized into the following major subject areas:

  • Natural Gas Piping: 12 questions.
  • LP Gas Piping: 12 questions.
  • Chimneys, Flues, and Vents: 10 questions.
  • Appliance Installation: 5 questions.
  • Sizing and Estimating: 5 questions.
  • Testing and Inspection: 3 questions.
  • Combustion Air: 3 questions.

Natural Gas Piping and LP Gas Piping are the two largest individual areas, with 12 questions each. Chimneys, Flues, and Vents account for another 10 questions. Together, these three categories make up most of the examination, so candidates should devote substantial study time to piping systems, venting, gas characteristics, materials, sizing, and installation practices.

Appliance Installation, Sizing and Estimating, Testing and Inspection, and Combustion Air should not be overlooked. These subjects connect closely with the larger piping and venting categories and may require candidates to apply multiple concepts within one question.

With 50 questions and two hours available, candidates have an average of about two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Efficient reference use can be valuable, but candidates should also develop enough trade knowledge to answer straightforward questions without searching the books unnecessarily.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-38 classification covers the specialty of installing equipment used with liquefied petroleum and natural gas. A contractor holding the full C-38 classification may perform work authorized under its related subclassifications.

The Pipes and Vents portion of the classification includes installing pipes from the source of liquefied petroleum or natural gas supply into a building or other structure and installing related vents. It also includes installation of and connections to above-ground tanks containing liquefied petroleum or natural gas within the authorized scope.

The Gas Appliances and Equipment portion includes installation of appliances and equipment that require connection to a supply of liquefied petroleum or natural gas.

The Heating and Ventilating portion includes fabrication and installation of systems used to heat air with liquefied petroleum or natural gas and circulate that heated air.

The areas explain why candidates need preparation across both mechanical and fuel-gas subjects. A C-38 contractor must understand the gas supply system, equipment connection, venting, combustion requirements, and installation methods as parts of an integrated system.

The contractor licensing process also includes requirements beyond technical knowledge. Applicants must satisfy the application, qualifying experience, examination, financial, and other applicable licensing requirements before a Nevada contractor license can be issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-38 study plan should follow the examination content areas and give the greatest amount of time to natural gas piping, LP-gas piping, and chimneys, flues, and vents.

Natural Gas Piping accounts for 12 questions. Candidates should study gas piping materials, installation practices, sizing concepts, pressure, fittings, valves, appliance connections, testing, and related system requirements. NFPA 54 is particularly useful for building knowledge in this area before test day.

LP Gas Piping also accounts for 12 questions. NFPA 58 should be used to study liquefied petroleum gas systems, container locations, piping, pressure regulation, system components, equipment, installation, and safety practices.

Candidates should understand that LP gas and natural gas have different characteristics and system requirements. Study the differences in storage, delivery, pressure regulation, equipment, and installation practices rather than treating the two fuel types as interchangeable.

Chimneys, Flues, and Vents account for 10 questions. Review venting principles, appliance venting, combustion products, vent sizing concepts, vent connectors, clearances, termination, and the relationship between appliances and the venting system.

Improper venting can create serious safety concerns, so candidates should understand why vent systems are designed and installed according to specific requirements. Use the Uniform Mechanical Code and NFPA 54 during preparation to reinforce these concepts.

Appliance Installation accounts for five questions. Review appliance connections, location considerations, access, clearances, shutoff requirements, equipment installation, support, ventilation, and manufacturer-related installation concepts.

Sizing and Estimating also accounts for five questions. Candidates should practice basic gas-system sizing concepts, demand calculations, pipe-sizing methods, and other trade calculations associated with fuel-gas installation.

Pipe-sizing questions often require a clear understanding of total demand, system length, pressure conditions, and the applicable sizing method. Practice reading tables and identifying the information required before selecting a pipe size.

Testing and Inspection accounts for three questions. Candidates should understand the purpose of pressure testing, inspection procedures, leak checks, and system verification before fuel-gas systems are placed into service.

Combustion Air accounts for three questions. Study why gas appliances require adequate combustion air and how room volume, openings, ventilation, appliance input, and installation conditions can affect combustion-air requirements.

Reference-navigation drills can improve performance on an open-book exam. Choose a topic such as LP-gas container location, appliance venting, combustion air, or mechanical equipment installation and practice locating the applicable provision in the approved books.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Use keywords from the question to guide the search. If a question concerns LP-gas storage or containers, NFPA 58 is likely the starting point. If it concerns mechanical appliance installation or general mechanical venting, the Uniform Mechanical Code may be more useful.

NFPA 54 should be handled differently because it is not an exam-room reference. After studying a section, close the book and answer practice questions from memory. Return to the reference only after completing the questions so you can identify what needs further review.

Practice questions should be used to diagnose weaknesses rather than simply memorize answers. If natural gas piping questions are difficult, dedicate additional time to pipe sizing and fuel-gas installation concepts. If venting questions are weak, return to chimney, flue, combustion-air, and appliance-venting material.

Timed practice is also useful. With 50 questions in two hours, candidates should learn to recognize when a reference search is worth the time and when an answer can be selected confidently from existing trade knowledge.

How does the Gas Equipment Contractor (C-38) exam work?

The Gas Equipment Contractor (C-38) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
