Exam Details

The Nevada C-38 Gas Equipment Contractor examination contains 50 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the examination, and the minimum passing score is 35 correct answers.

The examination content includes the following subject areas:

  • Natural Gas Piping: 12 questions.
  • LP Gas Piping: 12 questions.
  • Chimneys, Flues, and Vents: 10 questions.
  • Appliance Installation: 5 questions.
  • Sizing and Estimating: 5 questions.
  • Testing and Inspection: 3 questions.
  • Combustion Air: 3 questions.

Natural Gas Piping and LP Gas Piping are the two largest individual sections of the examination, with 12 questions each. Chimneys, Flues, and Vents account for another 10 questions. Candidates should give these areas significant study time while still preparing for appliance installation, sizing, testing, inspection, and combustion-air questions.

With 50 questions and two hours available, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Reference books can be useful for confirming technical requirements, but searching every question may use too much time. Candidates should develop enough trade knowledge to recognize straightforward answers and reserve code lookups for questions where confirmation is useful.

The examination may require candidates to apply practical gas-equipment knowledge in addition to locating code information. Preparation should therefore combine code study with an understanding of fuel-gas systems, mechanical equipment, piping, venting, combustion, pressure, appliances, and safe installation practices.

  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor application must designate the person who provides the trade qualification for the requested classification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable experience involving gas piping, vents, appliances, equipment, heating, ventilation, liquefied petroleum gas, natural gas, or related work.

Exam preparation can begin while the licensing application is being assembled. Starting early gives candidates more time to become comfortable with both the Uniform Mechanical Code and NFPA 58 rather than attempting to learn the references immediately before testing.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-38 classification is for contractors installing equipment used with liquefied petroleum and natural gas. A contractor holding the full C-38 classification may perform the work authorized under its related subclassifications.

The C-38A Pipes and Vents subclassification includes installation of pipes from the source of liquefied petroleum or natural gas supply into a building or other structure and installation of related vents. Its scope also includes installation of and connections to above-ground tanks containing liquefied petroleum or natural gas.

The C-38B Gas Appliances and Equipment subclassification includes installation of appliances and equipment requiring connection to a supply of liquefied petroleum or natural gas.

The C-38C Heating and Ventilating subclassification includes fabrication and installation of systems used to heat air with liquefied petroleum or natural gas and circulate that air.

The full C-38 classification therefore involves several connected areas of trade knowledge. Candidates should understand the gas supply, piping, appliances, equipment, venting, combustion, and heating-system requirements that work together in a complete installation.

The Nevada contractor licensing process includes more than passing the trade examination. Applicants must also satisfy the applicable application, qualifying experience, financial, examination, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license can be issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-38 study plan should follow the examination content areas and devote substantial time to natural gas piping, LP-gas piping, and venting.

Natural Gas Piping accounts for 12 questions. Candidates should review piping materials, valves, fittings, pressure concepts, sizing principles, appliance connections, system installation, testing, and general fuel-gas piping practices.

Because the exam-room package focuses on the Uniform Mechanical Code and NFPA 58, candidates should build strong natural-gas knowledge during preparation instead of assuming every natural-gas question can be answered through a code search on test day.

LP Gas Piping also accounts for 12 questions. NFPA 58 is especially important for this subject. Review LP-gas containers, regulators, piping systems, equipment, pressure, installation requirements, clearances, location considerations, and safety.

Candidates should understand the practical differences between liquefied petroleum gas and natural gas systems. Fuel characteristics, storage, pressure regulation, delivery, and equipment arrangements can affect installation requirements.

Chimneys, Flues, and Vents account for 10 questions. Review appliance venting, vent connectors, flues, clearances, vent-system operation, combustion products, termination, and the relationship between appliances and their vent systems.

Venting knowledge is critical because gas-fired appliances must safely discharge combustion products. Candidates should understand both the purpose of a venting requirement and how different installation conditions may affect the correct approach.

Appliance Installation accounts for five questions. Study equipment location, access, clearances, connections, shutoff provisions, support, ventilation, and installation considerations associated with gas-burning appliances.

Sizing and Estimating accounts for five questions. Candidates should practice fuel-gas sizing concepts, system demand, piping capacity, length, equipment input, and other calculations associated with gas-system design and installation.

When working with tables, identify the information required before attempting to select an answer. A sizing question may depend on system length, demand, pressure, pipe material, or other conditions. Reading the question carefully can prevent unnecessary searches.

Testing and Inspection accounts for three questions. Review pressure testing, leak checking, inspection, system verification, and the procedures used to determine whether a gas installation is ready for service.

Combustion Air accounts for three questions. Candidates should understand why gas appliances require adequate combustion air and how equipment input, enclosure conditions, openings, and ventilation can affect the installation.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Timed reference-navigation practice can improve exam efficiency. Choose a topic such as LP-gas container location, appliance venting, combustion air, or mechanical equipment installation and practice locating the applicable requirement in the appropriate book.

NFPA 58 should become familiar enough that candidates can quickly move between major LP-gas subjects. Practice using the table of contents, index, definitions, and chapter organization rather than relying on random page searches.

The Uniform Mechanical Code should also be used repeatedly for mechanical-system topics. Become familiar with its structure so questions involving equipment, venting, combustion air, or gas-related mechanical requirements can be approached efficiently.

Practice questions should be used to identify weak areas. If LP-gas questions are difficult, spend additional time in NFPA 58. If appliance, venting, or combustion-air questions are challenging, return to the relevant mechanical-code material.

Do not treat open-book preparation as a substitute for studying. Candidates who understand gas-system principles can use the books more strategically because they know what kind of requirement they are trying to confirm.

A balanced preparation routine should include reading, code searches, calculations, trade questions, and timed practice. Combining these methods helps candidates develop both technical knowledge and effective open-book exam habits.

How does the Gas Equipment Contractor (C-38) exam work?

The Gas Equipment Contractor (C-38) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
