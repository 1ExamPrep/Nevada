About this Finish Carpentry Contractor (C-3B) package

Prepare for the Nevada Finish Carpentry Contractor (C-3B) trade examination with online exam prep organized around the specific knowledge areas tested for this specialty classification. The course focuses on fabrication, installation, finish-carpentry materials, finishing methods, hand tools, construction safety, machines, plans, and estimating, giving candidates a structured way to prepare for Nevada's closed-book C-3B examination.

The Nevada C-3B Finish Carpentry classification is regulated by the Nevada State Contractors Board. Its scope includes the installation, repair, and finishing of cabinets, sash casing, door casing, wooden flooring, baseboards, countertops, and other millwork. The classification does not include installation of ceramic tile, marble, or artificial or cultured stone.

The C-3B examination is closed book. Candidates cannot take the technical references into the examination room, which makes preparation and retention especially important. Instead of depending on reference navigation during testing, candidates should develop a working understanding of finish-carpentry methods, materials, tools, safety practices, fabrication procedures, and installation techniques before exam day.

The study references for this examination include Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA), Finish Carpenter's Manual, Jim Tolpin, 1993, and The Complete Illustrated Guide to Furniture and Cabinet Construction, 2001. Each book supports a different part of the examination, from construction safety to practical finish-carpentry methods and cabinet fabrication.

Online exam prep provides an organized approach to these subjects through trade-focused review and practice-oriented preparation. Candidates can use the course to identify weaker areas, reinforce important concepts, and build familiarity with the terminology and problem-solving methods used in finish carpentry.

Exam Details

The Nevada C-3B Finish Carpentry examination is administered through PSI as part of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable Nevada State Contractors Board qualification process and receive examination eligibility before testing.

  • Exam: Nevada C-3B Finish Carpentry
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 30
  • Time Allowed: 1 hour
  • Minimum Passing Score: 21 correct answers

The examination is divided into eight subject areas:

  • Fabrication: 6 questions
  • Installation: 5 questions
  • Materials: 5 questions
  • Finishing: 4 questions
  • Hand Tools: 3 questions
  • Safety: 3 questions
  • Machines: 2 questions
  • Plans and Estimating: 2 questions

Fabrication is the largest individual category. Candidates should understand measuring, layout, cutting, fitting, joinery, assembly, cabinet components, millwork fabrication, and the steps required to produce accurate finish-carpentry work.

Installation accounts for five questions and should include cabinets, sash and door casing, baseboards, countertops, wooden flooring, trim, millwork, fastening, fitting, alignment, and final adjustment. Candidates should understand both the sequence of installation and the methods used to produce a professional finished result.

Materials also accounts for five questions. Preparation should include wood products, manufactured materials, fasteners, adhesives, hardware, trim components, cabinet materials, and other products used in finish carpentry. Understanding how material characteristics affect cutting, fastening, installation, and finishing can help candidates evaluate technical questions more effectively.

Finishing includes four questions. Study surface preparation, sanding, filling, coating concepts, final appearance, and the steps used to prepare finished woodwork and millwork.

Hand Tools, Safety, and Machines require candidates to understand appropriate tool selection, safe operation, common hazards, and practical applications. Plans and Estimating adds questions involving dimensions, quantities, measurements, and basic interpretation of project information.

Closed Book Test

The Nevada C-3B Finish Carpentry trade examination is a closed-book test. The technical books used to prepare the examination are not allowed into the examination center.

The following references should be used during preparation but cannot be taken into the C-3B trade exam:

  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
  • Finish Carpenter's Manual, Jim Tolpin, 1993
  • The Complete Illustrated Guide to Furniture and Cabinet Construction, 2001

Closed-book testing changes the way candidates should prepare. Instead of concentrating on where information is located in a book, study sessions should emphasize understanding, repetition, practical application, and recall.

The Finish Carpenter's Manual should be used to review finish-carpentry procedures, tools, trim, fitting, installation, materials, and practical woodworking methods. Candidates should become comfortable recognizing the correct procedure for common finish-carpentry situations.

The Complete Illustrated Guide to Furniture and Cabinet Construction supports study of cabinetry, furniture construction, fabrication, components, joinery, materials, and assembly techniques. Cabinet and millwork knowledge is particularly useful because fabrication and installation represent a substantial part of the examination.

29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA) supports preparation for the Safety portion of the examination. Candidates should understand common construction hazards and appropriate safety practices without depending on the reference during testing.

Because candidates have only one hour for 30 questions, effective preparation should also include pacing. The average testing time is approximately two minutes per question, so candidates should practice reading questions carefully while making decisions efficiently.

Licensing Steps

The C-3B examination is one part of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Applicants must satisfy the applicable Nevada State Contractors Board requirements in addition to preparing for and completing the trade examination.

  1. Confirm the C-3B classification. Make sure Finish Carpentry matches the work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. Determine who will satisfy the applicable trade qualification requirements for the contractor license.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare records demonstrating finish-carpentry experience related to the C-3B classification.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor application. Gather the required business, qualifier, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application. Provide the required materials for Nevada State Contractors Board review.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow the fingerprint and background procedures required during the contractor licensing process.
  7. Provide applicable financial information. Nevada evaluates financial responsibility as part of contractor licensing.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Qualified applicants may proceed with testing after the applicable application review.
  9. Prepare for the C-3B trade examination. Study Fabrication, Installation, Materials, Finishing, Hand Tools, Safety, Machines, and Plans and Estimating.
  10. Prepare for contractor management requirements. Nevada contractor applicants generally must satisfy the applicable Business and Law examination requirement unless an approved waiver applies.
  11. Complete the required examinations. Satisfy the applicable trade and contractor management requirements.
  12. Complete final licensing requirements. Address applicable financial responsibility, bonding, and remaining Board requirements before license issuance.

Passing the C-3B trade examination does not by itself issue a Nevada contractor license. The examination should be approached as one important part of the complete licensing process.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-3B Finish Carpentry classification authorizes the installation, repair, and finishing of cabinets, sash casing, door casing, wooden flooring, baseboards, countertops, and other millwork.

The classification specifically excludes installation of ceramic tile, marble, and artificial or cultured stone. Candidates should understand these boundaries because contractor classifications define the type of work a licensed business is authorized to perform.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the trade qualifying individual generally must demonstrate at least four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the classification requested.

The qualifying experience generally must have been obtained within the 15 years immediately preceding the application. Experience documentation should clearly describe the type of finish-carpentry work performed and how that experience relates to the C-3B classification.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes applicable financial responsibility, bonding, and background requirements. These obligations are separate from passing the trade examination and must be completed as part of the overall licensing process.

Applicants generally must satisfy the applicable trade and contractor management qualification requirements unless an approved waiver applies. The contractor licensing process therefore requires preparation for more than the technical C-3B examination alone.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-3B study plan should follow the exam outline rather than reviewing finish carpentry as one broad subject. Dividing preparation into the eight tested categories helps candidates see where additional work is needed.

Prioritize Fabrication. With six questions, Fabrication is the largest category. Review measuring, layout, cutting, joinery, fitting, assembly, cabinet construction, and millwork fabrication.

Give Installation substantial attention. Installation contains five questions. Review cabinet placement, casing, baseboards, wooden flooring, countertops, trim, millwork, fastening, alignment, and fitting procedures.

Develop strong Materials knowledge. Materials also contains five questions. Learn the characteristics and applications of wood products, manufactured materials, hardware, fasteners, adhesives, trims, and cabinet components.

Study Finishing as a separate category. Four questions focus on finishing. Review sanding, surface preparation, filling, coating concepts, and procedures used to create completed finish work.

Know your hand tools. Review tool selection, measurement tools, cutting tools, shaping tools, fastening tools, and the safe methods associated with each.

Review machine use and safety. Candidates should understand common woodworking machines, their applications, and basic safe operating principles.

Study OSHA material for retention. Because the Safety reference cannot be used during the exam, review safety principles until common hazards and protective measures can be recognized without looking them up.

Practice Plans and Estimating. Review dimensions, measurements, quantities, material calculations, and interpretation of basic project information.

Use timed practice questions. Thirty questions in one hour means candidates need to maintain a steady pace. Practice sessions can help develop the habit of reading carefully without spending unnecessary time on a single question.

Review mistakes by category. If missed questions repeatedly involve fabrication, finishing, materials, or safety, use those results to guide the next study session. Focused review is generally more useful than repeating subjects that are already familiar.

How does the Finish Carpentry Contractor (C-3B) exam work?

The Finish Carpentry Contractor (C-3B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
