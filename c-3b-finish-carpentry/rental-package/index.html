Exam Details

The Nevada Finish Carpentry Contractor (C-3B) trade examination focuses on knowledge associated with finish carpentry and related woodworking and installation practices. Candidates should prepare for concepts involving cabinets, millwork, trim, furniture construction, measuring, layout, fitting, fastening, hand tools, power tools, materials, and construction safety.

Finish carpentry requires a higher level of visual accuracy than many rough construction activities. Small errors in layout, cutting, fastening, alignment, or fitting can affect the finished appearance of cabinets, trim, casing, baseboards, and other millwork. That makes precision an important part of exam preparation.

Candidates should become comfortable with practical questions involving the selection of tools, materials, joints, fasteners, and installation methods. Study should also include cabinet construction, door and drawer components, hardware, finish woodworking techniques, and common jobsite safety practices.

Because the C-3B trade test is closed book, candidates should focus on building working knowledge rather than relying on reference navigation. Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-3B study plan should divide preparation into manageable categories such as finish carpentry methods, cabinets, woodworking, trim, tools, layout, materials, safety, and business and law.

Cabinet construction should be a major study area. Review cabinet boxes, frames, doors, drawers, shelves, backs, hardware, fasteners, joints, and assembly methods. Understanding how these parts work together makes installation questions easier to interpret.

Cabinet installation requires accurate measuring and sequence. Study leveling, plumb, shimming, fastening, joining, spacing, alignment, door adjustment, drawer operation, and final fit.

Furniture and cabinet joinery should also receive focused review. Learn common woodworking joints and understand where different types are used. Joinery affects strength, durability, alignment, and appearance.

Trim and millwork should include baseboards, casing, moldings, built-ins, and other finished wood components. Review measuring, cutting, fitting, fastening, corner treatment, and methods for producing consistent finished work.

Mitering and coping are especially useful finish carpentry concepts. Candidates should understand when each method is appropriate and how accurate fitting affects the final appearance of trim.

Doors, drawers, and hardware should also be reviewed. Study hinges, slides, adjustments, clearances, alignment, installation, and component relationships.

Measuring and layout should be practiced throughout preparation. Finish carpentry depends on accuracy, so candidates should be comfortable checking dimensions, square, level, plumb, alignment, spacing, and reveals.

Hand and power tools are another important study category. Review the intended uses of common saws, drills, routers, nailers, sanders, chisels, planes, measuring tools, and other woodworking equipment.

OSHA construction safety should be incorporated into regular study. Candidates should understand safe practices involving ladders, hand tools, power tools, materials, work areas, electrical hazards, and personal protective equipment.

Business and law preparation should be treated as a separate study track. Use the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada along with the course materials to build familiarity with contractor business topics and licensing responsibilities.

The 6 months of course access allows candidates to work through preparation over a longer period rather than compressing all study into a short schedule. A consistent weekly routine can help candidates retain more information and identify weak areas earlier.

Practice questions should be completed without the trade books open to match the closed-book nature of the examination. When a question is missed, return to the highlighted section in the reference and review the underlying concept.

The goal is to turn book knowledge into practical understanding. Because the trade exam is closed book, candidates should be able to recognize the correct procedure, tool, material, or safety practice without needing to search for it.

How does the Finish Carpentry Contractor (C-3B) exam work?

The Finish Carpentry Contractor (C-3B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
