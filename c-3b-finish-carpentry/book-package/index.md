## Exam Details

The Nevada C-3B Finish Carpentry trade examination evaluates knowledge associated with the installation, repair, and finishing of cabinets, trim, flooring, countertops, millwork, and related finish carpentry components.

Candidates should prepare for questions that require practical understanding rather than simple memorization. Finish carpentry often involves selecting the correct material, tool, joint, fastening method, installation sequence, or measuring procedure for a particular situation.

Important areas of preparation include cabinets, casework, doors and trim, baseboards, moldings, countertops, wooden flooring, furniture and cabinet construction, woodworking techniques, hardware, fastening methods, layout, measuring, cutting, finishing, and jobsite safety.

Precision is a major theme in finish carpentry. Small measuring or layout errors can create visible gaps, uneven reveals, poorly fitting cabinets, misaligned trim, or other workmanship problems. Candidates should therefore be comfortable with common measuring techniques, layout practices, cutting procedures, and methods for checking level, plumb, square, and alignment.

Cabinet and millwork questions may involve component identification, construction techniques, installation, fastening, adjustment, hardware, joints, and materials. Candidates should understand how cabinets and built-in components are assembled and how they are properly installed in finished spaces.

Safety should also be part of exam preparation. Finish carpenters frequently work with portable power tools, saws, ladders, hand tools, adhesives, fasteners, and other equipment that can create jobsite hazards. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 provides important construction safety material for study.

## Closed Book Test

Repeated study is especially useful for safety material. Instead of trying to memorize a large number of isolated requirements at once, connect safety concepts to actual activities such as using a circular saw, operating a table saw, working from a ladder, handling materials, or maintaining a clean work area.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A structured Nevada C-3B study plan should divide preparation into practical categories such as cabinets, trim and millwork, doors, flooring, countertops, woodworking, tools, measurements, installation methods, and construction safety.

**Cabinet installation and construction** should be a major area of preparation. Candidates should understand cabinet components, face frames, frameless construction concepts, shelves, backs, doors, drawers, hardware, fastening methods, and installation procedures.

Study how cabinets are positioned, leveled, plumbed, shimmed, secured, joined, and adjusted. Proper cabinet installation requires accurate layout and attention to surrounding walls, floors, ceilings, appliances, and other finished components.

**Furniture and cabinet joints** should also be reviewed. Become familiar with common woodworking joints and understand why different joints are chosen for particular applications. Joinery affects strength, appearance, alignment, and the durability of finished woodworking.

**Doors and drawers** require additional preparation. Review cabinet-door construction, hinge types, drawer construction, slides, hardware, adjustment, clearances, fitting, and common installation considerations.

**Trim and millwork** are central to finish carpentry. Study baseboards, casing, moldings, jambs, built-ins, and other decorative or functional wood components. Understand how each component is measured, cut, fitted, fastened, and finished.

**Mitering and coping** should be practiced carefully. Finish carpenters frequently encounter inside and outside corners that require precise cutting and fitting. Candidates should understand when different techniques are used and how inaccurate cuts affect the finished appearance.

**Door casing and sash casing** fall directly within the Nevada C-3B scope. Review layout, reveals, fastening, joint alignment, measuring, fitting, and methods for producing consistent finished work around openings.

**Baseboards** should be studied as both a measuring and installation subject. Candidates should understand corner treatment, joints, fastening, wall irregularities, and how baseboard installation relates to finished flooring and other trim.

**Wooden flooring** is also included in the C-3B scope. Preparation should include general installation concepts, material handling, layout, fastening, fitting, transitions, and finish considerations appropriate to wood flooring work.

**Countertops** require careful measuring, fitting, fastening, joining, and coordination with cabinets. Candidates should understand basic countertop installation concepts and how inaccuracies in cabinet placement can affect the finished countertop.

**Measuring and layout** should be practiced throughout preparation rather than treated as a separate subject. Finish carpentry demands accuracy, so candidates should be comfortable using common measuring tools and checking square, level, plumb, dimensions, and alignment.

**Material selection** can influence both appearance and performance. Study common woods, manufactured panels, cabinet materials, trim products, fasteners, adhesives, finishes, and hardware used in finish carpentry.

**OSHA construction safety** should be incorporated into regular study sessions. Review requirements and safe practices associated with ladders, hand tools, power tools, material handling, electrical hazards, personal protective equipment, housekeeping, and other conditions finish carpenters may encounter.

Scenario-based questions are useful for closed-book preparation. Ask yourself how you would install a cabinet on an uneven wall, fit trim at an inside corner, correct a door that does not align, choose a joint for a cabinet component, or safely perform a task with a portable power tool.

Practice questions should be completed without the references open. After each session, review incorrect answers and determine whether the problem came from terminology, measuring, installation technique, woodworking knowledge, or safety.

Repeated recall is one of the most useful approaches for a closed-book exam. The more often candidates retrieve the information from memory and apply it to realistic trade situations, the more familiar the material becomes.

## How does the Finish Carpentry Contractor (C-3B) exam work?

The Finish Carpentry Contractor (C-3B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
