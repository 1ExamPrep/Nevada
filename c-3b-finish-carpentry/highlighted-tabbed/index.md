## Exam Details

The Nevada Finish Carpentry Contractor (C-3B) examination focuses on trade knowledge associated with finish carpentry and related woodworking and installation practices.

Candidates should prepare for practical questions involving finish work, cabinet and furniture construction, measurements, layout, tools, materials, installation methods, joinery, fastening, and safety.

Finish carpentry is different from rough carpentry because finished work is highly visible. Small errors in measuring, cutting, alignment, joint preparation, or installation can affect the final appearance of cabinets, trim, doors, baseboards, and other millwork.

That makes accuracy a major part of preparation. Candidates should be comfortable with common measuring procedures, checking level and plumb, maintaining square, establishing consistent reveals, fitting components to irregular surfaces, and selecting appropriate tools and fastening methods.

Cabinet-related knowledge should also receive careful study. Review basic cabinet components, case construction, face frames, doors, drawers, shelves, backs, hardware, fastening, alignment, installation, and adjustment.

Furniture and woodworking knowledge can reinforce these subjects by helping candidates understand joinery, material behavior, assembly methods, hardware, and the relationships between individual components.

Construction safety is another important part of professional finish carpentry. Candidates should review safe practices involving hand tools, power tools, ladders, material handling, personal protective equipment, work areas, and other construction-related hazards.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-3B study plan should divide preparation into major finish carpentry categories and review each area repeatedly until the information can be applied without the books open.

**Cabinet construction** should be an important study area. Review cabinet boxes, face frames, doors, drawers, shelves, backs, hardware, fastening, joints, alignment, and installation.

**Cabinet installation** requires accurate measuring and careful sequence. Study how cabinets are laid out, leveled, plumbed, shimmed, joined, secured, and adjusted after installation.

**Furniture and cabinet joinery** should also be reviewed. Learn common woodworking joints and understand where different joints are used. Joinery affects strength, appearance, alignment, and the durability of finished work.

**Trim and millwork** are central to finish carpentry. Candidates should study baseboards, casing, moldings, built-ins, and other finish components. Review measuring, cutting, fitting, fastening, and methods for achieving consistent joints and clean finished appearance.

**Mitering and coping** are especially important finish carpentry techniques. Understand how inside and outside corners are approached and why different methods may be selected depending on the material and installation.

**Door and window trim** should be reviewed for layout, reveal, fastening, alignment, joint preparation, and fitting around openings.

**Baseboard installation** should include corner treatment, joints, fastening, wall irregularities, transitions, and relationships with finished flooring.

**Countertop-related finish work** should include measuring, fitting, alignment, support, fastening, and coordination with cabinets and surrounding surfaces.

**Woodworking materials** are another useful study category. Review common solid woods, sheet goods, trim materials, fasteners, adhesives, hardware, and finishing products commonly encountered in cabinet and finish carpentry work.

**Hand and power tools** should be studied both for function and safety. Candidates should understand the appropriate uses of common saws, drills, routers, nailers, sanders, chisels, measuring tools, planes, and other woodworking equipment.

**Layout and measurement** should be part of every study session. Finish carpentry depends heavily on accuracy, so candidates should be comfortable checking dimensions, level, plumb, square, alignment, spacing, and consistent reveals.

**OSHA construction safety** should also be reviewed regularly. Finish carpenters frequently work with cutting tools, ladders, power equipment, fasteners, materials, and active jobsite conditions. Study the safety practices associated with those activities.

## How does the Finish Carpentry Contractor (C-3B) exam work?

The Finish Carpentry Contractor (C-3B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
