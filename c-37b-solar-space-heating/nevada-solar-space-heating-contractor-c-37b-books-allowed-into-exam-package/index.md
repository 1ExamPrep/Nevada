## State Requirements

Nevada identifies **C-37B** as the Solar Space Heating subclassification under the broader C-37 Solar Contractor classification.

The C-37B scope covers the **installation of systems for the collection of solar energy to heat water or air which is then used to heat structures housing persons, animals, or inanimate objects**.

The scope includes **storage, piping, and heat exchangers**. Equipment used for pools is excluded from the C-37B Solar Space Heating classification.

Typical C-37B work can involve solar collectors, circulation piping, pumps, storage tanks, heat exchangers, valves, controls, insulation, and related thermal equipment used for space heating applications.

Understanding how those components operate together is important because the examination tests complete system concepts rather than only isolated components. The exam outline combines System Design, Piping, Collectors, Pumps and Storage Systems, System Valves and Controls, and Safety.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-37B preparation plan should begin with **System Design** because it represents 15 of the 45 examination questions.

Candidates should understand how solar collectors, piping, pumps, storage tanks, heat exchangers, valves, controls, and heating distribution components work together as a complete solar space heating system rather than memorizing each component separately.

Follow the path of thermal energy through the system. Understand how solar energy is captured by the collectors, transferred into water or air, moved through the system, stored when necessary, and ultimately used to heat a structure.

**Piping** represents 10 questions and is one of the strongest areas for direct Uniform Plumbing Code preparation. Candidates should review pipe materials, fittings, joining methods, supports, routing, insulation, valves, and connections.

The **Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018** should be used throughout preparation rather than only during the final days before the examination. Repeated use helps candidates become more familiar with the structure of the code.

Practice locating code requirements by topic. Start with a question involving pipe material, identify the important term, use the index, and locate the applicable provision. Repeat the process with fittings, supports, valves, and connections.

**Definitions** can be important because plumbing terminology may have specific code meanings. Understanding those definitions can help determine which requirement applies to an installation.

**Tables** should receive regular attention. Important requirements involving materials, supports, sizing, or installation conditions may appear in table format rather than paragraph text.

**Exceptions** can modify a general requirement. Candidates should read surrounding language carefully so they do not overlook conditions that change how a rule applies.

**Cross-references** may direct the reader to another section containing additional requirements. Practice following those references rather than assuming the first section contains the complete answer.

**Collectors** accounts for 7 questions. Candidates should understand collector purpose, layout, orientation, mounting concepts, circulation, and how collectors integrate with the rest of a solar heating system.

Review how collector arrangement can affect flow and how thermal energy moves from the collector into the working medium.

The C-37B scope involves systems that may heat **water or air**. Candidates should understand that solar space heating can use different thermal-transfer approaches depending on the system design.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

**Pumps and Storage Systems** accounts for 5 questions. Candidates should understand circulation pumps, storage tanks, heat exchangers, thermal storage concepts, and the relationship between collected energy and heating demand.

Review how pumps move fluid through collectors and piping and how storage can allow collected solar heat to be used later when needed.

**Heat exchangers** deserve focused study because they are specifically included in the C-37B scope. Candidates should understand their purpose and how they transfer thermal energy between separate parts of a heating system.

**System Valves and Controls** accounts for 3 questions. Candidates should review shutoff valves, check valves, balancing devices, temperature controls, differential controls, and other components that regulate operation.

**Safety** accounts for 5 questions. Candidates should prepare for practical hazards involving roof access, ladders, elevated work, piping tools, hot surfaces, electrical control components, lifting, and other construction activities.

Because this package contains only the exam-room-approved Uniform Plumbing Code, candidates should make sure their broader preparation also covers solar system design, collectors, pumps, storage, heat exchangers, controls, and safety through technical study materials and trade experience.

**Heat transfer** is central to solar space heating. Candidates should understand how solar radiation becomes usable thermal energy and how that heat is transferred into water or air for heating a structure.

**Storage** is an important design consideration because solar energy availability and space-heating demand do not always occur at the same time. Candidates should understand the general role of thermal storage in a complete system.

**System flow** should also receive attention. Proper circulation affects collector performance and heat delivery, making balanced flow an important practical concept.

**Freeze protection** can be relevant because collectors and piping may be exposed to outdoor temperatures. Candidates should understand general system-design approaches used to protect exposed components from damaging conditions.

**Insulation** helps reduce unwanted heat loss between the collector, storage system, heat exchanger, and heating load. Candidates should understand why solar thermal piping is insulated and how outdoor exposure can affect performance.

**Thermal expansion** should also be considered in systems exposed to elevated temperatures. Materials, supports, and system layout all affect how pipe movement is accommodated.

Once individual topics become familiar, move into mixed-question practice. The actual examination can shift quickly from a piping requirement to a system-design question, collector concept, storage issue, control question, or safety topic.

Timed UPC lookup drills can improve open-book performance. Select a plumbing topic and practice locating the applicable requirement within a limited amount of time.

Do not expect the Uniform Plumbing Code to answer every question. The UPC is most useful for targeted plumbing requirements, while many practical solar space heating questions require retained technical knowledge.

When a practice question is missed, identify the cause. The issue may involve weak system knowledge, unfamiliar terminology, slow code navigation, or difficulty understanding how multiple components interact.

Correcting the underlying weakness is more useful than memorizing one answer. This approach builds stronger preparation for unfamiliar questions on test day.

Time management should also be part of preparation. Answer familiar questions efficiently, use the UPC for targeted lookups, mark difficult questions, and return to them after completing items that can be answered more quickly.

## How does the Solar Space Heating Contractor (C-37B) exam work?

The Solar Space Heating Contractor (C-37B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
