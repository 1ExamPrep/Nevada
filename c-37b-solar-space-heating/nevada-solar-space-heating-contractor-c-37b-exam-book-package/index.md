## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-37B preparation plan should begin with **System Design** because it represents 15 of the 45 examination questions.

Candidates should understand how solar collectors, piping, pumps, storage tanks, heat exchangers, valves, controls, and heating distribution components function together rather than memorizing each item separately.

Follow the path of thermal energy through the system. Understand how solar energy is captured by collectors, transferred into water or air, moved through the system, stored when necessary, and ultimately used to heat a structure.

**Piping** represents 10 questions and should receive significant attention. Review pipe materials, fittings, joining methods, supports, routing, insulation, thermal expansion, valves, and connections.

The **Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018** should be used throughout preparation. Regular navigation practice can help candidates locate code requirements more efficiently under examination conditions.

Practice using the UPC by topic. Start with a question involving pipe materials, identify the important term, use the index, and locate the applicable provision. Repeat the process with fittings, supports, valves, and connections.

**Definitions** can be important because code terminology may have a specific meaning. Understanding the definition can help candidates identify which requirement applies to a particular installation.

**Tables** should receive regular attention. Important requirements involving materials, support spacing, sizing, or installation conditions may appear in tables.

**Exceptions** can change how a general rule applies. Candidates should read surrounding code language carefully instead of stopping at the first sentence that appears relevant.

**Cross-references** may direct the reader to another section containing additional requirements. Practice following these references so the complete requirement is understood.

**Collectors** represents 7 questions. Candidates should understand how solar collectors absorb energy and how collector orientation, arrangement, mounting, circulation, and connections affect system operation.

Review the differences between collecting heat into a water-based system and collecting heat into an air-based system. The C-37B scope includes solar energy used to heat either water or air before that energy is used to heat a structure.

**Pumps and Storage Systems** accounts for 5 questions. Candidates should understand circulation pumps, storage tanks, thermal storage concepts, heat exchangers, and the relationship between available solar energy and heating demand.

Review how storage can allow heat collected during one period to be used later and how pumps maintain circulation through collectors, piping, and heat exchangers.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

**System Valves and Controls** represents 3 questions. Candidates should review shutoff valves, check valves, balancing devices, differential controls, temperature controls, pressure-related components, and other devices that regulate system operation.

**Safety** accounts for 5 questions. Use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 to prepare for hazards involving roof access, ladders, elevated work, personal protective equipment, tools, electrical components, lifting, and other construction activities.

Because the OSHA reference is not allowed into the trade examination room, candidates should build retained safety knowledge during preparation.

The **Solar Water and Pool Heating Design and Installation Manual** can help candidates connect multiple exam topics. Use it to study collectors, system layouts, circulation, piping, pumps, storage, heat exchangers, valves, and controls.

Because the manual is not allowed into the examination room, active recall is useful. After reviewing a system diagram or technical topic, close the book and describe the flow path and purpose of each major component from memory.

**Heat transfer** is central to solar space heating. Candidates should understand how solar radiation becomes usable thermal energy and how that heat is transferred into water or air for building heating.

**Heat exchangers** should receive focused study because they are specifically included in the C-37B scope. Candidates should understand their purpose and how thermal energy can move between separate fluid circuits.

**Storage** is another important concept. Solar energy availability and space-heating demand do not always occur at the same time, so storage can be an important part of system design.

**System flow** should also receive attention. Proper circulation affects collector performance and heat delivery, making balanced flow an important practical consideration.

**Freeze protection** can be relevant because solar thermal components and piping may be exposed to outdoor temperatures. Candidates should understand general methods used to protect exposed equipment from freezing conditions.

**Insulation** helps reduce heat loss between the collector, storage system, heat exchanger, and heating load. Candidates should understand why insulation is important and how outdoor exposure can affect system performance.

**Thermal expansion** should also be considered in systems exposed to elevated temperatures. Pipe movement, material characteristics, and support methods can all affect installation.

A useful study schedule should follow the exam outline. Spend the largest blocks of time on System Design and Piping, followed by Collectors, Pumps and Storage Systems, Safety, and System Valves and Controls.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

Once individual topics become familiar, move into mixed-question practice. The examination can shift quickly from a piping requirement to a system-design question, collector concept, storage issue, control question, or safety topic.

Timed UPC lookup drills can improve open-book performance. Select a plumbing topic and practice locating the applicable requirement within a limited amount of time.

Do not expect the Uniform Plumbing Code to answer every question. The UPC is most useful for targeted plumbing requirements, while many practical solar space heating questions require retained technical knowledge.

When a practice question is missed, identify the cause. The issue may involve weak system knowledge, unfamiliar terminology, slow code navigation, or difficulty understanding how components interact.

Correcting the underlying weakness is more useful than memorizing one answer. This approach builds stronger preparation for unfamiliar questions on test day.

Time management should also be part of preparation. Answer familiar questions efficiently, use the UPC for targeted lookups, mark difficult questions, and return to them after completing items that can be answered more quickly.

## How does the Solar Space Heating Contractor (C-37B) exam work?

The Solar Space Heating Contractor (C-37B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
