## State Requirements

Nevada identifies **C-15A Roofing** as a subclassification within Classification C-15 Roofing and Siding.

The C-15A classification authorizes the **installation, application, alteration, and repair of watertight and weatherproof material used on roofs**.

Roofing materials within the classification can include asphalt, pitch, tar, felt, flax, shakes, shingles, aluminum, tile, slate, urethane, and photovoltaic roof tiles intended to serve in place of other roofing materials.

The classification does not authorize installation of siding or devices used for the internal control of the effects of weather. It also does not authorize installation of conduits, inverters, batteries, or electrical connections associated with photovoltaic systems.

The distinction is important when preparing for the photovoltaic portion of the examination. Roofing contractors may encounter photovoltaic roof products within the roofing scope, while associated electrical installation work requires the appropriate electrical authorization.

The C-15A trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-15A study plan should begin with **Steep Roofing**, the largest exam category. Candidates should review steep-slope roof coverings, underlayments, roof decks, fastening, flashing, valleys, slopes, drainage, roof edges, transitions, and common installation conditions.

**Roofing Substrates** should include deck and sheathing conditions, structural support, deterioration, surface preparation, attachment, moisture conditions, and compatibility between the substrate and the roofing system installed above it.

**Built-up Bituminous Roofing** should include low-slope roofing concepts, membranes, bituminous materials, surfacing, flashing, system components, application concepts, and repair considerations.

**Elastomeric Roofing** should include membrane systems, seams, penetrations, flashing, attachment, roof edges, drainage, and common repair conditions.

**Roofing Repairs** should be approached as a diagnostic subject. Candidates should understand how to recognize damaged roof coverings, failed flashing, deteriorated membranes, substrate problems, potential leak sources, and other conditions that can affect roof performance.

**Sheet Metal and Flashing** may represent only 2 questions, but flashing is fundamental to weatherproof roofing. Review valleys, roof-to-wall intersections, penetrations, edges, curbs, and transitions where water must be directed away from vulnerable parts of the roof.

**Photovoltaic Systems** should be reviewed from the roofing perspective. Candidates should understand how photovoltaic roof products can interact with roofing systems and where the C-15A roofing scope ends when electrical components are involved.

**Safety** deserves serious attention because roofing frequently involves working at elevation. Candidates should understand fall hazards, safe access, ladders, scaffolds, tools, material handling, and general jobsite safety concepts.

The **International Building Code, 2018** should be used during every study session. The highlighted and tabbed format can help candidates return to important roofing-related code material efficiently while building familiarity with the overall organization of the book.

Begin with the table of contents and index. Identify roofing-related topics and practice moving from a keyword to the applicable section. This can be especially helpful when a question does not immediately reveal which chapter or provision applies.

Definitions should also be reviewed carefully. Building-code terminology can affect how a roofing question is interpreted, and understanding the correct definition can help candidates identify the right code section more quickly.

When using the IBC, candidates should avoid stopping at the first phrase that appears relevant. Read enough surrounding material to understand the full requirement, including exceptions, subsections, tables, and cross-references.

## How does the Roofing Contractor (C-15A) exam work?

The Roofing Contractor (C-15A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
