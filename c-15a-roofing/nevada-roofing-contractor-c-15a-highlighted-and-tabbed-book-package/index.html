Exam Details

The Nevada C-15A Roofing Contractor examination contains 50 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the examination and must answer 38 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Safety: 5 questions
  • Roofing Substrates: 7 questions
  • Steep Roofing: 17 questions
  • Roofing Repairs: 4 questions
  • Elastomeric Roofing: 6 questions
  • Built-up Bituminous Roofing: 7 questions
  • Sheet Metal and Flashing: 2 questions
  • Photovoltaic Systems: 2 questions

Steep Roofing is the largest exam category with 17 questions. Candidates should devote substantial study time to roof coverings, underlayments, fastening, deck conditions, flashing, valleys, drainage, roof edges, slopes, materials, and installation practices.

Roofing Substrates and Built-up Bituminous Roofing each account for 7 questions. Candidates should understand roof decks, sheathing, structural support, surface preparation, low-slope roof systems, bituminous materials, surfacing, flashing, and repair concepts.

Elastomeric Roofing accounts for 6 questions. This area can include membrane materials, seams, attachment, flashing, penetrations, drainage, roof edges, and repair considerations.

Safety accounts for 5 questions, while Roofing Repairs accounts for 4. Sheet Metal and Flashing and Photovoltaic Systems each account for 2 questions. The exam therefore requires broad roofing knowledge rather than preparation in only one type of roof system.

State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-15A Roofing as a subclassification within Classification C-15 Roofing and Siding.

The C-15A classification authorizes the installation, application, alteration, and repair of watertight and weatherproof material used on roofs.

Roofing materials within the classification can include asphalt, pitch, tar, felt, flax, shakes, shingles, aluminum, tile, slate, urethane, and photovoltaic roof tiles intended to serve in place of other roofing materials.

The classification does not authorize the installation of siding or devices used for the internal control of the effects of weather. It also does not authorize installation of conduits, inverters, batteries, or electrical connections associated with photovoltaic systems.

The distinction is important when preparing for the photovoltaic portion of the exam. Roofing contractors may work with photovoltaic roof products within the roofing scope, while electrical installation work remains outside the C-15A classification.

The C-15A trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-15A study plan should begin with Steep Roofing, the largest examination category. Candidates should understand common steep-slope roof coverings, underlayments, fastening, roof decks, flashing, valleys, drainage, slopes, transitions, and repair conditions.

Roofing Substrates should include deck and sheathing conditions, structural support, surface preparation, attachment, deterioration, moisture, and compatibility between the substrate and the roofing system installed above it.

Built-up Bituminous Roofing should include low-slope roofing components, bituminous materials, surfacing, membranes, flashing, application concepts, and repair practices.

Elastomeric Roofing should include membrane materials, seams, flashing, penetrations, attachment methods, roof edges, drainage, and repair considerations.

Roofing Repairs should be studied as a diagnostic subject. Candidates should understand common leak sources, failed flashing, damaged membranes, deteriorated coverings, substrate problems, and other conditions that affect roof performance.

Sheet Metal and Flashing represents a smaller exam category but remains essential to watertight roofing. Review valleys, penetrations, transitions, roof-to-wall intersections, edges, curbs, and other locations where water must be directed away from vulnerable areas.

Photovoltaic Systems should be reviewed in the context of roofing work. Candidates should understand roofing considerations associated with photovoltaic components and the limits of the C-15A classification when electrical work is involved.

Safety deserves serious attention because roofing work frequently occurs at elevation. Review fall protection, access, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, material handling, tools, and common construction hazards.

The International Building Code, 2018 should be used throughout preparation. The highlighted and tabbed format can help candidates return to important roofing sections efficiently while still learning the overall organization of the code.

Use the table of contents and index for navigation drills. Select a roofing topic, locate the applicable section, and read enough surrounding material to understand the entire requirement.

Candidates should pay attention to definitions, exceptions, tables, and cross-references. A code question can depend on more than one section, so stopping at the first familiar phrase may lead to an incorrect interpretation.

The NRCA manuals should be used to strengthen technical knowledge of steep-slope, membrane, metal panel, and SPF roofing systems. Compare the characteristics of each system so material and installation differences become easier to recognize.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Roofing Construction and Estimating can support preparation involving roof geometry, quantities, measurements, material estimating, and construction methods. Candidates should be comfortable working through practical estimating concepts without relying entirely on the book.

The carpentry reference can strengthen knowledge of the structure beneath the roof covering, including framing, sheathing, substrates, and general building construction relationships.

Because the study-only books are not allowed into the trade examination room, candidates should gradually shift from book-supported review to recall. Read and study a subject, then close the reference and answer practice questions without looking up every answer.

For the International Building Code, use timed lookup practice instead. The goal is to become faster at locating the specific provision needed during an open-book exam.

Practice questions should initially be organized by subject. Work separately on steep roofing, substrates, built-up roofing, elastomeric roofing, repairs, safety, flashing, and photovoltaic concepts.

Once the individual subjects are familiar, move into mixed practice. The actual exam can move rapidly from one roofing system to another, so candidates should become comfortable changing topics without losing pace.

Time management should be part of every practice session. With 50 questions in two hours, spending too long on one difficult lookup can reduce the time available for easier questions.

A balanced approach is to answer familiar roofing questions efficiently, use the IBC for targeted confirmation, mark difficult questions, and return to them after completing the easier items.

How does the Roofing Contractor (C-15A) exam work?

The Roofing Contractor (C-15A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
