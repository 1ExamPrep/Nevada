## State Requirements

Nevada identifies **C-15A Roofing** as a subclassification within Classification C-15 Roofing and Siding.

The C-15A classification authorizes the **installation, application, alteration, and repair of watertight and weatherproof material used on roofs**.

Roofing materials within the classification can include asphalt, pitch, tar, felt, flax, shakes, shingles, aluminum, tile, slate, urethane, and photovoltaic roof tiles intended to serve in place of other roofing materials.

The classification does not authorize the installation of siding or devices used for the internal control of the effects of weather. It also does not authorize the installation of conduits, inverters, batteries, or electrical connections associated with photovoltaic systems.

The distinction is important for the photovoltaic portion of the exam. Roofing contractors may work with photovoltaic roof tiles as part of the roofing assembly, while electrical connections remain outside the C-15A scope.

The C-15A trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-15A preparation plan should begin with **Steep Roofing**, the largest exam category. Candidates should understand common steep-slope roof systems, roof coverings, underlayments, fastening, flashing, valleys, roof edges, drainage, and basic repair conditions.

**Roofing Substrates** should include deck conditions, sheathing, structural support, surface preparation, moisture conditions, attachment, deterioration, and how the condition of the substrate affects the roofing system installed above it.

**Built-up Bituminous Roofing** should include low-slope system components, membranes, bituminous materials, surfacing, flashing, application concepts, and repair considerations.

**Elastomeric Roofing** should include membrane materials, seams, flashing, penetrations, attachment concepts, roof edges, drainage, and repair practices.

**Roofing Repairs** should be approached as a diagnostic subject. Candidates should understand common leak sources, deteriorated materials, damaged flashing, failed membrane conditions, and other situations that can affect roof performance.

**Sheet Metal and Flashing** is a smaller exam category but is fundamental to roofing. Candidates should understand valleys, penetrations, transitions, roof-to-wall intersections, edges, curbs, and other locations where flashing is used to direct water away from vulnerable areas.

**Photovoltaic Systems** should be reviewed in the context of roofing work. Candidates should understand the difference between photovoltaic roof products that function as roofing material and electrical system components that fall outside the C-15A classification.

**Safety** is especially important in roofing because much of the work is performed at elevation. Candidates should understand fall hazards, access, ladders, scaffolds, material handling, tools, and basic jobsite safety principles.

The **International Building Code, 2018** should be used during every study session. Begin with the table of contents and index, then practice locating roofing provisions and related requirements.

Candidates should not rely only on memorizing section numbers. A better approach is to understand how the IBC is organized so a question about roof coverings, fire classification, rooftop conditions, or construction requirements can be connected to the appropriate chapter or section.

Definitions deserve careful attention because code terminology can affect how a question is interpreted. Review terms related to roofing systems, construction types, materials, and building elements that can appear in roofing code provisions.

When using the IBC, candidates should read enough surrounding text to understand the full requirement. Exceptions, subsections, tables, notes, and cross-references can change how a general rule applies.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

A useful study drill is to select a topic such as roof coverings, fire classification, roof drainage, rooftop equipment, roof assemblies, or structural requirements and locate the applicable code material within a set time.

Repeat the process until navigation becomes more familiar. The goal is to improve efficiency so the code can be used for targeted confirmation during the exam rather than as a substitute for roofing knowledge.

Practice questions should first be organized by topic. Work separately on steep roofing, substrates, low-slope systems, repairs, flashing, photovoltaic concepts, and safety. Once individual subjects become more familiar, move into mixed practice.

Mixed practice is important because the actual examination can move quickly from one roofing system to another. Candidates should be ready to switch from a steep-slope question to substrates, membrane roofing, repairs, or safety without losing focus.

Time management should also be included in preparation. With 50 questions in two hours, spending several minutes on one difficult lookup can reduce the time available for easier questions.

A balanced open-book approach is to answer familiar roofing questions efficiently, use the International Building Code for targeted confirmation, mark difficult questions, and return to them after completing the easier portion of the exam.

## How does the Roofing Contractor (C-15A) exam work?

The Roofing Contractor (C-15A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
