Exam Details

The Nevada C-15A Roofing Contractor examination contains 50 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the test and must answer 38 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Safety: 5 questions
  • Roofing Substrates: 7 questions
  • Steep Roofing: 17 questions
  • Roofing Repairs: 4 questions
  • Elastomeric Roofing: 6 questions
  • Built-up Bituminous Roofing: 7 questions
  • Sheet Metal and Flashing: 2 questions
  • Photovoltaic Systems: 2 questions

Steep Roofing is the largest examination category with 17 questions. Candidates should spend significant preparation time on steep-slope roof coverings, underlayments, flashing, valleys, roof edges, drainage, roof decks, fastening, and installation practices.

Roofing Substrates and Built-up Bituminous Roofing each account for 7 questions. These areas can require an understanding of deck conditions, sheathing, structural support, surface preparation, low-slope roofing components, bituminous materials, surfacing, and repair considerations.

Elastomeric Roofing accounts for 6 questions, while Safety accounts for 5. Roofing Repairs accounts for 4 questions, and Sheet Metal and Flashing and Photovoltaic Systems each account for 2 questions.

The examination therefore requires broad roofing knowledge rather than concentration on only one type of roof system. Candidates should be comfortable moving among steep-slope, low-slope, membrane, repair, flashing, safety, and photovoltaic-related topics.

State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-15A Roofing as a subclassification within Classification C-15 Roofing and Siding.

The C-15A classification authorizes the installation, application, alteration, and repair of watertight and weatherproof material used on roofs.

Roofing materials within the classification can include asphalt, pitch, tar, felt, flax, shakes, shingles, aluminum, tile, slate, urethane, and photovoltaic roof tiles intended to serve in place of other roofing materials.

The classification does not authorize installation of siding or devices used for the internal control of the effects of weather. It also does not authorize installation of conduits, inverters, batteries, or electrical connections associated with photovoltaic systems.

The distinction is important when studying photovoltaic roofing topics. A C-15A contractor may work with photovoltaic roof products within the roofing scope, while electrical connections and related electrical equipment require the appropriate electrical authorization.

The C-15A trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-15A study plan should begin with Steep Roofing, the largest examination category. Review roof coverings, underlayments, roof decks, fastening, valleys, slopes, flashing, roof edges, drainage, and common installation conditions.

Roofing Substrates should include deck and sheathing conditions, structural support, surface preparation, deterioration, moisture, attachment, and compatibility between the substrate and the roof system installed above it.

Built-up Bituminous Roofing should include low-slope roofing concepts, membranes, bituminous materials, surfacing, flashing, application methods, system components, and repair considerations.

Elastomeric Roofing should include membrane materials, seams, flashing, penetrations, attachment, drainage, roof edges, and common repair conditions.

Roofing Repairs should be approached as a diagnostic subject. Candidates should understand common leak sources, deteriorated materials, failed flashing, damaged membranes, substrate conditions, and other problems that can affect roof performance.

Sheet Metal and Flashing represents a smaller exam category but remains fundamental to weatherproof roofing. Review valleys, roof-to-wall intersections, penetrations, curbs, roof edges, and transitions where water must be directed away from vulnerable areas.

Photovoltaic Systems should be reviewed from the roofing perspective. Candidates should understand how photovoltaic roof products interact with roofing systems and where roofing work ends and electrical work begins.

Safety deserves serious attention because roofing frequently involves work at elevation. Candidates should understand fall hazards, access, ladders, scaffolds, tools, material handling, and general jobsite safety concepts.

The International Building Code, 2018 should be used throughout the entire preparation period. Begin with the table of contents and index, then practice locating roofing-related code provisions by subject.

The highlighted and tabbed format can make repeated review more organized. Candidates should still learn the structure of the code so they understand why a particular chapter or section applies to a roofing question rather than relying only on a tab location.

Definitions should receive careful review. Code questions can depend on the exact meaning of a technical term, and understanding the terminology can make it easier to identify the applicable requirement.

When using the IBC, read enough surrounding material to understand the complete rule. Exceptions, tables, subsections, and cross-references can affect how a general provision applies.

A useful study drill is to choose a topic such as roof coverings, rooftop requirements, fire classification, roofing materials, structural conditions, or roof assemblies and locate the applicable code provision under a time limit.

How does the Roofing Contractor (C-15A) exam work?

The Roofing Contractor (C-15A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
