## State Requirements

Nevada identifies **C-15A Roofing** as a subclassification within Classification C-15 Roofing and Siding.

The C-15A classification authorizes the **installation, application, alteration, and repair of watertight and weatherproof material used on roofs**.

Roofing materials within the classification can include asphalt, pitch, tar, felt, flax, shakes, shingles, aluminum, tile, slate, urethane, and photovoltaic roof tiles intended to serve in place of other roofing materials.

The classification does not authorize installation of siding or devices used for the internal control of the effects of weather. It also does not authorize installation of conduits, inverters, batteries, or electrical connections associated with photovoltaic systems.

The distinction is important when preparing for the photovoltaic portion of the examination. Roofing contractors may encounter photovoltaic roofing components within the roofing scope, while associated electrical installation work remains outside the C-15A classification.

The C-15A trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-15A preparation plan should begin with **Steep Roofing**, the largest exam category. Study roof coverings, slopes, underlayments, fastening, roof decks, valleys, edges, flashing, drainage, and installation practices.

**Roofing Substrates** should include deck and sheathing conditions, surface preparation, structural support, deterioration, moisture, attachment, and compatibility between the substrate and the roof system installed above it.

**Built-up Bituminous Roofing** should include low-slope roof components, bituminous materials, surfacing, membranes, flashing, application concepts, and repair considerations.

**Elastomeric Roofing** should include membrane materials, seams, attachment, penetrations, flashing, roof edges, drainage, and repair conditions.

**Roofing Repairs** should be studied as a diagnostic subject. Candidates should understand common leak sources, deteriorated materials, failed flashing, damaged membranes, substrate problems, and other conditions that affect roof performance.

**Sheet Metal and Flashing** represents a smaller exam category but remains fundamental to weatherproof roofing. Review valleys, penetrations, roof-to-wall intersections, edges, curbs, transitions, and other areas where flashing directs water away from vulnerable parts of the roof.

**Photovoltaic Systems** should be reviewed from the roofing perspective. Candidates should understand roofing considerations associated with rooftop photovoltaic components and the boundary between roofing work and electrical work.

**Safety** deserves serious attention because roofing work often occurs at elevation. Study fall protection, access, ladders, scaffolds, tools, personal protective equipment, material handling, and other common construction hazards.

The **International Building Code, 2018** should be used regularly throughout the six-month course access period. Practice locating roofing provisions with the table of contents and index, then read related sections, definitions, tables, and exceptions.

The highlighted and tabbed format can support more efficient review. Candidates should still learn the overall structure of the IBC so they understand why a particular chapter or section applies rather than relying only on visual markers.

The NRCA roofing manuals can be used to compare multiple roof systems. Understanding the differences between steep-slope, membrane, metal panel, and SPF roofing can make exam questions easier to interpret.

**Roofing Construction and Estimating** can help reinforce measurement, quantities, roof geometry, materials, and estimating concepts. Candidates should practice working through common roofing calculations and material relationships without depending entirely on the reference.

The carpentry book supports understanding of roof framing, sheathing, structural components, and the building systems beneath the finished roof covering. These concepts are especially useful when studying substrates and roof construction.

## How does the Roofing Contractor (C-15A) exam work?

The Roofing Contractor (C-15A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
