Exam Details

The Nevada C-21F Chilled and Hot Water Systems trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates authorized to test through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-21F Chilled and Hot Water Systems
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 50
  • Time Allowed: 2 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 35 correct answers

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Piping: 15 questions
  • Water Cooling Equipment: 10 questions
  • Water Heating Equipment: 10 questions
  • Valves and Controls: 8 questions
  • Safety: 7 questions

Piping is the largest individual section of the examination. Candidates should review piping materials, joining methods, system layouts, supports, valves, fittings, circulation, expansion, insulation relationships, equipment connections, and code requirements that apply to chilled-water and hot-water systems.

Water Cooling Equipment includes equipment and principles associated with the production and transfer of chilled water. Candidates should understand chillers, coils, cooling towers, pumps, heat-transfer relationships, circulation, equipment components, and common system arrangements.

Water Heating Equipment requires knowledge of equipment used to generate and distribute heated water. Preparation should include boilers, circulation equipment, storage tanks, pumps, piping, controls, and related mechanical-system principles.

Valves and Controls focuses on the components used to regulate system flow, temperature, pressure, and equipment operation. Candidates should understand the purpose and application of valves, control devices, pumps, and related components in chilled-water and hot-water systems.

Safety represents seven questions. Candidates should study jobsite hazards, personal protective equipment, ladders, tools, material handling, hot-work considerations, equipment safety, and other construction practices relevant to mechanical-system installation.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-21F Chilled and Hot Water Systems subclassification authorizes the installation of boilers, chillers, coils, cooling towers, piping, pumps, storage tanks, valves, and other equipment used in the production and transfer of water for the control of temperatures.

The scope requires candidates to understand how mechanical components work together as complete hydronic systems. Work may involve the equipment that produces heated or chilled water as well as the piping, pumps, tanks, valves, controls, and related components used to transfer that water through the system.

Candidates should distinguish the C-21F scope from other C-21 subclassifications involving refrigeration, air conditioning, maintenance, solar air conditioning, sheet metal, or industrial piping. The C-21F subclassification specifically centers on chilled-water and hot-water systems used for temperature control.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document at least four years of qualifying experience within the applicable experience period required by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

Qualifying experience should relate directly to the requested classification. Applicants should maintain organized records describing the equipment installed or serviced, the work performed, responsibilities held, and the period during which the experience was obtained.

Passing the C-21F trade exam does not automatically issue a contractor license. Applicants must also satisfy the applicable management qualification, financial responsibility, bonding, background, and other Nevada State Contractors Board requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong C-21F study plan should begin with the examination weighting. Piping represents 15 of the 50 questions, while Water Cooling Equipment and Water Heating Equipment each account for 10. Together, these three subjects make up most of the examination.

Prioritize piping. Review materials, fittings, joining methods, system layouts, supports, flow relationships, circulation, equipment connections, expansion considerations, valves, and applicable mechanical and plumbing-code provisions.

Study water cooling equipment. Review chillers, cooling towers, coils, pumps, storage systems, heat transfer, circulation, operating relationships, and equipment components used in chilled-water systems.

Study water heating equipment. Review boilers, pumps, storage tanks, controls, valves, piping, heat-transfer equipment, and system-operation principles associated with heated-water systems.

Review valves and controls. Understand the function of common valves, control devices, pumping equipment, temperature controls, and related components that regulate hydronic-system operation.

Prepare for safety. Use OSHA to review jobsite hazards, protective equipment, ladders, tools, materials, and safe construction practices. Safety represents seven questions and should be treated as a significant exam category.

Practice code navigation. Work directly with the Uniform Mechanical Code, Uniform Plumbing Code, and OSHA reference during study. Learn which reference applies to different types of questions so you do not waste time searching the wrong book.

Use the study-only books for technical depth. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning and Low Pressure Boilers can help reinforce equipment operation and system concepts that go beyond direct code questions.

Use timed practice. Candidates have two hours for 50 questions. Timed exercises can help develop a realistic pace and improve decisions about when a reference lookup is necessary.

The Chilled and Hot Water Systems Contractor (C-21F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board.
