Exam Details

The Nevada C-21F Chilled and Hot Water Systems trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates authorized to test through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-21F Chilled and Hot Water Systems
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 50
  • Time Allowed: 2 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 35 correct answers

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Piping: 15 questions
  • Water Cooling Equipment: 10 questions
  • Water Heating Equipment: 10 questions
  • Valves and Controls: 8 questions
  • Safety: 7 questions

Piping is the largest individual section of the C-21F examination. Candidates should review piping materials, fittings, joining methods, supports, layouts, equipment connections, circulation, flow relationships, valves, and applicable mechanical and plumbing-code requirements.

Water Cooling Equipment accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should understand chillers, cooling towers, coils, pumps, system circulation, heat transfer, storage components, and the basic operating relationships found in chilled-water systems.

Water Heating Equipment also accounts for 10 questions. Preparation should include boilers, pumps, tanks, piping, controls, valves, and the equipment used to generate, circulate, and transfer heated water.

Valves and Controls represents eight questions. Candidates should understand the function of common valves, control devices, temperature-control components, flow-control devices, and related equipment used to regulate hydronic systems.

Safety represents seven questions. Candidates should prepare for jobsite hazards, personal protective equipment, tools, ladders, material handling, fall protection, and other construction-safety requirements applicable to mechanical-system installation.

With 50 questions and two hours available, candidates should practice maintaining a steady pace. Reference navigation should be efficient enough that code lookups do not consume excessive time.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-21F Chilled and Hot Water Systems examination is an open-book test. The exam-room-approved references included in this package are:

  • Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018
  • Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018
  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)

Candidates should practice with all three books throughout preparation. The goal is not simply to know that the references are available, but to understand which book applies to different categories of questions and where important material is generally located.

The Uniform Mechanical Code should be used for repeated practice involving mechanical equipment, piping, hydronic systems, appliances, and related code provisions.

The Uniform Plumbing Code should be used for piping, materials, fittings, water-system provisions, installation requirements, and related plumbing-code topics.

OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 should be used for construction-safety preparation involving jobsite hazards, protective equipment, tools, ladders, falls, material handling, and other safety requirements.

A strong open-book strategy is selective. Candidates should avoid opening a reference for every question. Questions based on familiar system operation, equipment, piping, or safety knowledge can often be answered directly, while specific code questions should be confirmed in the appropriate book.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-21F Chilled and Hot Water Systems subclassification authorizes the installation of boilers, chillers, coils, cooling towers, piping, pumps, storage tanks, valves, and other equipment used to produce and transfer water for the control of temperatures.

The classification requires an understanding of both heating and cooling systems. Candidates should know how major components work together and how water is circulated through piping, pumps, equipment, storage components, valves, and controls.

Boilers and related heating equipment are central to hot-water systems, while chillers and cooling towers are important components in chilled-water systems. Piping provides the connection between these components and the rest of the system, making piping knowledge especially important for C-21F candidates.

The classification is part of Nevada's broader C-21 Refrigeration and Air-Conditioning category but focuses specifically on chilled-water and hot-water systems rather than the entire range of refrigeration or air-conditioning work.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document at least four years of qualifying experience within the applicable experience period required by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

Qualifying experience should relate directly to the requested classification. Applicants should maintain organized records describing the equipment and systems worked on, responsibilities held, and the time period during which the experience was obtained.

Passing the C-21F trade examination does not automatically result in contractor license issuance. Applicants must also satisfy applicable management qualification, financial responsibility, bonding, background, and other Nevada State Contractors Board requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-21F study plan should combine technical system knowledge with efficient use of the three approved references. Because the exam contains code, equipment, piping, controls, and safety material, candidates should practice identifying the type of question before choosing a reference.

Prioritize piping. Piping is the largest category with 15 questions. Review piping materials, joining methods, fittings, supports, valves, flow relationships, circulation, equipment connections, and applicable mechanical and plumbing-code requirements.

Review water cooling equipment. Study chillers, cooling towers, coils, pumps, circulation, heat-transfer relationships, and common chilled-water-system components.

Review water heating equipment. Study boilers, pumps, tanks, piping, heat-transfer equipment, and common hot-water-system components.

Study valves and controls. Understand how valves and control devices regulate temperature, pressure, flow, and equipment operation within hydronic systems.

Prepare for safety. Review OSHA requirements and common safety practices involving personal protective equipment, ladders, tools, material handling, and mechanical-equipment installation.

Practice Mechanical Code navigation. Become familiar with the Uniform Mechanical Code and practice locating sections connected with equipment, hydronic systems, mechanical piping, and related requirements.

Practice Plumbing Code navigation. Use the Uniform Plumbing Code to locate information involving materials, fittings, installation methods, and water-system requirements.

Practice OSHA navigation. Become comfortable identifying and locating applicable construction-safety requirements without spending excessive time searching.

Use timed reference exercises. The C-21F exam allows two hours for 50 questions. Timed practice can help candidates determine when a reference lookup is worthwhile and how quickly they need to locate information.

Know which book to use. Efficient reference selection is important. A mechanical-equipment question is more likely to point toward the UMC, a piping or water-system question may require the UPC, and a safety question may require OSHA.

How does the Chilled and Hot Water Systems Contractor (C-21F) exam work?

The Chilled and Hot Water Systems Contractor (C-21F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
