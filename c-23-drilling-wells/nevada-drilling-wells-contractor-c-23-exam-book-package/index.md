## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong C-23 study plan should reflect the official examination weighting and balance regulatory preparation with practical drilling knowledge.

**Prioritize General Regulations.** The category contains 10 questions, more than any other individual section. Use Nevada Administrative Code 534 regularly and learn where major well-related requirements are located.

**Study casings and screens.** Review the purpose, selection, placement, and installation of casing and screen systems. Understand how these components affect well integrity, water entry, and contamination protection.

**Review grouting and sealing.** Candidates should understand why sealing and grouting are important, where they are used, and how proper construction helps protect underground water resources.

**Prepare for pumps and tanks.** Review pumps, pressure tanks, storage tanks, installation relationships, and the role these components play in a complete water-well system.

**Study drilling and boreholes.** Review boring, drilling methods, excavation, borehole construction, drilling conditions, equipment, and practical field considerations.

**Focus on contamination prevention.** The C-23 scope specifically involves providing a supply of uncontaminated water. Candidates should understand how proper drilling, casing, sealing, grouting, and well completion help protect water quality.

**Review testing procedures.** Understand the purpose of well testing and the types of information that testing can provide about the completed water-well system.

**Prepare for safety.** Use OSHA study material to reinforce safe practices involving drilling equipment, excavation, tools, machinery, personal protective equipment, and other well-drilling hazards.

**Practice code navigation.** Because Nevada Administrative Code 534 can be used during the examination, candidates should practice locating requirements under timed conditions. Learn the structure of the regulation and use key terms from practice questions to direct searches.

**Know when to use the book.** Not every question will require a regulation lookup. Practical questions may be answered more efficiently from drilling knowledge developed through the study-only references.

**Practice timed questions.** Candidates have 90 minutes for 45 questions. Maintaining an average pace of approximately two minutes per question can help ensure enough time remains for difficult regulatory lookups.

**Connect regulations to field practice.** Regulations are easier to remember when candidates understand why they exist. Relating Nevada requirements to casing, sealing, contamination prevention, pumps, and well construction can make preparation more practical and effective.

## How does the Drilling Wells Contractor (C-23) exam work?

The Drilling Wells Contractor (C-23) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
