Exam Details

The Nevada C-23 Drilling Wells trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates authorized to test as part of the Nevada contractor licensing process.

  • Exam: Nevada C-23 Drilling Wells
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 45
  • Time Allowed: 1.5 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 32 correct answers

The examination is divided into the following subject areas:

  • General Regulations: 10 questions
  • Drilling and Boreholes: 5 questions
  • Casings and Screens: 6 questions
  • Grouting and Sealing: 6 questions
  • Contamination Prevention: 5 questions
  • Testing: 3 questions
  • Safety and General Knowledge: 4 questions
  • Pumps and Tanks: 6 questions

General Regulations is the largest individual exam category with 10 questions. This makes Nevada Administrative Code 534 especially important. Candidates should become comfortable locating requirements involving underground water, well construction, drilling, casing, sealing, and other regulatory topics.

Casings and Screens, Grouting and Sealing, and Pumps and Tanks each account for six questions. Together, these categories represent a major portion of the examination and should receive dedicated preparation time.

Drilling and Boreholes and Contamination Prevention each contain five questions. Candidates should understand practical drilling procedures as well as the methods used to protect groundwater and maintain an uncontaminated water supply.

Safety and General Knowledge includes four questions, while Testing includes three. These smaller categories should still be included in a complete study plan because a passing score requires consistent performance across the exam.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-23 classification is designated for drilling wells and installing pumps, pressure tanks, and storage tanks that are considered integral to water wells.

The classification includes installing and repairing water wells, pressure tanks, storage tanks, and pumps through activities such as boring, drilling, excavating, casing, cementing, dewatering, and cleaning to provide a supply of uncontaminated water.

The scope means candidates should understand the complete water-well system rather than only the drilling process. Preparation should include boreholes, casing, screens, sealing, grouting, contamination protection, pumps, tanks, testing, and safe work practices.

Protecting underground water from contamination is a major part of well construction. Candidates should understand how proper casing, sealing, grouting, site practices, and well completion procedures support water quality and well integrity.

Pumps and tanks are also part of the C-23 scope. Candidates should review the relationship between the well, pump, pressure tank, and storage tank and understand the general functions these components serve within a completed water-well system.

Nevada Administrative Code 534 is particularly important because it establishes requirements affecting underground water and wells. Candidates should develop enough familiarity with the regulation to identify where key rules are located during the open-book examination.

Passing the C-23 trade examination does not automatically issue a Nevada contractor license. Applicants must also complete the other applicable Nevada State Contractors Board licensing requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-23 study plan should balance Nevada regulation review with practical water-well construction knowledge. The exam includes several technical categories that cannot be answered simply by locating a rule in NAC 534.

Prioritize General Regulations. With 10 questions, this is the largest category. Practice identifying the parts of NAC 534 that address well construction, underground water, regulatory responsibilities, and related Nevada requirements.

Study drilling and boreholes. Review boring, drilling methods, borehole preparation, drilling conditions, equipment, and practical construction procedures associated with water wells.

Review casings and screens. Candidates should understand the purpose and placement of casing and screen components and how they contribute to the function and integrity of a well.

Study grouting and sealing. Review why grouting and sealing are important and how these practices help protect the well and underground water from contamination.

Focus on contamination prevention. The C-23 classification specifically involves providing uncontaminated water. Candidates should understand how well construction practices affect groundwater protection and water quality.

Review testing. Candidates should understand the purpose of well testing and the types of information testing can provide about a completed well system.

Prepare for pumps and tanks. Review pumps, pressure tanks, storage tanks, their relationship to water wells, and their role in delivering and storing water.

Review safety and general knowledge. Water-well drilling can involve heavy equipment, moving machinery, excavation, drilling hazards, and other jobsite risks. Candidates should include safety preparation in their study plan.

Practice NAC 534 navigation. Because the regulation is permitted in the exam room, candidates should practice finding relevant sections under timed conditions rather than relying on slow searches during the actual test.

Use key terms. Identify important words in a question and use them to narrow the search. Terms involving casing, drilling, sealing, contamination, pumps, or well construction may help guide reference navigation.

Know when not to use the book. Some questions test practical drilling knowledge rather than a specific Nevada regulation. Candidates should answer those efficiently instead of searching NAC 534 unnecessarily.

Practice timed questions. With 45 questions and 90 minutes, candidates should aim for steady pacing and avoid spending too much time on a single difficult lookup.

How does the Drilling Wells Contractor (C-23) exam work?

The Drilling Wells Contractor (C-23) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
