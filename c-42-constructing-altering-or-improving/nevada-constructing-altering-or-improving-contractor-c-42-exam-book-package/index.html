Exam Details

The Nevada C-42 Constructing, Altering or Improving Video Service Networks trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who have received examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-42 Constructing, Altering or Improving Video Service Networks
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 35
  • Time Allowed: 1 hour
  • Minimum Passing Score: 25 correct answers

The C-42 examination is divided into three primary subject areas:

  • NEC: 10 questions
  • NESC: 2 questions
  • Cable-General: 23 questions

Cable-General is the largest exam category with 23 of the 35 questions. Candidates should devote significant preparation time to general video, data, and communications cabling concepts. This may include cable characteristics, installation practices, routing, terminations, connectors, system components, testing considerations, and general industry practices relevant to video service networks.

NEC accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should practice using the 2017 National Electrical Code and become familiar with provisions that affect communications, cable installations, grounding, bonding, separation, wiring methods, and related electrical requirements.

NESC accounts for two questions. Although the National Electrical Safety Code is not permitted inside the examination room, candidates should review the 2012 edition during preparation so applicable safety and system concepts can be recalled without the book on test day.

With only one hour to complete 35 questions, pacing is important. Candidates have less than two minutes per question on average. Efficient code navigation and strong cable knowledge can help prevent excessive time from being spent on individual questions.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-42 Constructing, Altering or Improving Video Service Networks examination is an open-book test with one designated reference permitted in the examination room.

The exam-room-approved reference included in this package is:

  • NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 edition

The 2017 National Electrical Code should be used throughout preparation. Candidates should become familiar with its structure, index, terminology, and the portions that apply to communications and cable installations.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not permitted. Candidates should prepare their reference before test day and become familiar with any highlighting or indexing they intend to use.

The following included study references are not allowed into the C-42 examination:

  • National Electrical Safety Code, 2012
  • Data, Voice, and Video Cabling

The books should be used during preparation to strengthen technical understanding. The National Electrical Safety Code supports preparation for the NESC portion of the examination, while Data, Voice, and Video Cabling supports the much larger Cable-General portion.

Because Cable-General represents 23 questions, candidates should not treat this as an NEC-only examination. The National Electrical Code is an important exam-room tool, but the majority of the test requires broader knowledge of cable systems and common industry practices.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the C-42 classification. Passing the trade examination is one component of the Nevada contractor licensing process.

  1. Confirm the C-42 classification. Determine that constructing, altering, or improving video service networks matches the contracting work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. Designate the person who will satisfy the applicable experience and examination requirements for the classification.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare records describing relevant work involving video service networks, communications cabling, cable installation, system components, routing, terminations, and related responsibilities.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor license application. Gather the required business information, qualifier information, experience documentation, financial information, and supporting materials.
  5. Submit the application. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the application and determines examination eligibility.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow the required fingerprint, disclosure, and background procedures associated with Nevada contractor licensing.
  7. Meet applicable financial responsibility requirements. Provide the financial documentation required for the requested contractor license.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Candidates required to test can proceed with scheduling after authorization.
  9. Schedule the C-42 examination through PSI. Arrange the trade examination after receiving eligibility.
  10. Prepare for the C-42 trade exam. Study cable-general topics, NEC requirements, NESC concepts, and efficient National Electrical Code navigation.
  11. Complete applicable contractor management requirements. Nevada contractor licensing also includes contractor management qualification in addition to the trade examination.
  12. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Satisfy applicable financial, bonding, background, and other Nevada State Contractors Board requirements before license issuance.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-42 classification is officially designated for Constructing, Altering or Improving Video Service Networks. A person holding a C-42 license may construct, alter, or improve video service networks.

The classification is specialized around video service network infrastructure rather than unrestricted electrical contracting. Candidates should understand the distinction between video service network work and activities that fall within other Nevada electrical or communications-related classifications.

The trade examination reflects this specialized scope by concentrating heavily on cable-general knowledge. Twenty-three of the 35 questions fall into the Cable-General category, while electrical-code and electrical-safety concepts account for the remaining questions.

Applicants pursuing a Nevada contractor license must also satisfy the licensing qualifications established by the Nevada State Contractors Board. For the standard licensing pathway, qualifying experience generally must demonstrate the required level of practical experience in the classification requested.

Experience documentation should clearly describe relevant duties rather than relying only on a job title. Work involving communications cabling, video service network construction, installation methods, cable routing, termination, testing, system components, and related supervision can help demonstrate the nature of the applicant's experience when applicable.

Passing the C-42 trade examination does not by itself issue a Nevada contractor license. Applicants must also satisfy applicable application, experience, management qualification, financial responsibility, bonding, background, and other Nevada State Contractors Board requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-42 preparation plan should begin with the exam weighting. Cable-General represents 23 questions, making practical cabling knowledge the clear priority.

Prioritize general cabling knowledge. Review cable types, applications, routing, installation methods, connectors, terminations, system components, testing principles, and common communications-cabling practices.

Study video service network concepts. Understand how cable systems and related components work together to provide video services and how installations may be constructed, altered, or improved.

Practice NEC navigation. Use the 2017 National Electrical Code regularly. Learn how to locate requirements involving communications wiring, cable systems, grounding, bonding, separation, wiring methods, and related installations.

Review NESC concepts before exam day. Because the National Electrical Safety Code cannot be brought into the examination room, candidates should study applicable concepts in advance and be prepared to answer those questions from knowledge.

Know which questions require the NEC. Not every C-42 question is an electrical-code lookup. Recognizing whether a question is primarily NEC-based or cable-general can save valuable testing time.

Practice using the index. The NEC index can be a valuable navigation tool when a candidate knows the terminology associated with the question. Practice identifying key terms and tracing them to the applicable article or section.

Develop familiarity instead of memorizing page numbers. Understanding the organization and article structure of the NEC is generally more useful than relying on page-number memorization, especially when navigating a large code book under time pressure.

Use timed practice. Candidates have one hour for 35 questions. Practice maintaining a steady pace, answering familiar cable questions efficiently, and reserving reference lookups for questions that genuinely require the code.

Review the largest category repeatedly. With nearly two-thirds of the examination devoted to Cable-General, candidates should return to cabling topics throughout their study plan rather than focusing primarily on the NEC.

How does the Constructing, Altering or Improving Contractor (C-42) exam work?

The Constructing, Altering or Improving Contractor (C-42) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
