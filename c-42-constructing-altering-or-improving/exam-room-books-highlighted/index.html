Exam Details

The Nevada C-42 Constructing, Altering or Improving Video Service Networks trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates authorized to test through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-42 Constructing, Altering or Improving Video Service Networks
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 35
  • Time Allowed: 1 hour
  • Minimum Passing Score: 25 correct answers

The examination is divided into three primary subject areas:

  • NEC: 10 questions
  • NESC: 2 questions
  • Cable-General: 23 questions

Cable-General is the largest examination category, accounting for 23 of the 35 questions. Candidates should prepare for practical topics involving cable systems, installation practices, cable routing, connectors, terminations, components, testing concepts, and general video service network knowledge.

NEC accounts for 10 questions. The highlighted and tabbed NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 edition included in this package supports preparation for code-based questions. Candidates should become familiar with provisions that relate to communications wiring, cable systems, grounding, bonding, wiring methods, separation, and other electrical-code requirements relevant to video service network installations.

NESC represents two questions. The National Electrical Safety Code is not included in this exam-room-approved package and is not part of the book allowed into the examination. Candidates should prepare for NESC-related concepts separately before test day.

The one-hour time limit makes efficient testing important. With 35 questions to complete, candidates have less than two minutes per question on average. Strong technical knowledge can help candidates answer many questions directly, while practiced NEC navigation can make necessary code lookups more efficient.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the C-42 classification. Passing the C-42 trade examination is one part of the Nevada contractor licensing process.

  1. Confirm the C-42 classification. Determine that constructing, altering, or improving video service networks matches the type of contracting work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. Designate the person who will satisfy the applicable experience and examination requirements for the license.
  3. Document relevant experience. Prepare records describing qualifying work involving video service networks, communications cabling, cable installation, routing, connectors, terminations, system components, testing, or related supervision.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor license application. Gather the required business information, qualifier information, experience documentation, financial information, and supporting materials.
  5. Submit the application. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the applicant's qualifications as part of the licensing process.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow required disclosure, fingerprint, and background procedures.
  7. Meet applicable financial responsibility requirements. Provide the financial information required for the contractor license application.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Candidates required to take the trade examination can proceed with scheduling after authorization.
  9. Schedule the C-42 examination through PSI. Arrange the trade examination after eligibility is established.
  10. Prepare for the C-42 trade exam. Study cable-general subjects, NEC requirements, NESC concepts, and efficient reference-navigation techniques.
  11. Complete applicable contractor management requirements. Nevada contractor licensing also includes contractor management qualification requirements in addition to the trade examination.
  12. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Satisfy applicable financial responsibility, bonding, background, and other Nevada State Contractors Board requirements before license issuance.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong C-42 study plan should begin with the examination weighting. Cable-General represents 23 questions, so candidates should dedicate a significant portion of their preparation to practical cabling concepts rather than focusing exclusively on the National Electrical Code.

Develop strong cable fundamentals. Review cable types, applications, system components, connectors, terminations, pathways, supports, routing, testing concepts, and general communications installation practices.

Understand video service network work. Candidates should be familiar with how cable systems and related components are installed, altered, or improved as part of video service network infrastructure.

Practice NEC navigation. Use the highlighted and tabbed 2017 National Electrical Code frequently. The goal is to know where to begin a search rather than opening the code book randomly during the exam.

Learn to use the index. Technical questions often contain words that can point candidates toward the correct NEC article or section. Practice identifying useful search terms and following index entries efficiently.

Review communications-related NEC material. Focus on code provisions relevant to communications systems, cable installation, grounding, bonding, separation, wiring methods, and related electrical requirements.

Understand grounding and bonding concepts. The topics can appear in code-based questions involving communications and cable installations. Candidates should know how to recognize when a question is asking about grounding or bonding and where to begin looking in the NEC.

Review cable separation and routing. Communications installations may involve requirements concerning routing, support, proximity to other systems, and protection from damage. These are useful areas for both practical and code-based preparation.

Practice timed lookups. With only one hour for 35 questions, candidates should develop a time limit for code searches. If a lookup is taking too long, it may be more productive to move forward and return to the question later.

Know when the NEC is not needed. Many Cable-General questions may test trade knowledge rather than a specific electrical-code provision. Candidates should avoid unnecessary lookups when they recognize the question as a practical cabling topic.

Use the highlighted sections actively. Highlighting is most effective when candidates understand why a section is important. During study, connect the highlighted language to practice questions and common installation topics.

Learn the tabs before exam day. Tabs provide navigational shortcuts, but candidates should know what each tab represents. Practice moving from a topic to the correct tab without hesitation.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Balance code study with technical knowledge. The C-42 examination includes only 10 NEC questions compared with 23 Cable-General questions. Candidates should maintain a study plan that reflects this distribution.

Review weak subjects repeatedly. Practice questions can help identify whether additional attention is needed in cabling terminology, connectors, termination methods, installation practices, grounding, bonding, system components, or NEC navigation.

How does the Constructing, Altering or Improving Contractor (C-42) exam work?

The Constructing, Altering or Improving Contractor (C-42) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
