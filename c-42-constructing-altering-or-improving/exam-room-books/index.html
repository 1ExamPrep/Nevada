Exam Details

The Nevada C-42 Constructing, Altering or Improving Video Service Networks trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who receive examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-42 Constructing, Altering or Improving Video Service Networks
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 35
  • Time Allowed: 1 hour
  • Minimum Passing Score: 25 correct answers

The examination is divided into three primary subject areas:

  • NEC: 10 questions
  • NESC: 2 questions
  • Cable-General: 23 questions

Cable-General is the largest portion of the examination with 23 questions. Candidates should expect the test to emphasize practical cabling knowledge, including cable types, installation practices, routing, connectors, terminations, system components, testing concepts, and general communications-cabling principles.

NEC accounts for 10 questions. The included NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 edition supports preparation for this portion of the exam. Candidates should become familiar with the sections that apply to communications systems, cable installations, grounding, bonding, wiring methods, and separation requirements.

NESC accounts for two questions. Although the National Electrical Safety Code is not included in this exam-room-only package, candidates should still understand the applicable safety concepts as part of their overall preparation.

With 35 questions to complete in one hour, time management matters. Candidates have less than two minutes per question on average. Efficient NEC navigation can help preserve time for cable-general questions that depend more heavily on trade knowledge.

Open Book Test

The National Electrical Code is a large technical reference, so candidates should avoid treating the open-book format as a substitute for preparation. The most effective approach is to learn where major topics are located and practice moving from a question to the appropriate article or section quickly.

Candidates should become comfortable using the index, table of contents, article structure, definitions, and cross-references. Familiarity with the book can make it easier to recognize whether a question is likely to involve communications systems, grounding, bonding, wiring methods, raceways, cable support, separation, or another code topic.

Because only 10 of the 35 questions are specifically categorized as NEC questions, candidates should also develop strong technical knowledge outside the code book. Cable-General represents most of the exam, so practical cabling knowledge remains essential.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the C-42 classification. Passing the trade examination is one part of the complete Nevada contractor licensing process.

  1. Confirm the C-42 classification. Determine that constructing, altering, or improving video service networks matches the type of contracting work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. Designate the person who will satisfy the applicable experience and examination requirements.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare records describing relevant work involving video service networks, communications cabling, cable installation, routing, terminations, system components, testing, and related responsibilities.
  4. Prepare the contractor license application. Gather the required business information, qualifier information, experience documentation, financial information, and supporting materials.
  5. Submit the application. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the applicant's qualifications before examination eligibility is established.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow required disclosure, fingerprint, and background procedures associated with Nevada contractor licensing.
  7. Meet applicable financial responsibility requirements. Provide the financial information required as part of the licensing process.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Candidates required to test can proceed with scheduling after authorization.
  9. Schedule the C-42 examination through PSI. Arrange the trade exam after receiving eligibility.
  10. Prepare for the trade examination. Review NEC requirements, cable-general topics, NESC concepts, and efficient code-navigation methods.
  11. Complete applicable contractor management requirements. Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification in addition to the trade exam.
  12. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Satisfy applicable financial, bonding, background, and other Nevada State Contractors Board requirements before license issuance.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-42 study plan should reflect the exam weighting. Because Cable-General represents 23 of the 35 questions, candidates should dedicate substantial preparation time to practical cabling knowledge while also building strong NEC navigation skills.

Practice cable terminology. Candidates should understand commonly used cable types, connectors, components, pathways, terminations, installation methods, testing concepts, and communications infrastructure terminology.

Study installation practices. Review cable routing, support, protection, separation, termination, and general workmanship considerations associated with video service network installations.

Develop NEC navigation skills. Use the 2017 National Electrical Code regularly rather than waiting until the final stage of preparation. Repeated navigation builds familiarity with the structure of the book and helps candidates recognize where information is likely to be located.

Use the index strategically. The NEC index is especially useful when the candidate can identify the correct technical term from the question. Practice extracting key words from a question and tracing them to the appropriate article or section.

Review grounding and bonding. Communications and cable systems can involve grounding and bonding requirements, making these concepts important areas for code-based study.

Understand separation requirements. Candidates should be familiar with how communications and cable installations relate to other wiring systems and where applicable separation or installation rules can be found.

Practice timed lookups. Candidates have only one hour for the entire 35-question exam. Practice finding NEC answers within a limited amount of time so a single difficult lookup does not interfere with the rest of the test.

Know when not to use the book. Many Cable-General questions may be answered from trade knowledge rather than a direct NEC lookup. Candidates should avoid searching the code book unnecessarily when the question is testing practical cabling knowledge.

Review weak areas repeatedly. Practice questions can help reveal whether more study is needed in code navigation, cable types, system components, installation practices, grounding, bonding, or general communications concepts.

Build familiarity with the approved reference. The National Electrical Code is most useful when the candidate has already practiced navigating it under study conditions. Familiarity can help reduce time spent searching and make the open-book format more effective.

How does the Constructing, Altering or Improving Contractor (C-42) exam work?

The Constructing, Altering or Improving Contractor (C-42) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
