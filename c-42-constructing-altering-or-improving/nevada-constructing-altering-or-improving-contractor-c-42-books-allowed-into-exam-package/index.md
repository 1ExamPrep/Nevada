## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-42 study plan should reflect the exam weighting. Because Cable-General represents 23 of the 35 questions, candidates should dedicate substantial preparation time to practical cabling knowledge while also building strong NEC navigation skills.

**Practice cable terminology.** Candidates should understand commonly used cable types, connectors, components, pathways, terminations, installation methods, testing concepts, and communications infrastructure terminology.

**Study installation practices.** Review cable routing, support, protection, separation, termination, and general workmanship considerations associated with video service network installations.

**Develop NEC navigation skills.** Use the 2017 National Electrical Code regularly rather than waiting until the final stage of preparation. Repeated navigation builds familiarity with the structure of the book and helps candidates recognize where information is likely to be located.

**Use the index strategically.** The NEC index is especially useful when the candidate can identify the correct technical term from the question. Practice extracting key words from a question and tracing them to the appropriate article or section.

**Review grounding and bonding.** Communications and cable systems can involve grounding and bonding requirements, making these concepts important areas for code-based study.

**Understand separation requirements.** Candidates should be familiar with how communications and cable installations relate to other wiring systems and where applicable separation or installation rules can be found.

**Practice timed lookups.** Candidates have only one hour for the entire 35-question exam. Practice finding NEC answers within a limited amount of time so a single difficult lookup does not interfere with the rest of the test.

**Know when not to use the book.** Many Cable-General questions may be answered from trade knowledge rather than a direct NEC lookup. Candidates should avoid searching the code book unnecessarily when the question is testing practical cabling knowledge.

**Review weak areas repeatedly.** Practice questions can help reveal whether more study is needed in code navigation, cable types, system components, installation practices, grounding, bonding, or general communications concepts.

**Build familiarity with the approved reference.** The National Electrical Code is most useful when the candidate has already practiced navigating it under study conditions. Familiarity can help reduce time spent searching and make the open-book format more effective.

## How does the Constructing, Altering or Improving Contractor (C-42) exam work?

The Constructing, Altering or Improving Contractor (C-42) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
