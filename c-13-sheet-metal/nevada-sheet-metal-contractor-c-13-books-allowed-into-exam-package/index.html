Exam Details

The Nevada C-13 Sheet Metal Contractor examination contains 60 questions. Candidates are allowed 2.5 hours to complete the examination and must answer 42 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Ducts: 20 questions
  • Chimneys, Flues, and Vents: 8 questions
  • Hoods, Ventilation, Equipment, and Food Service Equipment: 10 questions
  • Metal Roofing Systems, Flashing, and Architectural Metals: 10 questions
  • Gutters and Downspouts: 5 questions
  • Tools and Equipment: 4 questions
  • Safety: 3 questions

Ducts is the largest examination category with 20 questions. Candidates should understand duct construction concepts, fittings, supports, connections, reinforcement, materials, installation practices, and applicable mechanical-code requirements.

Hoods, Ventilation, Equipment, and Food Service Equipment accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should review ventilation arrangements, exhaust systems, hoods, equipment-related clearances, and the mechanical code provisions associated with these systems.

Metal Roofing Systems, Flashing, and Architectural Metals also accounts for 10 questions. This area connects sheet metal work to the building envelope and requires familiarity with roof systems, flashing, edges, penetrations, transitions, architectural metals, and weatherproof construction.

Chimneys, Flues, and Vents accounts for 8 questions and requires understanding of venting arrangements, materials, clearances, installation, and applicable mechanical requirements.

Gutters and Downspouts, Tools and Equipment, and Safety make up the remaining examination subjects. These smaller categories should still be included in a balanced preparation plan.

State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-13 as the specialty classification for using sheet metal. The classification covers fabrication and installation of sheet metal across several different construction applications.

The scope includes cornices, flashings, gutters, leaders, rainwater downspouts, pans, food service equipment, ducts, louvers, patented chimneys, flues, and metal roof systems.

The classification does not include fabrication or installation of equipment that uses refrigeration as an integral part of the system. Candidates should understand this distinction when separating sheet metal work from refrigeration and air-conditioning work.

Because the C-13 scope includes both mechanical and architectural applications, preparation should address duct systems and ventilation as well as roofing metals, flashings, gutters, exterior sheet metal details, and related building-code requirements.

The C-13 trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-13 preparation plan should begin with Ducts because this subject represents 20 of the 60 examination questions. Candidates should review duct materials, fabrication concepts, fittings, reinforcement, supports, connections, installation, and code-related requirements.

The Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018 should be used extensively for duct and ventilation preparation. Practice finding relevant chapters and sections using both the table of contents and index.

Definitions should receive careful attention. Mechanical-code questions can depend on the exact meaning of terms used for ducts, vents, equipment, clearances, or ventilation systems.

Hoods, Ventilation, Equipment, and Food Service Equipment should also receive significant study time. Review exhaust arrangements, ventilation concepts, hoods, clearances, duct routing, equipment relationships, and applicable mechanical requirements.

Chimneys, Flues, and Vents should include materials, clearances, installation arrangements, terminations, and relationships among appliances, vent systems, and surrounding construction.

For these subjects, candidates should practice moving between general requirements, tables, exceptions, and referenced subsections rather than stopping at the first sentence that appears relevant.

Metal Roofing Systems, Flashing, and Architectural Metals is a major C-13 topic and should be studied with the International Building Code. Review metal roofing assemblies, flashing, roof edges, penetrations, roof-to-wall transitions, architectural metal applications, and weatherproof construction.

Gutters and Downspouts should include rainwater collection, outlets, leaders, downspouts, supports, joints, and relationships between roof drainage components.

Tools and Equipment requires practical trade knowledge. Candidates should understand common measuring, cutting, forming, bending, fastening, joining, and fabrication tools used in sheet metal work.

Safety should remain part of preparation even though it represents a smaller portion of the examination. Sheet metal work can involve sharp edges, elevated work, ladders, scaffolds, power tools, fabrication equipment, and material handling hazards.

Since this package contains only the exam-room-approved references, candidates should supplement code navigation with retained trade knowledge from experience, coursework, or other study materials.

A useful code-study method is to divide practice into two tracks. Use the Uniform Mechanical Code for mechanical-system questions and the International Building Code for architectural and building-construction questions.

Timed navigation drills are especially useful. Choose a topic such as ducts, ventilation, chimneys, flashing, or metal roofing and locate the applicable section under a set time limit.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Repeat the same drill with variations in terminology. Exam questions may use wording that differs from the exact index term, so candidates should become comfortable identifying related terms and alternative search paths.

Tables deserve particular attention because code answers are often found in tabular material rather than narrative paragraphs. Practice identifying table titles, column headings, notes, and exceptions.

Cross-references are also important. A code section may direct candidates to another chapter or provision that contains the complete requirement. Following these references efficiently can save time during an open-book exam.

Mixed practice should combine both code books. For example, a practice session might include ductwork, roof flashing, ventilation, chimneys, gutters, and architectural metals so candidates become comfortable deciding which code to open first.

Time management should be built into preparation. With 60 questions in 2.5 hours, candidates should avoid spending too long on one difficult lookup while easier questions remain unanswered.

A balanced open-book strategy is to answer familiar trade questions efficiently, perform targeted code lookups when confirmation is needed, mark difficult items, and return to them after completing faster questions.

The goal is not to memorize every section of both codes. Instead, candidates should become familiar enough with the organization of the books to know where to begin searching and how to narrow the search efficiently.

How does the Sheet Metal Contractor (C-13) exam work?

The Sheet Metal Contractor (C-13) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
