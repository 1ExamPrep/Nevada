Exam Details

The Nevada C-13 Sheet Metal Contractor examination contains 60 questions. Candidates are allowed 2.5 hours to complete the examination and must answer 42 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Ducts: 20 questions
  • Chimneys, Flues, and Vents: 8 questions
  • Hoods, Ventilation, Equipment, and Food Service Equipment: 10 questions
  • Metal Roofing Systems, Flashing, and Architectural Metals: 10 questions
  • Gutters and Downspouts: 5 questions
  • Tools and Equipment: 4 questions
  • Safety: 3 questions

Ducts is the largest examination category with 20 questions. Candidates should spend substantial preparation time on duct construction, fittings, reinforcement, supports, connections, dimensions, materials, installation methods, and mechanical code requirements.

Hoods, Ventilation, Equipment, and Food Service Equipment and Metal Roofing Systems, Flashing, and Architectural Metals each account for 10 questions. Together, these areas represent a significant portion of the examination and require candidates to understand both mechanical ventilation and exterior sheet metal applications.

Chimneys, Flues, and Vents accounts for 8 questions. Candidates should understand venting arrangements, clearances, materials, installation requirements, and the code relationships that apply to mechanical systems.

Gutters and Downspouts, Tools and Equipment, and Safety make up the remaining questions. Although they represent smaller sections individually, these subjects should not be overlooked because a few correct answers can make a meaningful difference on a 60-question examination.

State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-13 as the specialty classification for using sheet metal. The classification covers fabrication and installation of sheet metal across a range of construction applications.

The authorized scope includes cornices, flashings, gutters, leaders, rainwater downspouts, pans, food service equipment, ducts, louvers, patented chimneys, flues, and metal roof systems.

The C-13 classification does not include fabrication or installation of equipment that uses refrigeration as an integral part of the system. Candidates should understand this boundary when distinguishing sheet metal work from refrigeration and air-conditioning work.

Because the classification spans mechanical, architectural, roofing, drainage, and food service applications, candidates need a broad understanding of sheet metal fabrication and installation rather than concentrating on ductwork alone.

The C-13 trade examination is one component of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable experience, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-13 preparation plan should begin with Ducts because this category represents 20 of the 60 examination questions. Review duct materials, construction, reinforcement, fittings, transitions, elbows, supports, connections, and installation practices.

Use the HVAC Duct Construction Standards to strengthen technical knowledge of metal and flexible duct systems. Since this reference is not allowed into the exam room, gradually transition from book-supported study to active recall and practice questions.

Hoods, Ventilation, Equipment, and Food Service Equipment should receive significant attention. Review ventilation concepts, exhaust systems, hoods, clearances, duct arrangements, and the requirements that apply to food service and mechanical installations.

The Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018 is especially important for these mechanical subjects. Practice finding applicable provisions using the index, table of contents, chapter headings, definitions, and tables.

Chimneys, Flues, and Vents should include materials, sizing concepts, installation, clearances, terminations, and relationships among equipment, vent systems, and surrounding construction.

Metal Roofing Systems, Flashing, and Architectural Metals accounts for 10 questions. Review metal roof assemblies, flashings, edges, penetrations, roof-to-wall conditions, joints, transitions, architectural sheet metal details, and weatherproofing practices.

The SMACNA Architectural Sheet Metal Manual can help candidates build practical familiarity with sheet metal details used to manage water and protect exterior building surfaces.

Gutters and Downspouts accounts for 5 questions. Review collection and drainage of rainwater, gutter profiles, joints, supports, outlets, leaders, downspouts, and connections between roof drainage components.

Tools and Equipment should include common measuring, cutting, bending, forming, joining, fastening, and fabrication tools. Candidates should understand which tools are appropriate for different sheet metal operations.

Safety should remain part of regular preparation even though it contains only 3 questions. Sheet metal work can involve sharp edges, elevated work, ladders, scaffolds, power tools, material handling, and fabrication equipment.

Because OSHA is a study-only reference, use it to build knowledge that can be recalled without the book. Practice recognizing hazards and choosing appropriate safe-work methods.

The International Building Code, 2018 should be reviewed alongside architectural sheet metal subjects. Candidates should practice locating requirements affecting roof systems, building construction, penetrations, flashing, exterior applications, and related structural conditions.

Timed code-navigation drills are useful. Select a topic such as ductwork, ventilation, roof flashing, chimneys, or metal roofing and locate the applicable section under a time limit.

More on test Information and Study Materials

For the study-only books, active recall can improve retention. Review a technical topic, close the reference, then explain the construction sequence, material requirement, or fabrication method from memory before checking the book again.

As preparation progresses, move from isolated subjects to mixed practice. The examination can shift quickly from ducts to flashing, ventilation, tools, gutters, chimneys, or safety, so candidates should become comfortable changing topics while maintaining pace.

Time management is also important. With 60 questions in 2.5 hours, spending too long on one difficult code lookup can reduce the time available for easier questions. Answer familiar items efficiently, use targeted code lookups when needed, and return to difficult questions after completing faster items.

How does the Sheet Metal Contractor (C-13) exam work?

The Sheet Metal Contractor (C-13) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
