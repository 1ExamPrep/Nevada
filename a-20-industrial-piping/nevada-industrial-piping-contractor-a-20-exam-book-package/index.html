Exam Details

The Nevada Industrial Piping Contractor (A-20) trade examination evaluates knowledge relevant to industrial piping work and the construction activities that support piping installations. Candidates should prepare for both technical code questions and practical trade questions.

Industrial piping preparation should include subjects such as piping materials, fittings, valves, system components, pipe supports, installation methods, system operation, mechanical requirements, excavation, trenching, backfilling, mathematics, and jobsite safety.

The Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018 should receive regular attention because it is the exam-room reference included with this package. Candidates should become familiar with its organization and practice locating requirements involving piping systems, equipment, ventilation, combustion-related provisions where applicable, installation requirements, clearances, and other mechanical topics.

Pipe and Excavation Contracting supports the field-construction side of preparation. Industrial piping projects can require trenches, underground pipe, bedding, alignment, backfill, compaction, and safe excavation practices. These subjects are closely connected to the A-20 scope.

Modern Hydronic Heating for Residential and Light Commercial Buildings helps reinforce knowledge of closed-loop piping systems, circulation, system design concepts, pumps, valves, controls, heat transfer, and related hydronic principles that can strengthen overall piping-system understanding.

Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters supports the calculation skills needed in piping work. Candidates should be comfortable with measurements, fractions, decimals, basic geometry, pipe-related calculations, elevations, offsets, and other trade mathematics.

OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports preparation for safety topics involving trenches, excavation, ladders, personal protective equipment, material handling, tools, equipment, and other hazards commonly found on industrial construction projects.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada A-20 Industrial Piping trade examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Applicants must satisfy the applicable licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the trade test.

  1. Confirm the A-20 Industrial Piping classification. Make sure the work the business intends to perform falls within Nevada's Industrial Piping scope.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor license application must designate the person responsible for providing the applicable trade qualification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable work history demonstrating experience related to industrial piping, piping systems, excavation, trenching, and associated construction activities.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Provide the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. Examination eligibility is established after the contractor application and qualifying experience are reviewed.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. The qualifying individual can schedule the required examinations after receiving authorization.
  7. Prepare for the Industrial Piping trade examination. Study mechanical code requirements, piping systems, excavation, hydronic concepts, mathematics, and construction safety.
  8. Prepare for Business and Law. Nevada contractor applicants are also subject to the applicable Business and Law examination requirement unless a waiver applies.
  9. Complete applicable examinations. Satisfy the examination requirements associated with the contractor license application.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish the other applicable requirements before the contractor license is issued.

State Requirements

Nevada classifies A-20 Industrial Piping under the General Engineering contractor classifications. The scope covers the fabrication and installation of pipes for the transmission of steam, gases, chemicals, and other substances required for industrial manufacturing or commercial operations.

The classification also includes related excavation, trenching, and backfilling. This is an important part of the scope because many industrial piping systems are installed underground or require substantial site preparation before piping can be placed.

Candidates should therefore prepare for a combination of piping and civil construction knowledge. Understanding piping materials and system components is important, but so is knowledge of trenches, excavation procedures, bedding, backfill, equipment, and construction safety.

The qualifying individual must establish the applicable experience required for the requested contractor classification as part of the Nevada licensing process. The state licensing process also involves requirements beyond the trade exam, including application review and other applicable business and licensing requirements.

Passing the A-20 examination is an important licensing step, but it does not independently create a Nevada contractor license. Applicants must complete all applicable requirements before the license can be issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada A-20 preparation plan should combine mechanical-code navigation with piping fundamentals, excavation knowledge, mathematical problem solving, system understanding, and construction safety.

Mechanical Code preparation should center on the Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018. Candidates should become comfortable locating requirements rather than simply reading the code from beginning to end. Practice with the index, chapter headings, definitions, tables, and cross-references.

Piping Systems should include materials, fittings, valves, supports, connections, system components, installation practices, testing concepts, and how piping systems are used to move steam, gases, chemicals, and other substances in commercial and industrial settings.

Excavation and Trenching deserve dedicated preparation because those activities are specifically included within the A-20 classification. Study trench layout, bedding, pipe placement, backfill, compaction, equipment, underground conditions, and safe excavation methods.

Hydronic Systems provide useful preparation for understanding circulating fluid systems. Review pumps, flow, piping arrangements, valves, controls, heat transfer, expansion, and system components. The goal is not simply to memorize terminology but to understand how a piping system functions as a complete system.

Trade Mathematics should be practiced regularly. Pipefitters need to work accurately with fractions, decimals, lengths, elevations, angles, offsets, areas, volumes, and related calculations. Candidates should solve problems without depending on the mathematics book during testing because that reference is not allowed inside the examination center.

Safety should include OSHA concepts associated with industrial construction. Excavation and trenching are especially important because piping work frequently requires below-grade installation. Candidates should also review personal protective equipment, ladders, tools, equipment, material handling, and other common hazards.

Study-only references should be used with active recall. Read a section, close the book, and explain the concept or solve a related problem without looking at the reference. Then return to the book to correct mistakes or reinforce weak areas.

Mathematics practice should follow the same approach. Work calculations repeatedly until the steps become familiar. Candidates who must repeatedly consult a formula explanation during study may have difficulty applying the concept efficiently during an exam where the book is unavailable.

More on test Information and Study Materials

The Uniform Mechanical Code should receive a different type of preparation because it can be used during testing. Create practice questions that require code lookups and time how long it takes to locate the relevant provision. If a particular type of search takes too long, review that section and repeat the lookup later.

When using the code, candidates should not stop at the first sentence containing the correct keyword. Read enough surrounding material to determine whether definitions, exceptions, tables, conditions, or referenced sections affect the requirement.

Industrial piping candidates should also practice identifying the nature of the system before searching. A piping question may involve installation, equipment connection, materials, support, system operation, or another technical issue. Recognizing the category helps narrow the search.

A balanced study schedule might divide preparation into mechanical code, piping systems, excavation, mathematics, hydronic concepts, and safety. Rotating through these subjects can help candidates maintain broad knowledge without neglecting a weaker area.

Practice questions can then be used as a diagnostic tool. If excavation questions are consistently difficult, return to Pipe and Excavation Contracting and OSHA. If calculation questions are slow, dedicate additional sessions to Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters. If code searches are taking too long, increase timed navigation exercises with the Uniform Mechanical Code.

How does the Industrial Piping Contractor (A-20) exam work?

The Industrial Piping Contractor (A-20) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
