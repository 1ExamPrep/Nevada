## Exam Details

The Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor trade examination is administered through PSI as part of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Candidates must first satisfy the applicable Nevada State Contractors Board qualification process before receiving authorization to test.

- **Exam:** Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor
- **Testing Provider:** PSI
- **Number of Questions:** 80
- **Time Allowed:** 4 hours
- **Passing Standard:** 70%, or 56 correct answers

The examination covers a wide range of electrical knowledge. Major areas include general electrical knowledge, services, feeders and branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies and equipment, electrical power, motors, low voltage, lighting, illuminated signs, fire detection and alarm systems, safety information, transmission lines, and overcurrent protection.

**General Electrical Knowledge** provides the foundation for the rest of the exam. Candidates should understand basic electrical theory, terminology, calculations, code organization, electrical materials, equipment, and common installation practices.

**Services, Feeders, and Branch Circuits** should include load calculations, service equipment, feeder sizing, branch-circuit requirements, conductor protection, disconnecting means, and the relationship between service, feeder, and branch-circuit systems.

**Grounding and Bonding** is a critical technical area. Preparation should include grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding, bonding requirements, grounded conductors, grounding electrode conductors, fault-current paths, and NEC Article 250 navigation.

**Conductors and Cables** should include ampacity, conductor sizing, insulation types, temperature limitations, adjustment and correction factors, cable types, conductor identification, and installation requirements.

**Raceways and Boxes** should include conduit and tubing systems, fittings, box sizing, conductor fill, raceway fill, support requirements, protection from damage, and installation methods.

**Electrical Power and Motors** can require both technical knowledge and calculations. Candidates should review transformers, motors, motor circuits, controllers, disconnects, overload protection, short-circuit protection, generators, and related electrical equipment.

**Fire Detection and Alarm Systems** should be studied with NFPA 72, 2013. Candidates should understand basic fire alarm system components, initiating devices, notification appliances, circuits, power supplies, system performance, testing concepts, and installation requirements.

## Open Book Test

The Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor trade examination is an **open-book test** with specific approved references permitted in the examination room.

The approved exam-room references for this preparation are:

- **National Electrical Code or National Electrical Code Handbook, 2017**
- **NFPA 72 - National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code, 2013**

The NEC should be used continuously during preparation. Candidates should become familiar with its article structure, definitions, tables, annex material, index, exceptions, calculations, and cross-references. Efficient NEC navigation is especially important on an 80-question electrical contractor examination.

Practice locating common requirements involving services, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, conductors, boxes, motors, special occupancies, equipment, and lighting. The goal is not to memorize the entire NEC but to know where information is organized and how to find it efficiently.

NFPA 72 should be used for fire alarm and signaling system preparation. Become familiar with the organization of the standard and practice finding requirements related to initiating devices, notification appliances, circuits, system power, installation, inspection, testing, and system performance.

The following study references are **not allowed into the C-2 trade examination:**

- **Ugly's Electrical References**
- **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)**
- **National Electrical Safety Code, 2012**
- **Lineman's and Cableman's Handbook**

The books should be used before the examination to build knowledge in electrical calculations, safety, transmission systems, utility work, electrical field practices, and technical concepts that may appear on the test. Because they are not available in the examination room, study should focus on understanding and retaining the material rather than depending on last-minute lookups.

## State Requirements

The Nevada C-2 Electrical classification authorizes electrical contracting work involving the installation of electrical wires, fixtures, appliances, apparatuses, raceways, and conduits. The classification is broad and requires candidates to understand electrical construction across multiple types of systems and applications.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the trade qualifying individual generally must demonstrate at least four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the requested classification.

The qualifying experience generally must have been obtained within the 15 years immediately preceding the application. Experience records should clearly describe the electrical work performed and demonstrate how that work relates to the C-2 Electrical classification.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes financial responsibility requirements, applicable contractor bonding, background requirements, and other conditions that must be completed in addition to the examination.

Applicants generally must satisfy both the trade qualification and contractor management requirements unless an approved waiver applies. Passing the C-2 examination does not by itself create an active Nevada contractor license.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-2 study plan should divide the material into code-heavy subjects, calculation-heavy subjects, safety topics, fire alarm material, and transmission-related knowledge. This makes it easier to identify exactly where additional review is needed.

**Master NEC navigation.** Practice locating Articles 110, 210, 220, 230, 240, 250, 300, 310, 314, 430, and other commonly used sections. Learn to move between articles, tables, definitions, and exceptions efficiently.

**Practice electrical calculations.** Review conductor sizing, load calculations, voltage relationships, motors, transformers, raceway fill, box fill, grounding conductors, and overcurrent protection. Use Ugly's Electrical References during study to reinforce formulas and field calculations.

**Study safety independently.** OSHA material is not available during the examination, so candidates should develop working knowledge of construction safety, electrical hazards, protective equipment, fall protection, and safe work practices before testing.

**Review transmission systems.** Use the National Electrical Safety Code and Lineman's and Cableman's Handbook to strengthen understanding of electrical supply systems, overhead and underground lines, conductors, distribution equipment, and utility-related practices.

**Prepare for fire alarm questions.** Use NFPA 72 regularly and become comfortable locating requirements for fire detection and signaling systems. Fire alarm preparation should combine basic system knowledge with reference-navigation practice.

**Use timed practice.** Four hours for 80 questions provides an average of three minutes per question. Practice deciding when to answer from knowledge and when a code lookup is necessary.

**Review missed questions by subject.** If several missed questions involve grounding, conductor sizing, motors, fire alarms, or transmission lines, direct the next study session toward that specific topic instead of repeating material already understood.

## How does the Electrical Contractor (C-2) exam work?

The Electrical Contractor (C-2) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
