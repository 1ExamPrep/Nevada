Exam Details

The Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor trade examination contains 80 questions. Candidates are allowed 4 hours to complete the exam, and the minimum passing score is 56 correct answers.

The examination covers the following subject areas:

  • General Electrical Knowledge: 6 questions.
  • Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits: 7 questions.
  • Grounding and Bonding: 7 questions.
  • Conductors and Cables: 8 questions.
  • Raceways and Boxes: 7 questions.
  • Special Occupancies and Equipment: 5 questions.
  • Electrical Power: 6 questions.
  • Motors: 6 questions.
  • Low Voltage: 2 questions.
  • Lighting: 5 questions.
  • Illuminated Signs: 2 questions.
  • Fire Detection and Alarm Systems: 2 questions.
  • Safety Information: 8 questions.
  • Transmission Lines: 5 questions.
  • Overcurrent Protection: 4 questions.

Conductors and Cables and Safety Information are the largest individual categories, with eight questions each. Services, Feeders, and Branch Circuits; Grounding and Bonding; and Raceways and Boxes each contain seven questions. Candidates should still prepare carefully for every category because the exam draws from a wide range of electrical work.

With 80 questions and four hours available, candidates have an average of approximately three minutes per question. Efficient code navigation, electrical calculations, and the ability to recognize when a question can be answered directly from trade knowledge can all help with time management.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book test with specific approved references. Candidates may use the National Electrical Code or National Electrical Code Handbook, 2017 and NFPA 72 - National Fire Alarm Code, 2013 during the examination.

The open-book format does not mean every book used to develop the examination can be taken into the testing center. Ugly's Electrical References, OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926, National Electrical Safety Code 2012, and Lineman's and Cableman's Handbook are study references but are not permitted inside the exam room.

The distinction makes preparation important. Candidates should use the non-exam-room references to learn electrical safety, utility practices, transmission concepts, formulas, and field knowledge before the examination. The information needs to become part of your working knowledge rather than something you expect to research during testing.

For the NEC and NFPA 72, repeated navigation practice is essential. Learn how the NEC articles are organized, become comfortable using the index and tables, and practice locating code requirements for grounding, conductors, services, motors, overcurrent protection, special occupancies, and other frequently tested topics.

NFPA 72 should be used to strengthen your understanding of fire detection, alarm, signaling, notification, installation, and system requirements. Even though fire alarm questions represent a smaller portion of the exam, candidates should know how to locate applicable material efficiently.

Licensing Steps

Passing the C-2 electrical examination is one part of obtaining a Nevada contractor license. Applicants must also complete the contractor application process and satisfy the applicable qualification requirements.

  1. Determine the correct classification. Confirm that Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor is the classification that matches the electrical contracting work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor application must identify the person who will provide the required trade qualification for the business.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare the experience information required to demonstrate qualification for the electrical classification.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Submit the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Receive examination eligibility. Examination eligibility is provided after the application is submitted and applicable experience requirements are reviewed.
  6. Prepare for the C-2 trade examination. Study all exam categories and become comfortable navigating the approved NEC and NFPA 72 references.
  7. Prepare for Business and Law. Nevada contractor applicants generally must also complete the Business and Law examination unless an applicable waiver applies.
  8. Complete required examinations. Take the C-2 trade examination and applicable Business and Law examination.
  9. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Satisfy applicable financial, bond, background, and other requirements before the contractor license is issued.

Applicants cannot schedule the Nevada contractor examination until they have submitted the required contractor application and received examination eligibility. Candidates can use the application review period to begin studying the C-2 references and building their electrical code-navigation skills.

State Requirements

Nevada contractor licensing is administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board. The C-2 classification applies to electrical contracting and allows a properly licensed contractor to install electrical wires, fixtures, appliances, apparatuses, raceways, and conduits within the scope of the classification.

The qualifying individual generally must demonstrate at least four full years of qualifying experience within the 15 years immediately before the application is filed. Qualifying experience may be at the journeyman, supervising employee, foreman, or contractor level within the applicable trade.

Nevada also requires contractor applicants to satisfy the applicable business, financial responsibility, background, examination, and bond requirements associated with licensing.

The qualifying individual generally must complete both the trade examination and Nevada Business and Law examination unless an examination waiver applies. Passing the C-2 examination alone does not automatically result in issuance of a contractor license.

Candidates should approach licensing as a combination of application preparation and examination preparation. Keeping experience documentation, application materials, and study work organized can help make the process easier to manage.

Test Information and Study Materials

The C-2 examination covers a wide range of electrical subjects, so candidates should avoid concentrating exclusively on the NEC articles they already know well. A structured study plan should address every subject area while allocating additional time to the categories containing the most questions.

Conductors and Cables should receive substantial attention. Practice conductor sizing, ampacity, insulation types, temperature limitations, adjustment and correction concepts, conductor identification, and installation requirements.

Safety Information is another major category. Review construction safety practices, electrical hazards, personal protective requirements, jobsite safety responsibilities, and OSHA-related concepts before the exam because the OSHA reference itself cannot be used during testing.

Services, Feeders, and Branch Circuits require familiarity with service requirements, feeder calculations, branch-circuit rules, load calculations, disconnects, and conductor sizing. Use the NEC repeatedly when reviewing these subjects.

Grounding and Bonding is a fundamental C-2 study area. Candidates should practice locating grounding electrode requirements, equipment grounding conductor rules, bonding provisions, separately derived system requirements, and related sizing tables.

Raceways and Boxes preparation should include raceway types, conduit fill, support requirements, box fill, installation restrictions, and practical wiring-method knowledge.

Motors and Electrical Power require both code knowledge and calculation skills. Review motor circuits, motor protection, disconnecting means, conductor sizing, transformers, power calculations, and related electrical concepts.

Transmission Lines should be studied with the National Electrical Safety Code and Lineman's and Cableman's Handbook before test day. These references cannot enter the exam, so candidates should focus on retaining the concepts and practices they contain.

Fire Detection and Alarm Systems should be reviewed with NFPA 72. Practice finding information in the reference so you do not lose excessive time when a fire alarm question requires a lookup.

Use practice questions to identify weak areas. When you miss a question, determine whether the issue was electrical knowledge, calculation technique, code interpretation, or slow reference navigation. Then return to the applicable reference and review the underlying concept.

For open-book practice, work under realistic time limits. Learning where information is located can be just as important as understanding the answer. Repeated code-navigation drills can help reduce the time spent searching during the actual examination.

How does the Electrical Contractor (C-2) exam work?

The Electrical Contractor (C-2) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
