## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-2 study plan should include electrical theory, practical trade knowledge, code navigation, calculations, fire alarm review, and timed practice.

**Services, Feeders, and Branch Circuits** should be reviewed using the NEC. Candidates should become familiar with service equipment, feeder requirements, branch-circuit rules, conductor sizing, load concepts, and applicable disconnect requirements.

**Grounding and Bonding** is another major area. Practice locating grounding electrode requirements, bonding rules, equipment grounding conductor requirements, separately derived systems, and applicable sizing provisions.

**Conductors and Cables** require familiarity with ampacity, insulation types, temperature limitations, adjustment and correction concepts, conductor identification, and installation requirements.

**Raceways and Boxes** should be studied with attention to conduit types, fill requirements, support methods, box fill, allowable uses, and restrictions associated with different wiring systems.

**Motors** and **Electrical Power** require both code knowledge and calculation skills. Candidates should practice motor conductor sizing, protection, disconnecting means, transformer concepts, and electrical power calculations.

**Overcurrent Protection** should also be reviewed carefully. Become familiar with circuit protection requirements, equipment ratings, conductor protection concepts, and the NEC articles and tables that apply.

**Special Occupancies and Equipment** may require navigating less frequently used NEC articles. Candidates should practice identifying the correct article based on the type of installation described in a question.

**Lighting** and **Illuminated Signs** should be part of the study plan as well. Review lighting circuits, equipment requirements, and applicable code provisions.

**Fire Detection and Alarm Systems** should be prepared using NFPA 72. Candidates should become comfortable finding information about alarm systems, notification, signaling, devices, installation, and testing requirements.

One of the most useful study methods is timed reference navigation. Select a practice question, determine which book should contain the answer, and give yourself a limited amount of time to locate the applicable section.

If the search takes too long, review the index or table of contents and repeat the process. Repetition can help improve the speed and confidence needed for an open-book electrical exam.

Practice questions should be used to identify weak areas rather than simply memorizing answers. When you miss a question, determine whether the issue came from electrical theory, code navigation, calculation technique, or misunderstanding the wording.

## How does the Electrical Contractor (C-2) exam work?

The Electrical Contractor (C-2) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
