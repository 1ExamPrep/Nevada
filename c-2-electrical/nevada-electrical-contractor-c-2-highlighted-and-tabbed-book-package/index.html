Test Information and Study Materials

The Nevada C-2 examination covers a wide range of electrical topics, so candidates should prepare using a balanced study plan instead of concentrating only on one or two areas.

General Electrical Knowledge should include electrical theory, terminology, practical electrical concepts, and an understanding of common electrical systems.

Services, Feeders, and Branch Circuits should be reviewed using the NEC. Candidates should study service equipment, feeder requirements, branch-circuit rules, conductor sizing, load concepts, and applicable disconnect requirements.

Grounding and Bonding is another important area. Practice locating grounding electrode requirements, bonding rules, equipment grounding conductor requirements, and related sizing provisions.

Conductors and Cables preparation should include ampacity, insulation types, conductor identification, temperature limitations, adjustment and correction concepts, and installation requirements.

Raceways and Boxes should be reviewed for conduit types, raceway requirements, box fill, support methods, allowable uses, and restrictions associated with different wiring systems.

Special Occupancies and Equipment often require navigating specific NEC articles. Candidates should practice identifying the correct article based on the type of installation described in a question.

Electrical Power and Motors require both code knowledge and calculation skills. Review motor conductor sizing, motor protection, disconnecting means, transformers, power calculations, and related electrical requirements.

Low Voltage, Lighting, and Illuminated Signs should also be included in preparation. Candidates should understand the applicable systems, terminology, and code provisions associated with these areas.

Fire Detection and Alarm Systems should be studied with NFPA 72. Practice locating information on fire alarm components, notification, signaling, installation, testing, and related system requirements.

Safety Information should be reviewed using OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 before test day. Because this reference is not allowed inside the examination center, candidates should focus on understanding and retaining the safety concepts rather than expecting to look them up during the exam.

Transmission Lines should be studied with the National Electrical Safety Code and Lineman’s and Cableman’s Handbook. These books provide important utility and transmission-related knowledge but are not available for exam-room use.

Overcurrent Protection should be reviewed through the NEC, including circuit protection, equipment ratings, conductor protection, and related code provisions.

The highlighted and tabbed books can help candidates organize all of these subjects more efficiently. Tabs make it easier to return to major topics, while highlighting can help important material stand out during review.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Practice questions should be used to identify weak areas. When you miss a question, determine whether the issue came from electrical theory, a calculation, code navigation, safety knowledge, or misunderstanding the wording.

For open-book practice, use timed navigation drills. Select a topic, determine whether the NEC or NFPA 72 is the correct reference, and practice locating the applicable section efficiently.

If a search takes too long, repeat it later. The goal is to build familiarity through repetition so reference navigation becomes more natural under testing conditions.

How does the Electrical Contractor (C-2) exam work?

The Electrical Contractor (C-2) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
