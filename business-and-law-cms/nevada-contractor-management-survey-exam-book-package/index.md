## Exam Details

The Nevada Contractor Management Survey examination is the general business and law examination used in the Nevada contractor licensing process. The Nevada State Contractors Board requires applicants subject to examination requirements to complete the applicable Contractor Management Survey and the trade examination associated with the requested license classification.

- **Exam:** Nevada Contractor Management Survey
- **Common Name:** CMS Examination
- **Testing Provider:** PSI
- **Scored Questions:** 60
- **Scored Testing Time:** 120 minutes
- **Non-Scored Questions:** 3
- **Additional Time for Non-Scored Questions:** 6 minutes
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 45 correct scored answers, or 75%

The scored portion of the Contractor Management Survey examination is divided into the following subject areas:

- **Licensing:** 10 questions
- **Estimating and Bidding:** 7 questions
- **Lien Law:** 3 questions
- **Financial Management:** 12 questions
- **Tax Laws:** 5 questions
- **Labor Laws:** 5 questions
- **Project Management:** 3 questions
- **Contracts:** 6 questions
- **Risk Management:** 4 questions
- **Environmental and Safety:** 5 questions

**Financial Management** is the largest individual content category with 12 questions. Candidates should be comfortable with fundamental contractor financial concepts, business records, financial statements, costs, cash flow, overhead, accounting-related terminology, and the financial responsibilities of operating a contracting business.

**Licensing** accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should understand Nevada contractor licensing requirements, responsibilities associated with a contractor license, qualifying-party concepts, license classifications, and rules affecting licensed contracting activity.

**Estimating and Bidding** represents seven questions. Study should include estimating principles, bid preparation, costs, overhead, labor, materials, and other considerations involved in preparing construction proposals.

**Contracts** accounts for six questions. Candidates should understand contract fundamentals, responsibilities of contracting parties, documentation, changes, payment considerations, and common construction contract concepts.

Tax Laws, Labor Laws, Risk Management, Environmental and Safety, Project Management, and Lien Law make up the remaining scored questions and should all receive dedicated preparation.

## Open Book Test

The Nevada Contractor Management Survey examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates may use the **Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada** during the examination.

The open-book format makes reference navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates should learn how the manual is organized, identify the major business and law sections, and practice finding information under timed conditions.

The manual should not be treated as a book that can simply be searched from beginning to end during the exam. With 60 scored questions and 120 minutes allotted to the scored portion, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes per scored question. Efficient navigation matters.

One useful approach is to first identify the subject area of the question. A question involving contractor license requirements should direct the candidate toward licensing material, while a question involving financial statements or business costs should point toward financial management. Questions involving notices, payment rights, or lien procedures should direct attention toward lien-law content.

Candidates should also become familiar with key terms used throughout construction business and law. Recognizing the correct terminology can make it much easier to locate an answer quickly in an open-book examination.

## Licensing Steps

The Contractor Management Survey examination is part of the broader Nevada contractor licensing process. The exact pathway depends on the classification requested and the applicant's circumstances, but the general process includes application review, qualification, examinations when required, and completion of the remaining licensing requirements.

1. **Select the appropriate Nevada contractor classification.** Determine the classification that matches the construction work the business intends to perform.
2. **Identify the qualifying individual.** Designate the person whose experience and qualifications will support the contractor license application.
3. **Document qualifying experience.** Prepare the applicable experience documentation required for the requested classification.
4. **Prepare the Nevada contractor license application.** Gather required business information, qualifier information, financial documentation, and supporting materials.
5. **Submit the application to the Nevada State Contractors Board.** The Board reviews the application and verifies the applicant's qualifications.
6. **Receive examination eligibility when testing is required.** An examination eligibility notice is provided after the application has been filed and the applicable qualifications have been reviewed.
7. **Schedule the required examinations.** Candidates who must test schedule the Contractor Management Survey examination and the applicable trade examination.
8. **Prepare for the CMS examination.** Study the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada and review the licensing, financial, estimating, contract, tax, labor, lien, project management, risk management, environmental, and safety topics represented on the exam.
9. **Prepare for the applicable trade examination.** Candidates should separately study the trade subjects and references associated with their requested classification.
10. **Complete the required examinations.** Satisfy the applicable business and law and trade examination requirements.
11. **Complete remaining licensing requirements.** Address applicable financial responsibility, bonding, background, and other requirements established by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

The Nevada State Contractors Board provides examination eligibility after an application is filed and the applicant's experience has been reviewed. Candidates should follow the instructions contained in their eligibility notice when scheduling required examinations.

## State Requirements

Nevada generally requires contractor license applicants who are subject to testing to pass both a **general business and law examination** and the **trade examination** applicable to the requested classification. The Contractor Management Survey serves as the general business and law examination.

The CMS examination is relevant across contractor classifications because operating a licensed contracting business requires more than technical trade ability. Contractors must also understand licensing responsibilities, bidding, contracts, financial management, labor requirements, taxes, risk, safety, and other business obligations.

Applicants receive examination eligibility after filing their application and completing the applicable qualification review. Candidates should not treat passing the Contractor Management Survey as the final step in licensing. The CMS exam is one component of the overall contractor license process.

Depending on the application, candidates may also need to pass a trade examination specific to the requested license classification. Applicants must complete all other applicable Nevada State Contractors Board requirements before a license can be issued.

The Nevada State Contractors Board may also determine whether an applicant qualifies for an examination waiver or other licensing pathway under applicable rules. Candidates should follow the requirements that apply to their individual application and classification.

Understanding these licensing responsibilities is also part of CMS preparation. The examination tests whether candidates understand the business and regulatory framework that accompanies licensed contracting activity in Nevada.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada Contractor Management Survey study plan should reflect the official content weighting rather than giving equal time to every subject.

**Prioritize Financial Management.** With 12 questions, this is the largest exam category. Review contractor financial concepts, financial statements, costs, business records, cash flow, overhead, and financial decision-making.

**Study Licensing carefully.** Licensing represents 10 questions. Candidates should understand the responsibilities that accompany a Nevada contractor license, the role of the qualifying individual, classification concepts, and key regulatory requirements.

**Review Estimating and Bidding.** Seven questions address estimating and bidding. Candidates should understand how costs, labor, materials, overhead, and other project considerations affect an estimate or bid.

**Practice Contract concepts.** Six questions cover contracts. Review contract formation, responsibilities, documentation, changes, payments, and common construction contract issues.

**Review Tax Laws and Labor Laws.** Each category represents five questions. Candidates should be familiar with contractor responsibilities involving taxes, workers, employment-related requirements, and business obligations.

**Study Environmental and Safety responsibilities.** The category also contains five questions and focuses on important responsibilities that affect contractor operations and jobsite management.

**Review Risk Management.** Four questions cover risk. Candidates should understand common contracting risks and basic approaches to managing business and project exposure.

**Do not overlook Lien Law.** Although lien law accounts for only three questions, it is an important area of contractor business knowledge. Review notices, payment rights, and the basic structure of lien-related responsibilities covered by the Nevada manual.

**Review Project Management.** Three questions address project-management principles. Candidates should understand scheduling, coordination, documentation, and management responsibilities involved in completing construction work.

**Practice timed book navigation.** The scored examination allows 120 minutes for 60 scored items. During preparation, practice identifying the subject of a question and finding the relevant portion of the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada efficiently.

**Use the manual consistently.** Candidates who repeatedly work with the reference during study can build familiarity with its organization. This is more useful than waiting until exam day to open the manual for the first time.

**Know which questions you can answer directly.** The open-book format does not require candidates to look up every answer. When a concept is familiar, answering directly can preserve time for more difficult questions that require reference confirmation.
