## Exam Details

The Nevada State Contractors Board requires qualifying individuals seeking contractor licensure to pass a general Business and Law examination known as the **Contractor Management Survey** or **CMS**, unless the applicant qualifies for an applicable examination waiver. Depending on the contractor classification being pursued, a separate trade examination may also be required.

The Nevada Contractor Management Survey examination is administered by **PSI Services LLC** on behalf of the Nevada State Contractors Board.

The current CMS examination contains **60 scored questions**. Candidates receive **120 minutes** for the scored portion of the examination.

The examination also includes **3 non-scored questions** with an additional **6 minutes** allotted for those items. This results in a total examination appointment time of approximately **126 minutes** for the CMS questions.

The minimum passing score is **45 correct answers out of 60 scored questions**, which equals **75%**.

The official examination content outline divides the 60 scored questions among the following subject areas:

- **Licensing:** 10 questions
- **Estimating and Bidding:** 7 questions
- **Lien Law:** 3 questions
- **Financial Management:** 12 questions
- **Tax Laws:** 5 questions
- **Labor Laws:** 5 questions
- **Project Management:** 3 questions
- **Contracts:** 6 questions
- **Risk Management:** 4 questions
- **Environmental and Safety:** 5 questions

Financial Management represents the largest individual content area with 12 scored questions, followed by Licensing with 10 questions. Estimating and Bidding, Contracts, Tax Laws, Labor Laws, Environmental and Safety, Risk Management, Project Management, and Lien Law are also part of the examination.

The distribution means candidates need a balanced preparation strategy. A contractor who is comfortable estimating construction work may still need significant preparation in licensing law, financial concepts, taxes, labor requirements, contracts, or lien law. Likewise, candidates with strong business backgrounds should still become familiar with the Nevada-specific contractor requirements contained in the approved reference.

## Open Book Test

The Nevada Contractor Management Survey examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates may use the approved **Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada** during the examination.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation an important part of exam preparation. Candidates have a limited amount of testing time and should not expect to read large sections of the manual for the first time while answering questions. Becoming familiar with the organization of the book before examination day can make it easier to locate the information associated with licensing, contracts, finances, tax laws, employment requirements, lien law, and other tested subjects.

The approved reference may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination session. However, the reference may not contain written notes. Candidates also may not write in or highlight their references during the examination.

Additional loose or attached papers are not permitted with the approved reference. Permanent tabs may be used to index the book, but temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed.

Candidates may also use a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator that complies with the testing requirements.

Flash cards can complement an open-book testing strategy by strengthening recognition of the material before you begin searching the manual. ## Licensing Steps

The Contractor Management Survey examination is part of the Nevada contractor licensing process administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board. Candidates must follow the state application and examination sequence before scheduling the required tests.

1. **Determine the contractor classification you are pursuing.** Nevada licenses contractors according to specific classifications. The classification determines whether a separate trade examination is required in addition to the CMS examination.
2. **Submit the contractor license application to the Nevada State Contractors Board.** Candidates may not schedule the licensing examinations before submitting the applicable contractor application.
3. **Complete the required experience documentation.** The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the qualifying individual’s experience as part of the application process.
4. **Receive examination eligibility.** After the application is submitted and the Board verifies the applicable experience requirements, the qualifying individual receives an Examination Eligibility form and Candidate ID number.
5. **Schedule the Contractor Management Survey examination.** Eligible candidates schedule their examination through PSI.
6. **Prepare for the CMS examination.** Study the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada, review the examination content areas, and practice navigating the approved reference.
7. **Take and pass the CMS examination.** Candidates must earn at least 45 correct answers out of 60 scored questions.
8. **Take the required trade examination when applicable.** Nevada generally requires a trade examination specific to the contractor classification in addition to the CMS examination, unless the classification or applicant qualifies for an applicable waiver.
9. **Complete the remaining licensing requirements.** Examination results are only one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must satisfy the other requirements established by the Nevada State Contractors Board.
10. **Receive approval before operating under the contractor license.** Passing the examination does not by itself authorize a person or business to engage in contracting activities that require Nevada licensure.

Candidates should pay close attention to the examination authorization process. The Nevada State Contractors Board states that applicants may not schedule their required examination until the contractor application has been submitted and an Examination Eligibility form has been issued.

## State Requirements

The **Nevada State Contractors Board** regulates contractor licensing in Nevada. The qualifying individual for a contractor license is generally required to pass the Business and Law Contractor Management Survey examination and the trade examination associated with the requested classification unless an applicable examination waiver applies.

The CMS examination is the general business and law portion of the licensing examination process. It is intended to evaluate whether a candidate understands important responsibilities associated with operating and managing a contracting business in Nevada.

Nevada’s licensing process begins with the contractor license application rather than with independent examination scheduling. The applicant submits the application to the Nevada State Contractors Board, and the Board reviews qualifying experience before issuing examination eligibility.

Once approved, the qualifying individual receives the information needed to schedule the required examination through PSI. Candidates pursuing classifications that require a trade examination will generally need to complete both the CMS and the applicable trade examination.

Nevada also establishes rules governing examination retakes. A candidate who fails an examination may reschedule after the required waiting period. The Nevada State Contractors Board states that if an examination is failed three times, the contractor license application becomes void under the applicable Nevada administrative requirements.

The Contractor Management Survey examination should therefore be treated as a significant part of the overall licensing process. Preparation should cover both the tested business principles and the Nevada-specific contractor laws and procedures identified in the approved reference.
