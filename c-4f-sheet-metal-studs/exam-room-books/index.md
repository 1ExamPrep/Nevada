## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C4-F preparation plan should focus primarily on **Metal Framing Installation** because this subject represents 16 of the 20 examination questions.

Begin with the basic components of a nonstructural sheet metal framing system. Candidates should understand the purpose of studs, tracks, framing around openings, ceiling framing components, and common connection points.

**Layout** is an important part of metal framing installation. Candidates should understand how walls and ceilings are positioned, how tracks are aligned, and how stud spacing affects the completed assembly.

**Stud installation** should include placement within tracks, alignment, spacing, orientation, fastening, and coordination with adjacent building components.

**Tracks** form the top and bottom boundaries of many nonstructural wall systems. Candidates should understand how tracks relate to studs, floors, ceilings, and openings.

**Openings** should receive dedicated study. Doorways, access openings, and other penetrations can require additional framing, and candidates should understand how sheet metal stud systems are arranged around these conditions.

**Ceiling framing** is also part of the classification. Review sheet metal framing relationships used for ceilings, including support, alignment, attachment, and coordination with other systems.

**Fasteners and connections** should be understood as part of overall framing stability. Candidates should know why proper attachment matters and how connection methods affect alignment and performance.

Although supplemental gypsum references are not included in this Books Allowed into Exam Package, candidates should still understand the relationship between sheet metal framing and gypsum wall and ceiling systems. Metal studs are often part of complete assemblies rather than standalone components.

**Fire-resistance** should be incorporated into International Building Code study. Some wall and ceiling systems must meet fire-resistance requirements, and candidates should know how to locate applicable code provisions.

Use the **International Building Code, 2018** regularly rather than waiting until immediately before the exam. Repeated navigation can help candidates become more comfortable with chapter organization and common search paths.

The **index** is especially useful when the exact code location is not known. Identify the main technical term used in the question and search for that term or a related phrase in the index.

The **table of contents** is useful when candidates know the general subject but not the exact section. This can help narrow the search before turning to the index or individual chapter provisions.

**Exceptions** can change the application of a general rule. Candidates should always check whether a code provision includes exceptions or conditions that affect the answer.

**Cross-references** should also be followed carefully. One code section may direct the reader to another chapter or provision that contains the complete requirement.

Timed lookup drills can improve open-book performance. Choose a topic such as wall assemblies, ceiling construction, fire resistance, building materials, or definitions and locate the applicable provision within a set time.

Practice questions should initially be divided between metal framing installation and code requirements. Once both areas become familiar, move into mixed-question sessions.

Mixed practice is useful because the examination can move from a practical installation question to an International Building Code question without warning.

Because the examination contains only 20 questions, careful reading is especially important. Candidates should distinguish between questions asking for practical installation knowledge and questions asking for a code-based requirement.

Time management should remain part of preparation. Answer familiar metal framing questions efficiently, use the IBC for targeted confirmation when needed, and return to difficult items after completing questions that can be answered quickly.

## How does the Sheet Metal Studs Contractor (C-4F) exam work?

The Sheet Metal Studs Contractor (C-4F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
