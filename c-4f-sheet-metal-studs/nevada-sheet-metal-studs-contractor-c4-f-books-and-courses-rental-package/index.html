Exam Details

The Nevada C4-F Sheet Metal Studs Contractor examination contains 20 questions. Candidates are allowed 1 hour to complete the examination and must answer 14 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into two main areas:

  • Code Requirements: 4 questions
  • Metal Framing Installation: 16 questions

Metal Framing Installation is the dominant subject area, accounting for 16 of the 20 questions. Candidates should devote most of their study time to practical framing knowledge rather than concentrating only on code lookup skills.

Preparation should include sheet metal studs, tracks, partitions, ceiling framing, layout, alignment, spacing, fastening, openings, connections, and coordination with gypsum wall and ceiling systems.

Code Requirements accounts for 4 questions. Candidates should understand how the International Building Code applies to wall assemblies, ceiling systems, construction materials, fire-resistance provisions, definitions, and other requirements associated with nonstructural framing.

Because the examination contains only 20 questions, each answer carries significant weight. Candidates should carefully distinguish between practical installation questions and questions that require use of the International Building Code.

State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-4F Sheet Metal Studs as a specialty subclassification focused on the erection and installation of sheet metal studs.

The authorized scope covers sheet metal studs used for framing ceilings and nonstructural partitions.

The classification does not authorize installation of wood framing. Candidates should therefore focus on light-gauge sheet metal framing systems rather than traditional wood framing methods.

The classification also does not authorize structural steel framing. C-4F work is centered on nonstructural sheet metal studs and related ceiling and partition framing.

Practical knowledge should include layout, track placement, stud spacing, alignment, fastening, wall openings, ceiling framing, and coordination with gypsum wall and ceiling systems.

Fire-resistance and building-code requirements are also important because many wall and ceiling assemblies must meet specific construction requirements. Candidates should understand how framing components relate to complete assemblies and how to locate applicable provisions in the International Building Code.

The C4-F trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, financial, business, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C4-F preparation plan should focus primarily on Metal Framing Installation because this subject represents 16 of the 20 examination questions.

Begin with the basic components of a nonstructural metal framing system. Candidates should understand studs, tracks, wall partitions, ceiling framing members, openings, and common connection points.

Layout is a fundamental skill. Review how partitions are positioned, how tracks are aligned, how stud spacing is established, and how framing relates to surrounding construction.

Stud installation should include placement within tracks, orientation, spacing, alignment, fastening, and coordination with openings and adjacent systems.

Track installation should include top and bottom track placement, attachment, alignment, and the relationship between tracks and studs.

Openings should receive dedicated study. Doorways, access openings, and other penetrations can require additional framing considerations, and candidates should understand how metal stud systems are arranged around these conditions.

Ceiling framing is part of the C-4F scope. Review support, alignment, attachment, spacing, and coordination with walls and other building components.

The Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th Edition is particularly useful for understanding how metal studs work within complete gypsum wall and ceiling assemblies. Review partitions, tracks, studs, rated assemblies, attachment, and typical construction sequences.

Because the Gypsum Construction Handbook is not allowed into the trade examination room, candidates should use it to build retained knowledge. After reviewing a topic, close the book and explain the installation sequence from memory.

The Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016 reference can reinforce general framing, layout, measurement, wall construction, ceiling relationships, and openings. Although wood framing itself is outside the C-4F scope, general construction knowledge can help candidates understand how nonstructural metal framing fits within a larger building system.

Fasteners and connections should be studied as part of overall framing performance. Proper attachment affects alignment, stability, and completion of the assembly.

Fire-resistance should be connected to both IBC study and gypsum-system study. Rated assemblies often depend on the complete combination of framing and board components rather than one material by itself.

The International Building Code, 2018 should be used throughout preparation. Practice locating wall, ceiling, materials, fire-resistance, and construction provisions using the index and table of contents.

How does the Sheet Metal Studs Contractor (C-4F) exam work?

The Sheet Metal Studs Contractor (C-4F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
