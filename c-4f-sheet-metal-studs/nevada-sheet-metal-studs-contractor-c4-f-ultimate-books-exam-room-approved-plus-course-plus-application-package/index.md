## Exam Details

The Nevada C4-F Sheet Metal Studs Contractor examination is part of the Nevada State Contractors Board licensing process. Nevada uses PSI to administer required contractor examinations after an applicant has submitted the license application and received examination eligibility.

The C4-F trade examination contains **20 questions**. Candidates are allowed **1 hour** to complete the examination and must answer **14 questions correctly** to pass.

The examination is divided into two principal subject areas:

- **Code Requirements:** 4 questions.
- **Metal Framing Installation:** 16 questions.

Metal Framing Installation represents the majority of the examination. Candidates should devote substantial preparation time to practical sheet metal framing concepts, including layout, track placement, stud spacing, alignment, wall openings, ceiling framing, fasteners, attachment, connections, and coordination with wall and ceiling assemblies.

Code Requirements account for four questions. Candidates should be comfortable using the International Building Code to locate provisions involving wall systems, ceiling assemblies, fire-resistance requirements, construction materials, definitions, tables, and other building-code subjects relevant to nonstructural sheet metal framing.

Because the examination contains only 20 questions, every question represents a meaningful portion of the final score. Careful reading, solid installation knowledge, and efficient reference use are important components of preparation.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C4-F study plan should emphasize **Metal Framing Installation** because it represents 16 of the 20 examination questions. Candidates should understand how nonstructural sheet metal framing systems are laid out, assembled, connected, aligned, and coordinated with walls and ceilings.

Begin with the main components of the system. Understand the purpose of studs and tracks, how tracks establish wall boundaries, how studs are positioned within tracks, and how framing is arranged around doors and other openings.

Stud spacing, alignment, orientation, fastening, and attachment deserve dedicated review. Candidates should understand why consistent alignment matters and how improper framing can affect the completed wall or ceiling assembly.

Ceiling framing should also be included in preparation because the C-4F scope specifically covers sheet metal studs used for framing ceilings as well as nonstructural partitions. Review support, alignment, attachment, and coordination with adjacent construction.

Openings can require additional framing considerations. Candidates should study how nonstructural metal framing is arranged around door openings, access openings, penetrations, and other interruptions in a wall or ceiling system.

Fasteners and connections are another important part of preparation. Understanding how framing components are connected helps candidates evaluate installation questions involving stability, alignment, track attachment, and framing continuity.

Code Requirements represent four questions, so candidates should incorporate regular International Building Code navigation into their study schedule. Focus on wall and ceiling requirements, fire-resistance provisions, construction materials, definitions, tables, and other subjects related to interior nonstructural framing.

The code index is one of the most useful navigation tools. Practice identifying the main technical term in a question and finding the corresponding index entry. From there, move to the listed section and read enough surrounding material to understand the complete requirement.

Tables and exceptions should receive particular attention. A code provision may state a general rule but then provide an exception or table that changes how the requirement applies. Candidates should avoid stopping at the first sentence that appears to answer the question.

Timed practice can help candidates become more comfortable with the one-hour testing period. Answer practical installation questions efficiently and use targeted code lookups when necessary. If a code search begins taking too long, move on and return to the question later if time remains.

## How does the Sheet Metal Studs Contractor (C-4F) exam work?

The Sheet Metal Studs Contractor (C-4F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
