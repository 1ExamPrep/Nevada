Exam Details

The Nevada C4-F Sheet Metal Studs Contractor examination contains 20 questions. Candidates are allowed 1 hour to complete the examination and must answer 14 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Code Requirements: 4 questions
  • Metal Framing Installation: 16 questions

Metal Framing Installation accounts for 16 of the 20 examination questions, making it the dominant subject area. Candidates should devote most of their preparation time to the practical methods used to lay out, assemble, secure, and complete nonstructural sheet metal framing systems.

Preparation should include metal studs, tracks, partitions, ceiling framing, openings, connections, fasteners, layout, spacing, alignment, bracing, and relationships between framing systems and gypsum assemblies.

Code Requirements accounts for 4 questions. These questions require candidates to understand how the International Building Code applies to wall systems, ceiling systems, fire-resistance requirements, materials, construction classifications, and other code provisions relevant to nonstructural metal framing.

Although the examination is short compared with many Nevada contractor exams, candidates should not underestimate the importance of preparation. With only 20 questions, each answer represents a significant portion of the overall score.

State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-4F Sheet Metal Studs as a subclassification related to painting and decorating specialty work.

The authorized C-4F scope covers the erection and installation of studs made of sheet metal for the framing of ceilings and for nonstructural partitioning.

The classification specifically does not include installation of wood or structural steel for framing. This distinction is important because the C-4F license is focused on sheet metal studs used in nonstructural framing systems rather than structural framing work.

Candidates should therefore concentrate on light-gauge sheet metal framing methods, nonstructural partitions, ceiling framing, wall assemblies, openings, tracks, studs, fasteners, alignment, and interfaces with gypsum construction.

Fire-resistance requirements can also be important because many interior partitions and ceiling assemblies are designed to achieve specific ratings. Candidates should understand how metal framing interacts with gypsum board and other components within tested or code-compliant assemblies.

The C-4F trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, financial, management, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C4-F preparation plan should focus heavily on Metal Framing Installation because this subject represents 16 of the 20 examination questions.

Begin with the basic components of a sheet metal framing system. Candidates should understand the roles of studs, tracks, headers, framing around openings, ceiling framing components, and other nonstructural framing elements.

Review layout and alignment. Proper framing requires accurate placement of tracks and studs, consistent spacing, plumb installation, proper connections, and coordination with openings and other building components.

Study wall partitions as complete assemblies rather than individual studs. Think about how bottom and top tracks, vertical studs, door and window openings, backing, blocking, gypsum board, and related components work together.

Ceiling framing should also receive attention because the C-4F classification includes framing ceilings with sheet metal studs. Candidates should understand common framing relationships, attachment, support, and alignment concepts associated with ceiling systems.

The Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th Edition is particularly useful for understanding the relationship between metal framing and gypsum board assemblies. Review wall systems, ceiling systems, rated assemblies, partitions, and installation sequences.

Because the Gypsum Construction Handbook is not allowed into the examination room, use it to build retained knowledge. After reviewing a section, close the book and explain the installation process from memory.

The Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016 reference can help reinforce general framing and construction concepts. Even though the C-4F classification does not authorize wood framing, knowledge of layout, wall construction, measurements, openings, and building relationships can make metal framing concepts easier to understand.

Study fasteners and connections as part of installation preparation. Candidates should understand how framing components are secured and why proper attachment matters to alignment, stability, and completion of the assembly.

Review openings for doors, access points, and other penetrations. Openings often require additional framing considerations, and candidates should understand how nonstructural framing is arranged around them.

Fire-resistance should be connected to both code study and gypsum-system study. The International Building Code may contain requirements affecting fire-resistance ratings, while gypsum references help candidates understand how framing and board components form complete assemblies.

The International Building Code, 2018 should be used throughout preparation. Practice using its index and table of contents to locate wall, ceiling, fire-resistance, materials, and construction-related provisions.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Definitions are especially useful when studying the IBC. A code question may depend on the precise meaning of a construction term, and identifying the correct definition can narrow the search quickly.

Tables should also receive regular attention. Important construction or fire-resistance information may appear in tables rather than in narrative sections, so candidates should practice reading headings, notes, and referenced conditions.

When studying code requirements, read enough surrounding material to understand the complete rule. Exceptions, subsections, notes, and cross-references can affect how a provision applies.

Timed lookup drills can help improve open-book performance. Choose a subject such as wall assemblies, fire resistance, ceiling systems, or construction materials and locate the applicable IBC provision within a limited amount of time.

Because only four examination questions are assigned to Code Requirements, candidates should avoid spending all their preparation time searching the IBC. The larger priority should remain the 16 questions focused on Metal Framing Installation.

A good study schedule might devote most sessions to metal framing installation while incorporating shorter IBC navigation drills. This keeps practical trade knowledge and code-reference skills balanced according to the exam outline.

As preparation progresses, move from subject-by-subject study into mixed practice. Combine questions involving framing layout, wall partitions, ceiling systems, gypsum assemblies, fire-resistance concepts, and IBC navigation.

The examination contains only 20 questions, so each question carries substantial weight. Candidates should carefully read the wording and distinguish between practical installation questions and questions that require a code lookup.

With one hour available, time management is still important. Answer familiar installation questions efficiently, use the IBC for targeted code confirmation, and return to more difficult items after completing questions that can be answered quickly.

How does the Sheet Metal Studs Contractor (C-4F) exam work?

The Sheet Metal Studs Contractor (C-4F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
