Exam Details

The Nevada C-4F Sheet Metal Studs trade examination is administered through PSI for eligible candidates pursuing the classification through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-4F Sheet Metal Studs
  • Number of Questions: 20
  • Time Allowed: 1 hour
  • Minimum Passing Score: 14 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination is divided into two primary subject areas:

  • Code Requirements: 4 questions
  • Metal Framing Installation: 16 questions

Metal Framing Installation is the dominant portion of the C-4F examination, accounting for 16 of the 20 questions. Candidates should devote the majority of their technical study time to the practical principles associated with sheet metal stud framing.

Preparation should emphasize understanding how metal framing components work together, how layouts are established, how framing members are positioned and connected, and how installation practices relate to completed wall and ceiling assemblies. Candidates should also understand common construction terminology and the relationship between metal framing and surrounding building components.

Code Requirements account for four questions. Although this is the smaller portion of the exam, these questions can be important because the International Building Code, 2018 is the approved exam-room reference. Candidates should practice locating code provisions efficiently and become familiar with the structure of the book before test day.

The exam contains 20 questions with one hour available, giving candidates an average of three minutes per question. Effective preparation should include both direct trade knowledge and efficient use of the IBC so candidates do not spend unnecessary time searching for information they already understand.

State Requirements

Nevada contractor licensing requires applicants to qualify for the classification they intend to hold. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews applicant experience and other licensing information before issuing examination eligibility.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual generally must demonstrate four years of qualifying experience within the 15 years immediately preceding the application. Experience should relate to the classification being requested and demonstrate the candidate's practical involvement in the trade.

Applicants should maintain detailed experience documentation describing the work performed, responsibilities held, and period during which the experience was obtained. Accurate and organized documentation can be important during the application review process.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification. Applicants generally must satisfy the Contractor Management Survey and Nevada Business and Law requirement unless an authorized waiver applies. Additional licensing requirements can include financial responsibility review, background procedures, contractor bonding, and other requirements administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board. Passing the C-4F trade examination alone does not result in issuance of a contractor license.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-4F study plan should reflect the weighting of the examination. With 16 of 20 questions devoted to Metal Framing Installation, practical framing knowledge deserves the majority of study time.

Prioritize metal framing installation. Candidates should review the components, terminology, layout concepts, installation methods, connections, and practical construction principles associated with sheet metal stud framing.

Understand framing relationships. Metal studs function as part of complete wall and ceiling assemblies. Study should include how framing interacts with tracks, gypsum systems, openings, adjacent construction, and other building components.

Strengthen construction fundamentals. Carpentry and Building Construction can reinforce layouts, measurements, framing concepts, construction sequencing, and general building practices that support metal-framing knowledge.

Use the Gypsum Construction Handbook for system understanding. Review partitions, ceilings, gypsum assemblies, framing relationships, and construction details that can help place metal stud work in a broader installation context.

Practice IBC navigation. Code Requirements represent four questions, and the International Building Code is the approved exam-room reference. Practice locating relevant sections under timed conditions rather than simply reading the code from beginning to end.

Study for recall from the non-approved books. Carpentry and Building Construction and the Gypsum Construction Handbook are not allowed into the examination, so important information from those references should be learned before test day.

Use timed practice. The exam allows one hour for 20 questions. Practice question sets can help candidates become comfortable with pacing while balancing direct knowledge and code lookups.

Review missed questions by category. If mistakes are concentrated in metal framing installation, direct additional study toward framing practices. If errors involve code provisions, spend more time navigating the International Building Code.

How does the Sheet Metal Studs Contractor (C-4F) exam work?

The Sheet Metal Studs Contractor (C-4F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
