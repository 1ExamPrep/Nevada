## State Requirements

Nevada identifies **C-4F Sheet Metal Studs** as a specialty subclassification focused on nonstructural sheet metal framing.

The authorized scope covers the erection and installation of sheet metal studs used for **framing ceilings and nonstructural partitions**.

The classification does not authorize installation of wood framing. It also does not authorize structural steel framing. Candidates should therefore focus on light-gauge metal framing systems rather than structural framing methods.

Practical knowledge should include wall layout, track placement, stud spacing, alignment, fastening, openings, ceiling framing, and coordination with gypsum wall and ceiling systems.

Fire-resistance and building-code requirements can also be relevant because many interior wall and ceiling systems are part of rated assemblies. Candidates should understand how framing components relate to gypsum construction and code-compliant assemblies.

The C4-F trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, financial, management, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong C4-F preparation plan should focus primarily on **Metal Framing Installation** because this area represents 16 of the 20 examination questions.

Begin with the basic components of a nonstructural metal framing system. Candidates should understand studs, tracks, wall partitions, ceiling framing components, openings, and common connection points.

**Layout** is a core framing skill. Review how partitions and ceilings are positioned, how tracks are aligned, how stud spacing is established, and how framing relates to openings and surrounding construction.

**Stud installation** should include placement within tracks, alignment, spacing, orientation, fastening, and coordination with other systems.

**Track installation** should include top and bottom track placement, alignment, attachment, and the relationship between tracks and studs.

**Openings** should receive dedicated study. Door openings, access openings, and other penetrations may require additional framing considerations and careful layout.

**Ceiling framing** is also part of the C-4F scope. Candidates should understand nonstructural ceiling framing relationships, support, alignment, attachment, and coordination with wall systems.

The **Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th Edition** is particularly useful for understanding how metal framing works as part of complete gypsum wall and ceiling assemblies. Review partitions, tracks, studs, rated systems, attachment, and typical construction sequences.

Because the Gypsum Construction Handbook is not allowed into the exam room, candidates should use the highlighted and tabbed format for organized study and then transition to active recall. After reviewing a topic, close the book and explain the installation sequence from memory.

The **Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016** reference can help reinforce layout, measurement, wall construction, ceiling relationships, openings, and general framing concepts. Even though wood framing is outside the C-4F scope, general construction knowledge can help candidates understand how nonstructural metal framing fits into a larger building system.

**Fasteners and connections** should be studied as part of overall framing performance. Proper connections affect alignment, stability, and completion of the assembly.

**Fire-resistance** should be connected to both IBC study and gypsum-system study. Rated assemblies often depend on the complete combination of framing and board components rather than a single material.

The **International Building Code, 2018** should be used throughout preparation. Practice locating wall, ceiling, material, fire-resistance, and construction provisions with the index and table of contents.

## How does the Sheet Metal Studs Contractor (C-4F) exam work?

The Sheet Metal Studs Contractor (C-4F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
