Exam Details

The Nevada Excavating and Grading Contractor (A-7) examination is a trade examination associated with the Nevada A-7 contractor classification. Candidates should be prepared for questions involving excavation, grading, trenching, earthmoving equipment, jobsite safety, construction planning, roadway and site work, underground utilities, and related field practices.

The A-7 classification is defined around the digging, movement, and placement of material forming the surface of the earth, other than water. The authorized work includes cutting, filling, excavating, grading, backfilling, trenching, and similar operations performed with hand tools, power tools, machinery, or explosives.

Because excavation and grading work frequently involves heavy equipment, changing site conditions, underground utilities, slopes, trenches, soil movement, compaction, and worker exposure to hazards, candidates should prepare for a combination of practical trade knowledge and safety-related questions.

Preparation should also include equipment applications. Excavating contractors use different machines depending on soil conditions, production requirements, haul distances, site limitations, and the type of work being performed. Understanding the purpose and limitations of excavators, loaders, dozers, graders, scrapers, compactors, trucks, and related equipment can be an important part of trade knowledge.

Candidates should also review excavation calculations and planning concepts, including cut-and-fill operations, material movement, grading sequences, site preparation, trench work, backfilling, compaction, drainage considerations, and project coordination.

Safety should be treated as a core study area. OSHA construction regulations address hazards that can arise around excavations, trenches, heavy equipment, material handling, access, protective systems, and general construction operations. Since the examination is closed book, candidates should study these concepts for understanding rather than expecting to search the OSHA reference during testing.

Closed Book Test

The Nevada A-7 Excavating and Grading Contractor trade examination is a closed-book test. Candidates are not permitted to rely on the reference books in this package while taking the trade examination.

This makes the way you study especially important. Instead of concentrating primarily on locating information in a book, candidates should focus on understanding and retaining the concepts covered by the references.

Use the books to build familiarity with excavation terminology, grading practices, equipment capabilities, trenching procedures, road and site construction requirements, construction safety, utility marking procedures, and field calculations. The goal is to recognize the correct principle or procedure when it appears in an exam question without needing to search for it.

A useful closed-book preparation method is to study one subject area at a time and then test yourself without the reference open. For example, review trench safety requirements, close the OSHA book, and answer practice questions from memory. Then return to the reference to identify what you missed.

The same approach can be used for equipment selection, excavation methods, grading, compaction, underground utility protection, roadway specifications, and construction planning. Repeated recall helps convert reference material into working knowledge.

Candidates should also practice distinguishing similar concepts. Excavation exams may present several answers that appear reasonable, but only one may represent the correct method, equipment choice, safety procedure, or construction practice. Understanding why an answer is correct is more useful than memorizing isolated facts.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada A-7 Excavating and Grading trade examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the trade exam.

  1. Determine the correct contractor classification. Confirm that the A-7 Excavating and Grading classification matches the scope of contracting work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor application must identify the person who provides the trade experience and qualification for the requested classification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable work experience involving excavation, grading, trenching, earthmoving, backfilling, equipment operation, or related activities.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Provide the business, qualifying individual, financial, experience, and supporting information required for the application.
  5. Submit the application for review. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the application and qualifying experience before examination eligibility is established.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. After approval to test, the qualifying individual can proceed with scheduling the examinations required for the application.
  7. Prepare for the A-7 trade examination. Study excavation, grading, equipment, safety, construction methods, and related topics using the reference books in this package.
  8. Complete the required examinations. Nevada contractor applicants generally must satisfy the applicable trade and Business and Law examination requirements unless an approved waiver applies.
  9. Complete remaining licensing requirements. All applicable licensing requirements must be satisfied before a contractor license can be issued.

Candidates can begin studying while preparing application documentation. Because the A-7 trade test is closed book, beginning early gives you more time to absorb technical material rather than attempting to memorize large amounts of information immediately before testing.

State Requirements

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates contractor licensing for the A-7 Excavating and Grading classification. The classification falls under Nevada's general engineering contractor classifications.

The A-7 scope covers the digging, movement, and placement of material forming the surface of the earth, excluding water. Contractors operating under this classification may perform cutting, filling, excavation, grading, backfilling, trenching, and similar earthwork operations using hand tools, power tools, machines, and explosives within the authorized scope.

Applicants must demonstrate qualification for the classification requested. The qualifying individual is responsible for establishing the required trade background associated with excavation and grading work.

The Nevada licensing process also includes requirements beyond the trade examination. Applicants must complete the applicable application, experience, business, financial, examination, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license can be issued.

Passing the A-7 trade examination is therefore an important part of the process, but it does not by itself result in contractor licensure. Candidates should manage exam preparation and licensing responsibilities together.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada A-7 study plan should divide preparation into major categories such as excavation methods, grading, equipment, safety, trenching, site work, utility awareness, and construction planning.

Excavation methods should be reviewed in detail. Understand the differences between common excavation operations, how material is removed and transported, how site conditions affect production, and how equipment choices influence the work.

Grading should include cut-and-fill operations, establishing elevations, slopes, drainage considerations, site preparation, finish grading, and the relationship between excavation and final site conditions.

Earthmoving equipment is another major preparation area. Candidates should understand the applications of excavators, bulldozers, loaders, graders, scrapers, compactors, trucks, and other machines commonly used for excavation and grading.

Equipment questions may require you to identify which machine is most appropriate for a particular operation. Review equipment capabilities, common production methods, operating limitations, and how haul distance or site conditions can affect equipment selection.

Soils and compaction are important because excavation and grading work depends heavily on material characteristics. Study basic soil behavior, moisture, compaction methods, density considerations, backfill practices, and the relationship between soil conditions and construction performance.

Trenching deserves focused preparation. Candidates should understand excavation hazards, trench stability, protective systems, safe access, spoil placement, equipment hazards, and worker protection principles.

OSHA construction safety should be studied as practical knowledge. Since the test is closed book, review the safety principles repeatedly and use practice questions to strengthen recall of proper jobsite procedures.

Underground utilities are particularly important for excavation contractors. Before digging, contractors must account for the possibility of buried utility lines and follow applicable notification and marking procedures. Study the Nevada One Call material and suggested marking guidelines carefully.

Road and bridge construction specifications should also be part of preparation. Earthwork frequently forms the foundation for road construction, making knowledge of excavation, embankments, grading, materials, preparation, and construction specifications valuable.

Construction planning should include sequencing work, selecting equipment, estimating production, organizing material movement, coordinating site activities, and maintaining efficient operations.

Practice questions are especially useful for a closed-book examination. After studying a chapter or topic, answer questions without consulting the reference. Then review incorrect answers and return to the relevant material.

Flashcards can also help with terminology, safety concepts, equipment applications, and construction principles. Focus on understanding relationships rather than memorizing disconnected phrases.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Another effective approach is scenario-based study. Ask yourself what equipment you would use for a specific site condition, how you would protect workers in a trench, what sequence you would use for grading operations, or what steps should occur before excavation begins near underground utilities.

Closed-book success depends heavily on recall and application. Regular review over time is generally more useful than attempting to absorb all of the material in a few long study sessions.

How does the Excavating and Grading Contractor (A-7) exam work?

The Excavating and Grading Contractor (A-7) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
