## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-37C preparation plan should begin with **System Design** because it represents 15 of the 45 examination questions.

Candidates should understand how collectors, piping, pumps, storage equipment, valves, controls, and heat-transfer components work together rather than memorizing each component in isolation.

Follow the movement of energy through a solar thermal system. Understand how energy is captured at the collector, transferred into a working medium, circulated through the system, stored when necessary, and eventually used for controlling air temperatures.

**Piping** represents 10 questions and should receive substantial study attention. Review pipe materials, fittings, joining methods, supports, routing, insulation, expansion, and connection requirements.

The **Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018** should be used throughout preparation. Practice locating code requirements by topic rather than relying only on remembered page numbers.

For example, choose a piping-material question, identify the relevant term, use the index, and locate the applicable UPC provision. Repeat the process with supports, fittings, valves, and connections.

**Definitions** can be important because technical terms may have specific code meanings. Understanding those definitions can help candidates determine which requirements apply.

**Tables** should receive regular attention. Important information involving materials, support requirements, sizing, or installation conditions may appear in tabular form.

**Exceptions** can modify a general rule. Candidates should read enough surrounding language to identify exceptions and qualifying conditions.

**Cross-references** may direct the reader to another section containing additional requirements. Practice following them instead of assuming the first section contains the full answer.

**Collectors** accounts for 7 questions. Candidates should understand collector purpose, mounting concepts, orientation, flow, circulation, connections, and how collectors integrate with the overall system.

Review how collector arrangement can affect system flow and how thermal energy is transferred from the collector into the circulating medium.

**Pumps and Storage Systems** accounts for 5 questions. Candidates should understand the role of circulation pumps, storage tanks, heat exchangers, and the relationship between collected energy and stored thermal capacity.

Review how pumps move fluid through collectors and piping and how storage helps make collected thermal energy available when needed.

**System Valves and Controls** represents 3 questions. Review shutoff valves, check valves, balancing devices, temperature controls, differential controls, and other components that regulate system operation.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

**Safety** represents 5 questions. Use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 to prepare for jobsite hazards involving ladders, roof access, elevated work, tools, electrical hazards, material handling, personal protective equipment, and other construction activities.

Because the OSHA reference is not allowed into the exam room, candidates should build retained safety knowledge before test day.

The **Solar Water and Pool Heating Design and Installation Manual** can help connect many C-37C subjects into complete system understanding. Use it to review collectors, circulation, piping layouts, pumps, storage, controls, and heat-transfer relationships.

Because the manual is not allowed into the examination room, active recall can be useful. After reviewing a system layout, close the book and explain the purpose and sequence of each major component from memory.

**Heat transfer** is an important solar thermal concept. Candidates should understand how solar energy is collected, transferred to a working medium, circulated, stored, and converted into usable thermal energy for air-temperature control.

**System balance** should also receive attention. Proper flow through collectors and piping can affect performance, making balanced circulation an important practical concept.

**Freeze protection** can be relevant because solar thermal equipment and piping may be exposed to outdoor temperatures. Candidates should understand general methods used to protect exposed system components.

**Insulation** is important because unwanted heat loss can reduce the amount of useful thermal energy available to the system. Review why solar thermal piping is insulated and how environmental exposure affects insulation selection.

**Thermal expansion** should also be considered in systems exposed to elevated temperatures. Pipe movement, supports, materials, and system layout can all affect how expansion is accommodated.

A useful study schedule should follow the examination outline. Spend the largest blocks of time on System Design and Piping, followed by Collectors, Pumps and Storage Systems, Safety, and System Valves and Controls.

Once individual topics become familiar, move into mixed-question practice. The actual examination can shift quickly among system design, piping, collectors, pumps, controls, and safety.

Timed UPC lookup drills can improve open-book performance. Choose a plumbing topic and practice locating the applicable section within a limited period.

Do not expect the UPC to answer every question. The code is most useful for targeted code-based requirements, while many technical questions require retained solar thermal knowledge.

## How does the Solar Air Conditioning Contractor (C-37C) exam work?

The Solar Air Conditioning Contractor (C-37C) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
