## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-37C preparation strategy should begin with **System Design** because it represents 15 of the 45 examination questions.

Candidates should understand how collectors, piping, pumps, storage equipment, valves, controls, and heat-transfer components work together instead of memorizing each part separately.

Follow the movement of energy through a complete solar thermal system. Understand how energy is captured at the collector, transferred into a working medium, circulated through piping, stored when necessary, and ultimately used in controlling air temperatures.

**Piping** represents 10 questions and is one of the strongest areas for direct use of the Uniform Plumbing Code. Candidates should review pipe materials, fittings, joining methods, supports, routing, insulation, valves, and connections.

The **Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018** should be used throughout preparation rather than only during the final days before the exam. Repeated use helps candidates become familiar with where information is located.

Practice finding code requirements by topic. Start with a question about pipe material, identify the key term, use the index, and locate the corresponding section. Repeat the process with fittings, supports, valves, and connections.

**Definitions** can be important because technical plumbing terms may have specific code meanings. Understanding those meanings can help candidates determine which section or requirement applies.

**Tables** should receive regular attention. Important information involving materials, supports, sizing, or installation conditions may appear in table format.

**Exceptions** can modify a general requirement. Candidates should read the surrounding language carefully so they do not overlook conditions that change how a rule applies.

**Cross-references** may direct the reader to another section containing additional requirements. Practice following those references instead of assuming the first section contains the complete answer.

**Collectors** accounts for 7 questions. Candidates should understand collector function, mounting concepts, flow relationships, circulation, connections, and how collectors integrate with the rest of a solar air-conditioning system.

Review how collector arrangement can affect system flow and how thermal energy moves from the collector into the working fluid.

**Pumps and Storage Systems** accounts for 5 questions. Candidates should understand the purpose of circulation pumps, storage equipment, heat exchangers, and the relationship between collected energy and stored thermal capacity.

Review how pumps influence movement through collectors and piping and how storage can help make thermal energy available when needed.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

**System Valves and Controls** accounts for 3 questions. Review shutoff valves, check valves, balancing devices, temperature controls, differential controls, and other components used to regulate system operation.

**Safety** accounts for 5 questions. Candidates should prepare for practical hazards involving roof access, ladders, elevated work, piping tools, hot surfaces, electrical control components, lifting, and other construction activities.

Because this package contains only the exam-room-approved Uniform Plumbing Code, candidates should make sure their broader study plan covers System Design, Collectors, Pumps and Storage Systems, System Valves and Controls, and Safety through technical study resources and trade experience.

**Heat transfer** is a central concept in solar air-conditioning preparation. Candidates should understand how collected solar energy moves through a working medium and becomes useful for controlling air temperatures.

**System balance** is also important. Proper flow through collectors and piping affects performance, making balanced circulation and component coordination important practical concepts.

**Freeze protection** can be relevant because solar thermal components may be exposed to outdoor temperatures. Candidates should understand general system-design concepts used to protect equipment and piping from damaging conditions.

**Insulation** plays an important role in maintaining useful thermal energy. Candidates should understand why piping is insulated and how environmental exposure can affect heat loss.

**Thermal expansion** should also be considered in systems exposed to elevated temperatures. Materials, supports, and system layout all influence how pipe movement is accommodated.

A useful study schedule should follow the exam outline. Spend the largest blocks of time on System Design and Piping, followed by Collectors, Pumps and Storage Systems, Safety, and System Valves and Controls.

Once individual topics become familiar, move into mixed-question practice. The actual examination can shift quickly from a piping requirement to a system-design question, collector concept, pump issue, control question, or safety topic.

Timed UPC lookup drills can improve open-book performance. Choose a plumbing topic and practice locating the applicable section within a limited amount of time.

Do not expect the Uniform Plumbing Code to answer every question. The code is most useful for targeted code-based requirements, while many practical questions depend on retained solar thermal knowledge.

When a practice question is missed, identify why. The issue may involve weak trade knowledge, unfamiliar terminology, slow reference navigation, or difficulty understanding the relationship among system components.

## How does the Solar Air Conditioning Contractor (C-37C) exam work?

The Solar Air Conditioning Contractor (C-37C) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
