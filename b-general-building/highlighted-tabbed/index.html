Exam Details

The Nevada General Building Contractor (B) trade examination evaluates broad knowledge associated with the construction and remodeling of buildings. General Building contractors coordinate multiple unrelated construction trades, making the exam broader than a single specialty examination.

Candidates should prepare for subjects involving sitework, concrete, masonry, structural components, metals, carpentry, thermal and moisture protection, doors and windows, drywall, finishes, safety, general building code, plan reading, estimating, and other building-construction concepts.

The International Building Code is an important reference for subjects involving occupancy, construction types, building limitations, fire protection, means of egress, structural provisions, and other general building requirements.

ACI 318-14 supports preparation involving reinforced concrete, structural members, reinforcement, anchorage, development, concrete cover, durability, and related structural concrete requirements.

ACI 530/530.1-13 supports structural masonry preparation involving masonry materials, reinforcement, mortar, grout, structural systems, construction requirements, and specifications.

The study-only books expand preparation beyond the exam-room code references. OSHA supports jobsite safety, Pipe and Excavation Contracting supports sitework and excavation, the Gypsum Construction Handbook supports drywall preparation, and the remaining references provide additional depth in steel joists, post-tensioning, masonry, and concrete construction.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada B General Building trade examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the trade examination.

  1. Confirm the General Building classification. Determine that the construction or remodeling work the business intends to perform falls within Nevada's B General Building scope.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor application must designate the individual providing the technical qualification for the requested classification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable experience demonstrating work in the General Building classification.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Provide the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. The application and qualifying experience must be reviewed before examination eligibility is issued.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. After eligibility is established, the qualifying individual can proceed with scheduling the required examination.
  7. Prepare for the General Building trade examination. Study general building code, concrete, masonry, sitework, safety, finishes, structural systems, and other applicable subjects.
  8. Complete required examinations. The qualifying individuals must satisfy the applicable trade and business and law examination requirements unless an authorized waiver applies.
  9. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish all other applicable requirements before a Nevada contractor license is issued.

Nevada requires the trade qualifying individual to have a minimum of four years of applicable work experience within the 15 years immediately preceding the contractor license application. Qualifying experience must relate to the specific classification requested.

State Requirements

The Nevada B General Building classification applies to contractors engaged in construction or remodeling of buildings or structures that provide support, shelter, or enclosure for persons, animals, chattels, or other movable property.

For work to qualify as a General Building project, it must involve three or more unrelated building trades or crafts, with the General Building contractor acting as the prime contractor and construction or remodeling of the building serving as the project's primary purpose.

The broad nature of the classification means General Building contractors commonly coordinate work involving concrete, masonry, framing, roofing, doors, windows, drywall, finishes, site preparation, and other construction systems.

The General Building classification does not automatically authorize direct performance of every specialty trade. Plumbing, electrical, refrigeration and air conditioning, and fire protection work require the applicable specialty license when those trades are performed directly.

The Nevada contractor licensing process also includes requirements beyond the trade examination. Applicants must satisfy applicable experience, application, business, financial, examination, and other licensing requirements before the contractor license can be issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada B General Building preparation plan should combine code navigation with practical construction knowledge. Because the classification covers multiple trades, candidates benefit from organizing study into major subject categories.

Building Code preparation should focus heavily on the International Building Code. Review occupancy, construction types, building limitations, means of egress, fire protection, structural provisions, and other major code topics.

Concrete preparation should combine structural requirements from ACI 318 with practical information from The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction. Study reinforcement, concrete cover, anchorage, development, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, and quality practices.

Masonry preparation should combine ACI 530/530.1 with Modern Masonry. Review structural masonry requirements while also building practical understanding of brick, block, stone, mortar, reinforcement, layout, and field methods.

Sitework and Excavation should include excavation, trenching, pipe installation, grading, backfill, equipment, and coordination of site activities. Pipe and Excavation Contracting supports these subjects.

Metals should include the handling and erection of steel joists and joist girders, field stability, placement, and related construction practices.

Drywall and Gypsum Construction should include wall and ceiling assemblies, gypsum board, framing, fastening, finishing, and related installation practices.

Post-Tensioning preparation should include tendon placement, handling, anchorage, stressing terminology, field procedures, and safety fundamentals.

Safety should be reviewed through OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Focus on common construction hazards involving fall protection, scaffolds, ladders, excavation, tools, equipment, material handling, and personal protective equipment.

The books that are not permitted inside the examination should be studied using active recall. Review a highlighted section, close the book, answer practice questions without using the reference, and then return to the appropriate tabbed area to correct missed concepts.

The three approved books should receive regular navigation practice. Work through timed code questions using the IBC, ACI 318, and ACI 530/530.1. Identify the subject, select the correct reference, and locate the applicable provision efficiently.

Highlighting helps important material stand out, but candidates should still read surrounding code language when necessary. Definitions, exceptions, tables, and cross-references may affect how a requirement applies.

Tabs can help candidates reach major subjects faster, but understanding the overall structure of each book is equally important. Candidates should become familiar with the index, table of contents, chapter organization, and technical terminology.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Practice questions should help direct study rather than simply test memory. If structural concrete questions are difficult, dedicate additional time to ACI 318. If masonry searches are slow, practice more frequently with ACI 530/530.1. If general code questions create problems, focus on navigation within the International Building Code.

General Building candidates should also practice construction calculations, plan reading, and estimating concepts. The broad nature of the classification rewards candidates who understand how building systems and multiple trades fit together on a complete project.

How does the General Building Contractor (B) exam work?

The General Building Contractor (B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
