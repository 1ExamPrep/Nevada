Exam Details

The Nevada B General Building Contractor examination contains 80 questions. Candidates are allowed 3 hours to complete the examination, and the minimum passing score is 56 correct answers.

The examination covers a broad range of general building subjects, including:

  • Sitework
  • Concrete
  • Masonry
  • Metals
  • Carpentry
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection
  • Doors and Windows
  • Drywall and Finishes
  • Safety
  • General Code, Plan Reading, and Estimating
  • Nevada One Call

Because the exam covers multiple construction trades, candidates should avoid concentrating exclusively on one area such as concrete or framing. General Building contractors are expected to understand how building systems are coordinated and how construction activities affect one another throughout a project.

With 80 questions and three hours available, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and fifteen seconds per question. Efficient decision-making is important. Code references should be used strategically when detailed confirmation is needed, while straightforward trade questions should be answered from existing knowledge when possible.

Plan reading and estimating also require practical preparation. Candidates should be comfortable interpreting dimensions, construction details, symbols, building systems, material quantities, and common construction calculations.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada B General Building examination is one component of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the trade examination.

  1. Confirm the General Building classification. Determine that the proposed contracting work falls within Nevada's Classification B scope.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor application must designate the individual who provides the required experience and trade qualification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. The qualifying individual should document applicable general building experience involving the construction or remodeling of buildings and coordination of multiple trades.
  4. Complete the contractor license application. Submit the required business, qualifying individual, financial, experience, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the application before examination eligibility is established.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. Once approved to test, the qualifying individual can proceed with scheduling the required examinations.
  7. Prepare for the B General Building examination. Study the major building trades and practice using the approved exam-room references.
  8. Complete applicable examinations. Candidates must satisfy the General Building trade examination and the Nevada Contractor Management Survey requirements unless an applicable waiver is approved.
  9. Complete remaining licensing requirements. All other applicable licensing requirements must be satisfied before the contractor license is issued.

Nevada also recognizes certain pathways that can affect the trade examination requirement, including approved licensure by endorsement and the NASCLA Accredited Examination for qualifying General Building applicants. Those pathways do not remove the other applicable Nevada licensing requirements.

State Requirements

Nevada defines a Classification B General Building Contractor as a contractor whose principal business involves construction or remodeling of buildings or structures intended to support, shelter, or enclose persons, animals, chattels, or other movable property.

A General Building project must require the use of more than two unrelated building trades or crafts, with the General Building contractor serving as the prime contractor and with construction or remodeling of the building as the project's primary purpose.

The General Building classification does not automatically authorize a contractor to self-perform every specialty trade. Plumbing, electrical, refrigeration and air conditioning, and fire protection work require the appropriate specialty license when the General Building contractor intends to perform that work directly.

Qualifying experience for a Nevada contractor license generally requires four full years of applicable experience within the 15 years immediately preceding the application. The experience must relate to the classification being requested and satisfy the applicable Nevada licensing standards.

Applicants should make sure their work history demonstrates genuine general building experience rather than experience limited to a single specialty trade. The B classification is centered on building construction and the coordination of multiple unrelated trades.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada General Building study plan should divide preparation among the major construction trades instead of reading every reference from beginning to end without a clear structure.

Sitework should include excavation, grading, trenching, underground utilities, site preparation, backfill, compaction, and construction coordination. Pipe and Excavation Contracting and the Nevada One Call materials support these subjects.

Concrete requires both code knowledge and practical construction understanding. Use ACI 318 for structural requirements and The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction for practical material handling, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, and quality concepts.

Masonry should include structural code provisions as well as field practices. ACI 530/530.1 supports structural masonry requirements, while Modern Masonry reinforces practical construction methods, mortar, units, layout, tools, and workmanship.

Metals should include structural steel-related concepts and safe handling of joists and joist girders. The steel joist technical digest can help candidates understand erection procedures and stability considerations before test day.

Carpentry preparation should include framing concepts, structural components, layout, building systems, openings, and coordination with other trades. Candidates should also understand how carpentry relates to code requirements in the International Building Code.

Thermal and Moisture Protection should include building-envelope concepts, insulation, roofing, flashing, moisture control, waterproofing, and related construction practices.

Doors and Windows should be reviewed for installation, openings, hardware, dimensions, code considerations, and coordination with wall systems and finishes.

Drywall and Finishes should include gypsum board systems, framing, fasteners, joints, finishing, assemblies, and related installation methods. The Gypsum Construction Handbook is especially useful for pre-exam study in this area.

Safety should receive regular attention through OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Because that book is not permitted in the examination room, candidates should learn major construction safety concepts before testing rather than relying on reference searches.

Plan Reading and Estimating should be practiced with actual construction-style problems. Review dimensions, scales, symbols, material quantities, square footage, cubic measurements, elevations, details, and coordination between plans and specifications.

Post-tensioning should be reviewed through the included Level 1 field fundamentals material. Understand terminology, tendon placement, anchorage, field handling, stressing concepts, and safety considerations.

For the open-book portion of preparation, practice timed navigation with the International Building Code, ACI 318, and ACI 530/530.1. Choose a subject, identify the likely reference, locate the applicable provision, and review the surrounding language.

More on test Information and Study Materials

For study-only references, use active recall. Study a topic, close the book, answer practice questions, and return to the reference afterward to correct mistakes. This helps prepare candidates for questions that cannot be answered through an exam-room lookup.

A balanced study routine should combine code navigation, practical construction knowledge, calculations, safety review, plan reading, and trade-specific practice. General Building candidates need breadth as well as depth because the examination spans many unrelated construction crafts.

How does the General Building Contractor (B) exam work?

The General Building Contractor (B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
