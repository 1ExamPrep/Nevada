Exam Details

The Nevada General Building Contractor (B) examination is administered by PSI for the Nevada State Contractors Board. Eligibility to schedule the examination is determined by the Board after the contractor license application and supporting qualifications have been reviewed.

The B trade examination contains 80 questions. Candidates are allowed three hours to complete the exam, and the minimum passing score is 56 correct answers. The examination is administered by computer at an approved PSI testing location. A small number of additional non-scored questions may also appear during the examination and count toward the total testing time.

The official examination outline distributes questions across the following subject areas:

  • Sitework: 6 questions
  • Concrete: 12 questions
  • Masonry: 6 questions
  • Metals: 12 questions
  • Carpentry: 7 questions
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection: 9 questions
  • Doors and Windows: 4 questions
  • Drywall and Finishes: 4 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions
  • Plan Reading and Estimating: 12 questions
  • Nevada One Call: 3 questions

The examination may include questions based on the listed references, trade knowledge, and accepted construction practices. Code-related questions are based on the code editions identified for the examination. Candidates should study the applicable editions rather than relying only on a newer or older code that may contain different provisions, numbering, tables, or requirements.

The Nevada State Contractors Board generally requires applicants to pass both the applicable trade examination and the Contractor Management Survey examination unless a waiver applies. Exam eligibility is issued after the Board reviews the application and confirms that the applicant or designated trade qualified individual satisfies the applicable requirements.

Open Book Test

The Nevada General Building Contractor (B) examination is an open-book test, but only specifically approved references may be taken into the examination room. For the current B examination, the approved exam-room references are the International Building Code, the listed ACI structural concrete standard, and the listed ACI masonry standard.

Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved books to the testing center. Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. They may not contain written notes, added loose papers, or unauthorized attachments. Permanent tabs may be used, but temporary tabs such as removable sticky notes are not permitted.

Writing in or highlighting a reference during the examination is prohibited. Candidates should complete all permitted highlighting, underlining, and tabbing before arriving at the testing center. Preparing books in advance can make it easier to move between major chapters, locate tables, identify commonly tested subjects, and avoid losing time searching through unfamiliar material.

Although the exam is open book, success depends on knowing how the references are organized. Attempting to research every question from the beginning can consume too much time. A stronger approach is to learn the major concepts, recognize which book applies to a question, and use the approved references to confirm details or locate exact code requirements.

A silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator may be used in the examination center. Candidates should review the current PSI Candidate Information Bulletin before test day for identification requirements, testing-center rules, security procedures, and any updates to the approved-reference policy.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada contractor licensing process begins with an application to the Nevada State Contractors Board. The applicant must identify the requested classification, designate the appropriate qualified individual, submit the required supporting documents, and complete the Board’s review process.

  1. Determine the appropriate classification. The B classification applies to qualifying general building work involving the construction or remodeling of buildings or structures and the coordinated use of more than two unrelated building trades or crafts.
  2. Select the qualified individual. The trade qualified individual may be an owner, officer, member, manager, or bona fide employee who is actively engaged in the contracting business.
  3. Document qualifying experience. The applicant must provide the required evidence of experience in the classification being requested.
  4. Submit the contractor license application. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the application, experience documentation, financial information, and other required materials.
  5. Complete fingerprint requirements. Applicants must submit fingerprints for the required state and national criminal history review.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. After the Board accepts the applicant’s qualifications for testing, an eligibility notice is issued with instructions for scheduling through PSI.
  7. Schedule and take the required examinations. Candidates schedule the B trade examination and the Contractor Management Survey examination unless an examination waiver has been approved.
  8. Complete remaining licensing requirements. After the examinations and application review are completed, the Board provides instructions regarding any remaining requirements associated with license issuance.

Passing an examination does not by itself issue a Nevada contractor license. The applicant must complete the full Board application process and satisfy all requirements applicable to the business, qualified individual, requested classification, and approved monetary limit.

State Requirements

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates contractor licensing in Nevada. Businesses and individuals performing work that falls within Nevada’s contractor licensing laws must hold the appropriate license before contracting, bidding, or performing regulated construction work.

The person serving as the trade qualified individual must generally have at least four full years of qualifying experience within the 15 years immediately preceding the application. Qualifying experience may include work as a journeyman, supervising employee, or contractor in the requested classification. Approved education or training may satisfy a portion of the experience requirement, subject to the Board’s rules and review.

The Board evaluates experience documentation to determine whether it demonstrates the depth and scope of work required for the requested classification. Applicants should provide complete information describing the type of construction performed, responsibilities held, projects completed, trades supervised, and level of decision-making authority.

The B classification is intended for general building work in which the primary purpose is the construction or remodeling of a building or structure and the project requires more than two unrelated building trades or crafts. A general building contractor acting as the prime contractor may coordinate multiple trades within the authorized scope. Specialty work outside that scope may require the appropriate specialty classification.

Nevada contractor licenses are also subject to financial responsibility requirements and a monetary limit. The monetary limit establishes the maximum value of work the contractor may undertake on one or more contracts at a single construction site or subdivision for one client. The Board determines the approved limit after reviewing the applicant’s financial information and other required materials.

Applicants should review the current Nevada State Contractors Board application materials before submitting their paperwork. Incomplete experience documentation, inconsistent business records, missing financial information, or unresolved application questions may delay the eligibility and licensing process.

Reference Books

Only the books identified in the Exam Room Approved Books section may be taken into the testing room. More on reference Books

  • International Building Code, 2018
    The model building code addresses occupancy classifications, construction types, fire protection, means of egress, structural provisions, materials, accessibility, special inspections, and other requirements relevant to commercial building construction.
  • ACI 318-14: Building Code Requirements for Structural Concrete and Commentary
    The structural concrete standard covers design and construction requirements for concrete systems, including materials, reinforcement, durability, structural members, development and splicing, and concrete construction provisions.
  • ACI 530/530.1-13: Building Code Requirements and Specification for Masonry Structures and Companion Commentaries
    The reference addresses the design, materials, construction, inspection, and quality requirements applicable to masonry structures.
  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
    The federal construction safety reference covers workplace requirements involving fall protection, scaffolds, ladders, excavations, electrical safety, personal protective equipment, material handling, concrete operations, steel erection, and other construction hazards.
  • Technical Digest No. 9 – Handling and Erection of Steel Joists and Joist Girders
    The technical digest focuses on the safe handling, placement, erection, bridging, and stabilization of steel joists and joist girders during construction.
  • Pipe and Excavation Contracting
    The trade reference explains excavation practices, underground piping, trenching, grading, equipment use, soil considerations, job planning, estimating, and related field procedures.
  • Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th Edition
    The handbook covers gypsum board products, wall and ceiling assemblies, fire-resistant construction, sound-control systems, installation methods, finishing practices, and common design details.
  • Suggested Marking Guidelines and The Nevada One Call Law
    The material addresses Nevada excavation-notification requirements and the uniform marking practices used to identify underground facilities before excavation begins.
  • Training and Certification of Field Personnel for Unbonded Post-Tensioning - Level 1 Field Fundamentals
    The reference introduces fundamental field practices for unbonded post-tensioning systems, including materials, installation, stressing operations, inspection, safety, and jobsite responsibilities.
  • Modern Masonry - Brick, Block, Stone, 10th Edition by Clois E. Kicklighter
    The masonry textbook reviews tools, materials, mortar, brick, concrete masonry units, stone construction, layout, estimating, safety, and accepted installation methods.
  • The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction, 4th Edition
    The practical concrete reference covers planning, materials, formwork, reinforcement, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, quality control, troubleshooting, and coordination of concrete construction.

Test Information and Study Materials

Effective preparation should reflect the weighting of the official exam outline. Concrete, metals, plan reading, and estimating account for a substantial portion of the examination, so candidates should dedicate adequate study time to those subjects while still reviewing every content area.

Begin by becoming familiar with the table of contents, chapter organization, index, major tables, and terminology in each approved book. Practice identifying which reference applies to a particular question. A structural concrete question may require ACI 318, a masonry design question may point to ACI 530/530.1, and a building code question may require the International Building Code.

Use permanent tabs to identify major chapters and frequently referenced subjects. Keep the tabbing system simple enough to scan quickly. Too many tabs can make a book harder to navigate, while a logical system based on major exam subjects can reduce search time.

For books that cannot be taken into the examination, focus on learning the underlying principles rather than trying to memorize every sentence. Review excavation safety, steel erection procedures, gypsum construction, post-tensioning fundamentals, masonry installation, concrete quality practices, and underground utility marking requirements until you can recognize the correct field procedure or safety principle without needing the reference in front of you.

Timed practice is also important. With 80 questions and three hours, candidates must manage approximately two minutes and fifteen seconds per question. Some questions will take less time, while calculations, plan interpretation, or code searches may require longer. Practice answering familiar questions first, marking difficult questions for review, and avoiding excessive time on a single lookup.

Is the Nevada General Building Contractor exam open book?

Yes. The B trade examination is open book, but only the approved International Building Code, ACI 318, and ACI 530/530.1 references may be taken into the examination room.

How many questions are on the Nevada B contractor exam?

The examination contains 80 questions. Candidates must answer at least 56 questions correctly to meet the minimum passing score.

How much time is allowed for the exam?

Candidates receive three hours to complete the Nevada General Building Contractor examination.

Can I take every listed reference book into the exam?

No. Most of the listed references are study materials only. The exam-room-approved books are the 2018 International Building Code, the approved ACI 318 edition, and the approved ACI 530/530.1 edition.

How does the General Building Contractor (B) exam work?

The General Building Contractor (B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
