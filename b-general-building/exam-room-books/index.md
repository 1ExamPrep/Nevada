## State Requirements

The Nevada B General Building classification applies to contractors whose principal business involves constructing or remodeling buildings or structures where the work requires coordination of more than two unrelated building trades or crafts.

The General Building contractor typically serves as the prime contractor on a project where construction or remodeling of the building is the primary purpose. This makes the classification broader than a single specialty license and requires familiarity with multiple construction systems.

General Building work may involve concrete, masonry, structural framing, carpentry, building-envelope systems, openings, finishes, and other related trades. Candidates should therefore prepare for a broad technical examination rather than approaching it as a single-trade test.

The B classification does not automatically authorize a contractor to self-perform every specialty trade. Certain specialty work requires the appropriate specialty classification when the General Building contractor intends to perform that work directly.

The Nevada licensing process also includes requirements beyond the trade examination. Applicants must satisfy the applicable experience, business, financial, examination, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license can be issued.

The qualifying individual must provide the trade experience required for the classification. Current Nevada requirements generally require at least four years of applicable experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor within the 15 years before the application is filed.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada B General Building study plan should combine construction knowledge with targeted reference-navigation practice.

**General Building Code** preparation should begin with the International Building Code. Candidates should become familiar with occupancy classifications, construction types, allowable building size, fire-resistance concepts, means of egress, structural provisions, and other major sections.

**Concrete** should be studied using both the IBC and ACI 318-14. Review reinforcement, concrete strength, placement-related requirements, structural members, cover, anchorage, development, and durability concepts.

**Masonry** should include structural requirements, reinforcement, grout, mortar, wall systems, and construction specifications. ACI 530/530.1-13 is the primary reference in this package for detailed masonry requirements.

**Metals** should include general structural concepts, framing, supports, connections, and coordination with other building systems. Candidates should understand how structural systems relate to the building-code requirements found in the IBC.

**Carpentry** preparation should include framing terminology, walls, floors, roofs, openings, structural components, layout, and coordination with other trades. The IBC can provide code context for structural and building requirements.

**Thermal and Moisture Protection** should include roofing, insulation, flashing, waterproofing, moisture control, weather protection, and related building-envelope concepts.

**Doors and Windows** should be studied for opening requirements, installation considerations, egress-related provisions, safety concepts, and coordination with wall assemblies.

**Drywall and Finishes** should include gypsum systems, interior finishes, wall and ceiling assemblies, fire-resistance considerations, and general installation knowledge.

**Safety** should be studied as practical construction knowledge. General Building contractors coordinate multiple trades, which means candidates should understand common hazards associated with excavation, ladders, scaffolds, fall protection, material handling, tools, equipment, and active construction sites.

**Plan Reading** should receive regular practice. Candidates should become comfortable reading dimensions, elevations, sections, symbols, schedules, details, and notes. Construction plans frequently require candidates to connect information found in more than one part of a drawing set.

**Estimating** should include common construction calculations involving area, volume, quantities, materials, and dimensions. Candidates should practice calculations rather than relying on memorized formulas without understanding how they apply.

For IBC navigation, begin by learning the broad chapter structure. Knowing whether a question concerns occupancy, egress, fire resistance, building size, structural requirements, or another major category can dramatically narrow the search.

For ACI 318-14, practice locating provisions by subject rather than attempting to memorize every section number. Structural concrete questions may involve reinforcement, anchorage, development, concrete cover, strength, or structural members.

## How does the General Building Contractor (B) exam work?

The General Building Contractor (B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
