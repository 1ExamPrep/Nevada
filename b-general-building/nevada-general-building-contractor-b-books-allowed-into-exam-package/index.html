Exam Details

The Nevada B General Building Contractor trade examination contains 80 questions. Candidates are allowed 3 hours to complete the examination, and the minimum passing score is 56 correct answers.

Major examination subject areas include:

  • Sitework
  • Concrete
  • Masonry
  • Metals
  • Carpentry
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection
  • Doors and Windows
  • Drywall and Finishes
  • Safety
  • General Code, Plan Reading, and Estimating
  • Nevada One Call

The broad content reflects the role of a General Building contractor. Candidates should understand how different building trades work together rather than concentrating exclusively on one specialty. Concrete, masonry, carpentry, metals, building-envelope systems, finishes, and sitework can all appear within the same general construction project.

With 80 questions in three hours, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and fifteen seconds per question. Efficient testing requires knowing when to rely on trade knowledge and when a code lookup is worthwhile.

General Code, Plan Reading, and Estimating should receive consistent preparation. Candidates may need to interpret construction information, calculate quantities, understand dimensions, evaluate building requirements, or determine which code provision applies to a particular scenario.

Open Book Test

The Nevada General Building Contractor (B) examination is an open-book test with specific references permitted inside the examination room.

The books included in this package are:

  • International Building Code, 2018
  • ACI 318-14: Building Code Requirements for Structural Concrete and Commentary
  • ACI 530/530.1-13 (2011 or 2013): Building Code Requirements and Specification for Masonry Structures and Companion Commentaries

Candidates should become familiar with all three books before the examination. The International Building Code is particularly broad, so preparation should include learning how its chapters are organized and how the index, definitions, tables, and cross-references can help locate information.

For structural concrete questions, candidates should become comfortable using ACI 318-14. Study reinforcement terminology, concrete requirements, structural members, development, anchorage, cover, durability, and related technical provisions.

For structural masonry questions, practice with ACI 530/530.1-13. Candidates should understand where to find requirements involving masonry materials, reinforcement, grout, mortar, structural elements, and construction specifications.

Open-book preparation should include timed lookup exercises. Start with a practice question, identify whether it concerns the IBC, structural concrete, or masonry, and then locate the applicable section without browsing randomly through the book.

Candidates should also read surrounding provisions when necessary. Building codes frequently include exceptions, definitions, tables, referenced sections, and conditions that affect how a requirement applies.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada B General Building trade examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the trade test.

  1. Confirm the General Building classification. Determine that the work the business intends to perform falls within Nevada's B General Building classification.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor application must designate the individual responsible for meeting the technical experience and trade examination requirements.
  3. Document qualifying experience. The trade qualifier must demonstrate applicable experience in the classification requested.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Provide the required business, qualifying individual, experience, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. The application must be reviewed before examination eligibility is issued.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. The qualifying individual can schedule the required examination after receiving eligibility information.
  7. Prepare for the B General Building trade examination. Review the major construction trades and practice using the approved code references.
  8. Prepare for Business and Law. The Management Qualifier must satisfy the applicable Nevada business and law examination requirement unless an authorized waiver applies.
  9. Complete the required examinations. Satisfy the examination requirements that apply to the contractor license application.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish all other applicable requirements before the Nevada contractor license is issued.

Current Nevada licensing requirements generally call for the trade qualifier to demonstrate a minimum of four years of qualifying experience within the 15 years immediately preceding the application. The experience must relate to the specific classification requested.

State Requirements

The Nevada B General Building classification applies to contractors whose principal business involves constructing or remodeling buildings or structures where the work requires coordination of more than two unrelated building trades or crafts.

The General Building contractor typically serves as the prime contractor on a project where construction or remodeling of the building is the primary purpose. This makes the classification broader than a single specialty license and requires familiarity with multiple construction systems.

General Building work may involve concrete, masonry, structural framing, carpentry, building-envelope systems, openings, finishes, and other related trades. Candidates should therefore prepare for a broad technical examination rather than approaching it as a single-trade test.

The B classification does not automatically authorize a contractor to self-perform every specialty trade. Certain specialty work requires the appropriate specialty classification when the General Building contractor intends to perform that work directly.

The Nevada licensing process also includes requirements beyond the trade examination. Applicants must satisfy the applicable experience, business, financial, examination, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license can be issued.

The qualifying individual must provide the trade experience required for the classification. Current Nevada requirements generally require at least four years of applicable experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor within the 15 years before the application is filed.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada B General Building study plan should combine construction knowledge with targeted reference-navigation practice.

General Building Code preparation should begin with the International Building Code. Candidates should become familiar with occupancy classifications, construction types, allowable building size, fire-resistance concepts, means of egress, structural provisions, and other major sections.

Concrete should be studied using both the IBC and ACI 318-14. Review reinforcement, concrete strength, placement-related requirements, structural members, cover, anchorage, development, and durability concepts.

Masonry should include structural requirements, reinforcement, grout, mortar, wall systems, and construction specifications. ACI 530/530.1-13 is the primary reference in this package for detailed masonry requirements.

Metals should include general structural concepts, framing, supports, connections, and coordination with other building systems. Candidates should understand how structural systems relate to the building-code requirements found in the IBC.

Carpentry preparation should include framing terminology, walls, floors, roofs, openings, structural components, layout, and coordination with other trades. The IBC can provide code context for structural and building requirements.

Thermal and Moisture Protection should include roofing, insulation, flashing, waterproofing, moisture control, weather protection, and related building-envelope concepts.

Doors and Windows should be studied for opening requirements, installation considerations, egress-related provisions, safety concepts, and coordination with wall assemblies.

Drywall and Finishes should include gypsum systems, interior finishes, wall and ceiling assemblies, fire-resistance considerations, and general installation knowledge.

Safety should be studied as practical construction knowledge. General Building contractors coordinate multiple trades, which means candidates should understand common hazards associated with excavation, ladders, scaffolds, fall protection, material handling, tools, equipment, and active construction sites.

Plan Reading should receive regular practice. Candidates should become comfortable reading dimensions, elevations, sections, symbols, schedules, details, and notes. Construction plans frequently require candidates to connect information found in more than one part of a drawing set.

Estimating should include common construction calculations involving area, volume, quantities, materials, and dimensions. Candidates should practice calculations rather than relying on memorized formulas without understanding how they apply.

For IBC navigation, begin by learning the broad chapter structure. Knowing whether a question concerns occupancy, egress, fire resistance, building size, structural requirements, or another major category can dramatically narrow the search.

For ACI 318-14, practice locating provisions by subject rather than attempting to memorize every section number. Structural concrete questions may involve reinforcement, anchorage, development, concrete cover, strength, or structural members.

More on test Information and Study Materials

For ACI 530/530.1-13, become familiar with the distinction between code requirements and specification-related material. Practice identifying masonry terminology and locating requirements associated with reinforced and unreinforced masonry construction.

Timed practice is valuable because the examination allows three hours for 80 questions. Give yourself a limited period for each lookup. If a search takes too long, mark the question during practice and return later. This helps develop a testing strategy that prevents one difficult lookup from consuming too much time.

Practice questions should also be used to identify weak subjects. If concrete questions are difficult, spend additional time with ACI 318. If masonry questions take too long, work more with ACI 530/530.1. If general building-code questions are challenging, increase navigation practice with the International Building Code.

Open-book preparation should always combine code use with construction knowledge. A candidate who understands the trade can recognize what a question is asking before beginning the lookup, which makes the reference books much more useful.

How does the General Building Contractor (B) exam work?

The General Building Contractor (B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
