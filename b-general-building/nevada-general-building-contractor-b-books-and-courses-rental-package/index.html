Exam Details

The Nevada General Building Contractor (B) trade examination contains 80 questions. Candidates are allowed 3 hours to complete the examination, and the minimum passing score is 56 correct answers.

The General Building examination covers a broad range of construction subjects because the B classification involves building projects that require coordination of multiple unrelated trades. Preparation should include:

  • Sitework and excavation
  • Concrete
  • Masonry
  • Metals
  • Carpentry
  • Thermal and moisture protection
  • Doors and windows
  • Drywall and finishes
  • Construction safety
  • General building code
  • Plan reading
  • Estimating
  • Nevada One Call concepts

The International Building Code provides the broad code foundation for the examination. ACI 318 supports structural concrete preparation, while ACI 530/530.1 supports structural masonry. The supplemental trade books expand preparation into subjects such as OSHA safety, steel joists, excavation, gypsum systems, post-tensioning, practical masonry, concrete workmanship, and underground utility awareness.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada General Building Contractor examination is one part of the overall contractor licensing process. Applicants should organize trade preparation, business and law preparation, and licensing documentation together.

  1. Confirm the B General Building classification. Make sure the requested classification matches the building construction or remodeling work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor application must designate the individual providing the applicable trade qualification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize experience demonstrating applicable General Building work and coordination of multiple building trades.
  4. Complete the contractor license application. Provide the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. Examination eligibility is established through the Nevada contractor licensing process.
  6. Prepare for the B General Building trade examination. Study building code, concrete, masonry, sitework, safety, structural systems, drywall, estimating, and related topics.
  7. Prepare for Business and Law. Use the included Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada and the course materials to prepare for the business portion of the licensing process.
  8. Complete applicable examinations. Satisfy the examinations required for the contractor license application unless an approved waiver applies.
  9. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish all other applicable requirements before the contractor license is issued.

State Requirements

The Nevada B General Building classification applies to contractors whose principal contracting business involves constructing or remodeling buildings or structures where the work requires coordination of multiple unrelated building trades or crafts.

The classification is broader than a single specialty trade. General Building projects can involve foundations, concrete, masonry, structural framing, roofing, doors, windows, drywall, finishes, sitework, and other building systems.

The qualifying individual must establish the applicable experience required for the classification. Nevada contractor licensing also involves business, financial, examination, application, and other requirements beyond simply passing the trade examination.

A General Building license does not automatically authorize direct performance of every specialty trade. Specialty work must be performed within the licensing authority applicable to that trade.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada B study plan should combine General Building code navigation, practical construction knowledge, safety review, plan reading, calculations, and active recall.

Building Code preparation should focus heavily on the International Building Code. Review occupancy classifications, construction types, means of egress, fire protection, building limitations, structural provisions, and other major code subjects.

Concrete should be studied using ACI 318 and The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction. ACI 318 supports code-related preparation, while the contractor guide helps reinforce practical placement, finishing, curing, consolidation, and quality concepts.

Masonry should combine ACI 530/530.1 with Modern Masonry. Candidates should understand structural requirements while also building familiarity with practical masonry materials, tools, mortar, grout, brick, block, stone, and field methods.

Sitework and Excavation should include trenching, excavation, pipe work, backfill, equipment, underground utilities, and construction sequencing. Pipe and Excavation Contracting and Nevada One Call material support this area.

Metals should include steel joist and joist girder handling, erection, stability, and coordination. The included Technical Digest provides focused preparation in this area.

Drywall and Finishes should include gypsum board, walls, ceilings, framing, fastening, joints, finishing, and assembly concepts. The Gypsum Construction Handbook provides targeted review.

Post-Tensioning should include terminology, tendon handling, placement, anchors, stressing concepts, and field fundamentals.

Construction Safety should receive consistent preparation through OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Review fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, excavation hazards, equipment, tools, material handling, and personal protective equipment.

Because many supplemental books cannot be brought into the trade exam, active recall is important. Study a section, close the reference, answer practice questions, and return to the highlighted and tabbed material to review mistakes.

The exam-approved IBC and ACI books should receive timed navigation practice. Candidates should identify the subject first, select the correct book, and then locate the applicable provision efficiently.

Plan reading and estimating should also be part of the study routine. Practice interpreting dimensions, sections, elevations, schedules, symbols, quantities, areas, volumes, and other common construction information.

The included 6 months of course access provides a structured period for working through these trade subjects and Nevada business and law material. A consistent schedule can help candidates divide a broad General Building examination into manageable study categories.

How does the General Building Contractor (B) exam work?

The General Building Contractor (B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
