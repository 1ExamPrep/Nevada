Exam Details

The Nevada General Building Contractor (B) examination is a broad trade exam covering the knowledge needed to coordinate and perform work associated with general building construction and remodeling.

The examination includes subjects such as:

  • Sitework
  • Concrete
  • Masonry
  • Metals
  • Carpentry
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection
  • Doors and Windows
  • Drywall and Finishes
  • Safety
  • General Code
  • Plan Reading
  • Estimating

Because the General Building examination covers multiple construction disciplines, preparation should be balanced. Candidates should avoid studying only concrete, masonry, or building code while ignoring other trade concepts that may appear on the exam.

The approved references are especially useful for code-based questions. The International Building Code supports broad building-code topics, while ACI 318 and ACI 530/530.1 provide more specialized technical guidance for concrete and masonry.

Plan reading and estimating should also be part of regular preparation. Candidates should be comfortable interpreting dimensions, construction details, sections, elevations, material quantities, and common construction calculations.

General Building candidates should also understand how different trades interact on a project. Concrete foundations affect framing, wall systems affect doors and windows, structural systems affect finishes, and sitework often affects the sequence of the entire building project.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada B General Building examination is one part of the overall contractor licensing process. Candidates must complete the applicable licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the trade examination.

  1. Confirm the General Building classification. Determine that the work the business intends to perform falls within the Nevada B General Building scope.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor application must designate the person providing the applicable trade qualification for the business.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable experience demonstrating work in general building construction and coordination of multiple building trades.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Provide the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. The application and qualifying experience must be reviewed before examination eligibility is issued.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. Once eligibility is established, the qualifying individual can proceed with scheduling the required examination.
  7. Prepare for the Nevada B trade exam. Review general building code, concrete, masonry, sitework, structural systems, carpentry, safety, finishes, and related topics.
  8. Complete applicable examinations. Satisfy the examinations required for the contractor license application unless an approved waiver applies.
  9. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish all other applicable requirements before the Nevada contractor license is issued.

Candidates can begin studying while licensing documentation is being prepared. Using the highlighted and tabbed references regularly gives candidates more time to become familiar with the books before testing.

State Requirements

The Nevada B General Building classification applies to contractors whose principal contracting business involves construction or remodeling of buildings or structures where the work requires coordination of more than two unrelated building trades or crafts.

The General Building contractor typically acts as the prime contractor on a project where construction or remodeling of the building is the primary purpose. This makes the classification broader than a single specialty license.

General Building projects can involve concrete, masonry, framing, structural components, roofing, doors, windows, drywall, finishes, sitework, and other building systems. Candidates should therefore prepare for an examination that requires broad construction knowledge.

The General Building classification does not automatically authorize the contractor to self-perform every specialty trade. Specialty work must be completed within the licensing authority that applies to the trade being performed.

The qualifying individual must establish the applicable experience required for the B classification as part of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Additional business, financial, application, examination, and licensing requirements also apply before a contractor license can be issued.

Passing the General Building examination is an important step, but it does not replace the other requirements associated with contractor licensure in Nevada.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada B General Building study plan should combine broad trade review with code-navigation practice using the highlighted and tabbed exam-room books.

General Building Code preparation should focus heavily on the International Building Code. Candidates should study occupancy classifications, construction types, fire protection, means of egress, structural provisions, building heights and areas, and other major code topics.

Concrete preparation should include both practical construction knowledge and familiarity with ACI 318. Review reinforcement, concrete cover, anchorage, development, structural members, durability, and related structural concrete provisions.

Masonry should include knowledge of structural walls, reinforcement, mortar, grout, masonry units, construction requirements, and the technical specifications addressed in ACI 530/530.1.

Sitework should include general knowledge of excavation, grading, drainage, site preparation, underground utilities, and coordination between site activities and building construction.

Metals should include structural components, supports, framing, connections, and how metal systems relate to other building components.

Carpentry preparation should include structural framing, walls, floors, roofs, openings, layout, sheathing, and the relationship between wood construction and building-code requirements.

Thermal and Moisture Protection should include insulation, roofing, waterproofing, flashing, weather protection, and moisture-control concepts associated with the building envelope.

Doors and Windows should be studied for openings, dimensions, installation, coordination with wall systems, and egress-related code requirements.

Drywall and Finishes should include gypsum assemblies, interior finishes, wall and ceiling systems, fire-resistance concepts, and installation knowledge.

Safety should be part of general construction preparation even though this package focuses on the books allowed into the exam. General Building contractors coordinate work involving ladders, scaffolds, excavation, tools, equipment, material handling, and other jobsite hazards.

Plan Reading should be practiced through dimensions, sections, elevations, schedules, symbols, details, and construction notes. Candidates should be comfortable connecting information found in different areas of a drawing set.

Estimating preparation should include material quantities, area, volume, length, basic construction calculations, and interpreting information from plans or project conditions.

For code-navigation practice, begin with a question and identify the subject before opening a book. If the issue is general building code, start with the IBC. If it concerns structural concrete, move to ACI 318. If it concerns structural masonry, use ACI 530/530.1.

How does the General Building Contractor (B) exam work?

The General Building Contractor (B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
