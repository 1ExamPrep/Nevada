## Exam Details

The Nevada C-21C Sheet Metal Contractor examination is administered by PSI for the Nevada State Contractors Board. Candidates must complete the applicable Nevada contractor application process and receive examination eligibility before scheduling the trade examination.

The C-21C examination contains **60 questions**. Candidates must answer at least **42 questions correctly** to meet the published passing requirement. The examination allows **2.5 hours** for completion.

The official content outline divides the examination into the following areas:

- **Ducts:** 20 questions.
- **Chimneys, Flues, and Vents:** 8 questions.
- **Hoods, Ventilation, Equipment, and Food Service Equipment:** 10 questions.
- **Metal Roofing Systems, Flashing, and Architectural Metals:** 10 questions.
- **Gutters and Downspouts:** 5 questions.
- **Tools and Equipment:** 4 questions.
- **Safety:** 3 questions.

Ducts make up the largest individual portion of the examination, accounting for 20 of the 60 questions. Candidates should therefore devote substantial preparation time to duct systems, materials, construction requirements, ventilation concepts, installation practices, and applicable mechanical code provisions.

Hoods, ventilation, equipment, and food-service equipment account for another 10 questions. Metal roofing systems, flashing, and architectural metals also account for 10 questions, making these areas important components of a complete preparation plan.

Chimneys, flues, and vents make up eight questions, while gutters and downspouts account for five. Tools, equipment, and safety complete the published examination outline. Candidates should prepare for the entire scope rather than concentrating exclusively on the largest categories.

## Open Book Test

The Nevada C-21C Sheet Metal Contractor examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates may bring specific approved references into the examination center, and the two books included in this package are approved for the C-21C trade examination.

The approved code references are the **Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018** and the **International Building Code, 2018**. These books can be used to answer applicable code questions during the examination.

Open-book testing does not mean every trade question can be answered by searching through the two code books. The examination may also include questions based on general sheet metal trade knowledge and accepted industry practices. Candidates should build technical knowledge before testing while learning how to use the permitted codes efficiently.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. PSI rules prohibit written notes in the references. Candidates may not write in or add highlighting to the books during the examination session.

Permanent tabs are permitted for organizing approved references. Temporary or removable tabs are not allowed. Candidates should prepare their permitted references before exam day and become comfortable using the same navigation system during study sessions.

With 60 questions and 2.5 hours available, efficient reference use is important. A productive strategy is to recognize which questions require a code lookup and which rely primarily on trade knowledge. Spending excessive time searching for a single answer can reduce the time available for the rest of the exam.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong preparation strategy should combine code navigation with sheet metal trade knowledge. Since only the approved references can be taken into the trade examination room, candidates should not rely on having every technical manual available during the test.

Ducts deserve substantial attention because they account for 20 questions. Candidates should review duct construction principles, materials, fittings, connections, supports, ventilation concepts, installation practices, and code provisions. Practice identifying whether a question can be answered using the Uniform Mechanical Code or whether it depends primarily on trade knowledge.

Hoods, ventilation equipment, and food-service equipment account for 10 questions. Become familiar with ventilation systems, exhaust concepts, hoods, clearances, duct requirements, and related mechanical code provisions. Using the Uniform Mechanical Code during practice questions can help candidates become faster at locating these requirements.

Metal roofing systems, flashing, and architectural metals also account for 10 questions. Preparation should cover the purpose and application of flashing, metal roof components, weather protection, architectural sheet metal systems, joints, transitions, and other related trade concepts.

Chimneys, flues, and vents represent eight questions. Candidates should understand how these components function, how they are installed, applicable clearance concepts, materials, terminations, and the way mechanical and building codes regulate their use.

Gutters and downspouts account for five questions. Review roof drainage concepts, fabrication, connections, outlets, leaders, downspouts, slopes, and common architectural sheet metal practices.

Tools and equipment make up four questions. Candidates should recognize common sheet metal fabrication and installation tools and understand their intended uses. Familiarity with cutting, bending, forming, joining, measuring, and fastening equipment can support this portion of the exam.

Safety accounts for three questions. Even though it is one of the smaller content areas, safety remains an essential part of professional sheet metal work. Candidates should understand common hazards involving elevated work, ladders, tools, sharp metal edges, material handling, personal protective equipment, and construction operations.

Code practice should be timed. Open the book only after deciding which subject and likely code provision applies. Learn how to move from an index term to the appropriate section and read enough surrounding material to identify exceptions, limitations, or related requirements.

It can also be useful to mark difficult practice questions for later review. Repeatedly searching the same code topic builds familiarity with its location and can make future lookups faster. Over time, candidates should develop a mental map of the major mechanical and building code sections related to C-21C work.

## How does the Sheet Metal Contractor (C-21C) exam work?

The Sheet Metal Contractor (C-21C) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
