## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-21E study plan should begin with **System Design** because it represents 15 of the 45 examination questions. Candidates should understand how collectors, piping, pumps, storage, valves, controls, and heat-transfer components work as a complete solar thermal system.

Instead of memorizing components independently, follow the movement of energy through the system. Understand how solar energy is captured at the collector, transferred into a working medium, circulated through the system, stored when necessary, and ultimately used in controlling air temperatures.

**Piping** represents 10 questions. Candidates should review pipe materials, fittings, supports, joining methods, routing, expansion, insulation, valves, and connections.

The **Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018** should be used regularly for piping preparation. Practice using the table of contents and index to locate requirements by topic rather than relying on remembered page numbers.

**Definitions** can be important when a question uses a technical plumbing term. Understanding the code definition can help determine which section or requirement applies.

**Tables** should also receive careful attention. Requirements involving materials, sizing, supports, or other conditions may be contained in tables rather than paragraph text.

**Exceptions** can alter how a general requirement applies. Candidates should read surrounding language carefully before selecting an answer.

**Cross-references** can lead to additional requirements. Practice following them when one section directs the reader to another chapter or provision.

**Collectors** represents 7 questions. Candidates should understand how solar collectors capture thermal energy and how collector placement, arrangement, circulation, and connections affect system operation.

Review mounting concepts, orientation, flow through collector arrays, collector connections, and practical installation considerations.

**Pumps and Storage Systems** accounts for 5 questions. Candidates should understand pump purpose, circulation, storage tanks, heat exchangers, and how stored thermal energy can be used for later temperature-control demands.

Review how pumps affect flow through collectors and piping, as well as the role of storage in maintaining system usefulness when solar input varies.

**System Valves and Controls** represents 3 questions. Candidates should review shutoff valves, check valves, balancing components, temperature controls, differential controls, pressure-related devices, and other components that influence operation.

**Safety** accounts for 5 questions. Candidates should use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 to study fall protection, ladders, roof work, personal protective equipment, electrical hazards, tools, material handling, and other construction safety topics.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

Because OSHA is not allowed into the examination room, candidates should build strong retained safety knowledge before test day.

The **Solar Water and Pool Heating Design and Installation Manual** should be used to connect individual exam subjects into complete system understanding. It can help reinforce collector layouts, circulation, piping, pumps, storage, valves, and controls.

Because the manual is not allowed into the exam room, active recall can be particularly useful. Review a system diagram or installation topic, close the book, and explain the sequence and purpose of the major components from memory.

**Heat transfer** is a central concept in solar air-conditioning preparation. Candidates should understand how collected solar energy moves through a working fluid or thermal system and becomes useful for controlling air temperatures.

**System balance** should also be studied. Proper circulation through collectors affects performance, so candidates should understand why balanced piping and appropriate flow are important.

**Freeze protection** can be relevant to outdoor solar thermal components. Candidates should understand general system-design approaches used to protect exposed equipment and piping from damaging environmental conditions.

**Insulation** is another important concept because unwanted heat loss can reduce system performance. Candidates should understand why piping is insulated and why the surrounding environment matters.

**Thermal expansion** should also be considered. Piping exposed to elevated temperatures can expand, making material selection, support, and allowance for movement important installation considerations.

A useful study schedule is to divide preparation according to the exam outline. Spend the most time on System Design and Piping, followed by Collectors, Pumps and Storage Systems, Safety, and System Valves and Controls.

Once each area becomes familiar, move into mixed-question practice. The actual examination may shift quickly between system design, piping, collectors, controls, or safety.

Timed code-navigation drills can help improve efficiency. Choose a piping subject and practice locating the relevant UPC provision within a limited amount of time.

Do not depend on finding every answer in the code. The Uniform Plumbing Code is most useful for targeted code questions, while many practical questions require retained knowledge from study and field experience.

When a practice question is missed, identify the reason. The issue may involve weak trade knowledge, unfamiliar terminology, slow code navigation, or difficulty understanding how system components interact.

## How does the Solar Air Conditioning Contractor (C-21E) exam work?

The Solar Air Conditioning Contractor (C-21E) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
