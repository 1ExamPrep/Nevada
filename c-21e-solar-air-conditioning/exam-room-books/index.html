Exam Details

The Nevada C-21E Solar Air Conditioning Contractor examination contains 45 questions. Candidates are allowed 1.5 hours to complete the examination and must answer 32 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Piping: 10 questions
  • System Design: 15 questions
  • Pumps and Storage Systems: 5 questions
  • Collectors: 7 questions
  • System Valves and Controls: 3 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions

System Design is the largest category with 15 questions. Candidates should spend substantial preparation time understanding complete solar thermal system layouts, circulation, storage, heat transfer, collector arrangements, pumps, valves, and controls.

Piping represents 10 questions and is one of the most important areas for use of the Uniform Plumbing Code. Candidates should be familiar with pipe materials, fittings, supports, joining methods, routing, connections, and related plumbing provisions.

Collectors accounts for 7 questions. Candidates should understand collector function, mounting considerations, flow relationships, circulation, and how collectors fit into a solar air-conditioning system.

Pumps and Storage Systems and Safety each account for 5 questions, while System Valves and Controls represents 3 questions.

With 45 questions in 1.5 hours, candidates have approximately two minutes per question on average. Efficient code navigation can help preserve time for questions that depend on retained trade knowledge.

State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-21E as the Solar Air Conditioning subclassification under the broader refrigeration and air-conditioning classification.

The C-21E scope covers the installation of collectors, piping, storage, and other equipment used in the conversion of solar energy to energy used for the control of air temperatures.

The classification is focused on solar thermal air-conditioning applications rather than photovoltaic electrical generation. Candidates should concentrate on systems that collect, transfer, store, and control solar-derived thermal energy for temperature control.

Practical work can involve collectors, circulation piping, pumps, storage equipment, valves, controls, heat-transfer components, and related installation work. Understanding how these elements operate together is an important part of C-21E preparation.

Code knowledge is also important because piping systems must be installed with appropriate materials, fittings, supports, valves, and connections. The Uniform Plumbing Code provides the exam-room reference for these requirements.

The C-21E trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-21E preparation plan should begin with System Design because it represents 15 of the 45 examination questions. Candidates should understand how collectors, piping, pumps, storage, valves, controls, and heat-transfer components work together as a complete solar thermal system.

Rather than memorizing individual components in isolation, follow the movement of energy through the system. Understand how solar energy is captured, transferred into a working medium, circulated through piping, stored when necessary, and ultimately used in controlling air temperatures.

Piping represents 10 questions and is one of the most important areas for Uniform Plumbing Code preparation. Candidates should review pipe materials, fittings, supports, joining methods, routing, insulation, valves, and connections.

Practice locating code requirements by subject. Begin with a question about a specific material, fitting, support condition, or valve, then use the index or table of contents to locate the relevant UPC section.

The Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018 should be used throughout preparation rather than only during the final days before the examination. Repeated use helps candidates become more familiar with how the book is organized.

Definitions can be especially important. A question may depend on the exact code meaning of a plumbing term, and checking the definition can help determine which requirement applies.

Tables should receive careful attention. Important requirements involving materials, supports, sizing, or other conditions may appear in tables rather than narrative text.

Exceptions can modify a general rule. Candidates should read enough surrounding code language to identify exceptions and special conditions before selecting an answer.

Cross-references may direct the candidate to another section containing additional requirements. Practice following them instead of assuming the first section contains the entire answer.

Collectors represents 7 questions. Candidates should understand the role of solar collectors, how they capture thermal energy, and how collector arrangement, circulation, and connections affect system operation.

Review mounting concepts, orientation, flow through collector arrays, and how collectors integrate with the rest of the system.

Pumps and Storage Systems accounts for 5 questions. Candidates should understand pump purpose, circulation, storage tanks, heat exchangers, and how stored thermal energy can be used for later temperature-control needs.

Review how pumps affect fluid movement through collectors and piping, and how storage can help maintain system usefulness when solar input varies.

More on test Information and Study Materials

System Valves and Controls represents 3 questions. Candidates should review shutoff valves, check valves, balancing devices, temperature controls, differential controls, and other components used to regulate system operation.

Safety accounts for 5 questions. Candidates should prepare for practical jobsite safety involving roof access, ladders, elevated work, tools, piping, hot surfaces, electrical control components, lifting, and other installation hazards.

Because this package contains only the exam-room-approved Uniform Plumbing Code, candidates should make sure their broader preparation also covers system design, collectors, pumps, storage, controls, and safety.

Heat transfer is a central concept in solar air-conditioning preparation. Candidates should understand how collected solar energy moves through a working fluid or thermal system and becomes useful for controlling air temperatures.

System balance should also be studied. Proper circulation through collectors affects performance, so candidates should understand the purpose of balanced piping arrangements and controlled flow.

Freeze protection can be important for outdoor solar thermal components. Candidates should understand general system-design concepts used to protect piping and equipment from damaging environmental conditions.

Insulation is also important because unwanted heat loss can reduce system performance. Candidates should understand why solar thermal piping is insulated and why environmental exposure matters.

Thermal expansion should be considered in hot piping systems. Elevated temperatures can cause piping to expand, making material selection, supports, and allowance for movement important installation considerations.

A useful study schedule is to divide preparation according to the exam outline. Spend the most time on System Design and Piping, followed by Collectors, Pumps and Storage Systems, Safety, and System Valves and Controls.

Once individual areas become familiar, move into mixed-question practice. The actual examination can shift quickly between system design, piping, collectors, controls, and safety.

Timed code-navigation drills can help improve open-book performance. Choose a piping subject and practice locating the applicable UPC section within a limited amount of time.

Do not depend on finding every answer in the code. Some questions will be better answered through retained trade knowledge, while the Uniform Plumbing Code should be used for targeted confirmation of code-based requirements.

When a practice question is missed, identify the reason. The issue may involve weak system knowledge, unfamiliar terminology, slow code navigation, or difficulty understanding the relationship between multiple system components.

Correcting the underlying weakness is more useful than memorizing a single answer. This creates stronger preparation for unfamiliar questions on the actual examination.

Time management should also be practiced. Answer familiar questions efficiently, use the UPC for targeted lookups when necessary, mark more difficult items, and return to them after completing questions that can be answered more quickly.

How does the Solar Air Conditioning Contractor (C-21E) exam work?

The Solar Air Conditioning Contractor (C-21E) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
