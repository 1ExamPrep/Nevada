Exam Details

The Nevada C-1K Industrial Piping trade examination is associated with a specialty classification under Nevada's Plumbing and Heating contractor classifications. The examination evaluates trade knowledge relevant to industrial piping and the technical requirements involved in installing and servicing piping systems used in industrial environments.

Candidates should prepare for topics involving piping materials, system components, valves, fittings, installation methods, equipment connections, mechanical requirements, code interpretation, trade calculations, process piping concepts, welding, and construction safety.

The Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018 should be used for preparation involving plumbing-related piping requirements, materials, fittings, installation methods, system components, testing, and other applicable plumbing provisions.

The Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018 supports preparation involving mechanical piping, mechanical equipment, installation requirements, ventilation, clearances, equipment connections, and other mechanical-system provisions applicable to industrial piping work.

Candidates should not expect the two code books to replace broader trade knowledge. The Industrial Piping examination can require understanding of practical subjects that must be learned before entering the testing center, making supplemental study and practice an important part of preparation.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-1K Industrial Piping classification covers the fabrication, installation, repair, and removal of pipes and equipment used for the transmission of water, steam, gas, chemicals, food, and other substances for industrial uses.

The scope distinguishes industrial piping from ordinary residential plumbing work. Industrial piping systems can be associated with manufacturing, processing, commercial facilities, and other applications where specialized piping is used to transport fluids, gases, chemicals, or process materials.

Candidates should therefore prepare for a combination of code knowledge and trade understanding. Piping materials, joining methods, valves, supports, equipment connections, system layout, safety, welding, and process piping concepts can all be important parts of industrial piping work.

The Nevada State Contractors Board requires applicants to qualify for the classification they are requesting. Examination eligibility is tied to the licensing application and review of qualifying experience.

Passing the C-1K trade examination is an important licensing step, but it does not independently result in issuance of a contractor license. Applicants must satisfy the other applicable licensing requirements before they can operate under the classification.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-1K study plan should combine code-reference navigation with practical industrial piping knowledge. Candidates should not rely exclusively on the UPC and UMC simply because those books can be used during the examination.

Uniform Plumbing Code Preparation should focus on finding technical requirements efficiently. Practice with piping materials, fittings, installation provisions, testing, system components, tables, and definitions. Learn which chapters contain the major piping-related subjects that are most relevant to industrial work.

Uniform Mechanical Code Preparation should include piping systems, mechanical equipment, ventilation, equipment connections, clearances, installation provisions, and related technical requirements. Practice using both the index and the overall chapter structure.

Piping Materials and Components should be studied beyond code navigation. Candidates should understand common pipe materials, fittings, valves, supports, joints, and system components, along with the purpose each component serves.

Industrial and Process Piping preparation should include the basic principles associated with systems used to transport water, steam, gases, chemicals, food, and other industrial substances. Candidates should understand that different services can require different materials, joining methods, equipment, and safety considerations.

Welding should be part of broader preparation because welded piping is common in many industrial applications. Review welding processes, joint preparation, positions, terminology, basic inspection concepts, and safe work practices.

Trade Mathematics should also receive regular practice. Industrial piping work can involve measurements, fractions, decimals, elevations, offsets, geometry, angles, and other calculations. Candidates should practice solving these problems without depending on an exam-room reference.

Facility Piping Systems should be studied for system arrangement, equipment connections, valves, supports, piping layouts, and the way piping functions as part of a larger industrial facility.

Safety should remain part of the study routine even though the Books Allowed into Exam Package contains only the UPC and UMC. Industrial piping work can involve welding, heavy materials, tools, elevated work, equipment, pressure, and other hazards that require strong safety awareness.

For the two approved code books, use timed navigation drills. Begin with a practice question and identify the subject before opening a reference. Decide whether the UPC or UMC is more appropriate, then locate the applicable section as efficiently as possible.

How does the Industrial Piping Contractor (C-1K) exam work?

The Industrial Piping Contractor (C-1K) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
