Exam Details

The Nevada Industrial Piping Contractor (C-1K) trade examination contains 50 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the examination, and the minimum passing score is 37 correct answers.

The examination evaluates knowledge relevant to industrial piping work and the practical skills needed to work with piping systems used in industrial facilities. Candidates should prepare for code interpretation, piping materials, system components, installation methods, mechanical requirements, welding, calculations, safety, and general industrial piping practices.

The Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018 should be studied for piping-related code provisions, materials, fittings, installation requirements, testing, drainage or water-related provisions where applicable, and the organization of plumbing-code requirements.

The Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018 supports preparation for mechanical-system requirements, equipment connections, piping, ventilation, combustion-related provisions where applicable, and other mechanical requirements relevant to industrial piping work.

The supplemental books expand preparation beyond code navigation. ASME B31.3 provides process piping concepts, Modern Welding reinforces welding fundamentals, Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters supports trade calculations, and Facility Piping Systems Handbook helps candidates understand how piping systems are arranged and used in industrial and commercial facilities.

Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning supports broader mechanical-system understanding, while OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 helps candidates prepare for construction-safety concepts that may be encountered on industrial jobsites.

  1. Confirm the C-1K Industrial Piping classification. Make sure the work the business intends to perform falls within Nevada's Industrial Piping specialty scope.

  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable work history demonstrating industrial piping experience related to the requested classification.

  6. Receive examination eligibility. Once authorized, the qualifying individual can schedule the applicable contractor examinations.
  7. Prepare for the C-1K trade examination. Study piping systems, plumbing and mechanical code requirements, process piping, welding, trade mathematics, mechanical systems, and construction safety.

  9. Complete applicable examinations. Nevada contractor applicants must satisfy the required trade and business and law examination requirements unless an authorized waiver applies.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish all other applicable requirements before the contractor license is issued.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-1K Industrial Piping classification is a specialty classification within Plumbing and Heating contracting. It authorizes the fabrication, installation, repair, and removal of pipes and equipment used for the transmission of water, steam, gas, chemicals, food, and other substances for industrial uses.

The scope can involve piping systems used in manufacturing facilities, processing operations, commercial facilities, and other industrial environments where fluids, gases, or materials must be moved through piping networks.

The classification requires candidates to understand more than basic plumbing. Industrial piping systems can involve process conditions, specialized materials, welding, equipment connections, system layout, safety, code requirements, and technical calculations.

The qualifying individual must establish the applicable trade qualification as part of the Nevada contractor license application. Nevada also requires applicants to complete the other applicable licensing requirements in addition to the C-1K trade examination.

Passing the trade examination is an important step, but it does not independently create a contractor license. Applicants must complete the full licensing process before performing work under the classification.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-1K preparation plan should combine code navigation with practical industrial piping knowledge. Candidates should be able to recognize piping materials, system components, joining methods, valves, supports, equipment connections, and common installation practices.

Plumbing Code Preparation should center on the Uniform Plumbing Code. Practice locating requirements using the index and table of contents instead of reading the book only from beginning to end. Become familiar with definitions, materials, installation provisions, testing requirements, tables, and technical terminology.

Mechanical Code Preparation should center on the Uniform Mechanical Code. Candidates should practice finding piping-related and equipment-related requirements and understand how the mechanical code is organized.

Process Piping should be studied through ASME B31.3. Industrial piping candidates should develop familiarity with process piping terminology, materials, fabrication, joining, examination, testing, and the general technical concepts associated with piping used in industrial facilities.

Because ASME B31.3 is not allowed inside the examination center, candidates should use active recall. Review a section, close the book, and explain the concept or answer practice questions without consulting the reference.

Welding preparation should include welding processes, equipment, electrodes or filler materials, joint preparation, positions, inspection concepts, and safety. Modern Welding can help candidates build familiarity with welding terminology and methods encountered in industrial piping work.

Trade Mathematics should receive regular practice. Industrial piping work can require accurate measurements, fractions, decimals, geometry, elevations, offsets, angles, and other calculations. Work problems repeatedly until the process becomes familiar without depending on the mathematics book.

Facility Piping Systems should be studied for system arrangement, components, valves, supports, equipment connections, piping materials, and the way industrial piping operates as part of a larger facility.

Mechanical Systems preparation can be reinforced through Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning. Understanding pumps, controls, heat transfer, equipment, and circulating systems can strengthen a candidate's overall ability to interpret industrial piping questions.

Safety should be reviewed through OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Industrial piping work may involve welding, lifting, tools, ladders, scaffolds, equipment, material handling, and other hazards that require strong safety awareness.

For the two exam-approved books, candidates should practice timed lookups. Read a question, identify the main subject, decide whether the Uniform Plumbing Code or Uniform Mechanical Code is more appropriate, and then locate the applicable requirement.

More on test Information and Study Materials

If a search takes too long, make note of the subject and repeat the lookup later. Repetition helps build familiarity with commonly used chapters and technical terms.

For study-only references, focus on recall instead of navigation speed. Candidates need to retain the important concepts because those books will not be available in the examination room.

A balanced study schedule can rotate among plumbing code, mechanical code, process piping, welding, mathematics, facility systems, mechanical fundamentals, and safety. This helps prevent candidates from becoming strong in one area while neglecting another important part of industrial piping preparation.

Practice questions should be used as a diagnostic tool. If welding questions are difficult, return to Modern Welding. If process piping terminology is weak, revisit ASME B31.3. If calculations take too long, increase work with Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters.

For code questions, repeated practice with the UPC and UMC can help candidates develop a more efficient search process. The goal is to know which book to open, where to begin, and how to confirm the requirement without unnecessary searching.

How does the Industrial Piping Contractor (C-1K) exam work?

The Industrial Piping Contractor (C-1K) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
