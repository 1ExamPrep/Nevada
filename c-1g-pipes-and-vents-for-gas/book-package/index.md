## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-1G study plan should divide preparation between natural gas piping, LP-Gas piping, venting, appliance installation, sizing, testing, inspection, combustion air, and reference navigation.

**Natural Gas Piping** should receive substantial attention because it accounts for 12 questions. Candidates should study pipe materials, fittings, regulators, shutoff valves, appliance connections, pressure considerations, sizing methods, and testing requirements.

Use **NFPA 54** regularly during natural gas study sessions. Practice locating piping tables, installation requirements, definitions, venting provisions, and appliance-related sections so the book becomes familiar before test day.

**LP Gas Piping** also accounts for 12 questions. Candidates should understand how LP-Gas systems differ from natural gas systems, including storage arrangements, regulators, pressure, container considerations, piping, clearances, and system protection.

**NFPA 58** should be the primary navigation reference for LP-Gas subjects. Learn the organization of the book and practice identifying which sections relate to containers, piping, regulators, installation, and safety.

**Chimneys Flues and Vents** accounts for 10 questions, making it another major area. Candidates should understand how combustion products are safely conveyed from an appliance to the outdoors and how venting requirements change based on appliance type and system configuration.

Study vent connectors, chimneys, listed vent systems, draft, sizing, clearances, support, termination, and the relationship between venting and appliance operation.

**Combustion Air** should be studied along with venting. Gas-fired appliances require adequate air for combustion, dilution, and ventilation. Candidates should understand why insufficient combustion air can create operating and safety problems.

**Appliance Installation** should include location, clearances, connections, shutoff devices, venting, combustion-air provisions, and basic requirements associated with gas-fired equipment.

**Sizing and Estimating** should include pipe sizing, system demand, appliance input, developed length, table navigation, and practical calculations. Candidates should practice using the applicable tables rather than simply reading them.

Timed sizing exercises can be especially helpful. Choose a sample system, identify the gas type, determine the appropriate sizing method, locate the correct table, and work through the calculation under time pressure.

**Testing and Inspection** should include leak testing, pressure-testing concepts, inspection sequence, and general system verification. Candidates should understand why a gas system must be tested before being placed into service.

The **Uniform Mechanical Code** should be used alongside NFPA 54 and NFPA 58. Candidates should become comfortable deciding which reference is most appropriate for a given question.

## How does the Pipes and Vents for Gas Contractor (C-1G) exam work?

The Pipes and Vents for Gas Contractor (C-1G) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
