Exam Details

The Nevada C-1G Pipes And Vents For Gas Contractor examination contains 50 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the exam and must answer 35 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Natural Gas Piping: 12 questions
  • LP Gas Piping: 12 questions
  • Chimneys Flues and Vents: 10 questions
  • Appliance Installation: 5 questions
  • Sizing and Estimating: 5 questions
  • Testing and Inspection: 3 questions
  • Combustion Air: 3 questions

Natural Gas Piping accounts for 12 questions. Candidates should understand piping materials, fittings, installation methods, sizing concepts, regulators, shutoff requirements, appliance connections, system pressure considerations, testing, and code provisions that apply to natural gas installations.

LP Gas Piping also accounts for 12 questions. Preparation should include LP-Gas piping systems, regulators, containers, fittings, installation requirements, system protection, clearances, and the differences between LP-Gas and natural gas systems.

Chimneys Flues and Vents accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should understand venting methods, vent connectors, chimneys, draft considerations, clearances, sizing, termination, and the relationship between appliances and the venting system serving them.

Appliance Installation and Sizing and Estimating each account for 5 questions. Candidates should be comfortable using code tables, understanding appliance input, evaluating piping capacity, and applying installation requirements to common gas-fired equipment.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-1G Pipes And Vents For Gas Contractor examination is an open-book test.

The following references in this package are allowed in the examination center:

  • Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018
  • NFPA 54 - Standard for National Fuel Gas Code, 2012
  • NFPA 58 - Standard for Liquid Petroleum Gas Code, 2014

Candidates are responsible for bringing their approved references to the examination center. The books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used for indexing, while temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not permitted.

The approved references may not contain written notes or additional loose or attached papers. Candidates should prepare the books before exam day and practice using the same reference setup during study sessions.

Open-book testing still requires strong technical knowledge. With 50 questions in two hours, candidates have a little over two minutes per question on average. Searching through three books without a clear idea of where to look can quickly consume valuable time.

A better strategy is to understand the subject first, identify which reference is most likely to contain the answer, and then use the appropriate code book strategically. Natural gas questions may lead candidates toward NFPA 54, LP-Gas questions may require NFPA 58, while mechanical, venting, appliance, or combustion-air requirements may also involve the Uniform Mechanical Code.

Repeated code-navigation practice can help candidates develop a clear sense of which book to open first for a particular topic.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-1G Pipes And Vents For Gas as a specialty subclassification under C-1 Plumbing and Heating Contracting.

The scope covers the installation of pipes from the source of supply of liquefied petroleum and natural gas into a building or structure and the installation of the related vents.

The scope connects two major parts of fuel-gas work: delivering gas safely to appliances and properly venting combustion products. Candidates should understand that piping and venting requirements work together within a complete gas installation.

Natural gas and LP-Gas systems share many general concepts, but they are not identical. Candidates should understand differences in system pressures, regulators, storage and supply arrangements, code requirements, and installation practices.

Venting is equally important because improperly sized or installed venting can affect appliance operation and safety. Candidates should study chimneys, flues, vent connectors, draft, clearances, terminations, sizing, and combustion-air requirements alongside gas piping.

The Nevada licensing process also includes requirements beyond the C-1G trade examination. Applicants must complete the applicable qualification, business and law, application, and other licensing requirements before receiving the contractor credential.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-1G study plan should divide preparation between natural gas piping, LP-Gas piping, venting, appliance installation, sizing, testing, inspection, combustion air, and reference navigation.

Natural Gas Piping should receive substantial attention because it accounts for 12 questions. Candidates should study pipe materials, fittings, regulators, shutoff valves, appliance connections, pressure considerations, sizing methods, and testing requirements.

Use NFPA 54 regularly during natural gas study sessions. Practice locating piping tables, installation requirements, definitions, venting provisions, and appliance-related sections so the book becomes familiar before test day.

LP Gas Piping also accounts for 12 questions. Candidates should understand how LP-Gas systems differ from natural gas systems, including storage arrangements, regulators, pressure, container considerations, piping, clearances, and system protection.

NFPA 58 should be the primary navigation reference for LP-Gas subjects. Learn the organization of the book and practice identifying which sections relate to containers, piping, regulators, installation, and safety.

Chimneys Flues and Vents accounts for 10 questions, making it another major area. Candidates should understand how combustion products are safely conveyed from an appliance to the outdoors and how venting requirements change based on appliance type and system configuration.

Study vent connectors, chimneys, listed vent systems, draft, sizing, clearances, support, termination, and the relationship between venting and appliance operation.

Combustion Air should be studied along with venting. Gas-fired appliances require adequate air for combustion, dilution, and ventilation. Candidates should understand why insufficient combustion air can create operating and safety problems.

Appliance Installation should include location, clearances, connections, shutoff devices, venting, combustion-air provisions, and basic requirements associated with gas-fired equipment.

Sizing and Estimating should include pipe sizing, system demand, appliance input, developed length, table navigation, and practical calculations. Candidates should practice using the applicable tables rather than simply reading them.

Timed sizing exercises can be especially helpful. Choose a sample system, identify the gas type, determine the appropriate sizing method, locate the correct table, and work through the calculation under time pressure.

Testing and Inspection should include leak testing, pressure-testing concepts, inspection sequence, and general system verification. Candidates should understand why a gas system must be tested before being placed into service.

The Uniform Mechanical Code should be used alongside NFPA 54 and NFPA 58. Candidates should become comfortable deciding which reference is most appropriate for a given question.

More on test Information and Study Materials

One useful preparation method is to create three separate topic lists, one for each approved book. Under the UMC, list mechanical and venting subjects. Under NFPA 54, list natural gas and fuel-gas subjects. Under NFPA 58, list LP-Gas subjects.

Then practice selecting the correct book before attempting the lookup. This can reduce wasted time during the examination.

Permanent tabs can help organize major sections, but candidates should still understand the general structure of each code. Tabs are most useful when they support knowledge rather than replace it.

Highlighting can also help important requirements stand out. During study sessions, candidates should repeatedly review highlighted code language so key provisions become easier to recognize.

The indexes and tables of contents are valuable tools. Candidates should practice finding a term, following the index entry to the applicable section, and reading enough surrounding language to determine whether the provision answers the question.

Practice questions should be completed with all three books nearby. Attempt the question first, determine which reference is most likely to contain the answer, and then perform the lookup if needed.

If a candidate repeatedly chooses the wrong reference for a topic, more subject organization may be needed. If the correct book is selected but the section takes too long to find, navigation drills should become the priority.

Because the exam contains 50 questions in two hours, time management matters. Candidates should practice moving past difficult questions and returning to them later instead of spending too long on a single code search.

How does the Pipes and Vents for Gas Contractor (C-1G) exam work?

The Pipes and Vents for Gas Contractor (C-1G) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
