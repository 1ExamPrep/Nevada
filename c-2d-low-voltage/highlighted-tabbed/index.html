Exam Details

The Nevada Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) trade examination evaluates technical knowledge related to low voltage electrical and communications systems. Candidates should prepare for both practical trade concepts and electrical-code questions involving the installation, alteration, and repair of limited-energy systems.

Grounding and bonding should receive careful study. Low voltage systems can involve grounding conductors, bonding requirements, equipment grounding, grounding electrodes, and connections between communications equipment and other electrical systems.

Conductors and cables are another major preparation area. Candidates should understand cable types, conductor characteristics, routing, support, physical protection, separation, termination, identification, and installation conditions.

Communications systems should be studied broadly. Telephone, computer networking, sound, cable television, closed-circuit video, and related systems can involve different cabling methods and equipment, but they share common installation concepts such as pathways, grounding, support, terminations, and system organization.

Raceways, boxes, and enclosures should also be reviewed. Candidates should understand how low voltage cables may be routed or protected, when wiring methods matter, and how electrical-code requirements can affect the physical installation.

Power supplies and transformers are also relevant. Many low voltage systems rely on power conversion or dedicated low voltage supplies, so candidates should understand the basic function of these components and how they relate to the connected system.

Preparation should also include the relationship between code requirements and practical installation. Knowing the general trade concept can help candidates determine where to look in the NEC and which terms to use when navigating the book.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada C-2D trade examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable Nevada contractor licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the trade exam.

  1. Confirm the C-2D classification. Make sure the low voltage work the business intends to perform falls within Nevada's Low Voltage Systems classification.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor license application must designate the person who will provide the applicable trade qualification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable work history involving low voltage systems, communications wiring, networking, sound, video, controls, fiber optics, or related electrical work.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Submit the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. Examination eligibility is established through the contractor licensing process.
  6. Receive examination authorization. Once eligible, the qualifying individual can schedule the applicable examination.
  7. Prepare for the C-2D trade examination. Study low voltage systems, communications, grounding and bonding, conductors, raceways, equipment, and electrical-code requirements.
  8. Practice with the highlighted and tabbed NEC. Use the 2017 National Electrical Code throughout preparation to improve reference navigation.
  9. Review telecommunications material. Use Telecommunications Wiring for additional trade study and closed-reference recall practice.
  10. Complete applicable examinations and licensing requirements. Finish the required steps associated with the contractor license application.

State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-2D Low Voltage Systems as a specialty area within electrical contracting. The classification covers the installation, alteration, and repair of systems that use fiber optics or operate at no more than 91 volts.

The classification includes telephone systems, sound systems, cable television systems, closed-circuit video systems, satellite dish antennas, instrumentation and temperature controls, computer networking systems, and landscape lighting.

Because the classification covers several technologies, candidates should develop a broad understanding of low voltage wiring rather than focusing on only one system type. A data network may use different cables and equipment from a sound system, while a temperature-control system may involve another set of components entirely.

Even with different technologies, several common principles remain important: conductor routing, grounding and bonding, proper support, equipment placement, power supplies, pathways, terminations, and compliance with applicable electrical requirements.

The Nevada contractor licensing process also includes requirements beyond the C-2D trade examination. Applicants must complete the applicable application, qualification, examination, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-2D preparation plan should combine highlighted code navigation, telecommunications review, practical low voltage concepts, and practice-oriented study.

Grounding and Bonding should be reviewed using the NEC. Practice finding terms, article locations, and requirements associated with grounding conductors, bonding, equipment grounding, and related connections.

Conductors and Cables should include conductor properties, cable types, support, routing, physical protection, separation, termination, identification, and environmental conditions.

Telephone and Communications Wiring should include pathways, system layouts, terminations, cabling, distribution points, equipment, and basic communications infrastructure.

Computer Networking Systems should be studied for structured cabling, network pathways, cable handling, equipment interfaces, and physical installation practices.

Sound Systems can involve speakers, amplifiers, control equipment, cabling, power supplies, and system distribution. Candidates should understand the installation concepts that support these systems.

Closed-Circuit Video and Cable Television Systems should be reviewed for cabling, equipment, signal distribution, power, pathways, grounding, and system layout.

Instrumentation and Temperature Controls can involve sensors, low voltage wiring, controllers, control equipment, and power supplies. Candidates should understand the purpose of these components and how they work together.

Landscape Lighting should be included in preparation because it falls within Nevada's C-2D scope. Review low voltage lighting equipment, power supplies, wiring, routing, and installation considerations.

Raceways and Boxes should be studied through the NEC. Candidates should understand when physical protection or enclosures are needed and how wiring methods relate to installation conditions.

Power Supplies and Transformers should be reviewed for their role in providing usable voltage to low voltage equipment. Candidates should understand basic voltage transformation, equipment connections, and installation considerations.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Timed NEC lookups can also improve preparation. Choose a set of code questions and track how long it takes to locate the relevant section. Subjects that consistently take too long should receive additional navigation practice.

If grounding lookups are slow, spend additional time with grounding terminology and code organization. If communications topics are difficult, revisit the telecommunications reference. If raceway or conductor questions are challenging, spend more time navigating those NEC subjects.

Mixed review can help candidates connect separate topics. A communications installation may involve cable routing, grounding, equipment, pathways, power supplies, and code requirements at the same time. Studying these subjects together can build a more complete understanding.

How does the Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) exam work?

The Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
