Exam Details

The Nevada Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) trade examination evaluates technical knowledge associated with low voltage electrical and communications systems. Candidates should be prepared to work with electrical concepts and code requirements relevant to systems such as communications wiring, networking, sound, video, controls, fiber optics, landscape lighting, and related limited-energy installations.

The NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 edition is an important part of preparation because candidates need to become comfortable locating applicable electrical requirements. A strong study strategy combines actual trade knowledge with effective code navigation rather than relying on the book to provide every answer from scratch.

Grounding and bonding should receive dedicated review. Low voltage systems may involve equipment grounding, bonding connections, grounding conductors, communications grounding, and relationships with other electrical systems. Candidates should understand both the purpose of these requirements and how to locate them efficiently in the NEC.

Conductors and cables should also be part of regular preparation. Study conductor and cable routing, support, termination, protection, identification, installation conditions, and the electrical requirements affecting different types of low voltage systems.

Communications systems can include telephone, networking, sound, cable television, closed-circuit video, and other related technologies. Each system may use different equipment and wiring, but candidates should recognize common installation principles such as proper pathways, support, separation, grounding, equipment placement, and terminations.

Preparation should also include raceways, boxes, enclosures, power supplies, transformers, and equipment. These subjects help candidates understand how low voltage systems are physically installed and how their components interface with the broader electrical system.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-2D preparation plan should combine low voltage trade knowledge, National Electrical Code navigation, business and law review, and practice-oriented course study.

Grounding and Bonding should be studied through repeated NEC navigation. Candidates should practice distinguishing between grounding, bonding, equipment grounding, and related concepts so they can identify the appropriate code area efficiently.

Conductors and Cables should be reviewed for routing, support, identification, termination, physical protection, and installation conditions. Different low voltage systems may use different cable types, but proper installation practices remain important across the classification.

Telephone and Communications Systems should be reviewed for cabling, pathways, equipment, grounding, connections, distribution, and installation principles.

Computer Networking Systems should include structured cabling, network pathways, cable handling, equipment interfaces, and physical installation considerations.

Sound Systems can involve speakers, amplifiers, control equipment, wiring, power supplies, signal distribution, and equipment connections. Candidates should understand the relationship between these components.

Cable Television and Closed-Circuit Video Systems can involve signal cabling, equipment, pathways, grounding, power, and distribution. These systems should be included as part of broader communications preparation.

Instrumentation and Temperature Controls may use sensors, controllers, low voltage wiring, control equipment, and power supplies. Candidates should understand how these pieces function together within a complete system.

Landscape Lighting should also be part of preparation. Review wiring, power supplies, equipment, routing, and installation principles associated with low voltage lighting systems.

Raceways and Boxes should be practiced through the NEC. Candidates should understand how cables are routed and protected and how physical installation conditions can affect wiring-method requirements.

Power Supplies and Transformers should be reviewed for their role in delivering the required voltage to low voltage systems. Understanding the basic relationship between the source, equipment, and connected load can help with both practical and code-based questions.

How does the Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) exam work?

The Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
