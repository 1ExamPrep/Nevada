## Exam Details

The Nevada Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) trade examination evaluates knowledge related to low voltage electrical systems and the technical responsibilities associated with installing, altering, and repairing those systems.

Candidates should prepare for questions involving **general electrical knowledge**. Even though C-2D work is focused on low voltage systems, a contractor still needs a working understanding of electrical terminology, voltage, current, resistance, power, conductor characteristics, grounding, equipment, and safe installation practices.

**Grounding and bonding** should receive dedicated study time. Candidates should understand grounding terminology, bonding concepts, grounding conductors, equipment grounding, and the role of grounding and bonding in protecting equipment and reducing electrical hazards.

**Conductors and cables** are another central preparation area. Low voltage systems depend on correct cable and conductor selection, routing, support, termination, protection, and identification. Candidates should understand how installation conditions affect wiring methods and how different low voltage systems may require different cabling approaches.

**Communications systems** are particularly relevant to C-2D work. Telephone, networking, sound, video, cable television, closed-circuit video, and related systems can require candidates to understand pathways, cabling, equipment connections, separation, grounding, and installation practices.

**Raceways, boxes, and pathways** should also be reviewed. Candidates should understand when wiring needs physical protection, how cables are routed through buildings, how enclosures and raceways are used, and how electrical-code requirements affect installation.

Preparation should also address **power supplies and equipment**. Many low voltage systems rely on transformers, power supplies, control equipment, communication hardware, or interfaces with other electrical systems. Candidates should understand the basic role of these components and the requirements affecting their installation.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong C-2D preparation strategy combines electrical-code navigation with practical low voltage knowledge. The NEC should be used regularly during practice, while Telecommunications Wiring should be used to strengthen understanding of communication cabling and installation concepts.

**General Low Voltage Knowledge** should include limited-energy circuits, equipment, cabling, voltage characteristics, wiring methods, and system installation concepts. **Instrumentation and Temperature Controls** should be studied as low voltage control systems that may involve sensors, controllers, wiring, power supplies, and equipment interfaces.

**Landscape Lighting** is specifically included within the Nevada C-2D classification. Candidates should recognize the electrical and installation considerations associated with low voltage landscape lighting systems.

**Raceways and Boxes** should be reviewed through the NEC. Understand how conductors and cables are routed and protected, how boxes and enclosures are used, and how wiring methods relate to the installation environment.

**Power Supplies** should be studied for their role in providing appropriate electrical power to low voltage equipment. Candidates should understand basic transformer and power-supply concepts and how the equipment relates to the connected system.

During NEC practice, begin each question by identifying the main topic. Use the index to locate a likely article or section, then read the surrounding requirements carefully. Pay attention to definitions, exceptions, tables, and references to other sections.

## How does the Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) exam work?

The Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
