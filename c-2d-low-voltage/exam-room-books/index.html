Exam Details

The Nevada Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) examination evaluates technical knowledge related to low voltage systems and the electrical requirements that apply to their installation, alteration, and repair.

Candidates should prepare for a combination of practical trade knowledge and code-based questions. The National Electrical Code provides an important reference for locating technical requirements, but candidates should also understand the underlying electrical concepts well enough to recognize what each question is asking.

Grounding and bonding should receive focused preparation. Low voltage systems can involve grounding conductors, bonding requirements, equipment grounding, grounding electrodes, and connections between communications equipment and building electrical systems.

Conductors and cables are another important study area. Candidates should understand conductor routing, protection, support, installation conditions, identification, termination, and the relationship between different cable types and system applications.

Communications systems should also be part of C-2D preparation. Telephone, data, sound, video, and similar systems rely on proper cabling, pathways, equipment placement, grounding, and installation practices.

Raceways, boxes, and pathways may be used to route and protect low voltage wiring. Candidates should be able to recognize when raceways, enclosures, or physical protection are required and where applicable requirements can be found in the NEC.

Equipment and power supplies should be reviewed as well. Low voltage systems often use transformers, power supplies, control devices, communication equipment, and interfaces with other electrical systems. Understanding how these components fit together can make code questions easier to interpret.

State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-2D Low Voltage Systems as a specialty area within electrical contracting. The classification covers the installation, alteration, and repair of systems using fiber optics or operating at no more than 91 volts.

The scope includes telephone systems, sound systems, cable television systems, closed-circuit video systems, satellite dish antennas, instrumentation and temperature controls, computer networking systems, and landscape lighting.

The variety means candidates should prepare across several different technologies. A data-network installation may involve different equipment from a sound system, while temperature controls or landscape lighting may rely on other low voltage components. Even so, all of these systems can involve common electrical principles such as conductor routing, grounding, protection, equipment placement, and proper wiring methods.

Candidates should also understand that the C-2D examination is only one part of the contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable qualification, application, examination, financial, and other requirements before a Nevada contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-2D study plan should combine practical low voltage knowledge with repeated National Electrical Code navigation.

Grounding and Bonding should receive dedicated review. Practice locating NEC requirements related to grounding conductors, bonding, equipment grounding, and grounding connections associated with communications and low voltage systems.

Conductors and Cables should be studied for size, type, routing, protection, support, identification, termination, and installation conditions. Candidates should understand that different systems may use different wiring methods while still being subject to applicable electrical requirements.

Telephone and Communications Systems should be reviewed for cabling, pathways, equipment, connections, and grounding. Candidates should understand how communications systems fit within the overall C-2D classification.

Computer Networking Systems should be studied for structured cabling concepts, pathways, cable routing, equipment interfaces, and physical installation practices.

Sound Systems can involve speakers, amplifiers, control equipment, wiring, power supplies, pathways, and terminations. Candidates should understand the basic installation considerations associated with these systems.

Cable Television and Closed-Circuit Video Systems should be reviewed for cabling, equipment, signal distribution, power requirements, routing, grounding, and system installation.

Instrumentation and Temperature Controls can involve sensors, control wiring, low voltage power supplies, controllers, and connections to building systems. Candidates should understand the basic function of these components and their installation requirements.

Landscape Lighting is also included within Nevada's C-2D scope. Preparation should include basic low voltage lighting concepts, wiring, equipment, transformers or power supplies, and installation considerations.

Raceways and Boxes should be reviewed through the NEC. Candidates should understand how wiring is routed and protected, how enclosures are used, and how physical installation conditions can affect code requirements.

Power Supplies and Transformers should be studied for their role in delivering appropriate voltage to low voltage equipment. Candidates should understand basic electrical relationships and how power equipment connects with the systems it serves.

When practicing with the NEC, begin each question by identifying the central subject. Avoid scanning the book without a search strategy. Use a specific keyword, locate the applicable article, and then read enough surrounding text to understand the full requirement.

Definitions can change the interpretation of a question, so check them when a term appears technical or unusually specific. Likewise, tables and exceptions should not be overlooked because they can affect the final answer.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Timed lookup practice can help candidates build efficiency. Choose a set of low voltage code questions and give yourself a realistic amount of time to find each answer. Record which subjects consistently take the longest.

If grounding questions are slow, spend additional time with NEC grounding terminology and organization. If communications questions are difficult, review the articles associated with communications systems. If conductor or raceway questions create problems, dedicate additional practice to those subjects.

Not every question should require a lengthy code search. Candidates should strengthen their general electrical and low voltage knowledge so that basic questions can be answered directly, leaving more time for code-based questions that require confirmation.

A useful preparation routine is to alternate between knowledge review and code navigation. Study a topic such as grounding, conductors, communications systems, or raceways, then complete several related questions using the NEC. This helps connect technical understanding with the location of the applicable code requirements.

How does the Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) exam work?

The Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
