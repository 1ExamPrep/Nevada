About this Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) package

Prepare for the Nevada Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) examination with an online exam prep course designed around low-voltage electrical knowledge, communications systems, National Electrical Code navigation, and the technical subjects represented on the Nevada trade examination. This online preparation provides an organized way to study conductors, grounding and bonding, sound and communications, intrusion alarm systems, power supplies, calculations, circuit protection, raceways, enclosures, and other C-2D topics.

The Nevada C-2D Low Voltage classification is regulated by the Nevada State Contractors Board. The classification covers the installation, alteration, and repair of systems using fiber optics or operating within the voltage limitations established for the classification. The scope includes telephone systems, sound systems, cable television systems, closed-circuit video systems, satellite dish antennas, instrumentation and temperature controls, computer networking systems, and landscape lighting.

The primary approved code reference for the C-2D trade examination is NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 Edition. Candidates should use the NEC throughout preparation to develop familiarity with its Articles, definitions, tables, conductor requirements, grounding and bonding provisions, circuit protection rules, raceways, enclosures, power supplies, and other electrical requirements relevant to low-voltage work.

Telecommunications Wiring by Clyde Herrick, 2001, 3rd Edition is also used as a study reference. This book is not allowed into the examination room, so candidates should use it during preparation to strengthen practical knowledge of communications cabling, wiring systems, telecommunications terminology, installation methods, and related low-voltage concepts.

The online format allows candidates to break preparation into manageable technical categories instead of treating the C-2D examination as one large subject. Students can focus additional review on calculations, communications, conductors, grounding and bonding, alarms, power supplies, or National Electrical Code navigation depending on their individual strengths and weaknesses.

Online exam preparation is especially useful for developing a combination of trade knowledge and reference-navigation skill. The goal is not to search the NEC for every answer. Candidates should understand common low-voltage concepts well enough to answer many questions directly while using the approved code book efficiently when a specific requirement needs to be located.

Exam Details

The Nevada C-2D Low Voltage trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who have received examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-2D Low Voltage
  • Number of Questions: 35
  • Time Allowed: 1 hour
  • Minimum Passing Score: 25 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The C-2D examination content outline is divided into nine technical areas:

  • Sound and Communications: 5 questions
  • Services, Feeders, Branch Circuits and Overcurrent Protection: 5 questions
  • Conductors: 5 questions
  • General Knowledge and Calculations: 6 questions
  • Grounding and Bonding: 4 questions
  • Power Supplies: 3 questions
  • Intrusion Alarm Systems: 5 questions
  • Utilization and General Use Equipment: 1 question
  • Raceways and Enclosures: 1 question

General Knowledge and Calculations is the largest individual examination category with six questions. Candidates should review fundamental electrical principles, low-voltage terminology, basic calculations, system relationships, and practical electrical concepts. Understanding the reasoning behind calculations is more useful than memorizing isolated formulas without knowing when they apply.

Sound and Communications contains five questions and directly reflects an important part of the C-2D classification. Preparation should include communications cabling, telephone systems, sound systems, computer networking concepts, cable television, and other communications applications within the authorized low-voltage scope.

Services, Feeders, Branch Circuits and Overcurrent Protection also contains five questions. Candidates should understand how electrical power is supplied and protected and should practice locating relevant provisions in the National Electrical Code.

Conductors contains five questions. Study should include conductor properties, identification, applications, installation considerations, and applicable NEC requirements. Candidates should become comfortable using code tables and related provisions when a conductor question requires reference research.

Intrusion Alarm Systems contains five questions. This category deserves dedicated preparation involving alarm-system components, wiring concepts, power, controls, signaling, and system operation.

Grounding and Bonding accounts for four questions. Candidates should understand the purpose of grounding and bonding, recognize the difference between related concepts, and know how to find applicable requirements in the NEC.

Power Supplies contains three questions, while Utilization and General Use Equipment and Raceways and Enclosures each contain one question. Although these sections contain fewer questions, they remain part of the examination and should not be ignored.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-2D Low Voltage trade examination is an open-book test. The approved code reference used for this preparation is:

  • NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 Edition

The 2017 National Electrical Code is an important exam-room tool, but effective use requires practice. Candidates should become familiar with the structure of the NEC before test day instead of attempting to learn the book during the examination.

Useful navigation practice includes working with the NEC index, Article numbers, definitions, tables, exceptions, cross-references, and the organization of individual code sections. Candidates should practice locating requirements involving conductors, grounding and bonding, services, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, raceways, enclosures, and equipment.

The approved reference may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before testing. Permanent tabs are permitted under the testing rules, while temporary tabs such as removable Post-it notes are not permitted. Candidates should prepare their approved reference in accordance with the examination-center requirements.

Telecommunications Wiring, Clyde Herrick, 2001, 3rd Edition is not allowed into the C-2D trade examination.

Because Telecommunications Wiring cannot be used during testing, candidates should use it to build practical understanding before examination day. Study topics can include cabling systems, communications wiring principles, installation practices, terminology, system organization, and other telecommunications concepts relevant to the C-2D trade.

Open-book does not mean unlimited lookup time. Candidates have one hour to complete 35 questions. A student who attempts to research every question can quickly fall behind. Preparation should therefore combine NEC navigation with enough trade knowledge to recognize answers that do not require a code search.

Repeated practice can help candidates decide when to use the code book. A specific code requirement may justify a lookup, while a general trade-knowledge question may be better answered directly. Developing that judgment is an important part of preparing for a timed open-book examination.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates licensing for the C-2D Low Voltage classification. Passing the trade examination is one part of the overall contractor licensing process.

  1. Confirm the C-2D classification. Determine that the Nevada Low Voltage classification matches the type of contracting work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the Trade Qualified Individual. Designate the person who will establish the required experience and technical qualification for the C-2D classification.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare acceptable records demonstrating relevant low-voltage experience in the requested classification.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor license application. Gather the required business, qualifier, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application to the Nevada State Contractors Board. The application must be reviewed before examination eligibility is issued.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow the fingerprint and background procedures required as part of Nevada contractor licensing.
  7. Provide applicable financial information. Nevada evaluates financial responsibility as part of the contractor licensing process.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Once the applicable requirements have been reviewed, the qualified individual can receive authorization to schedule required examinations.
  9. Schedule the C-2D trade exam through PSI. Use the examination eligibility information to arrange testing.
  10. Prepare for the C-2D examination. Review communications, calculations, conductors, grounding and bonding, intrusion alarms, power supplies, circuit protection, raceways, equipment, and NEC navigation.
  11. Complete the Business and Law requirement. Nevada contractor applicants generally must also satisfy the Contractor Management Survey examination unless an applicable waiver applies.
  12. Complete final licensing requirements. Address applicable financial responsibility, bonding, and other Nevada State Contractors Board requirements before license issuance.

Candidates should organize both exam preparation and licensing documentation. Studying for the trade examination does not replace the experience, application, financial, background, or other requirements associated with obtaining a Nevada contractor license.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-2D Low Voltage classification covers the installation, alteration, and repair of qualifying systems that use fiber optics or operate within the voltage limit established for the classification.

The scope includes telephone systems and sound systems, which makes communications knowledge an important part of both practical C-2D work and examination preparation.

Cable television and closed-circuit video systems are also included in the classification. Candidates should understand basic cabling, system organization, conductors, connections, pathways, and related installation concepts.

The classification also covers satellite dish antennas, instrumentation and temperature controls, computer networking systems, and landscape lighting within the authorized low-voltage scope.

For the standard Nevada licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual generally must have a minimum of four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the specific trade category requested.

The four years of qualifying experience must generally have been gained within the 15 years immediately preceding the filing of the application. Applicants should maintain clear experience documentation that describes work relevant to the C-2D classification.

Nevada distinguishes between Management and Trade Qualified Individuals. A single person may serve in both roles when all applicable qualifications are met, or separate individuals may satisfy the management and trade requirements.

The Management Qualified Individual generally satisfies the Nevada Business and Law, or Contractor Management Survey, examination requirement. The Trade Qualified Individual establishes the applicable experience and trade qualification for C-2D.

Applicants must also address financial responsibility, bonding, background requirements, and other licensing conditions. Passing the C-2D trade exam does not by itself create or activate a Nevada contractor license.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-2D study plan should follow the examination content weighting while combining trade knowledge with National Electrical Code practice.

Prioritize General Knowledge and Calculations. Prioritize General Knowledge and Calculations is the largest individual section with six questions. Review low-voltage electrical principles, terminology, calculations, and common system relationships.

Study Sound and Communications. Review telephone, sound, data, computer networking, cable television, communications wiring, and related concepts. Use Telecommunications Wiring to develop knowledge that can be recalled without the book during testing.

Review Services and Overcurrent Protection. Understand services, feeders, branch circuits, and protective devices while practicing the related NEC provisions.

Develop strong conductor knowledge. Review conductor applications, identification, electrical characteristics, and applicable code requirements. Practice using NEC tables where appropriate.

Prepare for Intrusion Alarm Systems. Alarm systems contain five questions, so they deserve focused attention. Review system components, wiring, power, signaling, and practical installation concepts.

Practice Grounding and Bonding. Learn both the technical purpose and the code organization associated with grounding and bonding. This can help when questions combine general knowledge with NEC requirements.

Review Power Supplies. Study how low-voltage systems receive power and the electrical principles associated with supply equipment and connected systems.

Do not ignore smaller categories. Raceways and Enclosures and Utilization and General Use Equipment contain fewer questions, but those points can still contribute to reaching the minimum passing score.

Practice NEC navigation under timed conditions. Choose a code topic, locate the relevant requirement, and track how efficiently you reach the correct section. The objective is purposeful navigation rather than memorizing every code section number.

Use practice-oriented preparation. After completing practice questions, review errors by category. Repeated mistakes in grounding, communications, calculations, conductors, alarms, or overcurrent protection show where additional study may be useful.

Practice exam pacing. With 35 questions and one hour available, candidates have limited time for each question. Timed practice can help build a steady rhythm and reduce the tendency to spend too long on difficult code searches.

How does the Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) exam work?

The Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
