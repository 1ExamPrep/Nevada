## Exam Details

The Nevada C-2D Low Voltage trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who receive examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

- **Exam:** Nevada C-2D Low Voltage
- **Number of Questions:** 35
- **Time Allowed:** 1 hour
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 25 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The C-2D examination content is divided into nine technical areas:

- **Sound and Communications:** 5 questions
- **Services, Feeders, Branch Circuits and Overcurrent Protection:** 5 questions
- **Conductors:** 5 questions
- **General Knowledge and Calculations:** 6 questions
- **Grounding and Bonding:** 4 questions
- **Power Supplies:** 3 questions
- **Intrusion Alarm Systems:** 5 questions
- **Utilization and General Use Equipment:** 1 question
- **Raceways and Enclosures:** 1 question

General Knowledge and Calculations is the largest individual category with six questions. Candidates should develop a dependable understanding of electrical principles, low-voltage terminology, basic calculations, circuit relationships, and practical system concepts.

Sound and Communications, Services and Overcurrent Protection, Conductors, and Intrusion Alarm Systems each contain five questions. Together, these subjects represent a substantial part of the examination. Preparation should include communications cabling, conductor selection, circuit arrangements, overcurrent protection concepts, intrusion alarm components, and the electrical principles that support low-voltage system installation.

Grounding and Bonding contains four questions. Candidates should understand the purpose of grounding and bonding and practice locating applicable requirements in the 2017 National Electrical Code.

Power Supplies contains three questions. Candidates should review power sources, low-voltage supply concepts, equipment requirements, and the relationship between power sources and the systems they serve.

Utilization and General Use Equipment and Raceways and Enclosures each contain one question. These are smaller sections, but they should not be ignored because every question contributes toward the required passing score.

With 35 questions in one hour, candidates need a steady test-taking pace. Strong preparation should combine trade knowledge with code-navigation skill so time is not lost searching the National Electrical Code for information that should already be understood from study.

## State Requirements

The Nevada C-2D Low Voltage classification covers qualifying low-voltage and fiber-optic systems within the scope established for the classification. This can include telephone systems, sound systems, cable television systems, closed-circuit video systems, satellite dish antennas, instrumentation and temperature controls, computer networking systems, and landscape lighting.

Candidates should understand that the C-2D license is a specialty contractor classification. Contractors should operate within the scope authorized by the license and recognize when work may require a different Nevada contractor classification.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual generally must demonstrate at least four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the requested classification.

Qualifying experience should reflect practical familiarity with low-voltage systems, communications wiring, conductors, grounding and bonding, power supplies, intrusion alarm systems, equipment, raceways, and related installation work.

Nevada contractor licensing includes both trade and management qualification requirements. One individual may serve as both the Management Qualified Individual and Trade Qualified Individual when all applicable requirements are satisfied, or separate individuals may fill those roles.

The Trade Qualified Individual establishes the required trade experience and qualification for the C-2D classification. The Management Qualified Individual generally satisfies the Nevada contractor Business and Law requirement unless an applicable waiver is approved.

The contractor licensing process also includes financial responsibility, background requirements, bonding, and a monetary limit associated with the license. Passing the C-2D trade exam is an important step, but it does not by itself complete the Nevada contractor licensing process.

## How does the Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) exam work?

The Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
