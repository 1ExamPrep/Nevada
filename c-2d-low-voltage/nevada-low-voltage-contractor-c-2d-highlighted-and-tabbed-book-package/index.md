## Exam Details

The Nevada Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) trade examination evaluates technical knowledge related to low voltage electrical and communications systems. Candidates should prepare for both practical trade concepts and electrical-code questions involving the installation, alteration, and repair of limited-energy systems.

**Grounding and bonding** should receive careful study. Low voltage systems can involve grounding conductors, bonding requirements, equipment grounding, grounding electrodes, and connections between communications equipment and other electrical systems.

**Conductors and cables** are another major preparation area. Candidates should understand cable types, conductor characteristics, routing, support, physical protection, separation, termination, identification, and installation conditions.

**Communications systems** should be studied broadly. Telephone, computer networking, sound, cable television, closed-circuit video, and related systems can involve different cabling methods and equipment, but they share common installation concepts such as pathways, grounding, support, terminations, and system organization.

**Raceways, boxes, and enclosures** should also be reviewed. Candidates should understand how low voltage cables may be routed or protected, when wiring methods matter, and how electrical-code requirements can affect the physical installation.

**Power supplies and transformers** are also relevant. Many low voltage systems rely on power conversion or dedicated low voltage supplies, so candidates should understand the basic function of these components and how they relate to the connected system.

Preparation should also include the relationship between code requirements and practical installation. Knowing the general trade concept can help candidates determine where to look in the NEC and which terms to use when navigating the book.

## State Requirements

Nevada identifies **C-2D Low Voltage Systems** as a specialty area within electrical contracting. The classification covers the installation, alteration, and repair of systems that use fiber optics or operate at no more than 91 volts.

The classification includes **telephone systems, sound systems, cable television systems, closed-circuit video systems, satellite dish antennas, instrumentation and temperature controls, computer networking systems, and landscape lighting**.

Because the classification covers several technologies, candidates should develop a broad understanding of low voltage wiring rather than focusing on only one system type. Even with different technologies, several common principles remain important: conductor routing, grounding and bonding, proper support, equipment placement, power supplies, pathways, terminations, and compliance with applicable electrical requirements.

## How does the Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) exam work?

The Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
