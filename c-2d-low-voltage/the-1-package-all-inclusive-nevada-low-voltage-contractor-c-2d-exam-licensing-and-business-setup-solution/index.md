## Exam Details

The Nevada C-2D Low Voltage trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who receive examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

- **Exam:** Nevada C-2D Low Voltage
- **Number of Questions:** 35
- **Time Allowed:** 1 hour
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 25 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The C-2D examination is divided into several technical areas that reflect the electrical and communications knowledge needed for low-voltage contracting work.

- **Sound and Communications:** 5 questions
- **Services, Feeders, Branch Circuits and Overcurrent Protection:** 5 questions
- **Conductors:** 5 questions
- **General Knowledge and Calculations:** 6 questions
- **Grounding and Bonding:** 4 questions
- **Power Supplies:** 3 questions
- **Intrusion Alarm Systems:** 5 questions
- **Utilization and General Use Equipment:** 1 question
- **Raceways and Enclosures:** 1 question

General Knowledge and Calculations is the largest individual category with six questions. Candidates should develop a dependable understanding of low-voltage electrical principles, terminology, calculations, system fundamentals, and practical field knowledge.

Sound and Communications, Services and Overcurrent Protection, Conductors, and Intrusion Alarm Systems each contain five questions. Together, these subjects make up a substantial portion of the examination. Preparation should include communications cabling, conductor selection, circuit arrangements, overcurrent protection, intrusion alarm components, and the electrical principles that support low-voltage system installation.

Grounding and Bonding contains four questions. Candidates should understand the purpose of grounding and bonding and practice locating applicable requirements in the 2017 National Electrical Code.

Power Supplies contains three questions. Candidates should review power sources, low-voltage supply concepts, and the way equipment receives and uses electrical power within qualifying systems.

Utilization and General Use Equipment and Raceways and Enclosures each contain one question. These smaller categories should still be reviewed because every question contributes toward the required passing score.

## Open Book Test

The Nevada C-2D Low Voltage trade examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates may use an approved edition of the National Electrical Code during the examination.

The approved exam-room reference included with this package is:

- **NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 Edition**

The National Electrical Code supports preparation involving conductors, grounding and bonding, services, feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, raceways, enclosures, power supplies, equipment, and other electrical requirements that may appear on the C-2D examination.

Candidates should practice navigating the NEC before exam day. Useful study habits include working with the index, Article structure, definitions, tables, numbered sections, exceptions, notes, and cross-references. Open-book testing is most effective when the candidate already understands how the code is organized.

**Telecommunications Wiring, Clyde Herrick, 2001, 3rd Edition** is a study reference and is not allowed into the C-2D examination center.

The book should be used to develop practical knowledge of communications wiring, cabling systems, installation concepts, telecommunications terminology, and related low-voltage trade practices before testing. Since it cannot be used during the exam, candidates should focus on understanding and recall rather than depending on the book for every technical answer.

Open-book testing does not mean that every question should be researched during the exam. With 35 questions and one hour available, candidates benefit from knowing which questions can be answered directly from trade knowledge and which questions are better handled with a targeted NEC lookup.

## State Requirements

The Nevada C-2D Low Voltage classification authorizes qualifying work involving fiber optics and systems that operate within the voltage limitations of the classification. The scope includes telephone systems, sound systems, cable television systems, closed-circuit video systems, satellite dish antennas, instrumentation and temperature controls, computer networking systems, and landscape lighting.

Candidates should understand that the C-2D license is a specialty contractor classification. Contractors should operate within the specific scope authorized by Nevada and avoid performing work that requires a different contractor classification.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual generally must demonstrate at least four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the requested classification.

Qualifying experience should be relevant to the C-2D scope and should demonstrate practical familiarity with low-voltage systems, conductors, communications wiring, system installation, grounding and bonding, power supplies, alarms, raceways, and other related work.

Nevada contractor licensing also uses both management and trade qualification roles. One individual may serve as both the Management Qualified Individual and the Trade Qualified Individual when the applicable requirements are satisfied, or separate people may fill those roles.

The Trade Qualified Individual establishes the required experience and trade qualification. The Management Qualified Individual generally satisfies the Nevada contractor Business and Law requirement unless an applicable waiver applies.

The Nevada contractor licensing process also includes financial responsibility, background requirements, bonding, and a monetary limit associated with the license. These requirements are separate from passing the C-2D trade examination and must be completed before final license issuance.

## How does the Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) exam work?

The Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
