## Exam Details

The Nevada Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) examination evaluates technical knowledge related to low voltage electrical systems and the requirements that apply to their installation, alteration, and repair.

Candidates should prepare for a combination of practical low voltage knowledge and code-based questions. The National Electrical Code is an important reference for locating technical requirements, but understanding the system involved is equally important. A candidate who recognizes whether a question involves grounding, conductors, communications wiring, equipment, or raceways can usually navigate the code more efficiently.

**Grounding and bonding** should receive careful study. Low voltage and communications systems can involve grounding conductors, bonding connections, equipment grounding, grounding electrodes, and interfaces with other electrical systems. Candidates should understand both the purpose of grounding and the way applicable requirements are organized in the NEC.

**Conductors and cables** are another important subject. Preparation should include cable routing, support, physical protection, identification, termination, conductor characteristics, and installation conditions. Candidates should understand that different low voltage systems may use different cable types while still being subject to applicable electrical requirements.

**Communications systems** should be reviewed broadly. Telephone, networking, sound, cable television, closed-circuit video, and related systems may involve different equipment, but many share common concerns involving cabling, pathways, grounding, support, protection, and terminations.

**Raceways, boxes, and enclosures** can also be part of low voltage installations. Candidates should understand when physical protection is required, how cables are routed through structures, and how code requirements affect the installation method.

**Power supplies, transformers, and equipment** should also be studied. Many low voltage systems depend on dedicated power sources, control equipment, transformers, or interfaces with other electrical systems. Understanding the basic function of these components can make code questions easier to interpret.

## State Requirements

Nevada identifies **C-2D Low Voltage Systems** as a specialty area within electrical contracting. The classification covers the installation, alteration, and repair of systems using fiber optics or operating at no more than 91 volts.

The classification includes **telephone systems, sound systems, cable television systems, closed-circuit video systems, satellite dish antennas, instrumentation and temperature controls, computer networking systems, and landscape lighting**.

Because the classification covers multiple low voltage technologies, candidates should prepare across a range of system types. A networking system may use different equipment and cabling from a sound system, while temperature controls or landscape lighting may require another installation approach.

Even with these differences, common electrical principles remain important. Proper conductor routing, grounding and bonding, support, protection, pathways, terminations, equipment placement, and power supplies can affect many types of low voltage installations.

The C-2D trade examination is only one part of the licensing process. Applicants must also complete the applicable qualification, application, examination, financial, and other contractor licensing requirements before a license is issued.

## How does the Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) exam work?

The Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
