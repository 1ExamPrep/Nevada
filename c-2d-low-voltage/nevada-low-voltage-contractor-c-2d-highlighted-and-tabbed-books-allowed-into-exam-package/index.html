Exam Details

The Nevada Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) examination evaluates technical knowledge related to low voltage electrical systems and the requirements that apply to their installation, alteration, and repair.

Candidates should prepare for a combination of practical low voltage knowledge and code-based questions. The National Electrical Code is an important reference for locating technical requirements, but understanding the system involved is equally important. A candidate who recognizes whether a question involves grounding, conductors, communications wiring, equipment, or raceways can usually navigate the code more efficiently.

Grounding and bonding should receive careful study. Low voltage and communications systems can involve grounding conductors, bonding connections, equipment grounding, grounding electrodes, and interfaces with other electrical systems. Candidates should understand both the purpose of grounding and the way applicable requirements are organized in the NEC.

Conductors and cables are another important subject. Preparation should include cable routing, support, physical protection, identification, termination, conductor characteristics, and installation conditions. Candidates should understand that different low voltage systems may use different cable types while still being subject to applicable electrical requirements.

Communications systems should be reviewed broadly. Telephone, networking, sound, cable television, closed-circuit video, and related systems may involve different equipment, but many share common concerns involving cabling, pathways, grounding, support, protection, and terminations.

Raceways, boxes, and enclosures can also be part of low voltage installations. Candidates should understand when physical protection is required, how cables are routed through structures, and how code requirements affect the installation method.

Power supplies, transformers, and equipment should also be studied. Many low voltage systems depend on dedicated power sources, control equipment, transformers, or interfaces with other electrical systems. Understanding the basic function of these components can make code questions easier to interpret.

For example, a grounding question should first be classified by topic. Determine whether the issue involves bonding, equipment grounding, grounding electrodes, or another grounding concept. That approach is faster than opening the code without a search strategy.

Candidates should practice with the index, article organization, definitions, tables, and cross-references. Repeated use of these navigation tools can help build familiarity with the NEC and make code lookups more efficient.

Definitions deserve particular attention because electrical terms can have specific meanings within the code. Tables and exceptions are also important, since a requirement may depend on conductor size, equipment rating, installation condition, or another technical detail.

State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-2D Low Voltage Systems as a specialty area within electrical contracting. The classification covers the installation, alteration, and repair of systems using fiber optics or operating at no more than 91 volts.

The classification includes telephone systems, sound systems, cable television systems, closed-circuit video systems, satellite dish antennas, instrumentation and temperature controls, computer networking systems, and landscape lighting.

Because the classification covers multiple low voltage technologies, candidates should prepare across a range of system types. A networking system may use different equipment and cabling from a sound system, while temperature controls or landscape lighting may require another installation approach.

Even with these differences, common electrical principles remain important. Proper conductor routing, grounding and bonding, support, protection, pathways, terminations, equipment placement, and power supplies can affect many types of low voltage installations.

The C-2D trade examination is only one part of the licensing process. Applicants must also complete the applicable qualification, application, examination, financial, and other contractor licensing requirements before a license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-2D preparation plan should combine practical low voltage knowledge with repeated National Electrical Code navigation using the highlighted and tabbed reference.

Grounding and Bonding should be studied regularly. Practice locating grounding terms, equipment grounding requirements, bonding provisions, grounding conductors, and related code sections.

Conductors and Cables should be reviewed for type, routing, protection, support, identification, termination, and installation conditions. Candidates should also understand how conductor requirements can vary based on system type and environment.

Telephone and Communications Systems should be studied for pathways, cables, equipment, grounding, connections, and basic system organization. Candidates should be able to identify the code area associated with communications questions.

Computer Networking Systems should be reviewed for structured cabling, network pathways, physical cable installation, equipment interfaces, and system organization.

Sound Systems can involve speakers, amplifiers, wiring, control equipment, power supplies, and signal distribution. Candidates should understand the basic installation concepts that apply to these systems.

Closed-Circuit Video and Cable Television Systems should be reviewed for cabling, equipment, signal distribution, grounding, pathways, and power requirements.

Instrumentation and Temperature Controls can involve sensors, controllers, low voltage wiring, equipment interfaces, and power supplies. Candidates should understand the basic relationship between these components.

Landscape Lighting is included within the Nevada C-2D classification. Review low voltage lighting wiring, equipment, power supplies, routing, and installation considerations.

Raceways and Boxes should be practiced through NEC navigation. Candidates should know how wiring methods, physical protection, and enclosures relate to low voltage installations.

Power Supplies and Transformers should be studied for their role in providing appropriate voltage to low voltage equipment. Candidates should understand basic voltage relationships and how these components connect to the systems they serve.

The tabs can help make practice more organized. Candidates can repeatedly use the same navigation points until major parts of the code become familiar. Over time, this can reduce the amount of searching needed to locate frequently reviewed subjects.

Timed lookup practice is especially useful. Choose a group of low voltage code questions and track how long it takes to identify and locate each applicable requirement. Topics that consistently take too long should receive additional study.

If grounding questions are slow, spend additional time with grounding terminology and code organization. If communications questions are difficult, practice locating the appropriate communications articles. If raceway or conductor questions cause problems, focus on those code areas in separate sessions.

Mixed practice can help candidates connect different subjects. A single low voltage installation may involve conductors, grounding, raceways, boxes, equipment, power supplies, and communications requirements. Studying those elements together can provide a more practical understanding of how the code applies in the field.

Candidates should also build enough trade knowledge to avoid looking up every simple concept. The NEC is most effective as a reference when the candidate already understands the issue and knows approximately where the answer should be located.

How does the Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) exam work?

The Low Voltage Contractor (C-2D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
