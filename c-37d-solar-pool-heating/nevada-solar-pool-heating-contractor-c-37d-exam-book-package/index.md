## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-37D preparation plan should begin with **System Design** because it represents 15 of the 45 examination questions.

Candidates should understand how solar collectors, piping, pumps, heat exchangers, valves, controls, and pool or spa circulation relationships work together as a complete heating system.

Follow the path of heat through the system. Understand how solar energy is absorbed by the collectors, transferred into the circulating medium, moved through piping, and ultimately delivered to the swimming pool or spa.

**Piping** represents 10 questions and should receive significant study attention. Review pipe materials, fittings, joining methods, supports, routing, insulation, valves, and connections.

The **Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018** should be used regularly during preparation. Practice locating requirements by topic rather than relying on remembered page numbers.

For example, choose a question involving pipe material, identify the key term, use the index, and locate the appropriate UPC section. Repeat the same process with fittings, supports, valves, and connections.

**Definitions** can be important because technical plumbing terms may have specific code meanings. Understanding those definitions can help determine which provision applies.

**Tables** should receive regular attention. Important requirements involving materials, supports, sizing, or installation conditions may appear in table form.

**Exceptions** can modify a general requirement. Candidates should read the surrounding code language carefully before choosing an answer.

**Cross-references** can direct the reader to another section containing additional requirements. Practice following them instead of assuming the first section contains the complete answer.

**Collectors** accounts for 7 questions. Candidates should understand collector purpose, installation concepts, flow relationships, mounting, orientation, and how collector arrays connect to the piping system.

Review how collector configuration can affect circulation and how solar energy is transferred from the collector surface into the working fluid.

**Pumps and Storage Systems** accounts for 5 questions. In pool heating applications, candidates should understand pump relationships, circulation, flow, and the movement of heated water through the system.

Review how proper circulation affects the ability of the collectors and heat exchanger to transfer energy efficiently.

**System Valves and Controls** accounts for 3 questions. Review shutoff valves, check valves, balancing devices, bypass arrangements, temperature controls, differential controls, and other components that regulate operation.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

**Safety** accounts for 5 questions. Use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 to prepare for hazards associated with roof access, ladders, elevated work, tools, electrical components, material handling, personal protective equipment, and other construction activities.

Because OSHA is not allowed into the examination room, candidates should build retained safety knowledge during preparation.

The **Solar Water and Pool Heating Design and Installation Manual** is especially useful for connecting multiple exam topics. Use it to study collector layouts, circulation, piping configurations, heat exchangers, valves, controls, and overall solar pool heating design.

Because this manual is not allowed into the examination room, active recall can help strengthen retention. Review a system diagram, close the manual, and explain the flow path and purpose of each major component from memory.

**Heat transfer** is central to solar pool heating. Candidates should understand how solar radiation is converted into thermal energy at the collector and how that heat is then transferred to pool or spa water.

**System flow** should also receive attention. Proper circulation through collectors and heat exchangers affects system performance, making flow relationships an important practical concept.

**Heat exchangers** are specifically part of the C-37D scope. Candidates should understand their purpose and how they allow thermal energy to move between different fluid circuits when used in a system.

**Freeze protection** can be relevant because collectors and piping may be exposed to outdoor temperatures. Candidates should understand general methods used to protect solar thermal components from damaging environmental conditions.

**Insulation** is important because unwanted heat loss can reduce system performance. Candidates should understand why solar thermal piping is insulated and how outdoor exposure can affect insulation considerations.

**Thermal expansion** should also be considered in piping systems exposed to elevated temperatures. Materials, supports, and layout can affect how movement is accommodated.

A useful study schedule should follow the examination outline. Spend the largest blocks of time on System Design and Piping, followed by Collectors, Pumps and Storage Systems, Safety, and System Valves and Controls.

Once individual topics become familiar, move into mixed-question practice. The actual examination can shift quickly from a piping requirement to a system-design question, collector concept, pump issue, control question, or safety topic.

## How does the Solar Pool Heating Contractor (C-37D) exam work?

The Solar Pool Heating Contractor (C-37D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
