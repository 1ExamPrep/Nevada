## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-37D preparation plan should begin with **System Design** because it represents 15 of the 45 examination questions.

Candidates should understand how solar collectors, piping, pumps, heat exchangers, valves, and controls work together as a complete solar pool heating system rather than memorizing each component separately.

Follow the path of heat through the system. Understand how solar energy is absorbed by the collectors, transferred into the circulating medium, moved through piping, and ultimately delivered to the swimming pool or spa.

**Piping** represents 10 questions and is one of the strongest areas for direct Uniform Plumbing Code preparation. Candidates should review pipe materials, fittings, joining methods, supports, routing, insulation, valves, and connections.

The **Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018** should be used throughout preparation rather than only during the final days before the examination. Repeated use helps make the organization of the code more familiar.

Practice locating code requirements by topic. Start with a question involving pipe material, identify the key term, use the index, and locate the appropriate section. Repeat the process with fittings, supports, valves, and connections.

**Definitions** can be important because technical plumbing terms may have specific code meanings. Understanding those definitions can help determine which requirement applies.

**Tables** should receive regular attention. Important information involving materials, supports, sizing, or installation conditions may appear in table form rather than narrative text.

**Exceptions** can modify a general requirement. Candidates should read the surrounding code language carefully so they do not overlook conditions that change how a rule applies.

**Cross-references** may direct the reader to another section containing additional requirements. Practice following those references instead of assuming the first section contains the complete answer.

**Collectors** accounts for 7 questions. Candidates should understand collector purpose, layout, mounting concepts, orientation, circulation, flow relationships, and how collector arrays connect with the rest of the solar pool heating system.

Review how collector arrangement can affect system flow and how solar energy is transferred from the collector surface into the working fluid.

**Pumps and Storage Systems** accounts for 5 questions. In solar pool heating applications, candidates should understand pump relationships, circulation, and how heated water moves through the system.

Review how proper circulation affects the ability of collectors and heat exchangers to transfer thermal energy efficiently.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

**System Valves and Controls** accounts for 3 questions. Candidates should review shutoff valves, check valves, balancing devices, bypass arrangements, temperature controls, differential controls, and other components that regulate operation.

**Safety** accounts for 5 questions. Candidates should prepare for practical hazards involving roof access, ladders, elevated work, piping tools, hot surfaces, electrical control components, lifting, and other construction activities.

Because this package contains only the exam-room-approved Uniform Plumbing Code, candidates should make sure their broader study plan also covers solar pool heating system design, collector layout, pumps, heat exchangers, valves, controls, and safety through technical study resources and trade experience.

**Heat transfer** is central to solar pool heating. Candidates should understand how solar radiation is converted into thermal energy at the collector and how that energy is transferred into pool or spa water.

**System flow** should also receive attention. Proper circulation through collectors and heat exchangers affects performance, making balanced flow an important practical concept.

**Heat exchangers** are specifically part of the C-37D scope. Candidates should understand their purpose and how they can transfer thermal energy between different fluid circuits when used in a system.

**Freeze protection** can be relevant because collectors and piping may be exposed to outdoor temperatures. Candidates should understand general approaches used to protect solar thermal components from damaging environmental conditions.

**Insulation** is important because unwanted heat loss can reduce system performance. Candidates should understand why solar thermal piping is insulated and how outdoor exposure can affect insulation considerations.

**Thermal expansion** should also be considered in piping systems exposed to elevated temperatures. Materials, supports, and layout all influence how pipe movement is accommodated.

A useful study schedule should follow the examination outline. Spend the largest blocks of time on System Design and Piping, followed by Collectors, Pumps and Storage Systems, Safety, and System Valves and Controls.

Once individual topics become familiar, move into mixed-question practice. The actual examination can shift quickly from a piping requirement to a system-design question, collector concept, pump issue, control question, or safety topic.

Timed UPC lookup drills can improve open-book performance. Choose a plumbing topic and practice locating the applicable section within a limited amount of time.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

When a practice question is missed, identify the reason. The issue may involve weak trade knowledge, unfamiliar terminology, slow code navigation, or difficulty understanding how system components interact.

Correcting the underlying weakness is more valuable than memorizing a single answer. This approach builds stronger preparation for unfamiliar questions on test day.

Time management should also be part of preparation. Answer familiar questions efficiently, use the UPC for targeted lookups, mark difficult questions, and return to them after completing items that can be answered more quickly.

## How does the Solar Pool Heating Contractor (C-37D) exam work?

The Solar Pool Heating Contractor (C-37D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
