Exam Details

The Nevada A-12 Excavating, Grading, Trenching and Surfacing Contractor trade examination contains 60 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the examination, and the minimum passing score is 42 correct answers.

The examination is organized into the following subject areas:

  • Earthwork Construction: 18 questions.
  • Tools and Equipment: 12 questions.
  • Paving and Surfacing Procedures: 20 questions.
  • Safety: 7 questions.
  • Nevada One-Call: 3 questions.

Paving and Surfacing Procedures is the largest portion of the exam, followed by Earthwork Construction and Tools and Equipment. Candidates should still prepare for every subject because safety and Nevada One-Call questions can have a meaningful impact on the final score.

With 60 questions and two hours available, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes per question. Since the examination is closed book, there is no opportunity to search reference material during testing. Candidates should practice recognizing the correct construction principle, equipment application, safety procedure, or paving method quickly and accurately.

The A-12 classification includes all work authorized under the A-7 Excavating and Grading classification, along with the mixing, fabrication, and placement of pavement and surfaces consisting of graded mineral aggregates, asphalt, or concrete products. Candidates should therefore prepare for both earthwork and surfacing knowledge.

Closed Book Test

The Nevada A-12 Excavating, Grading, Trenching and Surfacing examination is a closed-book test. The books included in this package are intended for study and preparation before the examination and are not allowed inside the examination center.

Closed-book testing requires a different study approach than an open-book contractor exam. Instead of focusing on where a rule appears in a reference, candidates should focus on understanding the rule, recognizing when it applies, and recalling the correct construction practice without consulting a book.

After studying a chapter or subject area, close the book and answer practice questions from memory. Then return to the reference to review missed concepts. This type of active recall is especially useful for earthwork terminology, equipment applications, paving procedures, concrete practices, OSHA safety requirements, and underground utility responsibilities.

Scenario-based study can also be effective. Consider what equipment would be appropriate for a particular excavation, how a trench should be protected, how material should be compacted, what steps are involved in asphalt placement, or what responsibilities apply before excavation begins near underground facilities.

Because candidates cannot consult these references during the exam, repeated review should continue until major trade concepts can be recognized and applied without book assistance.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada A-12 trade examination is one component of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Candidates must satisfy the applicable licensing requirements in addition to completing the trade exam.

  1. Confirm the correct classification. Determine that the A-12 Excavating, Grading, Trenching and Surfacing classification matches the work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the trade qualifying individual. The business must designate an individual who meets the experience requirements for the requested classification.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare experience records showing applicable work in excavation, grading, trenching, surfacing, paving, earthwork, or closely related construction activities.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Provide the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the application and experience documentation.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. Examination scheduling can proceed after the applicant receives authorization to test.
  7. Prepare for the A-12 trade examination. Study earthwork, paving, surfacing, equipment, safety, and Nevada One-Call subjects using the references in this package.
  8. Complete required examinations. The qualifying individual generally must pass the applicable trade examination and Business and Law examination unless an approved waiver applies.
  9. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Financial responsibility, bonding, and other applicable requirements must be satisfied before the contractor license is issued.

Because the A-12 examination is closed book, candidates benefit from beginning their study process early. The reference list covers a wide range of heavy civil construction topics, and consistent preparation makes it easier to build understanding across multiple subjects.

State Requirements

The Nevada A-12 Excavating, Grading, Trenching and Surfacing classification is part of the state's general engineering contractor classifications.

The A-12 scope includes all work authorized under the A-7 Excavating and Grading classification. That includes digging, moving, and placing earth material through cutting, filling, excavating, grading, backfilling, trenching, and similar operations. The A-12 classification additionally includes the mixing, fabrication, and placement of pavement and surfaces consisting of graded mineral aggregates, asphalt, or concrete products.

The trade qualifying individual generally must have at least four full years of qualifying experience within the 15 years immediately before the contractor license application is filed. The experience must be in the specific classification requested and may be established through qualifying work as a journeyman, supervising employee, or contractor.

Accepted education or training may be used toward a portion of the experience requirement under Nevada licensing rules. Applicants must also meet applicable financial responsibility and bonding requirements before a contractor license is issued.

The qualifying individual generally must pass the Nevada Business and Law examination and the applicable A-12 trade examination unless an authorized waiver applies. Examination scheduling takes place after the application is submitted, experience requirements are reviewed, and examination eligibility is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

The A-12 exam content outline provides a useful framework for organizing study time. Candidates should divide preparation among Earthwork Construction, Tools and Equipment, Paving and Surfacing Procedures, Safety, and Nevada One-Call.

Earthwork Construction accounts for 18 questions. Study excavation methods, grading, cut-and-fill operations, backfilling, compaction, soil handling, site preparation, drainage considerations, trenching, and material movement. Candidates should understand how soil conditions and project requirements influence the methods used in the field.

Tools and Equipment accounts for 12 questions. Review excavators, loaders, bulldozers, graders, scrapers, compactors, trucks, paving machines, rollers, and other equipment commonly used in heavy construction. Understand what each machine is designed to do and how site conditions, production requirements, and haul distances influence equipment selection.

Paving and Surfacing Procedures accounts for 20 questions, making it the largest section of the exam. Candidates should review asphalt paving, aggregate surfaces, concrete surfaces, base preparation, material placement, compaction, finishing, quality control concepts, and the equipment used to create finished pavement.

The Hot Mix Asphalt Paving Handbook should be used to develop a practical understanding of asphalt paving procedures. Study material delivery, placement, paving equipment, compaction, rolling patterns, temperature-related considerations, joints, and factors that influence finished pavement quality.

The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction should be used for concrete-related preparation. Review concrete materials, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, construction practices, and quality considerations relevant to concrete pavement and surfaces.

Safety accounts for seven questions. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 is particularly important for excavation and trenching contractors because earthwork can expose employees to cave-ins, falling materials, heavy equipment, hazardous access conditions, and underground utilities.

Study trench protective systems, access and egress concepts, spoil placement, equipment hazards, inspections, personal protective equipment, and safe operating practices. Since the book cannot enter the examination, focus on understanding how safety rules apply in practical scenarios.

Nevada One-Call accounts for three questions. Although this is the smallest section, candidates should not ignore it. Excavation contractors have important responsibilities involving underground facility notification and damage prevention.

Study the Suggested Marking Guidelines and Nevada One Call Law so you understand the basic responsibilities associated with requesting utility locations, recognizing markings, protecting underground facilities, and coordinating excavation activities around known utilities.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Practice questions should be incorporated throughout preparation. After reviewing a topic, answer questions without opening the book. Record the subjects you miss and return to those areas for additional study.

Equipment questions are often easier when learned through application. Instead of simply memorizing equipment names, connect each machine to the type of job it performs. Think about what would be used to excavate material, push soil, fine-grade a surface, transport material, compact a base, or place asphalt.

Paving questions should also be studied as a process. Review how the underlying surface is prepared, how material is delivered and placed, how compaction is performed, and how quality problems can develop when one part of the sequence is incorrect.

Earthwork calculations and quantity concepts should be practiced regularly. Candidates should be comfortable with common measurements, volume concepts, slopes, grades, production considerations, and other mathematical applications used in excavation and grading work.

Because the test provides approximately two minutes per question on average, timed practice can help candidates improve decision-making. Work through sets of questions without reference books to reproduce the closed-book testing environment.

How does the Excavating, Grading, Trenching and Surfacing Contractor (A-12) exam work?

The Excavating, Grading, Trenching and Surfacing Contractor (A-12) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
