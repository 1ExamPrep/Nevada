## State Requirements

The Nevada A-12 Excavating, Grading, Trenching and Surfacing classification is part of the state's general engineering contractor classifications.

The A-12 scope includes all work authorized under the A-7 Excavating and Grading classification. That includes digging, moving, and placing earth material through cutting, filling, excavating, grading, backfilling, trenching, and similar operations. The A-12 classification additionally includes the mixing, fabrication, and placement of pavement and surfaces consisting of graded mineral aggregates, asphalt, or concrete products.

The trade qualifying individual generally must have at least **four full years of qualifying experience within the 15 years immediately before the contractor license application is filed**. The experience must be in the specific classification requested and may be established through qualifying work as a journeyman, supervising employee, or contractor.

Accepted education or training may be used toward a portion of the experience requirement under Nevada licensing rules. Applicants must also meet applicable financial responsibility and bonding requirements before a contractor license is issued.

The qualifying individual generally must pass the Nevada Business and Law examination and the applicable A-12 trade examination unless an authorized waiver applies. Examination scheduling takes place after the application is submitted, experience requirements are reviewed, and examination eligibility is issued.

## Test Information and Study Materials

The A-12 exam content outline provides a useful framework for organizing study time. Candidates should divide preparation among Earthwork Construction, Tools and Equipment, Paving and Surfacing Procedures, Safety, and Nevada One-Call.

**Earthwork Construction** accounts for 18 questions. Study excavation methods, grading, cut-and-fill operations, backfilling, compaction, soil handling, site preparation, drainage considerations, trenching, and material movement. Candidates should understand how soil conditions and project requirements influence the methods used in the field.

**Tools and Equipment** accounts for 12 questions. Review excavators, loaders, bulldozers, graders, scrapers, compactors, trucks, paving machines, rollers, and other equipment commonly used in heavy construction. Understand what each machine is designed to do and how site conditions, production requirements, and haul distances influence equipment selection.

**Paving and Surfacing Procedures** accounts for 20 questions, making it the largest section of the exam. Candidates should review asphalt paving, aggregate surfaces, concrete surfaces, base preparation, material placement, compaction, finishing, quality control concepts, and the equipment used to create finished pavement.

The Hot Mix Asphalt Paving Handbook should be used to develop a practical understanding of asphalt paving procedures. Study material delivery, placement, paving equipment, compaction, rolling patterns, temperature-related considerations, joints, and factors that influence finished pavement quality.

The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction should be used for concrete-related preparation. Review concrete materials, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, construction practices, and quality considerations relevant to concrete pavement and surfaces.

**Safety** accounts for seven questions. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 is particularly important for excavation and trenching contractors because earthwork can expose employees to cave-ins, falling materials, heavy equipment, hazardous access conditions, and underground utilities.

Study trench protective systems, access and egress concepts, spoil placement, equipment hazards, inspections, personal protective equipment, and safe operating practices. Since the book cannot enter the examination, focus on understanding how safety rules apply in practical scenarios.

**Nevada One-Call** accounts for three questions. Although this is the smallest section, candidates should not ignore it. Excavation contractors have important responsibilities involving underground facility notification and damage prevention.

Study the Suggested Marking Guidelines and Nevada One Call Law so you understand the basic responsibilities associated with requesting utility locations, recognizing markings, protecting underground facilities, and coordinating excavation activities around known utilities.

## How does the Excavating, Grading, Trenching and Surfacing Contractor (A-12) exam work?

The Excavating, Grading, Trenching and Surfacing Contractor (A-12) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
