Exam Details

The Nevada C-15 Roofing and Siding Contractor examination contains 60 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the exam and must answer 45 questions correctly to pass.

The examination covers the following major subject areas:

  • Safety
  • Roofing Substrates
  • Steep Roofing
  • Roofing Repairs
  • Elastomeric Roofing
  • Built-up Bituminous Roofing
  • Sheet Metal and Flashing
  • Siding
  • Photovoltaic Systems

Steep Roofing is a major part of the exam. Candidates should understand steep-slope roof coverings, underlayments, fastening, roof decks, slopes, valleys, flashings, edges, drainage, and common installation practices.

Roofing Substrates should include sheathing, structural support, surface preparation, moisture conditions, attachment, deterioration, and compatibility between the substrate and the roof system installed above it.

Siding is an important subject unique to the broader C-15 classification. Candidates should understand siding materials, exterior wall weatherproofing, fastening, soffits, expansion and contraction, flashing, openings, transitions, and the relationship between siding and the building envelope.

Elastomeric Roofing and Built-up Bituminous Roofing require candidates to understand low-slope system components, materials, membranes, surfacing, seams, attachment, flashing, penetrations, drainage, and repair conditions.

Safety, roofing repairs, sheet metal and flashing, and photovoltaic-related subjects should also be included in a balanced study plan because the C-15 examination moves across multiple roofing and siding disciplines.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-15 Roofing and Siding as a specialty contractor classification. The classification encompasses roofing and siding work and includes the work authorized under its applicable subclassifications.

The roofing portion includes installation, application, alteration, and repair of watertight and weatherproof materials used on roofs. Roofing systems can include a variety of coverings and materials used to protect buildings from weather.

The siding portion covers installation, alteration, and repair of siding used to make exterior walls watertight and weatherproof. Candidates should understand how siding integrates with sheathing, openings, soffits, flashing, trim, and other exterior wall components.

The broader C-15 classification also encompasses related insulation and waterproofing work authorized under its subclassifications. Candidates should therefore understand the building envelope as a complete weather-protection system rather than treating roofs and exterior walls as unrelated components.

The C-15 trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong C-15 preparation plan should begin with Steep Roofing. Review roof coverings, underlayments, fastening, roof decks, valleys, flashing, slopes, penetrations, drainage, and roof edges.

Roofing Substrates should include sheathing, structural support, moisture, deterioration, attachment, surface preparation, and compatibility with the finished roofing system.

Siding should receive dedicated study time. Candidates should understand siding materials, soffits, fastening, expansion and contraction, openings, flashing, exterior wall weatherproofing, and moisture-management concepts.

The highlighted and tabbed ASTM D 4756 reference can help candidates study PVC siding and soffit installation practices. Use it to reinforce technical knowledge before transitioning to practice without the book.

Built-up Bituminous Roofing should include low-slope system components, bituminous materials, surfacing, flashing, attachment, and repair concepts.

Elastomeric Roofing should include membrane materials, seams, penetrations, flashing, roof edges, drainage, attachment, and repair conditions.

Metal Panel and SPF Roofing should be reviewed as distinct system types. Candidates should understand their materials, installation methods, joints, coatings, insulation relationships, and maintenance considerations.

Roofing Repairs should be studied as a diagnostic subject. Candidates should understand common leak sources, deteriorated roofing materials, failed flashing, membrane damage, substrate issues, and how different symptoms can point to different causes.

Sheet Metal and Flashing supports both roofing and siding. Review valleys, roof-to-wall intersections, penetrations, windows, doors, curbs, edges, and transitions where water must be directed away from vulnerable building-envelope areas.

Safety should include fall hazards, access, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, tools, and material handling. Because OSHA is not available during the trade exam, candidates should build retained knowledge rather than relying on last-minute lookup.

The International Building Code, 2018 should be used throughout preparation. Practice navigating to roofing, exterior-wall, construction-material, and structural provisions using the table of contents and index.

The highlighted and tabbed IBC can support efficient review, but candidates should still learn why specific sections apply. The goal is to understand the organization of the code rather than simply memorizing tab locations.

Definitions should receive regular attention because building-code terminology can affect how roofing and siding questions are interpreted. Review key terms and their relationship to specific code requirements.

When using the IBC, read enough surrounding material to understand the complete requirement. Exceptions, tables, subsections, and cross-references may affect how a provision applies.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Roofing Construction and Estimating can support preparation involving roof geometry, measurements, material quantities, and estimating. Candidates should become comfortable with practical relationships among roof size, slope, materials, and quantities.

Carpentry and Building Construction supports knowledge of roof framing, wall construction, sheathing, structural components, and substrates. These topics help candidates understand the building systems that roofing and siding attach to.

The photovoltaic guideline can help candidates understand how rooftop photovoltaic components interact with roof coverings, flashing, weatherproofing, and roof-system performance.

Because most references in this package are not allowed into the exam, gradually move from book-supported study to active recall. Review a topic, close the book, and answer practice questions without immediately looking for the answer.

For the IBC, use timed code-navigation drills instead. Choose a roofing or siding topic, identify the likely section, find the applicable provision, and repeat until navigation becomes more efficient.

Mixed practice should combine roofing and siding subjects. The C-15 exam can move quickly from steep roofing to siding, safety, membranes, flashing, estimating, substrates, or photovoltaic topics.

Time management should be included in every practice session. With 60 questions in two hours, candidates should avoid spending too long on one difficult lookup while easier questions remain unanswered.

A balanced preparation strategy combines technical study, active recall, timed IBC navigation, and mixed practice questions.

How does the Roofing and Siding Contractor (C-15) exam work?

The Roofing and Siding Contractor (C-15) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
