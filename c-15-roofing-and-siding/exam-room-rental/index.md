## State Requirements

Nevada recognizes **C-15 Roofing and Siding** as a specialty contractor classification that encompasses roofing and siding work and work authorized under its applicable subclassifications.

The roofing portion includes the installation, application, alteration, and repair of watertight and weatherproof materials used on roofs. Candidates should understand how roof coverings, substrates, flashing, drainage, and other components work together to protect the structure from weather.

The siding portion includes installation, alteration, and repair of siding used to make exterior walls watertight and weatherproof. Candidates should understand the relationships among siding, sheathing, soffits, trim, openings, flashing, and moisture-management components.

The broader C-15 classification also encompasses related insulation and waterproofing work authorized under its subclassifications. For exam preparation, it is useful to approach roofing and siding as connected parts of a building-envelope system rather than unrelated trades.

The C-15 trade examination is one component of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy the applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-15 preparation strategy combines trade knowledge with efficient reference navigation. Because the exam covers both roofing and siding, candidates should build a study schedule that gives attention to multiple building-envelope systems.

Start with **Steep Roofing**. Review common roof-covering concepts, underlayments, fastening, roof slopes, valleys, edges, penetrations, flashing, drainage, and relationships among roofing components.

For **Roofing Substrates**, review sheathing, deck preparation, structural support, deterioration, moisture conditions, attachment, and compatibility between the substrate and the final roof system.

**Siding** deserves a separate preparation block. Review exterior wall coverings, fastening, openings, soffits, flashing, transitions, weather resistance, and moisture management. Pay attention to how wall systems connect with roofs, windows, doors, and other exterior components.

**Built-up Bituminous Roofing** and **Elastomeric Roofing** require an understanding of low-slope systems, membranes, surfacing, seams, flashing, attachment, penetrations, drainage, and repair conditions.

**Roofing Repairs** should be approached from a troubleshooting perspective. Candidates should understand common leak sources, damaged roof coverings, failed flashing, membrane deterioration, substrate conditions, and how to identify the likely source of a roofing problem.

**Sheet Metal and Flashing** connects multiple exam subjects. Review valleys, penetrations, roof-to-wall intersections, curbs, edges, openings, and other locations where water must be directed away from vulnerable areas.

**Photovoltaic Systems** should be reviewed from the roofing perspective. Candidates should understand how rooftop photovoltaic components can affect roof coverings, flashing, waterproofing, and the overall roof assembly.

**Safety** should remain part of regular preparation. Roofing and siding work can involve fall hazards, ladders, scaffolds, elevated work, tools, personal protective equipment, and material handling.

The **International Building Code, 2018** should be used throughout study rather than introduced only immediately before the examination. Regular use helps candidates understand where different code subjects are located and how sections relate to one another.

The highlighted and tabbed format can make review more organized. However, candidates should still learn the chapter structure, table of contents, index, definitions, tables, exceptions, and cross-references so they understand where to search when a question uses unfamiliar wording.

Definitions are especially useful in code preparation because an exam question can depend on the precise meaning of a building-code term. When a term is unfamiliar, locating the applicable definition can help identify the correct code path.

## How does the Roofing and Siding Contractor (C-15) exam work?

The Roofing and Siding Contractor (C-15) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
