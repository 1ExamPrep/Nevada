## State Requirements

Nevada recognizes **C-15 Roofing and Siding** as a specialty contractor classification. The classification covers work associated with roofing and siding and encompasses the work authorized under its applicable subclassifications.

The roofing portion covers the installation, application, alteration, and repair of watertight and weatherproof materials used on roofs. Roofing materials can include asphalt, pitch, tar, felt, flax, shakes, shingles, aluminum, tile, slate, urethane, and photovoltaic roof tiles intended to serve in place of other roofing materials.

The siding portion covers the installation, alteration, and repair of siding used to make exterior walls watertight and weatherproof. This can involve common siding materials and associated exterior wall components within the authorized scope.

The broader C-15 classification also encompasses related insulation and waterproofing work authorized under its subclassifications. Candidates should therefore understand how roofing, siding, flashing, moisture control, substrates, and exterior weather protection work together as part of the building envelope.

The C-15 trade examination is one part of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-15 preparation plan should begin with **Steep Roofing**. Review roof coverings, underlayments, roof decks, slopes, fastening, valleys, flashing, penetrations, drainage, and roof edges.

**Roofing Substrates** should include sheathing, structural support, deterioration, moisture, preparation, attachment, and compatibility with the roof system installed above.

**Siding** should receive dedicated study time. Candidates should understand the relationship between siding, soffits, openings, flashing, exterior wall surfaces, attachment, weatherproofing, and moisture management.

The **International Building Code, 2018** should be used to review exterior wall provisions as well as roofing sections. Candidates should become familiar with how the code addresses roof assemblies, exterior wall construction, materials, structural conditions, and other building-envelope requirements.

**Built-up Bituminous Roofing** should include low-slope roof system concepts, membranes, bituminous materials, surfacing, flashing, application methods, and repair considerations.

**Elastomeric Roofing** should include membrane materials, seams, flashing, penetrations, attachment, roof edges, drainage, and repair conditions.

**Roofing Repairs** should be studied as a diagnostic subject. Candidates should understand common leak sources, failed flashing, deteriorated coverings, membrane damage, substrate problems, and how to identify likely causes of roof failure.

**Sheet Metal and Flashing** is important to both roofing and siding. Candidates should understand flashing at valleys, penetrations, roof-to-wall intersections, windows, doors, curbs, edges, and transitions where water must be directed away from vulnerable areas.

**Photovoltaic Systems** should be reviewed from a roofing perspective. Candidates should understand how rooftop photovoltaic components can affect roof coverings, flashing, waterproofing, access, and other roof-system considerations.

**Safety** should include common construction and roofing hazards such as fall exposure, ladders, scaffolds, tools, personal protective equipment, and material handling.

The **IBC index** is a valuable navigation tool. When the correct chapter is not immediately known, identify the technical term used in the question and use the index to find the likely section.

Candidates should also become familiar with the table of contents and chapter structure. Knowing whether a topic is primarily a roofing, exterior-wall, fire-resistance, structural, or general building requirement can reduce search time.

Definitions deserve careful review because the meaning of a code term can affect which requirement applies. A roofing or siding question may depend on understanding the exact terminology used in the IBC.

When locating a code section, candidates should read enough surrounding material to understand the complete requirement. Exceptions, tables, subsections, and cross-references can change how a general rule applies.

A useful practice exercise is to choose topics such as roof coverings, exterior walls, roof assemblies, siding-related construction, fire classification, rooftop conditions, or structural requirements and locate the applicable provisions under timed conditions.

Practice questions should first be organized by subject. Work separately on roofing, siding, substrates, low-slope systems, repairs, flashing, safety, and photovoltaic topics. Once individual areas become more familiar, move into mixed practice.

Mixed practice is important because the actual C-15 exam can move rapidly from one building-envelope subject to another. Candidates should become comfortable switching between roofing, siding, flashing, substrates, and code navigation without losing pace.

Time management should be included in preparation. With 60 questions in two hours, spending too long on one code lookup can reduce the time available for easier questions.

## How does the Roofing and Siding Contractor (C-15) exam work?

The Roofing and Siding Contractor (C-15) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
