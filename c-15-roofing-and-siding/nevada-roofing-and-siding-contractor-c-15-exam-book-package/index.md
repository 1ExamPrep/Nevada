## State Requirements

Nevada recognizes **C-15 Roofing and Siding** as a specialty contractor classification. A contractor holding the primary C-15 classification may perform the work authorized under the C-15 subclassifications.

The roofing portion covers the installation, application, alteration, and repair of watertight and weatherproof materials used on roofs. Roofing materials can include asphalt, pitch, tar, felt, flax, shakes, shingles, aluminum, tile, slate, urethane, and photovoltaic roof tiles intended to serve in place of other roofing materials.

The siding portion covers the installation, alteration, and repair of siding using materials such as wood, aluminum, enameled steel, plastic, and glass to make exterior walls watertight and weatherproof.

The broader C-15 classification also encompasses insulation and waterproofing work authorized under its subclassifications. This makes the primary C-15 classification more comprehensive than a license limited only to C-15A Roofing or C-15B Siding.

Candidates should understand how roofing, siding, insulation, waterproofing, flashing, substrates, and weather-resistant construction work together as part of the building envelope.

The C-15 trade examination is one component of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong C-15 preparation plan should begin with **Steep Roofing**. Review roof coverings, underlayments, deck conditions, slopes, fastening, valleys, flashing, penetrations, drainage, and roof edges.

**Roofing Substrates** should include sheathing, structural support, moisture, deterioration, preparation, attachment, and compatibility with the finished roof system.

**Siding** deserves dedicated study because it is a major difference between the C-15 and C-15A exams. Review wood, metal, plastic, and other siding materials, installation concepts, attachment, expansion and contraction considerations, soffits, flashing, and weather-resistant exterior wall construction.

The ASTM PVC siding and soffit reference can support preparation involving installation methods for rigid PVC products. Candidates should understand the relationship between siding, trim, openings, soffits, exterior wall surfaces, and water management.

**Built-up Bituminous Roofing** and **Elastomeric Roofing** should include materials, membrane systems, seams, surfacing, attachment, flashing, penetrations, drainage, and repair conditions.

**Metal Panel and SPF Roofing** should be reviewed as distinct systems. Candidates should recognize how materials, attachment, joints, insulation, coatings, and installation methods differ from steep-slope shingles or membrane systems.

**Roofing Repairs** should be studied as a diagnostic subject. Review common leak sources, failed flashing, damaged materials, membrane deterioration, substrate problems, and the relationship between the symptom and the underlying cause.

**Sheet Metal and Flashing** supports both roofing and siding work. Candidates should understand flashing around valleys, roof-to-wall intersections, penetrations, windows, doors, edges, curbs, and other transition areas where water must be directed away from the building envelope.

**Safety** is especially important for roofing and siding work because projects can involve ladders, scaffolds, elevated work, power tools, material handling, and fall hazards. OSHA should be used to build safety knowledge that can be recalled independently during the exam.

The **International Building Code, 2018** should be used regularly during preparation. Practice finding roofing and exterior-wall provisions using the table of contents and index, then follow related sections, definitions, tables, and exceptions.

Because most books in this package are not allowed into the exam, candidates should gradually move from book-supported study to active recall. Review a technical subject, close the reference, and answer practice questions without immediately looking up the solution.

For the IBC, practice timed navigation instead. Select a topic, identify the likely code location, find the applicable section, and repeat until the process becomes more efficient.

## How does the Roofing and Siding Contractor (C-15) exam work?

The Roofing and Siding Contractor (C-15) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
