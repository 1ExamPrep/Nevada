Exam Details

The Nevada C-15 Roofing and Siding Contractor examination contains 60 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the examination and must answer 45 questions correctly to pass.

The examination covers the following major subject areas:

  • Safety
  • Roofing Substrates
  • Steep Roofing
  • Roofing Repairs
  • Elastomeric Roofing
  • Built-up Bituminous Roofing
  • Sheet Metal and Flashing
  • Siding
  • Photovoltaic Systems

The C-15 exam requires candidates to understand both roofing and siding systems. This broader scope distinguishes it from the C-15A Roofing subclassification and requires preparation across multiple parts of the building envelope.

Steep Roofing should receive significant study time. Candidates should understand roof coverings, underlayments, fastening, roof decks, slopes, valleys, flashing, penetrations, roof edges, drainage, and installation relationships.

Roofing Substrates should include sheathing, structural support, moisture, deterioration, surface preparation, attachment, and compatibility between the substrate and the finished roofing system.

Siding is a core C-15 subject. Candidates should study exterior wall coverings, attachment methods, soffit applications, flashing, openings, weatherproofing, expansion and contraction, and moisture-management concepts.

Built-up Bituminous Roofing and Elastomeric Roofing require knowledge of low-slope roofing systems, membrane materials, seams, surfacing, attachment, flashing, penetrations, drainage, and repair conditions.

Safety, roofing repairs, sheet metal and flashing, and photovoltaic-related roofing concepts complete the broad range of topics candidates should be prepared to address.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-15 Roofing and Siding as a specialty contractor classification covering roofing and siding work and the work authorized under its applicable subclassifications.

The roofing portion includes the installation, application, alteration, and repair of watertight and weatherproof materials used on roofs. Candidates should understand how roof coverings, substrates, flashing, drainage, and weather-resistant assemblies work together.

The siding portion covers the installation, alteration, and repair of siding used to make exterior walls watertight and weatherproof. Candidates should understand how siding integrates with wall construction, sheathing, trim, openings, soffits, flashing, and other exterior components.

The broader C-15 classification also encompasses related insulation and waterproofing work authorized under its subclassifications. This makes knowledge of the overall building envelope important for candidates preparing for the C-15 trade examination.

The trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-15 preparation plan should combine technical study, code navigation, active recall, and timed practice. Because the examination covers both roofing and siding, candidates should build a study schedule that gives appropriate attention to both sides of the classification.

Steep Roofing should include roof coverings, underlayments, slopes, fastening, valleys, roof edges, penetrations, flashing, and drainage. Use the NRCA steep-slope reference to reinforce system knowledge, then practice answering questions without relying on the study-only book.

Roofing Substrates should include sheathing, structural support, moisture, deterioration, surface preparation, attachment, and compatibility with the finished roof system.

Siding should receive dedicated study time. Review materials, attachment, soffits, flashing, openings, expansion and contraction, weatherproofing, and moisture management. The ASTM siding and soffit reference can support focused preparation in this area.

Built-up Bituminous Roofing and Elastomeric Roofing should include membranes, system components, surfacing, seams, attachment, flashing, penetrations, drainage, and common repair conditions.

Metal Panel and SPF Roofing should be studied as distinct systems. Candidates should understand materials, fastening or attachment, joints, coatings, insulation relationships, and maintenance considerations.

Roofing Repairs should be approached as a diagnostic subject. Review common leak sources, failed flashing, damaged membranes, deteriorated roof coverings, substrate problems, and conditions that can affect roof performance.

Sheet Metal and Flashing is important to both roofing and siding. Candidates should understand flashing at valleys, penetrations, curbs, roof-to-wall intersections, windows, doors, roof edges, and other building-envelope transitions.

Safety should include common roofing and construction hazards such as fall exposure, ladders, scaffolds, tools, personal protective equipment, and material handling. Because the OSHA reference is not allowed into the trade exam, candidates should build retained safety knowledge during preparation.

Photovoltaic Systems should be reviewed from the roofing perspective. Candidates should understand how rooftop photovoltaic components interact with roofing materials, waterproofing, flashing, access, and roof-system performance.

Roofing Construction and Estimating supports preparation involving roof measurements, geometry, quantities, and practical estimating concepts. Candidates should become comfortable interpreting dimensions and connecting roof size and slope with material quantities.

Carpentry and Building Construction supports understanding of roof framing, wall systems, sheathing, structural components, and substrates. This knowledge helps candidates understand what roofing and siding materials attach to and how the underlying structure can affect installation.

For the study-only technical books, transition gradually from book-supported review to active recall. Read a subject, close the book, and answer practice questions without immediately looking up the solution.

The included 6 months of course access can be used alongside the highlighted and tabbed books to create a consistent study schedule, reinforce trade topics, practice exam-oriented questions, and improve reference-navigation skills.

As exam day approaches, candidates should move into mixed practice sessions that combine roofing, siding, substrates, flashing, safety, estimating, low-slope roofing, and code-navigation questions.

Time management should also be practiced. With 60 questions in two hours, spending too long on one difficult code lookup can reduce the time available for easier questions.

A balanced open-book approach is to answer familiar trade questions efficiently, use the International Building Code for targeted confirmation, flag difficult items, and return to them after completing easier questions.

How does the Roofing and Siding Contractor (C-15) exam work?

The Roofing and Siding Contractor (C-15) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
