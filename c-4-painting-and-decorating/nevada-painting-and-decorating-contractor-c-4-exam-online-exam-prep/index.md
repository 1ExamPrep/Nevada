## Exam Details

The Nevada C-4 Painting and Decorating Contractor trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who receive examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

- **Exam:** Nevada C-4 Painting and Decorating Contractor
- **Number of Questions:** 55
- **Time Allowed:** 2 hours
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 39 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The examination content is divided into nine technical areas:

- **Materials:** 8 questions
- **Surface Preparation:** 12 questions
- **Application:** 7 questions
- **Tools:** 6 questions
- **Problems and Prevention:** 4 questions
- **Safety:** 5 questions
- **Drywall:** 5 questions
- **Color:** 3 questions
- **Sheet Metal:** 5 questions

**Surface Preparation** is the largest individual exam category with 12 questions. Candidates should understand cleaning, scraping, sanding, patching, priming, removal or treatment of previous coatings, substrate evaluation, wallcovering preparation, and other procedures used before applying a finish.

**Materials** contains eight questions. Preparation should include paints, primers, varnishes, stains, shellacs, protective coatings, waxes, wallcovering products, adhesives, gypsum products, and other materials associated with painting and decorating work.

**Application** contains seven questions. Candidates should review brush, roller, and spray application, coating sequence, coverage, wallcovering installation, finishing methods, and the relationship between the selected material and the application procedure.

**Tools** contains six questions. Study should include brushes, rollers, spray equipment, sanding tools, scrapers, ladders, surface-preparation equipment, drywall tools, and other equipment used throughout painting, decorating, and gypsum-related work.

**Safety**, **Drywall**, and **Sheet Metal** each contain five questions. These sections are significant because the C-4 classification extends beyond standard painting. Candidates should prepare for construction safety, gypsum products, drywall installation, finishing methods, metal framing, and related procedures.

**Problems and Prevention** contains four questions. Candidates should understand common painting and coating failures, the conditions that cause them, and the methods used to prevent or correct problems.

**Color** contains three questions. Although smaller than the other sections, color remains part of the examination and should be included in a complete preparation plan.

## Closed Book Test

The Nevada C-4 Painting and Decorating Contractor examination is a **closed-book test**. The study references are used during preparation but are not allowed into the trade examination.

The technical study references are:

- **Painting & Decorating Craftsman's Manual and Textbook, Eighth Edition, 1995**
- **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)**
- **Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th Edition**

Closed-book testing requires candidates to develop strong recall. Instead of relying on page locations or searching a reference while testing, students should focus on understanding materials, surface preparation, tools, application procedures, safety requirements, drywall, metal framing, and troubleshooting well enough to apply those concepts directly.

Surface preparation deserves substantial attention because it is the largest exam category. Candidates should understand why different surfaces require different preparation procedures and how contamination, moisture, previous coatings, damaged substrates, improper sanding, inadequate cleaning, or incorrect priming can affect finished work.

Painting and decorating materials should be studied in context. Candidates should understand when primers, paints, stains, varnishes, shellacs, protective coatings, waxes, adhesives, and wallcovering materials are appropriate and how surface conditions can influence material selection.

The Gypsum Construction Handbook supports preparation for drywall and sheet-metal-related subjects. Candidates should review gypsum board products, installation concepts, finishing, metal framing, and related construction procedures before examination day.

OSHA preparation should focus on safety concepts that must be recalled without the book. Candidates should review ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, fall hazards, respiratory concerns, ventilation, tools, and other construction-safety issues relevant to painting and decorating work.

Practice-oriented study is especially valuable for a closed-book examination. ## State Requirements

The Nevada C-4 Painting and Decorating classification is a specialty contractor classification with a broader scope than painting alone.

The classification includes application of paint, varnish, shellac, stain, protective coatings, and wax. It also includes attaching papers, fabrics, and plastics to building surfaces, examining and preparing surfaces for painting and wallcovering, and installing drywall, metal studs, and acoustical tile.

The broad scope explains why the C-4 exam includes Drywall and Sheet Metal in addition to traditional painting subjects such as Materials, Surface Preparation, Application, Tools, Problems and Prevention, Safety, and Color.

Candidates should understand the distinction between C-4 Painting and Decorating and narrower painting classifications. Someone pursuing the full C-4 classification should prepare for the additional gypsum, framing, wallcovering, and decorating-related subjects that fall within the broader scope.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual generally must demonstrate at least four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the requested classification.

The qualifying experience generally must have been obtained within the 15 years immediately preceding the filing of the application. Applicants should maintain organized documentation describing the type of C-4 work performed and the period during which the work was completed.

Nevada contractor licensing distinguishes between management and trade qualification. One individual may fulfill both roles when the applicable requirements are met, or separate qualified individuals may be designated.

The Trade Qualified Individual establishes the applicable experience and trade qualification for C-4. The Management Qualified Individual generally satisfies the Nevada Business and Law requirement unless an applicable waiver is approved.

Financial responsibility, background requirements, contractor bonding, and other licensing conditions are handled separately from the technical examination. Passing the C-4 trade exam does not by itself result in issuance of a Nevada contractor license.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-4 study plan should reflect both the weighting of the exam and the closed-book format. Surface Preparation deserves the greatest emphasis, followed by Materials, Application, Tools, Safety, Drywall, Sheet Metal, Problems and Prevention, and Color.

**Prioritize Surface Preparation.** Review cleaning, sanding, scraping, patching, priming, existing coatings, substrate evaluation, moisture conditions, wallcovering preparation, and other factors that affect finished work.

**Study Materials carefully.** Review paints, primers, varnishes, stains, shellacs, protective coatings, waxes, adhesives, wallcoverings, gypsum products, and related materials.

**Review Application methods.** Study brush, roller, and spray application, wallcovering installation, coating sequence, coverage, handling, and workmanship considerations.

**Know the Tools.** Become familiar with painting tools, spray systems, sanding equipment, scrapers, ladders, drywall tools, and other equipment used across the C-4 scope.

**Prepare Safety for recall.** Since OSHA cannot be used during the examination, candidates should learn ladder, scaffold, PPE, fall-protection, respiratory, ventilation, tool, and material-handling concepts before test day.

**Review Drywall and Sheet Metal.** Use the Gypsum Construction Handbook to strengthen knowledge involving gypsum products, drywall installation, finishing, and metal framing.

**Study Problems and Prevention.** Review common coating failures, preparation mistakes, application problems, and the conditions that can lead to poor finished results.

**Review Color concepts.** Color contains fewer questions but remains part of the examination and should not be ignored.

**Use active recall.** After studying a topic, put the reference aside and explain the concept from memory. This directly supports preparation for a closed-book test.

**Review missed questions by category.** If repeated mistakes occur in Surface Preparation, Drywall, Safety, or another subject, direct additional study toward that category rather than repeating the entire course without focus.

**Practice under timed conditions.** Candidates have two hours to complete 55 questions. Timed practice can help establish a steady pace and improve comfort answering without reference books.

## How does the Painting and Decorating Contractor (C-4) exam work?

The Painting and Decorating Contractor (C-4) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.

- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
