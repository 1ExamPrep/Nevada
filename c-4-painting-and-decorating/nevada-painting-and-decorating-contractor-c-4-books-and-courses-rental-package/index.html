Exam Details

The Nevada C-4 Painting and Decorating Contractor examination contains 55 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the exam and must answer 39 questions correctly to pass.

The examination covers the following content areas:

  • Materials: 8 questions
  • Surface Preparation: 12 questions
  • Application: 7 questions
  • Tools: 6 questions
  • Problems and Prevention: 4 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions
  • Drywall: 5 questions
  • Color: 3 questions
  • Sheet Metal: 5 questions

Surface Preparation is the largest individual content area. Candidates should understand how substrates are inspected, cleaned, repaired, sanded, scraped, patched, sealed, primed, and otherwise prepared before paint, coatings, wallcoverings, or finishes are applied.

Materials requires familiarity with paints, primers, sealers, varnishes, stains, shellacs, protective coatings, wallcoverings, gypsum products, fasteners, framing materials, and other products used within the C-4 scope.

Application includes brush, roller, and spray methods, along with practical knowledge of how materials are applied to different surfaces and under different job conditions.

Drywall and Sheet Metal are important because the C-4 classification extends beyond painting alone. Candidates should prepare for gypsum board systems, metal studs, fastening, finishing, and related interior construction knowledge.

State Requirements

Nevada's C-4 Painting and Decorating Contractor classification covers a broad range of painting, finishing, wallcovering, drywall, and related interior work.

The scope includes applying paint, varnish, shellac, stain, protective coatings, and wax to building surfaces and appurtenances. It also includes attaching papers, fabrics, and plastics to surfaces and preparing surfaces for painting and wallcovering.

The C-4 classification also includes installation of drywall, metal studs, and acoustical tile. This broader scope is reflected in the exam through drywall and sheet metal questions in addition to traditional painting and decorating subjects.

Candidates should therefore prepare for complete interior finishing systems rather than paint application alone. Understanding how substrates, framing, gypsum systems, surface preparation, coating materials, and finishing methods work together can improve overall trade knowledge.

The licensing process also includes requirements beyond the C-4 examination. Applicants must complete the applicable Nevada application, qualification, examination, business and law, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-4 study plan should balance painting trade knowledge, drywall and interior systems, construction safety, and Nevada business and law preparation.

Surface Preparation deserves significant attention because it is the largest examination category. Candidates should understand cleaning, scraping, sanding, patching, repairing, sealing, priming, and correcting substrate conditions before finishes are applied.

Different surfaces require different preparation methods. Wood, gypsum board, masonry, metal, and previously coated surfaces can each respond differently to moisture, contamination, damage, or existing finishes.

Materials should include paints, primers, sealers, stains, varnishes, shellacs, protective coatings, wallcoverings, gypsum products, framing materials, and related construction supplies. Candidates should understand what each material is intended to do and where it is commonly used.

Application Methods should include brush, roller, and spray techniques. Candidates should understand how equipment selection, surface condition, product type, and application technique affect the finished result.

Brush and Roller Work should include tool selection, coating distribution, coverage, finish quality, and correct application procedures.

Spray Application should include spray equipment, pressure, spray patterns, overspray control, masking, ventilation, material preparation, and safe operating practices.

Problems and Prevention should include common coating defects such as peeling, blistering, cracking, poor adhesion, uneven finish, and other failures. Candidates should understand how poor preparation, moisture, incompatible materials, or incorrect application can contribute to these problems.

Tools should include brushes, rollers, spray equipment, scrapers, sanding tools, drywall tools, ladders, scaffolds, cutting tools, and other equipment used within the C-4 scope.

Drywall should include gypsum board installation, fastening, joints, taping, finishing, framing, and related wall and ceiling construction. The Gypsum Construction Handbook provides focused preparation for this area.

Sheet Metal should include metal stud and interior framing knowledge. Candidates should understand common materials, layout, fastening methods, and installation practices used in these systems.

Safety should be studied using OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Painting and decorating contractors may work with ladders, scaffolds, elevated surfaces, power equipment, spray systems, solvents, temporary electrical power, and other jobsite hazards.

Personal Protective Equipment should include protection for the eyes, skin, respiratory system, and other hazards encountered during surface preparation, coating application, drywall work, and construction activities.

Color is a smaller part of the examination, but candidates should still understand basic color relationships and painting terminology used in decorating work.

How does the Painting and Decorating Contractor (C-4) exam work?

The Painting and Decorating Contractor (C-4) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
