Exam Details

The Nevada C-4 Painting and Decorating Contractor examination contains 55 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the examination and must answer 39 questions correctly to pass.

The current examination content is divided into the following areas:

  • Materials: 8 questions
  • Surface Preparation: 12 questions
  • Application: 7 questions
  • Tools: 6 questions
  • Problems and Prevention: 4 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions
  • Drywall: 5 questions
  • Color: 3 questions
  • Sheet Metal: 5 questions

Surface Preparation is the largest examination category with 12 questions. Candidates should understand the importance of inspecting, cleaning, repairing, sanding, scraping, patching, sealing, and priming surfaces before applying coatings or wallcoverings. Poor preparation can affect adhesion, appearance, durability, and overall finish quality.

Materials accounts for 8 questions. Candidates should become familiar with paints, primers, sealers, varnishes, stains, shellacs, protective coatings, wallcoverings, drywall products, fasteners, framing materials, and other products used within the C-4 classification.

Application accounts for 7 questions. Preparation should include brushing, rolling, spraying, coating application, wallcovering installation, and the correct sequence of work for different surfaces and materials.

Tools accounts for 6 questions. Candidates should understand the correct use of painting tools, spray equipment, surface preparation tools, drywall tools, ladders, scaffolding, and other equipment used in painting and decorating work.

Drywall and Sheet Metal each account for 5 questions. These subjects reflect the broader C-4 scope and should not be ignored during preparation.

Closed Book Test

The Nevada C-4 Painting and Decorating Contractor examination is a closed-book test.

The references included with this package are intended for study before the examination. Candidates may not use these books during the C-4 trade exam.

A closed-book format changes the way candidates should prepare. Reading alone is not enough. After reviewing a topic, candidates should close the book and test their ability to explain the material from memory. Practice questions, flashcards, verbal summaries, and repeated review can help reinforce information before test day.

For example, after studying surface preparation, candidates should be able to explain the steps required for preparing wood, drywall, masonry, metal, or previously painted surfaces without reopening the book. They should also understand how surface defects and environmental conditions can affect coating performance.

The same approach should be used for drywall. After studying gypsum board installation and finishing, candidates should be able to recall basic concepts involving framing, fastening, joints, finishing, and common gypsum-system practices.

Safety preparation should also be based on retention. Candidates should understand the principles behind ladder safety, scaffold safety, fall protection, personal protective equipment, electrical hazards, tools, and other construction workplace requirements covered by OSHA.

Since the examination contains 55 questions in two hours, candidates should also practice maintaining a steady pace. Strong recall can reduce the time spent debating basic terminology or trade concepts.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-4 study plan should reflect the weighting of the examination and devote additional time to surface preparation, materials, application, tools, drywall, sheet metal, and safety.

Surface Preparation should receive the most attention because it is the largest category on the exam. Candidates should understand cleaning, scraping, sanding, patching, repairing, removing loose material, treating contaminated surfaces, sealing, and priming.

Different substrates require different preparation methods. Wood, gypsum board, masonry, metal, and previously coated surfaces can each require different procedures before a finish is applied.

Materials should include primers, sealers, paints, stains, varnishes, shellacs, protective coatings, wallcoverings, drywall products, joint materials, framing materials, and related supplies. Candidates should understand the purpose of each product and where it is commonly used.

Application should include brush, roller, and spray techniques. Candidates should understand how to choose the appropriate application method based on the material, substrate, finish, and job conditions.

Wallcovering application should also be studied. The C-4 scope includes attaching papers, fabrics, and plastics to building surfaces, so candidates should understand preparation, layout, adhesive concepts, installation methods, and finishing practices.

Tools should include brushes, rollers, spray equipment, scrapers, sanding tools, drywall tools, ladders, scaffolds, cutting tools, and other equipment used throughout the C-4 scope.

Problems and Prevention should include common coating defects such as peeling, blistering, cracking, poor adhesion, uneven finish, and other failures. Candidates should understand how poor preparation, unsuitable materials, environmental conditions, or incorrect application can contribute to these problems.

Safety should be studied using OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Painting and decorating work can involve elevated work areas, solvents, spray equipment, ladders, scaffolds, power tools, and respiratory hazards.

Drywall should include gypsum board installation, fastening, joints, taping, finishing, framing, and related interior construction practices. The Gypsum Construction Handbook is particularly useful for this portion of the examination.

Sheet Metal should also be included in the study schedule. The C-4 classification includes metal studs and related interior construction work, so candidates should understand common materials, fastening methods, layout concepts, and installation practices associated with these systems.

Color is a smaller category, but candidates should still understand basic color relationships and painting terminology used in decorating work.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Because the examination is closed book, active recall should be incorporated into every study session. Read a section, close the reference, and explain the concept without looking back at the page.

Practice questions are another useful tool. When a question is missed, return to the applicable reference and study the underlying concept rather than memorizing only the correct answer.

Flashcards can help reinforce terminology, coating characteristics, tools, safety rules, drywall concepts, and other facts that must be recalled without reference assistance.

Short, focused study sessions can also improve retention. One session might cover surface preparation, another painting materials, another drywall, and another OSHA safety. Rotating subjects can help candidates build balanced knowledge across the entire exam outline.

Candidates should also connect the books rather than treating them as unrelated resources. For example, a drywall surface may require proper gypsum installation and finishing before painting can begin. Understanding both construction and finishing processes creates stronger practical knowledge.

How does the Painting and Decorating Contractor (C-4) exam work?

The Painting and Decorating Contractor (C-4) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
