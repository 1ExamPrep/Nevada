## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-4 study plan should follow the examination weighting and spend additional time on the largest content areas while still reviewing every subject.

**Surface Preparation** should receive the most attention. Candidates should understand how to prepare wood, drywall, masonry, metal, and previously coated surfaces. Cleaning, scraping, sanding, patching, sealing, priming, and correcting defects should all be part of the study routine.

**Materials** should include paints, primers, sealers, stains, varnishes, shellacs, protective coatings, wallcoverings, gypsum products, and related supplies. Candidates should understand the purpose of each material and the type of surface or application where it may be used.

**Application Methods** should include brushing, rolling, and spraying. Candidates should understand how equipment selection, surface conditions, coating type, and application technique influence the finished result.

**Brush and Roller Application** should include tool selection, coverage, loading, consistency, and finish quality.

**Spray Application** should include spray equipment, pattern, pressure, overspray control, masking, material preparation, ventilation, and safe operation.

**Problems and Prevention** should include common coating failures such as peeling, blistering, cracking, poor adhesion, uneven finish, and other defects. Candidates should understand how poor preparation, moisture, incompatible materials, or incorrect application can contribute to failures.

**Tools** should include brushes, rollers, spray equipment, scrapers, sanding equipment, drywall tools, ladders, scaffolds, cutting tools, and other equipment used in painting and decorating work.

**Drywall** should include gypsum board installation, fastening, joints, taping, finishing, framing, and general wall and ceiling construction. The Gypsum Construction Handbook can be particularly useful for strengthening knowledge in this area.

**Sheet Metal** should include basic metal stud and related interior framing knowledge. Candidates should understand common materials, fastening methods, layout, and installation practices associated with metal framing systems.

**Safety** should be reviewed using OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Painting and decorating contractors often work at elevation and may use ladders, scaffolds, spray equipment, solvents, tools, and temporary power, making construction safety an important part of preparation.

**Personal Protective Equipment** should include appropriate protection for eyes, skin, respiratory exposure, and other hazards encountered during surface preparation, spraying, and coating application.

**Color** is a smaller content area, but candidates should still review basic color relationships and terminology used in painting and decorating work.

## How does the Painting and Decorating Contractor (C-4) exam work?

The Painting and Decorating Contractor (C-4) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
