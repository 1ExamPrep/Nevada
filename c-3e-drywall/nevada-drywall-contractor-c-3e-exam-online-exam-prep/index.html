Exam Details

The Nevada C-3E Drywall trade examination is part of the contractor licensing process administered for the Nevada State Contractors Board through PSI. Candidates must first complete the applicable licensing application and qualification process before receiving examination eligibility.

The trade exam is designed to measure knowledge directly related to the C-3E Drywall classification. Preparation should focus on the practical skills, materials, methods, and code requirements associated with drywall and gypsum construction.

Drywall installation is a central area of study. Candidates should understand board layout, measuring, cutting, fastening, attachment methods, panel orientation, wall construction, ceiling construction, openings, joints, sheathing, and coordination with supporting framing.

Taping and finishing should also receive substantial attention. Review joint treatment, tape, joint compounds, corner treatments, finishing sequences, surface preparation, and techniques used to produce finished gypsum wallboard surfaces.

Gypsum materials and systems are another important part of preparation. Candidates should be familiar with different gypsum panel products, sheathing, fasteners, compounds, accessories, trims, and assemblies and understand how they are used in different construction conditions.

Acoustical tile is included within the C-3E scope and should not be overlooked. Candidates should review the basic materials, components, and installation considerations associated with acoustical ceiling work.

Sheet-metal studs for ceilings and nonstructural partitioning are also included in the C-3E scope. Candidates should understand how these framing systems relate to drywall installation, layout, attachment, and finished partition systems.

The International Building Code adds the code-based component of preparation. Candidates should be prepared to identify applicable requirements involving gypsum board, interior construction, materials, assemblies, and other code provisions relevant to C-3E work.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-3E Drywall trade examination permits the approved technical code reference in the examination room. For this examination, the approved reference identified for exam-room use is the International Building Code, 2018.

The exam-room reference is:

  • International Building Code, 2018

Candidates should practice with this book repeatedly before exam day. Open-reference testing is most effective when the candidate already understands the trade and knows approximately where a requirement is located before beginning a search.

Useful IBC navigation skills include working with the index, locating definitions, identifying relevant chapters, following section numbering, interpreting tables, reading exceptions, and following cross-references to related requirements.

The following references are useful for study but are not allowed into the C-3E trade examination:

  • Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016
  • Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th Edition

Because these books will not be available during the examination, use them to build knowledge that can be recalled independently. They are especially useful for understanding practical construction relationships, gypsum systems, walls, ceilings, framing, fastening, finishing, materials, and accessories.

A strong study strategy combines trade knowledge with efficient use of the IBC. Candidates should avoid relying on the code book for basic information that can be learned before testing. Reference time is better spent confirming requirements that depend on a specific code provision.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada C-3E trade examination is one part of the overall contractor licensing process. Candidates pursuing this classification must satisfy the applicable Nevada State Contractors Board requirements before the license is issued.

  1. Confirm the C-3E classification. Make sure the Drywall subclassification matches the type of work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. Designate the person who will satisfy the trade qualification requirements for the C-3E classification.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare records showing relevant experience involving drywall, gypsum systems, ceilings, nonstructural partitions, and related work.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor application. Gather the required business, qualifying-party, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application. Provide the required application materials to the Nevada State Contractors Board for review.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow the fingerprint and background procedures required as part of Nevada contractor licensing.
  7. Provide applicable financial information. Financial responsibility is part of the Nevada contractor licensing process.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Qualified applicants may proceed with testing after the Board verifies the applicable requirements.
  9. Prepare for the C-3E trade examination. Review installation, finishing, gypsum materials, sheathing, acoustical tile, nonstructural metal framing, and International Building Code navigation.
  10. Prepare for Business and Law. Nevada contractor applicants generally must satisfy the applicable contractor management examination requirement unless an approved waiver applies.
  11. Complete the required examinations. Satisfy the applicable trade and contractor management requirements.
  12. Complete final licensing requirements. Address applicable financial, bonding, and remaining Nevada State Contractors Board requirements before license issuance.

Exam preparation should be treated as one part of the licensing plan. Passing the trade examination does not by itself issue a Nevada contractor license. Candidates must complete the other applicable licensing requirements as well.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-3E Drywall subclassification authorizes the installation, taping, and finishing of drywall, panels, and assemblies of gypsum wallboard and sheathing. It also includes acoustical tile and studs made of sheet metal for framing ceilings and nonstructural partitioning.

The scope is specific to drywall and related systems. Contractors should understand the limits of the classification and should not treat a C-3E license as unrestricted authority to perform unrelated specialty or structural contracting work.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the trade qualifying individual generally must demonstrate at least four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the specific classification requested.

The qualifying experience generally must have been obtained within the 15 years immediately preceding the application. Experience documentation should clearly describe the type of work performed and demonstrate how the work relates to the C-3E Drywall classification.

Nevada may recognize qualifying education or training toward part of the experience requirement when the applicable Board standards are satisfied. Candidates using education or training toward experience should provide the required documentation as part of the application process.

The Nevada contractor licensing process also includes financial responsibility, applicable contractor bonding, background requirements, and additional Board conditions. These requirements are separate from the C-3E examination.

Applicants generally must satisfy both trade qualification and contractor management requirements unless an approved waiver applies. The business and its qualifying individual must remain properly structured within the Nevada licensing framework.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-3E study plan should be organized around the actual work authorized by the classification. Candidates should build preparation around installation, taping and finishing, gypsum materials, sheathing, acoustical tile, nonstructural sheet-metal framing, code navigation, and practical construction knowledge.

Start with installation fundamentals. Review measuring, board layout, cutting, fastening, attachment, panel placement, walls, ceilings, openings, and sheathing. Candidates should understand how a drywall assembly is put together from the framing outward.

Develop strong finishing knowledge. Review tapes, joint compounds, corner treatments, finishing sequences, sanding and surface preparation concepts, and the progression from installed panels to completed surfaces.

Study gypsum products and systems. Learn how different panel products, fasteners, compounds, trims, and accessories are used. Understanding the purpose of the material can make technical questions easier to evaluate than simple memorization.

Review nonstructural metal framing. Sheet-metal studs used for ceilings and nonstructural partitions are specifically included in the C-3E scope. Candidates should understand layout, framing relationships, and how these systems support drywall assemblies.

Do not overlook acoustical tile. Acoustical tile is part of the classification scope. Include ceiling-related products and installation considerations in the study plan rather than concentrating only on gypsum wallboard.

Practice International Building Code navigation. Use the IBC during study sessions so the book becomes familiar. Practice locating definitions, relevant chapters, sections, tables, and cross-references rather than only reading code provisions passively.

Use Carpentry and Building Construction for broader context. General construction knowledge helps candidates understand how drywall interfaces with walls, ceilings, framing, openings, and other building components.

Use the Gypsum Construction Handbook for focused trade review. Study gypsum materials, wallboard systems, installation, fastening, finishing, ceilings, sheathing, and accessories. Because the book is not available in the exam room, concentrate on building practical understanding and recall.

Work through practice-oriented questions. Practice helps candidates apply technical knowledge and identify which subjects require further study. Missed questions should become a guide for the next review session.

Separate knowledge questions from reference questions. Some questions can be answered through a strong understanding of the trade, while others require a specific code lookup. Learning to recognize the difference can make preparation more efficient.

How does the Drywall Contractor (C-3E) exam work?

The Drywall Contractor (C-3E) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
