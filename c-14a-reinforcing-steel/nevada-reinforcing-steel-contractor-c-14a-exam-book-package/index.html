Exam Details

The Nevada C-14A Reinforcing Steel Contractor trade examination evaluates knowledge associated with reinforcing-steel fabrication, placement, tying, post-tensioning, field procedures, construction safety, and related trade practices.

Candidates should be prepared to work with reinforcing-bar terminology, bar identification, reinforcing layouts, placement requirements, supports, splices, ties, bending concepts, construction drawings, and field coordination. Post-tensioning is also part of the C-14A scope and should receive dedicated study time.

Trade knowledge should extend beyond recognizing individual components. Candidates should understand why reinforcing steel is positioned in particular locations, how bar placement relates to concrete cover and structural performance, how reinforcing assemblies are maintained during concrete placement, and how field conditions can affect the completed installation.

Post-tensioning preparation should include the components of an unbonded tendon system, anchorages, wedges, sheathing, stressing procedures, tendon handling, inspection considerations, and safe work practices around tensioned systems.

Safety should be integrated into every subject. Reinforcing-steel contractors may work around protruding bars, elevated slabs, formwork, cranes, bundled steel, cutting and bending equipment, concrete operations, and post-tensioning systems. Candidates should understand both general construction safety and the hazards specific to reinforcing-steel work.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-14A Reinforcing Steel as a subclassification under C-14 Steel Reinforcing and Erection.

The authorized scope includes the fabrication, placement, and tying of steel reinforcing bars or rods, and post-tensioning used to reinforce concrete buildings and structures.

This means the classification covers both conventional reinforcing steel and post-tensioning activities within reinforced concrete construction. Candidates should understand reinforcing bars as part of a complete structural system rather than as isolated pieces of steel.

Reinforcing work begins before concrete is placed. Contractors may need to interpret reinforcing drawings, identify bars, coordinate deliveries, stage material, fabricate or prepare reinforcing assemblies, place supports, install bars, tie intersections, maintain spacing, and coordinate with formwork and embedded items.

During concrete placement, reinforcement must remain in the intended position. Field crews need to understand how movement, inadequate supports, improper tying, interference with other trades, or careless concrete operations can affect the finished work.

Post-tensioning adds another level of specialized knowledge. Tendons and anchorages must be installed and handled according to the intended system, and stressing operations require careful procedures and strict attention to safety.

The C-14A trade examination is one part of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, Business and Law, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-14A study plan should begin with reinforcing-bar fundamentals. Candidates should understand common reinforcing terminology, bar identification, placement concepts, supports, ties, splices, clearances, bends, and how reinforcing steel is shown on drawings.

Bar Placement should receive significant attention. Reinforcement must be installed in the intended location and maintained there while concrete is placed. Candidates should understand the purpose of chairs, bolsters, spacers, ties, and other supports used to maintain position.

Tying Reinforcement should be studied as a field procedure. Different reinforcing configurations may require different tying approaches, and ties help keep bars properly positioned during construction. Candidates should understand the practical purpose of tying rather than viewing it as a purely mechanical task.

Splices and Development should be reviewed as part of reinforcing continuity. Candidates should understand basic lap-splice concepts, mechanical connection concepts, bar continuity, and why reinforcing details must match the structural design.

Bar Identification and Detailing should include the information used to distinguish bar sizes, shapes, locations, quantities, and fabrication requirements. The CRSI Manual of Standard Practice can help candidates become more familiar with industry conventions.

Reinforcing Drawings should be studied carefully. Candidates should practice reading plans and details showing slabs, beams, walls, columns, footings, reinforcing mats, openings, additional reinforcement, and other structural conditions.

Plan-reading practice is particularly valuable for a closed-book examination because candidates need to recognize reinforcing relationships without relying on a reference during the test.

Concrete Cover should be understood as part of reinforcing placement. The location of reinforcing steel within a concrete member affects protection, durability, and structural behavior. Candidates should understand why bars cannot simply be positioned wherever installation is easiest.

Field Coordination is another important topic. Reinforcement frequently intersects with formwork, sleeves, conduits, embedded items, anchor bolts, openings, and other trades. Candidates should understand the need to follow the design while coordinating conflicts appropriately.

Material Handling should be reviewed because reinforcing bars are heavy, long, and difficult to move safely. Bundled reinforcing steel may require cranes or other equipment, and improper handling can expose workers to crushing, struck-by, pinch-point, and lifting hazards.

Post-Tensioning should receive dedicated study time because it is specifically included in the C-14A scope. Candidates should understand the basic purpose of post-tensioning and how tendons are used to apply compressive forces to concrete.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Study the basic components of an unbonded post-tensioning system, including the tendon, prestressing steel, protective sheathing, anchorages, wedges, stressing equipment, and related hardware.

Tendon Installation should include placement, support, routing, coordination, protection, and preparation for concrete placement. Tendons must be maintained in the intended profile and protected from damage before stressing.

Stressing Operations require particular attention to procedure and safety. Candidates should understand the purpose of stressing, the role of anchors and wedges, and why personnel must remain clear of hazardous areas during stressing operations.

Post-Tensioning Safety should be treated as a major study subject. High forces are involved, and improper procedures can create serious hazards. Candidates should understand controlled work areas, equipment condition, worker positioning, communication, and safe handling practices.

OSHA Construction Safety should be integrated throughout the study process. Reinforcing-steel work can involve elevated surfaces, floor openings, protruding reinforcing bars, material handling, cranes, tools, formwork, concrete placement, and fall exposure.

Fall Protection deserves attention because reinforcing-steel installers often work on elevated walls, decks, columns, and structural members. Candidates should understand basic fall-hazard recognition and the importance of suitable protection and access.

Impalement Hazards should also be reviewed. Protruding reinforcing steel can create severe hazards when workers are exposed to falls or movement around unfinished reinforcing assemblies.

Personal Protective Equipment should be understood in relation to the task being performed. Reinforcing work may require appropriate head, eye, hand, foot, and fall protection depending on field conditions.

Because the examination is closed book, candidates should gradually shift away from reading and toward active recall. Study a topic, close the reference, and explain the key steps, terminology, or safety requirements from memory.

Flashcards can help with bar terminology, post-tensioning components, safety concepts, and common field practices. Practice questions can help determine whether the information is being retained well enough to use without a reference.

A useful study sequence is to move from reinforcing fundamentals to bar placement, drawings, supports, ties, splices, post-tensioning, field safety, and then mixed practice. This allows candidates to build a foundation before combining subjects in exam-style review.

How does the Reinforcing Steel Contractor (C-14A) exam work?

The Reinforcing Steel Contractor (C-14A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
