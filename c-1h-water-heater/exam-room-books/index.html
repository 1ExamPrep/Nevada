State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-1H as the Water Heaters subclassification under the broader C-1 Plumbing and Heating classification.

The C-1H scope covers the installation, replacement, and repair of water heaters.

The classification includes connection to existing lines for potable water and gas, the necessary venting, and other incidental connections associated with the water heater.

The scope makes C-1H a specialty classification rather than an unrestricted plumbing classification. Candidates should understand the work directly associated with water heater installation and the limits of the classification.

Typical C-1H work can involve potable water piping, shutoff valves, gas connections, venting, drainage, safety devices, temperature and pressure protection, equipment placement, and final inspection of the completed installation.

The C-1H trade examination is one component of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business, financial, and administrative requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-1H study plan should begin with the basic water heater installation process. Candidates should understand how a water heater is positioned, connected, vented when applicable, protected, and placed into service.

Water heater code provisions should be reviewed regularly in the Uniform Plumbing Code. Candidates should practice locating the applicable section rather than only reading it once during preparation.

Potable water connections are directly included within the C-1H scope. Candidates should understand how water heaters connect to existing potable water lines and how valves, fittings, and piping materials relate to those installations.

Piping materials should be studied from both a practical and code perspective. Candidates should understand why material selection and compatibility matter in water heater installations.

Valves should receive careful attention. Review the role of shutoff valves and other devices associated with water heater operation and service.

Temperature and pressure protection is another important water heater topic. Candidates should understand the purpose of safety devices and why they must be installed correctly.

Drainage and discharge should also be part of preparation. Water heater systems may include components that require proper discharge arrangements, and candidates should understand how those conditions relate to plumbing-code requirements.

Gas connections are included within the C-1H scope when connecting a water heater to an existing gas line. Candidates should understand the relationship among equipment connection, shutoff, safe operation, and venting.

Venting is an important area for fuel-fired water heaters. Candidates should understand why proper venting is necessary and how incorrect venting can affect safe equipment operation.

Equipment placement should also be reviewed because the location of a water heater can affect access, serviceability, venting, drainage, and final connection work.

The Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018 should be treated as both a study reference and an exam-navigation tool. Candidates should practice using the index instead of relying on broad page-by-page searching.

Definitions can affect how a code requirement is interpreted. When a technical term is unclear, locating the definition first can make the related code section easier to understand.

Tables may contain important requirements that are not repeated in surrounding text. Candidates should practice reading table headings, notes, and conditions carefully.

Exceptions can modify general requirements. Candidates should read the surrounding code language before deciding whether a provision applies to the installation described in a question.

Cross-references may direct the reader to another section containing additional requirements. Following those references during preparation can make the process more familiar on test day.

Timed code-navigation drills can help candidates prepare for the 45-minute exam. Choose a topic such as water heaters, potable water, valves, drainage, piping, or safety devices and practice locating the applicable provision within a limited amount of time.

Because candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and fifteen seconds per question, unnecessary searching can quickly reduce available time.

A useful study routine is to divide preparation between retained water heater knowledge and UPC navigation practice. One study session might focus on technical water heater concepts, while another focuses on locating the applicable code requirements.

Practice questions should be used to identify weak areas. When a question is missed, determine whether the problem came from weak technical knowledge, unfamiliar terminology, slow code navigation, or difficulty interpreting the requirement.

Return to the relevant UPC section and study the concept rather than memorizing only one answer. This creates stronger preparation for unfamiliar exam questions.

Mixed-topic practice is useful once individual subjects become familiar. Alternate between water heater installation questions, potable water questions, safety-device questions, and targeted code lookups.

Time management should also be incorporated into preparation. Answer familiar questions efficiently, use the code for targeted confirmation, and avoid spending too much time on a single question.

How does the Water Heater Contractor (C-1H) exam work?

The Water Heater Contractor (C-1H) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
