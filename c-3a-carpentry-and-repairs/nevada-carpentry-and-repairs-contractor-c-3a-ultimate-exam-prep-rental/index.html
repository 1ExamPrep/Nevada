Exam Details

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs Contractor trade examination is administered through PSI for applicants who have received examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs Contractor
  • Number of Questions: 46
  • Time Allowed: 2 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 33 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The current C-3A examination content outline is divided into six major subject areas:

  • Safety: 6 questions
  • Windows and Doors: 3 questions
  • Rough Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Finish Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Siding: 3 questions
  • Drywall: 2 questions

Rough Carpentry and Finish Carpentry are the largest sections of the examination, with 16 questions each. Together, they account for 32 of the 46 questions, making them the most important areas to prioritize during preparation.

Rough Carpentry study can include form work, framing systems, floor framing, wall framing, roof framing, trusses, sheathing, subflooring, exterior stairs, railings, structural components, materials, layout, measurements, fasteners, and common construction techniques.

Finish Carpentry can include doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, trim, measurement, fitting, alignment, fastening, finish materials, and installation procedures. Candidates should understand both the practical steps involved in finish work and the terminology associated with common carpentry applications.

Safety represents six questions. Candidates should review construction hazards, personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, fall protection, tools, material handling, and other jobsite safety topics. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports preparation for these subjects even though it is not allowed in the C-3A examination room.

Windows and Doors and Siding each contribute three questions, while Drywall contributes two. These smaller categories should remain part of a complete study plan because every correct answer contributes toward the required passing score.

Nevada contractor applicants generally must also satisfy the Contractor Management Survey requirement. The Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada is included with this Ultimate package to support preparation for contractor management and Business and Law subjects.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs Contractor examination is an open-book test, but only the International Building Code, 2018 is approved for use inside the examination room.

Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved copy of the International Building Code. The approved reference may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used, while temporary removable tabs such as Post-it notes are not permitted.

Candidate-added writing is not permitted in the exam-room reference, and loose papers or unauthorized materials may not be inserted into the book. Candidates should prepare the reference in advance and become familiar with the organization of the code before test day.

The International Building Code should be used regularly during study sessions so candidates become comfortable with the table of contents, index, chapter organization, numbered sections, tables, and cross-references. Efficient code navigation can help candidates avoid spending too much time searching during the examination.

The other technical references included with this package are not permitted in the C-3A exam room. Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA), Carpentry and Building Construction, and Finish Carpenter's Manual are study-only references for the trade examination.

This means candidates should approach preparation in two ways. Code-based topics should be practiced with the International Building Code, while trade-knowledge subjects should be studied deeply enough that the information can be recalled without access to the supporting carpentry and safety references.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the C-3A Carpentry and Repairs contractor classification. Applicants must complete the applicable experience, application, examination, financial, and licensing requirements before a contractor license can be issued.

  1. Confirm the C-3A classification. Determine that Carpentry and Repairs matches the scope of work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The applicant must designate a person who meets the experience and technical qualifications for the C-3A classification.
  3. Document qualifying carpentry experience. The trade qualifier must establish the required experience within the time period recognized by the Nevada State Contractors Board.
  4. Prepare the contractor license application. Submit the required business, qualifier, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Complete applicable fingerprint and background requirements. Individuals subject to the licensing process must complete the required background procedures.
  6. Provide financial documentation. Nevada reviews financial responsibility as part of the licensing process and uses financial information when establishing the license monetary limit.
  7. Receive examination eligibility. After the Board accepts the application qualifications, the candidate may proceed with required testing.
  8. Schedule the C-3A trade exam through PSI. Use examination eligibility information to arrange the trade examination.
  9. Prepare for the C-3A examination. Study Rough Carpentry, Finish Carpentry, Safety, Windows and Doors, Siding, Drywall, and International Building Code navigation.
  10. Prepare for the Contractor Management Survey. Use the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada to study the applicable Business and Law topics.
  11. Complete the required examinations. Meet the examination requirements applicable to the contractor license.
  12. Complete final licensing requirements. Satisfy the applicable bond, financial, and other requirements established before license issuance.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs classification includes form work, framing, and rough carpentry used to construct framed structures. It also includes repairs and installation of individual components of framing systems and associated carpentry work.

The classification covers subflooring, siding, exterior staircases and railings, roof decking, truss members, sheathing, doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, and related structural and finish carpentry activities within the authorized scope.

The trade qualifying individual for a standard Nevada contractor license generally must have at least four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the requested classification. The qualifying experience must have been obtained within the 15 years immediately preceding the application.

Accepted education from an accredited college, university, or recognized training program may satisfy a portion of the experience requirement when the education meets Nevada State Contractors Board requirements.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes financial-responsibility requirements. Financial documentation is used in determining the monetary limit under which the contractor may operate.

A contractor bond is required as part of the licensing process, with the amount established by the Nevada State Contractors Board. The contractor license is issued to the business entity, while the qualifying individual fulfills the applicable trade or management qualification role.

Nevada contractor applicants generally must satisfy both trade and management qualification requirements. A single individual may fulfill both roles when qualified, or separate individuals may serve as trade and management qualifiers.
