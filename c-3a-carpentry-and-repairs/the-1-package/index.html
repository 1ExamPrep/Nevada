Exam Details

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs Contractor trade examination is administered through PSI for applicants who receive examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs Contractor
  • Number of Questions: 46
  • Time Allowed: 2 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 33 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The C-3A examination is organized around six major subject areas:

  • Safety: 6 questions
  • Windows and Doors: 3 questions
  • Rough Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Finish Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Siding: 3 questions
  • Drywall: 2 questions

Rough Carpentry and Finish Carpentry are the two largest examination categories, with 16 questions each. Together they make up most of the trade exam, so candidates should devote substantial study time to framing, construction methods, finish work, measurements, materials, installation techniques, and related carpentry knowledge.

Rough Carpentry preparation can include form work, wall framing, floor framing, roof framing, truss systems, sheathing, subflooring, exterior stairs, railings, structural components, layout, measurements, fasteners, and common construction practices.

Finish Carpentry preparation can include doors, windows, trim, cabinets, fixtures, fitting, fastening, alignment, finish materials, measurements, and installation procedures. Candidates should develop an understanding of both terminology and practical methods.

Safety accounts for six questions and should be treated as a separate study category. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports preparation involving construction hazards, personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, fall protection, tools, and material handling.

Windows and Doors, Siding, and Drywall contribute fewer questions individually, but each remains important to the final result. A balanced study plan should cover all six exam categories while assigning the most time to Rough Carpentry and Finish Carpentry.

Nevada contractor applicants also prepare for the Contractor Management Survey examination. The Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada included with The 1 Package supports preparation for the business, licensing, management, and contractor-law portion of the licensing process.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs Contractor trade examination is open book, but only the International Building Code, 2018 is approved for use in the examination room.

Candidates should become comfortable using the International Building Code before test day. Preparation should include navigating the table of contents, index, chapters, numbered sections, tables, exceptions, and related code provisions so information can be located efficiently during a timed examination.

The Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA), Carpentry and Building Construction, and Finish Carpenter's Manual are study materials and are not allowed in the C-3A trade examination room.

The distinction makes C-3A preparation a combination of open-book code work and closed-reference trade knowledge. Candidates should practice locating building-code provisions with the IBC while studying carpentry techniques, safety concepts, and practical construction topics until they can be recalled without relying on the supporting books.

Reference-navigation practice should be deliberate. Instead of simply reading the International Building Code from beginning to end, candidates can practice identifying an exam topic, choosing a likely index term, locating the correct chapter or section, and confirming the requirement efficiently.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs subclassification authorizes carpentry work that includes form work, framing or rough carpentry necessary to construct framed structures, installation and repair of framing-system components, subflooring, siding, exterior staircases and railings, roof decking, truss members, and sheathing.

The classification also includes fabrication, installation, and repair of wood and wood products used for structural and finishing purposes, including doors, windows, cabinets, and fixtures. Nevada's current classification language also addresses certain door, window, frame, stud, and similar products made from materials that emulate traditional milled wood and can be installed using traditional carpentry joining methods.

A standard Nevada contractor applicant must establish the qualifications of the designated trade qualifier. Qualifying experience is generally based on relevant work as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the classification being requested.

The standard experience pathway generally requires four years of qualifying experience within the 15 years immediately preceding the application. Education or approved training may satisfy a portion of the experience requirement when accepted under Nevada licensing rules.

Nevada contractor licensing also addresses financial responsibility. Financial information is considered when the Nevada State Contractors Board establishes the monetary limit associated with a contractor license.

Contractor bonding is also part of the licensing process. The required bond is established as part of the Board's licensing determination. Contractors must maintain applicable licensing and business requirements while operating under the issued license.

How does the Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) exam work?

The Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
