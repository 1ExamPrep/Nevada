Exam Details

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs trade examination is administered through PSI for eligible candidates pursuing the classification through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs
  • Number of Questions: 46
  • Time Allowed: 2 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 33 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination covers the following subject areas:

  • Safety: 6 questions
  • Windows and Doors: 3 questions
  • Rough Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Finish Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Siding: 3 questions
  • Drywall: 2 questions

Rough Carpentry and Finish Carpentry are the two largest examination categories, with 16 questions each. Together, they account for most of the test. Candidates should be prepared for framing, structural carpentry, sheathing, floors, walls, roofs, doors, windows, cabinets, trim, fixtures, measurements, materials, and practical installation methods.

Safety is another important area with six questions. Although safety references are not part of this Books Allowed into Exam Package, candidates should still prepare thoroughly for common construction hazards, ladders, scaffolds, tools, personal protective equipment, fall hazards, and other jobsite safety subjects.

Windows and Doors and Siding each account for three questions. These areas can involve installation, repair, alignment, fastening, framing around openings, exterior weather protection, and code-related requirements.

Drywall accounts for two questions. Candidates should understand basic drywall installation relationships, framing coordination, repair principles, and materials even though this is a smaller exam category.

With 46 questions and two hours available, candidates have an average of approximately two and a half minutes per question. Effective exam preparation should include timed reference-navigation practice so that code searches do not consume too much of the available testing time.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs subclassification covers formwork, framing, and rough carpentry necessary to construct framed structures. It also includes installation and repair of individual framing-system components and related carpentry work.

Authorized work within the classification includes qualifying subflooring, siding, exterior staircases and railings, roof decking, truss members, sheathing, doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, and related carpentry products and repairs.

The classification also recognizes certain doors, door frames, windows, window frames, studs, and similar members made from wood, formed sheet metal, vinyl, aluminum, and comparable materials when they emulate traditional milled wood and can be installed using traditional carpentry joining methods without welding or similar joining processes.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must have four years of qualifying experience within the applicable experience period required by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

Applicants should maintain organized documentation describing the work performed, responsibilities held, and the period during which the experience was obtained. Experience should relate directly to the C-3A classification.

Additional Nevada contractor licensing requirements can include management qualification, financial responsibility review, contractor bonding, background procedures, and other Board requirements. Passing the C-3A trade examination alone does not result in automatic issuance of a contractor license.

How does the Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) exam work?

The Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
