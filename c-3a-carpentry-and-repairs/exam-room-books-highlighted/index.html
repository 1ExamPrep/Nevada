Exam Details

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs trade examination is administered through PSI for eligible candidates pursuing the classification through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs
  • Number of Questions: 46
  • Time Allowed: 2 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 33 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination covers the following subject areas:

  • Safety: 6 questions
  • Windows and Doors: 3 questions
  • Rough Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Finish Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Siding: 3 questions
  • Drywall: 2 questions

Rough Carpentry and Finish Carpentry are the largest categories, accounting for 32 of the 46 exam questions. Candidates should spend substantial preparation time reviewing framing, walls, floors, roofs, sheathing, subflooring, doors, windows, cabinets, trim, fixtures, measurements, materials, layouts, fastening, and installation principles.

Safety includes six questions and should also receive dedicated study. Candidates should understand common construction hazards, safe tool use, ladders, scaffolds, fall protection, personal protective equipment, material handling, and other safety principles associated with carpentry work.

Windows and Doors and Siding each account for three questions. Preparation should include framing around openings, installation, alignment, fastening, repair concepts, weather protection, and applicable building-code requirements.

Drywall represents two questions. Although this is the smallest individual category, candidates should still understand basic drywall installation relationships, framing coordination, and common repair principles.

With 46 questions and two hours available, candidates have an average of a little more than two and a half minutes per question. Efficient pacing and effective use of the approved code book can be important when a question requires a specific building-code lookup.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs subclassification covers the construction of formwork, framing, and rough carpentry necessary to construct framed structures. It also includes installation or repair of individual components of framing systems and related carpentry work.

Authorized work includes qualifying subflooring, siding, exterior staircases and railings, roof decking, truss members, sheathing, doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, and other structural or finish carpentry products within the scope of the classification.

The classification also includes certain doors, door frames, windows, window frames, studs, and similar components made from wood, formed sheet metal, vinyl, aluminum, and similar materials when they emulate traditional milled wood and can be installed using traditional carpentry joining methods without welding or comparable joining processes.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual generally must document four years of qualifying experience within the applicable experience period required by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

Applicants should maintain organized records describing the carpentry work performed, responsibilities held, and the period during which the experience was obtained. Experience should relate directly to the classification being requested.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification, financial responsibility review, background requirements, contractor bonding, and other licensing conditions. Passing the C-3A trade examination alone does not automatically result in license issuance.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-3A study plan should combine practical carpentry knowledge with efficient use of the International Building Code. The highlighted and tabbed book is most useful when candidates practice with it repeatedly before test day.

Prioritize rough carpentry. Review framing systems, wall construction, floor systems, roof framing, sheathing, subflooring, truss members, structural components, measurements, layouts, fastening methods, and related trade concepts.

Strengthen finish carpentry knowledge. Study doors, windows, trim, cabinetry, fixtures, fitting, measurements, materials, and detail-oriented installation methods.

Practice code navigation. Use the International Building Code to find requirements related to structures, roofs, exterior walls, stairs, guards, openings, doors, windows, and other relevant building subjects.

Review windows and doors. Candidates should understand opening preparation, framing, installation, fastening, alignment, repair, and related code concerns.

Study siding. Review exterior siding installation, fastening, weather protection, substrate conditions, alignment, and repair concepts.

Prepare safety separately. Safety is a dedicated exam category. Candidates should review construction hazards and safe work practices even though this exam-room package contains only the approved IBC reference.

Include drywall review. Basic drywall installation, framing relationships, materials, and repair concepts should be included in the study plan.

Use timed reference searches. Practice locating information within a set time limit. This can help candidates avoid spending too much time on a single code question during the actual examination.

Know when to rely on trade knowledge. The International Building Code is a tool, not a substitute for carpentry experience and study. Candidates should avoid unnecessary searches when they already know the answer.

How does the Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) exam work?

The Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
