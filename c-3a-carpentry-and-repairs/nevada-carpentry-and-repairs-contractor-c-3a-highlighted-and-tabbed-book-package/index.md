## Exam Details

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs trade examination is administered through PSI for eligible candidates pursuing the classification through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

- **Exam:** Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs
- **Number of Questions:** 46
- **Time Allowed:** 2 hours
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 33 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The examination content is divided into the following areas:

- **Safety:** 6 questions
- **Windows and Doors:** 3 questions
- **Rough Carpentry:** 16 questions
- **Finish Carpentry:** 16 questions
- **Siding:** 3 questions
- **Drywall:** 2 questions

**Rough Carpentry** and **Finish Carpentry** dominate the examination with 16 questions each. Together they account for 32 of the 46 questions, making these two subjects the core of C-3A preparation.

Rough carpentry study should include framing systems, floor and wall construction, roof framing, sheathing, subflooring, truss-related concepts, measurements, layouts, fastening, materials, and general structural carpentry practices.

Finish carpentry preparation should include doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, trim, millwork, measurements, fitting, fastening, finishing details, tools, and practical installation principles. Finish Carpenter's Manual provides focused study material for this portion of the trade.

Safety accounts for six questions. Candidates should prepare for common construction-site hazards and safe work practices involving ladders, fall protection, tools, personal protective equipment, material handling, and other carpentry-related safety concerns.

Windows and Doors and Siding each account for three questions, while Drywall accounts for two. These smaller categories should still receive dedicated study time because each correct answer contributes toward the required passing score.

## State Requirements

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs subclassification includes the construction of formwork, framing, and rough carpentry necessary to construct framed structures, along with installation or repair of individual framing-system components and associated carpentry work.

The authorized scope includes qualifying subflooring, siding, exterior staircases and railings, roof decking, truss members, sheathing, and fabrication, installation, remodeling, and repair of wood and wood products for structural and finishing purposes, including doors, windows, cabinets, and fixtures.

The classification also accommodates certain doors, door frames, windows, window frames, studs, and similar components manufactured from materials that emulate traditional milled wood when they can be installed using traditional carpentry joining methods without welding or similar joining processes.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualifier must generally document at least four years of qualifying experience within the 15 years immediately preceding the filing of the contractor license application.

Experience should relate to the classification requested and demonstrate practical work at the qualifying level. Applicants should maintain organized records describing the work performed, responsibilities held, and the period during which the experience was obtained.

Nevada licensing requirements extend beyond the C-3A examination. The licensing process also includes management qualification, application review, financial responsibility requirements, bonding, background procedures, and other requirements administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

## How does the Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) exam work?

The Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
