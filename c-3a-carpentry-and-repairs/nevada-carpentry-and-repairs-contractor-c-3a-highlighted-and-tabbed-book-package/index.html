Exam Details

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs trade examination is administered through PSI for eligible candidates pursuing the classification through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs
  • Number of Questions: 46
  • Time Allowed: 2 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 33 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination content is divided into the following areas:

  • Safety: 6 questions
  • Windows and Doors: 3 questions
  • Rough Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Finish Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Siding: 3 questions
  • Drywall: 2 questions

Rough Carpentry and Finish Carpentry dominate the examination with 16 questions each. Together they account for 32 of the 46 questions, making these two subjects the core of C-3A preparation.

Rough carpentry study should include framing systems, floor and wall construction, roof framing, sheathing, subflooring, truss-related concepts, measurements, layouts, fastening, materials, and general structural carpentry practices.

Finish carpentry preparation should include doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, trim, millwork, measurements, fitting, fastening, finishing details, tools, and practical installation principles. Finish Carpenter's Manual provides focused study material for this portion of the trade.

Safety accounts for six questions. Candidates should prepare for common construction-site hazards and safe work practices involving ladders, fall protection, tools, personal protective equipment, material handling, and other carpentry-related safety concerns.

Windows and Doors and Siding each account for three questions, while Drywall accounts for two. These smaller categories should still receive dedicated study time because each correct answer contributes toward the required passing score.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs examination is an open-book test. The approved exam-room reference included in this highlighted and tabbed package is:

  • International Building Code, 2018

The International Building Code can be used during the examination, making code-navigation practice an important part of C-3A preparation. Candidates should learn the general structure of the IBC and become comfortable using chapters, sections, definitions, tables, and the index to locate information efficiently.

Useful navigation practice can include requirements related to framing, structural construction, roof assemblies, exterior walls, openings, stairs, guards, doors, windows, and other building provisions associated with carpentry and repair work.

The PSI examination rules permit approved reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not permitted. This package is prepared as a highlighted and tabbed set to support organized study and reference navigation.

Licensing Steps

Passing the Nevada C-3A trade examination is one part of the overall contractor licensing process. Candidates pursuing the classification should understand the broader steps associated with Nevada licensure.

  1. Confirm the C-3A classification. Determine that Carpentry and Repairs corresponds with the work the contracting business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the Trade Qualifier. Designate the individual who will satisfy the applicable technical experience and examination requirements.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare the required work-experience documentation for the C-3A classification.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor license application. Organize business information, qualifier information, experience records, financial documents, and other required materials.
  5. Submit the application. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the application and verifies experience before issuing examination eligibility.
  6. Receive examination authorization. Candidates may schedule the required exam after the Board establishes eligibility.
  7. Schedule the C-3A examination through PSI. Use the examination eligibility information to arrange testing.
  8. Prepare for the trade examination. Study rough carpentry, finish carpentry, safety, windows and doors, siding, drywall, and International Building Code navigation.
  9. Prepare for Nevada Business and Law. Nevada generally requires the applicable construction management examination in addition to the classification-specific trade exam unless a qualifying waiver applies.
  10. Complete applicable licensing requirements. Address the remaining financial responsibility, bonding, background, and Board requirements associated with license issuance.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs subclassification includes the construction of formwork, framing, and rough carpentry necessary to construct framed structures, along with installation or repair of individual framing-system components and associated carpentry work.

The authorized scope includes qualifying subflooring, siding, exterior staircases and railings, roof decking, truss members, sheathing, and fabrication, installation, remodeling, and repair of wood and wood products for structural and finishing purposes, including doors, windows, cabinets, and fixtures.

The classification also accommodates certain doors, door frames, windows, window frames, studs, and similar components manufactured from materials that emulate traditional milled wood when they can be installed using traditional carpentry joining methods without welding or similar joining processes.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualifier must generally document at least four years of qualifying experience within the 15 years immediately preceding the filing of the contractor license application.

Experience should relate to the classification requested and demonstrate practical work at the qualifying level. Applicants should maintain organized records describing the work performed, responsibilities held, and the period during which the experience was obtained.

Nevada licensing requirements extend beyond the C-3A examination. The licensing process also includes management qualification, application review, financial responsibility requirements, bonding, background procedures, and other requirements administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong C-3A study plan should place the greatest emphasis on Rough Carpentry and Finish Carpentry because those two categories represent most of the examination.

Prioritize rough carpentry. Review framing, floors, walls, roofs, sheathing, subfloors, trusses, layouts, measurements, fastening, materials, and structural carpentry concepts.

Strengthen finish carpentry. Study doors, windows, trim, cabinets, fixtures, fitting, measurements, millwork, tools, and installation details using the Finish Carpenter's Manual and Carpentry and Building Construction.

Learn safety material for recall. OSHA is not allowed in the testing center, so candidates should develop familiarity with construction safety concepts before exam day.

Practice IBC navigation. Use the highlighted and tabbed International Building Code regularly and practice locating carpentry-related requirements within a limited amount of time.

Review doors, windows, and siding. Study installation, framing around openings, fastening, alignment, exterior weather protection, and repair principles.

Include drywall in the study plan. Although only two questions are assigned to drywall, candidates should understand basic installation relationships and framing coordination.

Use timed practice. With 46 questions in two hours, candidates should develop a steady pace and learn when a code lookup is justified.

How does the Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) exam work?

The Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
