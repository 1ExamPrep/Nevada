Exam Details

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs Contractor trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who have received examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs Contractor
  • Number of Questions: 46
  • Minimum Passing Score: 33 correct answers
  • Time Allowed: 2 hours
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination is divided into six major subject areas:

  • Safety: 6 questions
  • Windows and Doors: 3 questions
  • Rough Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Finish Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Siding: 3 questions
  • Drywall: 2 questions

Rough Carpentry and Finish Carpentry are the largest sections, with 16 questions each. Together they account for 32 of the 46 questions on the exam. Candidates should make these two categories the core of their preparation while still reviewing the smaller subjects that contribute to the passing score.

Rough Carpentry preparation can include form work, framing systems, wall framing, floor framing, roof framing, trusses, roof decking, sheathing, subflooring, structural components, exterior stairs, railings, layout, materials, measurements, fasteners, and common construction techniques.

Finish Carpentry preparation can include doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, trim, measurements, fitting, alignment, fastening, installation methods, finishing practices, and the proper use of carpentry tools and materials.

Safety represents six questions. Candidates should understand construction-site safety involving personal protective equipment, fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, tools, material handling, and other hazards encountered during carpentry work.

Windows and Doors and Siding contain three questions each, while Drywall contains two. Although these categories are smaller, candidates should include them in the overall preparation schedule rather than focusing exclusively on the two largest sections.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs Contractor examination is an open-book test, but only the International Building Code, 2018 is approved for use inside the trade examination room.

Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved reference to the examination center. The International Building Code may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used to help organize important sections and improve navigation.

Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not permitted. References may not contain candidate-added writing, and loose or attached papers are not permitted with the approved exam-room book. Candidates may not add highlighting or writing during the examination session.

Because the International Building Code is the only listed reference permitted inside the C-3A trade exam, candidates should practice navigating it under timed conditions. Learn how to use the index, table of contents, chapter organization, numbered sections, tables, and cross-references so code questions can be researched efficiently.

The Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA), Carpentry and Building Construction, and Finish Carpenter's Manual are not allowed in the examination room. These references should be used to build trade knowledge before testing rather than as exam-day lookup tools.

The creates two distinct preparation needs. Candidates should develop fast IBC navigation for code-related questions while studying practical carpentry and safety material deeply enough to answer those questions from knowledge.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the C-3A Carpentry and Repairs contractor classification. Candidates pursuing licensure must complete the applicable application, experience, examination, financial, and licensing requirements.

  1. Determine that C-3A is the appropriate classification. Review the Carpentry and Repairs scope and confirm that it matches the work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor applicant must designate an individual with the required experience and technical qualifications for the C-3A classification.
  3. Document qualifying work experience. Prepare acceptable records supporting the qualifier's experience in carpentry and repairs work.
  4. Submit the Nevada contractor application. Provide the Nevada State Contractors Board with the applicable business, qualifier, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Complete background requirements. Follow the fingerprint and background procedures required during the contractor application process.
  6. Address financial responsibility. Submit the financial information required for the requested contractor license and monetary limit.
  7. Receive examination eligibility. After the application and experience are reviewed, eligible candidates receive authorization to proceed with required testing.
  8. Schedule the C-3A examination through PSI. Use the eligibility information to arrange the trade examination.
  9. Prepare for the trade exam. Study Rough Carpentry, Finish Carpentry, Safety, Windows and Doors, Siding, Drywall, and International Building Code navigation.
  10. Complete the Contractor Management Survey requirement. Nevada contractor applicants generally must also satisfy the applicable Business and Law examination requirement unless an approved waiver applies.
  11. Complete final licensing requirements. Satisfy applicable bond, financial, and other requirements before the contractor license is issued.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs classification authorizes the construction of form work, framing, and rough carpentry necessary to construct framed structures. It also includes installation or repair of individual components of framing systems and related rough carpentry work.

The classification includes the construction or installation of subflooring, siding, exterior staircases and railings, roof decking, truss members, and sheathing. It also covers fabrication, installation, remodeling, and repair of wood and wood products for structural and finishing purposes, including doors, windows, cabinets, and fixtures.

Doors, door frames, windows, window frames, studs, and similar members within the classification may include certain materials that emulate traditional milled wood and can be installed using traditional carpentry joining methods such as glue, nails, screws, and bolts without requiring welding.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, a trade qualifying individual generally must demonstrate four years of qualifying experience in the classification within the 15 years immediately preceding the application. Qualifying experience can include work performed as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification requirements. Applicants generally must satisfy the Contractor Management Survey requirement in addition to the trade examination unless an applicable examination waiver applies.

Financial responsibility is also evaluated during licensing. The Nevada State Contractors Board uses applicable financial information when determining the monetary limit associated with the contractor license.

A contractor bond is required before final license issuance, with the amount determined through the licensing process. Applicants should also complete the applicable business-registration and background requirements associated with their Nevada contractor application.
