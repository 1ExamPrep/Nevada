About this Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) package

Prepare for the Nevada Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) trade examination with a focused book package covering building-code requirements, rough and finish carpentry, framing, construction safety, doors and windows, siding, roof construction, and repair work associated with the C-3A classification.

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs subclassification is regulated by the Nevada State Contractors Board. The classification covers formwork, framing and rough carpentry necessary to construct framed structures, installation and repair of framing-system components, subflooring, siding, exterior staircases and railings, roof decking, truss members, sheathing, doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, and other qualifying carpentry and repair work.

The C-3A scope also includes qualifying doors, door frames, windows, window frames, studs, and similar members made from wood, formed sheet metal, vinyl, aluminum, and similar products when they emulate traditional milled wood and can be installed using traditional carpentry joining methods without welding or similar joining processes.

The exam book package includes the International Building Code, 2018, which is the approved exam-room reference used for this preparation. The package also includes Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA), Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016, and Finish Carpenter's Manual by Jim Tolpin, 1993 as technical study references.

The OSHA, Carpentry and Building Construction, and Finish Carpenter's Manual references are not allowed into the C-3A trade examination. They should be used during preparation to build knowledge that can be recalled during testing. Candidates should become comfortable with framing, carpentry installation, safety practices, materials, doors and windows, siding, roof construction, finishing principles, and related trade concepts before exam day.

Because the examination is open book with the International Building Code, effective preparation should combine hands-on carpentry knowledge with efficient code navigation. Candidates should know when a question can be answered from trade knowledge and when the IBC should be used to confirm a specific requirement.

Exam Details

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs trade examination is administered through PSI for eligible candidates pursuing the classification through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs
  • Number of Questions: 46
  • Time Allowed: 2 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 33 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination covers the following major areas:

  • Safety: 6 questions
  • Windows and Doors: 3 questions
  • Rough Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Finish Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Siding: 3 questions
  • Drywall: 2 questions

Rough Carpentry and Finish Carpentry are the two largest exam categories, with 16 questions each. Together, these categories account for most of the trade examination. Candidates should devote substantial preparation time to framing systems, roof and floor construction, sheathing, structural carpentry, trim work, doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, measurements, layouts, materials, and practical installation methods.

Safety accounts for six questions. Candidates should review common construction hazards, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, power tools, material handling, fall protection, and other safety topics associated with carpentry work.

Windows and Doors and Siding each contain three questions. Candidates should understand installation and repair principles, framing around openings, alignment, fastening, weather protection, exterior construction, and relevant code requirements.

Drywall accounts for two questions. Even though this is the smallest category, candidates should understand basic drywall materials, installation relationships, framing coordination, and repair concepts.

With 46 questions and two hours available, candidates have an average of approximately two and a half minutes per question. Study sessions should include timed practice so candidates can develop a comfortable pace before test day.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs examination is an open-book test. The approved exam-room reference used for this preparation is:

  • International Building Code, 2018

The International Building Code should be used regularly throughout preparation. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of the book and practice locating requirements involving building construction, framing, structural components, exterior walls, doors, windows, stairs, guards, roof assemblies, and other topics related to the C-3A scope.

Open-book testing does not mean every answer should be searched during the examination. Candidates who understand the trade can answer many practical carpentry questions directly and reserve the IBC for questions that require a specific code requirement or technical confirmation.

The following books are not allowed into the C-3A trade examination:

  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
  • Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016
  • Finish Carpenter's Manual, Jim Tolpin, 1993

Because these references cannot be used during the exam, candidates should use them to develop knowledge for recall. Safety procedures, framing terminology, installation methods, finish carpentry techniques, tools, materials, measurements, and construction practices should become familiar before testing.

Carpentry and Building Construction is especially useful for strengthening broad trade knowledge across rough and finish carpentry. Finish Carpenter's Manual supports preparation involving trim, doors, windows, cabinetry, millwork, and detail-oriented carpentry work. OSHA supports the safety portion of the examination.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the C-3A Carpentry and Repairs subclassification. Passing the trade examination is one part of the complete contractor licensing process.

  1. Confirm the C-3A classification. Determine that Carpentry and Repairs matches the type of contracting work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. A qualifying party must be designated for the business seeking the contractor license.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare records showing applicable experience in carpentry and repair work within the requested classification.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor license application. Organize business information, qualifier information, experience documentation, financial information, and required supporting materials.
  5. Submit the application. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the applicant's qualifications before examination eligibility is established.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow fingerprint and background procedures required by the Nevada licensing process.
  7. Address financial responsibility requirements. Provide applicable financial documentation required by the Board.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Qualified applicants can proceed with testing after authorization.
  9. Schedule the C-3A trade examination. Arrange the Carpentry and Repairs test through PSI after receiving eligibility.
  10. Prepare for the trade exam. Study rough carpentry, finish carpentry, safety, windows and doors, siding, drywall, and International Building Code navigation.
  11. Prepare for Nevada Business and Law. Nevada contractor applicants generally must satisfy the applicable Contractor Management Survey and Business and Law examination requirement unless an approved waiver applies.
  12. Complete applicable examinations. Meet trade and management qualification requirements.
  13. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Address applicable bonding, financial responsibility, and other Nevada State Contractors Board requirements before license issuance.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs subclassification covers the construction of formwork, framing, and rough carpentry necessary to construct framed structures. It also includes installation or repair of individual components of framing systems and associated rough carpentry work.

Examples within the classification include subflooring, siding, exterior staircases and railings, roof decking, truss members, sheathing, doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, and other qualifying structural or finish carpentry products.

The current Nevada classification language also recognizes certain doors, door frames, windows, window frames, studs, and similar members manufactured from wood, formed sheet metal, vinyl, aluminum, and comparable products when they emulate traditional milled wood and can be installed using traditional carpentry joinery without welding or similar joining methods.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document at least four years of qualifying experience within the 10 years immediately preceding the application.

Qualifying experience should relate directly to the classification requested. Applicants should maintain organized documentation showing the type of carpentry work performed, responsibilities held, and the period during which the experience was obtained.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes examination, financial responsibility, background, and bonding requirements. Passing the C-3A trade examination alone does not automatically result in issuance of a contractor license.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-3A study plan should reflect the examination weighting. Rough Carpentry and Finish Carpentry together represent 32 of the 46 exam questions, so these two areas should receive the greatest amount of preparation.

Prioritize rough carpentry. Review framing systems, floors, walls, roof framing, sheathing, subfloors, trusses, structural components, measurements, layouts, fastening, and installation practices.

Strengthen finish carpentry knowledge. Study doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, trim, millwork, fitting techniques, materials, measurements, and detail-oriented installation work.

Review windows and doors. Understand framing around openings, installation, alignment, repair, fastening, and applicable building-code considerations.

Study siding. Review exterior siding installation, weather protection, fastening, substrate conditions, alignment, and repair concepts.

Prepare safety for recall. OSHA is not allowed into the examination. Candidates should learn common safety principles before test day rather than relying on reference navigation.

Review drywall basics. Even though drywall is a small part of the C-3A examination, candidates should understand basic materials, installation relationships, framing coordination, and repair concepts.

Practice IBC navigation. Use the 2018 International Building Code regularly and practice finding carpentry-related requirements within a limited amount of time.

Use timed practice. Candidates have two hours for 46 questions. Timed study sessions help develop pacing and teach candidates when to answer directly and when to use the IBC.

Review missed questions by category. If mistakes are concentrated in rough framing, focus additional study there. If finish carpentry, safety, siding, or code navigation creates difficulty, direct the next study session toward that subject.

How does the Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) exam work?

The Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.

  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
