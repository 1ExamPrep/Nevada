Exam Details

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates authorized to test through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 46
  • Time Allowed: 2 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 33 correct answers

The C-3A examination is organized around the following subject areas:

  • Safety: 6 questions
  • Windows and Doors: 3 questions
  • Rough Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Finish Carpentry: 16 questions
  • Siding: 3 questions
  • Drywall: 2 questions

Rough Carpentry and Finish Carpentry are the largest portions of the examination, with 16 questions each. Together, these two subjects account for most of the test. Candidates should prioritize framing systems, floor and wall construction, roof framing, sheathing, subflooring, truss components, measurements, layouts, fastening, doors, windows, cabinets, trim, fixtures, fitting, and other practical carpentry concepts.

Safety accounts for six questions. Candidates should review safe construction practices involving tools, ladders, fall hazards, material handling, personal protective equipment, and other common jobsite conditions.

Windows and Doors and Siding each represent three questions, while Drywall represents two questions. These smaller categories remain important because each correct answer contributes toward the passing score.

With 46 questions and two hours available, candidates should develop a consistent testing pace. Reference navigation should be practiced in advance so code lookups do not consume unnecessary time during the examination.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the C-3A Carpentry and Repairs subclassification. Passing the trade examination is one part of the complete contractor licensing process.

  1. Confirm the C-3A classification. Make sure Carpentry and Repairs matches the work the contracting business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. Determine who will serve as the Trade Qualifier and satisfy the applicable experience and technical qualification requirements.
  3. Document qualifying work experience. Prepare the required experience records showing work related to the C-3A classification.
  4. Prepare the contractor license application. Gather business information, qualifier details, experience documentation, financial information, background disclosures, and other required materials.
  5. Submit the application to the Nevada State Contractors Board. The Board reviews the application before examination authorization is issued.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Contractor applicants and listed individuals must follow required disclosure and fingerprint procedures.
  7. Meet financial responsibility requirements. Provide the financial information required for the requested license.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. After the Board verifies the applicable requirements, candidates who must test receive authorization to schedule examinations.
  9. Schedule the C-3A examination through PSI. Use the eligibility information provided during the licensing process.
  10. Prepare for the C-3A trade examination. Review rough carpentry, finish carpentry, safety, doors and windows, siding, drywall, and International Building Code navigation.
  11. Prepare for Nevada Business and Law. Use the included Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada for the applicable management examination requirement.
  12. Complete required examinations. Satisfy the trade and management testing requirements unless an applicable examination waiver has been approved.
  13. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Address bonding, financial responsibility, insurance when applicable, and any remaining Nevada State Contractors Board requirements before license issuance.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs subclassification authorizes qualifying carpentry work involving formwork, framing, rough carpentry, and installation or repair of individual components of framing systems.

The scope includes construction or installation of subflooring, siding, exterior staircases and railings, roof decking, truss members, sheathing, and fabrication, installation, or repair of wood and wood products used for structural and finishing purposes.

Examples of finish-related work within the classification include doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, and related carpentry products. Certain doors, door frames, windows, window frames, studs, and similar members may also be made from formed sheet metal, vinyl, aluminum, and similar materials when they emulate traditional milled wood and can be installed using traditional carpentry joining methods without welding or comparable joining processes.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualifier must generally document at least four years of qualifying experience within the 15 years immediately preceding the filing of the application.

The qualifying experience should be directly related to the classification requested and demonstrate work at the journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor level. Applicants should maintain detailed records describing the type of work performed and the time period in which the experience was obtained.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification, financial responsibility review, background requirements, bonding, and other conditions administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board. Passing the C-3A examination alone does not automatically issue the contractor license.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-3A preparation plan should begin with the examination weighting. Rough Carpentry and Finish Carpentry together account for 32 questions, so these subjects should receive the greatest share of study time.

Review rough carpentry thoroughly. Study wall, floor, and roof framing; sheathing; subflooring; truss components; layouts; measurements; fasteners; structural carpentry relationships; and installation methods.

Strengthen finish carpentry knowledge. Review doors, windows, cabinets, trim, fixtures, fitting, materials, measurement, alignment, fastening, and installation principles.

Practice International Building Code navigation. Work directly with the highlighted and tabbed IBC during preparation. Practice finding relevant requirements without spending excessive time searching.

Study safety concepts. Safety accounts for six questions and should be included in the preparation schedule. Review jobsite hazards, safe use of tools, ladders, fall protection, protective equipment, and general construction safety practices.

Review windows and doors. Focus on framing around openings, installation, fastening, alignment, weather protection, repairs, and applicable building-code relationships.

Prepare for siding and drywall. Although these sections contain fewer questions, candidates should still understand common materials, installation concepts, framing relationships, repair principles, and exterior weather-protection considerations.

Use timed reference exercises. With two hours for 46 questions, candidates should become comfortable making quick decisions about whether a code lookup is necessary.

Use the 6 months of course access strategically. Early study can focus on building a technical foundation and learning the references. Later study sessions can emphasize practice questions, code navigation, review of weak areas, and timed preparation.

Prepare for Business and Law separately. The Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada should be used to review the management side of contractor licensing, including the material associated with the Nevada contractor management examination.

How does the Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) exam work?

The Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
