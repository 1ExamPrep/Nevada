## State Requirements

The Nevada C-3A Carpentry and Repairs subclassification authorizes qualifying carpentry work involving formwork, framing, rough carpentry, and installation or repair of individual components of framing systems.

The scope includes construction or installation of subflooring, siding, exterior staircases and railings, roof decking, truss members, sheathing, and fabrication, installation, or repair of wood and wood products used for structural and finishing purposes.

Examples of finish-related work within the classification include doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, and related carpentry products. Certain doors, door frames, windows, window frames, studs, and similar members may also be made from formed sheet metal, vinyl, aluminum, and similar materials when they emulate traditional milled wood and can be installed using traditional carpentry joining methods without welding or comparable joining processes.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualifier must generally document at least four years of qualifying experience within the 15 years immediately preceding the filing of the application.

The qualifying experience should be directly related to the classification requested and demonstrate work at the journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor level. Applicants should maintain detailed records describing the type of work performed and the time period in which the experience was obtained.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification, financial responsibility review, background requirements, bonding, and other conditions administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board. Passing the C-3A examination alone does not automatically issue the contractor license.

## How does the Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) exam work?

The Carpentry and Repairs Contractor (C-3A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
