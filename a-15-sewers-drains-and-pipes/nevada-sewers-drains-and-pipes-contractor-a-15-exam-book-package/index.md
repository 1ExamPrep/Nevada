## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada A-15 preparation plan should begin with **Septic Tanks and Cesspools** because this is the largest examination category. Review wastewater disposal concepts, system components, site-related considerations, and the information contained in the WTS-22 and WTS-23 fact sheets.

Practice navigating the approved fact sheets under timed conditions. Locate specific subjects, identify related requirements, and become familiar with how the material is organized. Efficient navigation is especially valuable when an exam question requires confirmation from the approved references.

**Underground Utilities and Concrete Pipe Installation** should include trench preparation, bedding, alignment, grade, pipe placement, joining, backfilling, and conditions that can affect installed pipe. Candidates should understand that the stability and support surrounding the pipe are important parts of a successful installation.

The **Installation Guide for Ductile Iron Pipe** supports study of another common underground piping material. Review handling, joining, installation procedures, trench conditions, bedding, alignment, and backfill concepts.

**Excavating and Grading** accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should understand excavation methods, grade control, trench dimensions, soil removal, bedding preparation, backfill, compaction, and practical considerations associated with underground utility work.

**Safety** accounts for 9 questions and deserves dedicated study time. Underground construction creates serious hazards, particularly when employees work in or near excavations and trenches. Review excavation hazards, access, protective systems, equipment, material handling, personal protective equipment, and general construction safety concepts.

Because OSHA is not allowed into the trade examination room, candidates should move from book-supported study to active recall. Read a safety subject, close the reference, and practice answering questions without immediately looking up the answer.

**Paving** is a smaller exam category, but underground pipe work can require restoration of streets and other paved surfaces after excavation. Review hot-mix asphalt placement, compaction, pavement preparation, and general restoration concepts.

**Nevada One Call** should also receive focused preparation. Contractors performing excavation need to understand utility-location and marking concepts associated with working around buried facilities.

The Suggested Marking Guidelines and Nevada One Call material should be studied until candidates can recognize common utility marking and excavation-notification concepts without relying on the reference during the exam.

A useful study method is to divide preparation into six blocks matching the exam outline: Safety, Underground Utilities, Excavating and Grading, Paving, Septic Tanks/Cesspools, and Nevada One Call.

## How does the Sewers, Drains and Pipes Contractor (A-15) exam work?

The Sewers, Drains and Pipes Contractor (A-15) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
