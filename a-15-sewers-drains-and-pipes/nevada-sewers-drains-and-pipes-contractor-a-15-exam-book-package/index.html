Exam Details

The Nevada A-15 Sewers, Drains, and Pipes Contractor examination contains 50 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the examination and must answer 35 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Safety - Code of Federal Regulations: 9 questions
  • Underground Utilities - Concrete Pipe Installation: 10 questions
  • Excavating and Grading: 10 questions
  • Paving: 3 questions
  • Septic Tanks/Cesspools: 15 questions
  • Nevada One Call: 3 questions

Septic Tanks and Cesspools is the largest individual examination category with 15 questions. Candidates should devote significant preparation time to wastewater disposal concepts, septic system components, placement considerations, and the Nevada environmental information contained in the approved WTS-22 and WTS-23 fact sheets.

Underground Utilities - Concrete Pipe Installation and Excavating and Grading each account for 10 questions. These areas require practical understanding of trench preparation, pipe bedding, installation, backfill, alignment, excavation methods, grade control, and underground utility construction.

Safety accounts for 9 questions and should be treated as a major exam subject. Underground pipe work can involve deep excavations, trench hazards, equipment, lifting, access, traffic exposure, and other construction risks.

Paving and Nevada One Call contain fewer questions, but they remain important. Utility contractors often disturb existing pavement and work around buried facilities, so candidates should understand pavement restoration concepts and utility-location responsibilities.

State Requirements

The Nevada A-15 Sewers, Drains, and Pipes classification covers the laying of cast-iron, steel, concrete, vitreous, and nonvitreous pipe for sewers, drains, and irrigation.

The classification also includes related excavating, grading, trenching, backfilling, paving, and surfacing. This means candidates need to understand the complete process surrounding underground pipe installation, not simply how individual pipe sections are joined.

The scope further includes the fabrication and erection of cesspools and septic tanks. This is reflected in the examination outline, where Septic Tanks/Cesspools represents the largest subject area.

Underground utility work requires coordination between pipe installation and earthwork. Proper trench preparation, grade, bedding, alignment, backfill, and compaction can affect the performance and durability of an installed sewer or drain system.

The A-15 trade examination is one component of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada A-15 preparation plan should begin with Septic Tanks and Cesspools because this is the largest examination category. Review wastewater disposal concepts, system components, site-related considerations, and the information contained in the WTS-22 and WTS-23 fact sheets.

Practice navigating the approved fact sheets under timed conditions. Locate specific subjects, identify related requirements, and become familiar with how the material is organized. Efficient navigation is especially valuable when an exam question requires confirmation from the approved references.

Underground Utilities and Concrete Pipe Installation should include trench preparation, bedding, alignment, grade, pipe placement, joining, backfilling, and conditions that can affect installed pipe. Candidates should understand that the stability and support surrounding the pipe are important parts of a successful installation.

The Installation Guide for Ductile Iron Pipe supports study of another common underground piping material. Review handling, joining, installation procedures, trench conditions, bedding, alignment, and backfill concepts.

Excavating and Grading accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should understand excavation methods, grade control, trench dimensions, soil removal, bedding preparation, backfill, compaction, and practical considerations associated with underground utility work.

Safety accounts for 9 questions and deserves dedicated study time. Underground construction creates serious hazards, particularly when employees work in or near excavations and trenches. Review excavation hazards, access, protective systems, equipment, material handling, personal protective equipment, and general construction safety concepts.

Because OSHA is not allowed into the trade examination room, candidates should move from book-supported study to active recall. Read a safety subject, close the reference, and practice answering questions without immediately looking up the answer.

Paving is a smaller exam category, but underground pipe work can require restoration of streets and other paved surfaces after excavation. Review hot-mix asphalt placement, compaction, pavement preparation, and general restoration concepts.

Nevada One Call should also receive focused preparation. Contractors performing excavation need to understand utility-location and marking concepts associated with working around buried facilities.

The Suggested Marking Guidelines and Nevada One Call material should be studied until candidates can recognize common utility marking and excavation-notification concepts without relying on the reference during the exam.

A useful study method is to divide preparation into six blocks matching the exam outline: Safety, Underground Utilities, Excavating and Grading, Paving, Septic Tanks/Cesspools, and Nevada One Call.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Begin with subject-by-subject study, then transition into mixed practice. Mixed questions help candidates become comfortable moving from excavation safety to concrete pipe installation, septic systems, paving, or utility marking without losing focus.

Calculation and measurement practice can also be useful where underground construction questions involve dimensions, grade, quantities, or practical field relationships. Candidates should be comfortable working through basic trade calculations efficiently.

Time management should be included in every practice session. With 50 questions in two hours, spending too much time on one difficult problem can reduce the time available for questions that can be answered more quickly.

A balanced strategy is to answer familiar trade questions efficiently, use WTS-22 and WTS-23 for targeted confirmation when appropriate, mark difficult questions, and return to them after completing easier items.

For the study-only books, focus on understanding principles rather than memorizing isolated sentences. Pipe installation, trenching, bedding, backfill, safety, paving, and utility-location topics are easier to retain when connected to the complete sequence of underground construction work.

How does the Sewers, Drains and Pipes Contractor (A-15) exam work?

The Sewers, Drains and Pipes Contractor (A-15) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
