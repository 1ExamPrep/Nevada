Exam Details

The Nevada A-15 Sewers, Drains, and Pipes Contractor examination contains 50 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the examination and must answer 35 questions correctly to pass.

The exam content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Safety - Code of Federal Regulations: 9 questions
  • Underground Utilities - Concrete Pipe Installation: 10 questions
  • Excavating and Grading: 10 questions
  • Paving: 3 questions
  • Septic Tanks/Cesspools: 15 questions
  • Nevada One Call: 3 questions

Septic Tanks/Cesspools is the largest section of the examination with 15 questions. This makes wastewater-related preparation especially important for A-15 candidates. The WTS-22 and WTS-23 fact sheets included in this package should be used regularly during study so candidates can become familiar with the material before test day.

Underground Utilities - Concrete Pipe Installation and Excavating and Grading each account for 10 questions. These topics require practical understanding of pipe installation, trench preparation, bedding, grade, alignment, excavation methods, backfill, and related underground construction practices.

Safety accounts for 9 questions and should also receive significant attention. Candidates should understand the hazards associated with trenching, excavations, equipment, access, material handling, and other construction activities commonly encountered on sewer and drain projects.

Paving and Nevada One Call each account for 3 questions. Although these sections contain fewer questions, candidates should still understand pavement restoration concepts and excavation-related utility-location responsibilities.

State Requirements

The Nevada A-15 Sewers, Drains, and Pipes classification covers the laying of cast-iron, steel, concrete, vitreous, and nonvitreous pipe for sewers, drains, and irrigation.

The classification also includes related excavating, grading, trenching, backfilling, paving, and surfacing. Candidates should therefore understand the full sequence of underground utility work, from excavation and trench preparation through pipe placement, backfill, compaction, and surface restoration.

The A-15 scope further includes the fabrication and erection of cesspools and septic tanks. This is reflected directly in the examination, where Septic Tanks/Cesspools is the largest content area.

Underground utility projects require close coordination between the pipe system and the surrounding soil and trench conditions. Proper grade, bedding, alignment, backfill, and compaction can affect system performance and long-term durability.

The A-15 trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy the applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada A-15 study plan should begin with Septic Tanks/Cesspools because this section contains 15 questions, the largest number assigned to any single subject area on the examination.

Use WTS-22 and WTS-23 throughout preparation rather than waiting until just before test day. Repeated use can help candidates understand how the fact sheets are organized and where key wastewater-related information can be found.

Practice reference navigation under timed conditions. Choose a wastewater or septic-system topic, locate the applicable information, and repeat the exercise until the search process becomes more natural.

Underground Utilities - Concrete Pipe Installation accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should understand pipe handling, trench preparation, bedding, alignment, grade, joints, backfill, and the basic construction relationships that affect underground pipe installation.

Excavating and Grading also accounts for 10 questions. Review excavation procedures, grading, trench geometry, bedding preparation, soil removal, backfill, compaction, and practical sequencing of underground utility work.

Safety accounts for 9 questions and should be included in every study cycle. Underground work can involve trench collapse hazards, restricted access, mobile equipment, falling materials, and other serious construction risks.

Because the safety reference itself is not part of this Books Allowed into Exam Package, candidates should build strong retained knowledge of the safety subjects covered by the examination.

Paving represents 3 questions. Sewer and drain installation can require excavation through existing paved areas, so candidates should understand general concepts involving pavement removal, preparation, asphalt placement, compaction, and restoration.

Nevada One Call also accounts for 3 questions. Candidates should understand the importance of utility-location procedures, excavation notification, markings, and working around buried facilities.

A useful study structure is to divide the examination into its six subject areas and work on them separately at first. This makes it easier to identify which topics need additional study.

After building familiarity with each subject, move to mixed-question practice. The actual examination can switch quickly from wastewater topics to pipe installation, excavation, safety, paving, or utility-location requirements.

For the open-book portion of preparation, focus on targeted reference use rather than broad searching. When a question appears to involve a wastewater topic, first identify the key subject and then determine where it is likely to appear within WTS-22 or WTS-23.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Time management should be practiced throughout the study period. With 50 questions and two hours, candidates should avoid spending an excessive amount of time on one difficult lookup.

A balanced test strategy is to answer familiar trade questions efficiently, use the approved fact sheets for targeted confirmation when applicable, mark difficult questions, and return to them after completing easier items.

Active recall can help with the subjects not covered by the approved exam-room materials. After reviewing excavation, pipe installation, safety, paving, or Nevada One Call concepts, close the study material and explain the topic from memory.

The method helps candidates identify whether they truly understand a trade concept or are simply recognizing information while a book is open.

For underground utility topics, try connecting individual concepts to the complete construction sequence. Think about locating existing utilities, excavating the trench, preparing grade and bedding, installing pipe, checking alignment, backfilling, compacting, and restoring the surface.

Connecting these subjects to an actual workflow can make technical information easier to retain and apply to exam questions.

How does the Sewers, Drains and Pipes Contractor (A-15) exam work?

The Sewers, Drains and Pipes Contractor (A-15) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
