Exam Details

The Nevada A-16 Paving of Streets, Driveways and Parking Lots trade examination contains 40 questions. Candidates receive 1.5 hours to complete the test and must answer 28 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Excavation, Compaction and Grading: 12 questions
  • Paving - Asphalt Paving Procedures: 14 questions
  • Paving - Asphalt Properties and General Uses: 3 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions
  • Traffic Control: 3 questions
  • Nevada One Call: 3 questions

Asphalt Paving Procedures is the largest single examination category with 14 questions. Candidates should understand the sequence of paving work from preparation of the base through placement and compaction of asphalt. Study should include paving equipment, material delivery, spreading, joints, rolling, compaction, surface quality, and common factors that affect finished pavement performance.

Excavation, Compaction and Grading accounts for 12 questions. Candidates should understand excavation methods, subgrade preparation, grading, moisture considerations, compaction, fill placement, equipment selection, and the relationship between a properly prepared foundation and the performance of the asphalt surface placed above it.

Safety represents 5 questions and should be studied as an important part of the trade rather than a separate topic. Paving work involves heavy equipment, traffic exposure, excavations, moving trucks, hot materials, tools, and active construction zones.

Traffic Control and Nevada One Call each account for 3 questions. Candidates should understand the importance of maintaining safe work zones and identifying underground facilities before excavation or grading begins.

Closed Book Test

The Nevada A-16 Paving of Streets, Driveways and Parking Lots examination is a closed-book test.

The references included in this package are intended for preparation before the examination. Candidates may not use these books during the A-16 trade test.

A closed-book exam requires a different preparation strategy from an open-book exam. Candidates should not rely on remembering where information appears in a reference. Instead, they should develop enough familiarity with the material to recognize concepts, select correct procedures, and apply trade knowledge without consulting a book.

Active recall is especially useful. After reviewing a section on asphalt compaction, for example, close the book and explain why compaction is necessary, what conditions influence it, and what can happen when pavement is not compacted correctly.

The same process can be used for excavation and grading. Candidates should be able to explain the basic sequence from excavation through subgrade preparation, fill placement, compaction, grading, and paving without looking back at the text.

Practice questions can help reinforce this method. When an answer is missed, return to the appropriate reference, review the underlying concept, and then test yourself again later without the book.

State Requirements

Nevada classifies A-16 Paving of Streets, Driveways and Parking Lots as a General Engineering subclassification.

The scope covers examination, excavation, grading, compacting, alteration and repair in the application of asphalt to streets, driveways, parking lots, boat ramps, and landing strips and taxiways for an airport.

The scope explains why the examination combines paving knowledge with excavation and grading. Asphalt performance depends heavily on the condition of the foundation below the pavement. Contractors must understand how to prepare subgrades, establish grade, compact material properly, and create suitable conditions for asphalt placement.

The classification also involves work around public travel areas and active construction zones, which makes traffic control and safety important. Depending on the project, contractors may also need to identify underground utilities before excavation or grading begins.

Nevada contractor licensing also requires completion of the applicable state application and qualification process. Examination eligibility is issued after the application is submitted and the qualifying requirements are met.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive A-16 study plan should reflect the exam weighting. The two largest areas, asphalt paving procedures and excavation, compaction, and grading, account for most of the examination and should receive the largest share of study time.

Asphalt Paving Procedures should include job preparation, base conditions, asphalt delivery, placement, spreading, screed operation, rolling, compaction, joints, temperature considerations, and overall pavement quality.

Candidates should understand the sequence of a paving operation and how individual steps affect one another. A paving crew cannot correct every problem at the surface. Poor grading or inadequate compaction beneath the pavement can contribute to later pavement failure.

Asphalt Properties and General Uses should include basic understanding of hot-mix asphalt materials and their use in streets, parking lots, driveways, ramps, and airport paving applications. Candidates should understand how material condition, placement practices, and compaction contribute to pavement performance.

Excavation study should include safe and efficient removal of material, site preparation, underground utility awareness, excavation equipment, and maintaining suitable conditions for subsequent grading and paving work.

Grading should include establishing elevations, preparing subgrade, controlling slope and drainage, and producing a foundation suitable for asphalt placement. Proper grade is essential because pavement must both support traffic and direct water appropriately.

Compaction should include the purpose of densification, moisture considerations, lifts, equipment, and the importance of achieving a stable base. Candidates should understand that compaction applies to both supporting earthwork and asphalt pavement.

Paving Equipment should include trucks, pavers, rollers, compactors, graders, excavators, and related construction equipment. Candidates should understand the general purpose of each machine and how it fits into a paving operation.

Safety should be studied using OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Paving crews may work near moving vehicles, heavy equipment, hot asphalt, excavations, tools, and changing site conditions. Candidates should understand basic jobsite hazard recognition and protective practices.

Traffic Control is important whenever paving work affects streets or other travel areas. Candidates should understand the purpose of controlling vehicles and pedestrians around an active construction zone and maintaining a safe environment for both workers and the public.

Nevada One Call preparation should include the importance of notifying the appropriate utility notification system before excavation, recognizing utility markings, and protecting underground facilities during construction.

More on test Information and Study Materials

The Standard Specifications for Road and Bridge Construction can help candidates become familiar with Nevada-specific roadway construction requirements. Focus on understanding how specifications establish expectations for materials, workmanship, construction procedures, and quality.

For a closed-book exam, candidates should turn each reading session into a recall exercise. After studying asphalt joints, for example, close the book and write down the main considerations from memory. Then compare your response with the reference and identify what was missed.

Another effective method is to organize preparation by job sequence. Begin with utility notification and site safety, continue through excavation and grading, then study base preparation, asphalt delivery, placement, compaction, joints, traffic control, and final pavement quality.

The sequence-based approach can make the material easier to remember because it connects individual exam topics to the way a paving project progresses in the field.

Practice questions should be used to identify weak subjects. If a candidate consistently misses compaction questions, additional time should be spent reviewing soil preparation and rolling procedures. If Nevada One Call questions are difficult, return to the marking and notification material and strengthen recall.

Short, repeated study sessions can be more productive than reading every book once. Rotate between paving, earthwork, safety, traffic control, and utility marking so that all exam categories remain fresh.

How does the Paving of Streets, Driveways and Parking Lots Contractor (A-16) exam work?

The Paving of Streets, Driveways and Parking Lots Contractor (A-16) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
