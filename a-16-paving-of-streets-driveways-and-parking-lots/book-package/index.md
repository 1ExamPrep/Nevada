Candidates should understand excavation methods, subgrade preparation, grading, moisture considerations, compaction, fill placement, equipment selection, and the relationship between a properly prepared foundation and the performance of the asphalt surface placed above it.

**Safety** represents 5 questions and should be studied as an important part of the trade rather than a separate topic. Paving work involves heavy equipment, traffic exposure, excavations, moving trucks, hot materials, tools, and active construction zones.

**Traffic Control** and **Nevada One Call** each account for 3 questions. Candidates should understand the importance of maintaining safe work zones and identifying underground facilities before excavation or grading begins.

A closed-book exam requires a different preparation strategy from an open-book exam. Candidates should not rely on remembering where information appears in a reference. Practice questions can help reinforce this method. When an answer is missed, return to the appropriate reference, review the underlying concept, and then test yourself again later without the book.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive A-16 study plan should reflect the exam weighting. The two largest areas, asphalt paving procedures and excavation, compaction, and grading, account for most of the examination and should receive the largest share of study time.

**Asphalt Paving Procedures** should include job preparation, base conditions, asphalt delivery, placement, spreading, screed operation, rolling, compaction, joints, temperature considerations, and overall pavement quality.

Candidates should understand the sequence of a paving operation and how individual steps affect one another. A paving crew cannot correct every problem at the surface. Poor grading or inadequate compaction beneath the pavement can contribute to later pavement failure.

**Asphalt Properties and General Uses** should include basic understanding of hot-mix asphalt materials and their use in streets, parking lots, driveways, ramps, and airport paving applications. Candidates should understand how material condition, placement practices, and compaction contribute to pavement performance.

**Excavation** study should include safe and efficient removal of material, site preparation, underground utility awareness, excavation equipment, and maintaining suitable conditions for subsequent grading and paving work.

**Grading** should include establishing elevations, preparing subgrade, controlling slope and drainage, and producing a foundation suitable for asphalt placement. Proper grade is essential because pavement must both support traffic and direct water appropriately.

**Compaction** should include the purpose of densification, moisture considerations, lifts, equipment, and the importance of achieving a stable base. Candidates should understand that compaction applies to both supporting earthwork and asphalt pavement.

**Paving Equipment** should include trucks, pavers, rollers, compactors, graders, excavators, and related construction equipment. Candidates should understand the general purpose of each machine and how it fits into a paving operation.

**Safety** should be studied using OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Paving crews may work near moving vehicles, heavy equipment, hot asphalt, excavations, tools, and changing site conditions. Candidates should understand basic jobsite hazard recognition and protective practices.

**Traffic Control** is important whenever paving work affects streets or other travel areas. Candidates should understand the purpose of controlling vehicles and pedestrians around an active construction zone and maintaining a safe environment for both workers and the public.

**Nevada One Call** preparation should include the importance of notifying the appropriate utility notification system before excavation, recognizing utility markings, and protecting underground facilities during construction.

## How does the Paving of Streets, Driveways and Parking Lots Contractor (A-16) exam work?

The Paving of Streets, Driveways and Parking Lots Contractor (A-16) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
