Exam Details

The Nevada B-7 Residential Remodeling examination is administered through PSI for qualifying candidates pursuing the classification through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada B-7 Residential Remodeling
  • Number of Questions: 50
  • Time Allowed: 2 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 35 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination content is divided among the following residential remodeling subjects:

  • Carpentry: 12 questions
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection: 7 questions
  • Windows and Doors: 5 questions
  • Drywall and Finishes: 15 questions
  • Safety: 6 questions
  • Estimating and Plan Reading: 5 questions

Drywall and Finishes is the largest individual content area with 15 questions. Preparation should include gypsum materials, drywall installation, finishing techniques, accessories, interior surfaces, tile-related finish work, and remodeling procedures that affect completed interior spaces.

Carpentry contains 12 questions and is another major portion of the examination. Candidates should study framing, structural wood components, rough carpentry, finish carpentry, cabinets, trim, fastening methods, tools, and practical remodeling procedures.

Thermal and Moisture Protection contains seven questions. Candidates should review weather protection, flashing, moisture control, insulation-related concepts, roofing concerns, sealants, and methods used to protect existing residential structures from water intrusion and environmental exposure.

Safety contains six questions. Preparation should include fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, tools, material handling, and other construction hazards associated with residential remodeling.

Windows and Doors and Estimating and Plan Reading each contain five questions. Candidates should understand installation principles, openings, components, dimensions, construction drawings, quantities, and project information used to plan and complete remodeling work.

State Requirements

The B-7 Residential Remodeling classification covers remodeling and improvement work involving an existing detached, stand-alone single-family residence or a single-family residential unit within a structure that does not extend more than three stories above ground and one story below ground.

A significant limitation of the classification is that it does not authorize the contractor to increase the existing enclosed space of the residential structure. Candidates should understand this limitation when determining whether the B-7 classification matches the work they intend to perform.

For the standard unrestricted Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual generally must establish at least four full years of qualifying experience within the 15 years immediately preceding the application. Qualifying experience must relate to the classification being requested.

Experience may be established through applicable work as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor. The applicant should maintain organized documentation describing the work performed and demonstrating experience relevant to residential remodeling.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification. The Management Qualified Individual generally satisfies the Contractor Management Survey and Nevada Business and Law requirements unless an applicable waiver is available.

The Nevada licensing process can also include financial responsibility review, contractor bonding, background procedures, and other requirements separate from the trade examination. Passing the B-7 examination alone does not complete the contractor licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive B-7 preparation strategy should reflect the weighting of the exam. Drywall and Finishes and Carpentry together represent more than half of the examination, making those areas major priorities.

Prioritize Drywall and Finishes. Study gypsum products, drywall installation, finishing, accessories, tile systems, substrates, and other interior finish subjects.

Strengthen Carpentry. Review framing, rough carpentry, finish carpentry, cabinets, structural wood components, tools, fastening methods, and remodeling procedures.

Practice IRC navigation. Work regularly with the 2018 International Residential Code and practice finding residential requirements under timed conditions.

Review Thermal and Moisture Protection. Study weather-resistant construction, flashing, moisture control, sealants, roofing-related issues, and protection of existing residential structures.

Prepare Windows and Doors. Review openings, installation principles, components, and common remodeling considerations.

Study Safety. Use OSHA material to reinforce fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, PPE, tools, and safe work procedures.

Practice Estimating and Plan Reading. Work with dimensions, quantities, drawings, construction details, materials, and project information.

Use timed practice. With 50 questions and two hours, candidates should build a consistent pace and learn to balance reference searches with direct trade knowledge.

How does the Residential Remodeling Contractor (B-7) exam work?

The Residential Remodeling Contractor (B-7) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • The B-7 Restricted License covers jobs up to $7,000 including labor and materials, needs no trade exam and is valid for 2 years.
  • A restricted license holder may not do life-safety trades such as electrical, plumbing or HVAC work.
  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
