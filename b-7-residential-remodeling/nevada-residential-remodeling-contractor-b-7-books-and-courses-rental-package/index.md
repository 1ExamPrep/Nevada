## Exam Details

The Nevada **B-7 Residential Remodeling Contractor** examination contains **50 questions**. Candidates are allowed **2 hours** to complete the examination and must answer **35 questions correctly** to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following areas:

- **Carpentry:** 12 questions
- **Thermal and Moisture Protection:** 7 questions
- **Windows and Doors:** 5 questions
- **Drywall and Finishes:** 15 questions
- **Safety:** 6 questions
- **Estimating and Plan Reading:** 5 questions

**Drywall and Finishes** is the largest examination category with 15 questions. Candidates should devote substantial study time to gypsum systems, drywall installation, finish materials, cabinets, tile installation, substrates, and other remodeling topics related to interior finishes.

**Carpentry** accounts for 12 questions. Review wall framing, floor framing, roof and ceiling systems, openings, stairs, headers, fastening, layout, tools, materials, and construction sequencing.

**Thermal and Moisture Protection** accounts for 7 questions. Candidates should understand waterproofing, flashing, weather protection, insulation, roofing interfaces, and the ways remodeling work can affect existing moisture-control systems.

**Safety** accounts for 6 questions, while **Windows and Doors** and **Estimating and Plan Reading** each account for 5 questions. A balanced preparation plan should include all six exam areas rather than concentrating only on carpentry and finishes.

## State Requirements

Nevada recognizes **B-7 Residential Remodeling** as a subclassification within Classification B General Building.

The B-7 scope covers the remodeling and improvement of an **existing detached, stand-alone single-family residence or a single-family residential unit within a structure that does not extend more than three stories above the ground and one story below the ground**.

An important limitation is that the B-7 classification does **not authorize the contractor to increase the existing enclosed space** of the residential structure.

This makes the classification particularly relevant to remodeling projects that improve existing residential areas without expanding the enclosed footprint. Work may involve framing modifications within the authorized scope, drywall, cabinets, tile, windows, doors, concrete-related repair, finishes, and moisture-protection work.

Residential remodeling differs from new construction because the contractor must work with existing walls, floors, ceilings, utilities, finishes, framing, and dimensions. Candidates should understand how new work connects to existing construction while maintaining appropriate code and workmanship requirements.

The B-7 trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada B-7 study plan should begin with **Drywall and Finishes**, the largest examination category. Candidates should review gypsum systems, drywall installation, finishing, cabinets, tile systems, substrates, and other interior remodeling work.

**Gypsum Construction** should include board types, fastening, joints, ceilings, partitions, sheathing, finishing, and the relationship between new drywall and existing wall systems.

**Tile Installation** should include substrate preparation, installation methods, material selection, layout, movement considerations, and moisture-related conditions. Bathrooms, showers, kitchens, and other residential remodeling areas often combine tile work with waterproofing and finish requirements.

**Cabinet and Finish Carpentry** preparation should include built-ins, joinery, layout, hardware, materials, assembly, and installation. Candidates should understand how cabinets relate to walls, floors, countertops, appliances, and surrounding finishes.

**Carpentry** accounts for 12 questions. Review wall framing, floor framing, roof and ceiling systems, openings, headers, stairs, sheathing, fastening, tools, lumber, and construction sequencing.

**Thermal and Moisture Protection** should include flashing, waterproofing, insulation, roofing interfaces, weather protection, and moisture management. These topics are especially important when new remodeling work connects to existing exterior walls, roofs, windows, bathrooms, and other moisture-sensitive assemblies.

**Windows and Doors** should include rough openings, structural support, installation, weather protection, flashing, hardware, and finish coordination.

**Safety** should include fall protection, personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, hand and power tools, electrical hazards, demolition conditions, housekeeping, and material handling.

**Estimating and Plan Reading** should include floor plans, elevations, sections, details, schedules, dimensions, symbols, notes, areas, volumes, and basic material takeoffs.

## How does the Residential Remodeling Contractor (B-7) exam work?

The Residential Remodeling Contractor (B-7) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- The B-7 Restricted License covers jobs up to $7,000 including labor and materials, needs no trade exam and is valid for 2 years.
- A restricted license holder may not do life-safety trades such as electrical, plumbing or HVAC work.
- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
