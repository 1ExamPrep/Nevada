## State Requirements

Nevada recognizes **B-7 Residential Remodeling** as a subclassification within Classification B General Building.

The B-7 scope covers the remodeling and improvement of an **existing detached, stand-alone single-family residence or single-family residential unit within a structure that does not extend more than three stories above ground and one story below ground**.

An important restriction is that the B-7 classification does **not authorize an increase in the existing enclosed space** of the residential structure. Candidates should distinguish remodeling and improvement work from projects that enlarge the home's enclosed footprint.

Residential remodeling may involve demolition and preparation, framing changes within the permitted scope, cabinets, drywall, tile, windows, doors, concrete-related repairs, finishes, and moisture-protection work. Candidates should understand how new materials and assemblies connect to existing construction.

The B-7 examination is only one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable experience, qualification, application, Business and Law, and other licensing requirements.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada B-7 study plan should start with **Drywall and Finishes**, the largest examination category. Review gypsum products, drywall installation, wall and ceiling systems, finish procedures, tile, cabinets, and the substrates that support finish materials.

**Gypsum Construction** should include board types, installation, fastening, joints, ceilings, partitions, sheathing, and finishing. Remodeling projects frequently require new gypsum work to connect cleanly with existing walls and ceilings.

**Tile Installation** should include substrate preparation, material selection, installation methods, layout, movement considerations, and moisture-related conditions. Bathrooms, showers, kitchens, and other remodeling areas often combine tile work with waterproofing and finish requirements.

**Cabinet and Finish Carpentry** preparation should include cabinet construction, built-ins, joinery, layout, hardware, materials, and installation concepts. Candidates should understand how cabinets relate to walls, floors, appliances, countertops, and surrounding finishes.

**Carpentry** accounts for 12 questions and should receive significant study time. Review wall framing, floor framing, roof and ceiling systems, openings, headers, stairs, sheathing, fastening, lumber, tools, and construction sequence.

Existing conditions are especially important in remodeling. Candidates may need to understand how to evaluate exposed framing, connect new work to existing members, work around openings, and coordinate repairs with surrounding construction.

**Thermal and Moisture Protection** accounts for 7 questions. Review waterproofing, flashing, insulation, weather barriers, roof interfaces, exterior openings, and methods used to manage moisture.

**Windows and Doors** accounts for 5 questions. Study rough openings, structural support, installation, flashing, weather protection, hardware, and finish coordination.

**Safety** accounts for 6 questions. OSHA study should include personal protective equipment, fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, electrical hazards, tools, material handling, and conditions created by demolition and renovation work.

**Estimating and Plan Reading** accounts for 5 questions. Candidates should practice reading residential floor plans, sections, elevations, details, schedules, dimensions, symbols, and notes.

Estimating preparation should include areas, volumes, measurements, and basic material quantities. Remodeling estimates often require candidates to determine both what must be removed and what new material is needed.

The highlighted and tabbed format can support quicker study sessions by helping candidates return to important material efficiently. However, a good exam strategy should not depend solely on tabs. Candidates should also know the subject matter well enough to choose the correct book quickly.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

A useful approach is to practice one reference at a time initially. Complete residential code questions with the IRC, safety questions with OSHA, carpentry questions with the carpentry book, and finish questions with the appropriate cabinet, gypsum, concrete, or tile reference.

Once those relationships become familiar, combine the subjects into mixed practice. Mixed review better reflects the examination because candidates must determine which reference applies before searching for an answer.

Timed navigation drills can also help. Select a subject and practice locating the relevant material within a limited period. Repeated exercises can strengthen familiarity with indexes, chapters, terminology, tables, and common reference locations.

Because the exam contains 50 questions in two hours, candidates should avoid spending excessive time on one lookup. If a question becomes difficult, moving forward and returning later can help preserve time for questions that can be answered more efficiently.

## How does the Residential Remodeling Contractor (B-7) exam work?

The Residential Remodeling Contractor (B-7) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- The B-7 Restricted License covers jobs up to $7,000 including labor and materials, needs no trade exam and is valid for 2 years.
- A restricted license holder may not do life-safety trades such as electrical, plumbing or HVAC work.
- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
