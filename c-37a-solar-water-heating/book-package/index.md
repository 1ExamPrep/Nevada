## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-37A preparation plan should begin with complete **solar water heating system design**. Candidates should understand how solar collectors, piping, pumps, storage tanks, heat exchangers, valves, and controls operate together rather than treating each component as an isolated subject.

Follow the movement of thermal energy through the system. Understand how solar energy is captured at the collector, transferred into water or another working medium, circulated through piping, stored when necessary, and ultimately used to provide heated potable water.

**Piping** should receive substantial attention. Review pipe materials, fittings, joining methods, supports, routing, insulation, thermal expansion, valves, and connections.

The **Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018** should be used throughout preparation rather than only during the final days before the exam. Repeated use helps candidates become more familiar with how the code is organized.

Practice locating code requirements by topic. Begin with a question involving pipe material, identify the key term, use the index, and locate the corresponding section. Repeat the same process with fittings, supports, valves, water heaters, storage tanks, and potable water provisions.

**Definitions** can be important because plumbing terminology may have a specific code meaning. Understanding the definition can help determine which requirement applies to an installation.

**Tables** should receive regular attention. Important requirements involving materials, supports, sizing, or installation conditions may appear in table format.

**Exceptions** can modify a general requirement. Candidates should read the surrounding code language carefully before selecting an answer.

**Cross-references** may direct the candidate to another section containing additional requirements. Practice following them instead of assuming the first section contains the complete answer.

**Collectors** are a core solar water heating component. Candidates should understand collector function, arrangement, mounting concepts, orientation, circulation, and how collectors transfer solar energy into the working fluid.

Review how collector configuration affects flow and how multiple collectors can operate as part of a larger thermal system.

**Pumps** should be studied in relation to circulation. Candidates should understand why pumps are used, how they move fluid through collectors and storage systems, and how improper circulation can affect performance.

**Storage tanks** are specifically important in solar water heating because solar energy availability and hot-water demand do not always occur at the same time. Candidates should understand how storage supports system operation.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

**Heat exchangers** may be used to transfer thermal energy between separate fluid circuits. Candidates should understand their purpose and the basic relationship between the collector loop and potable water.

**System Valves and Controls** should include shutoff valves, check valves, balancing devices, temperature controls, differential controls, and other components used to regulate operation and circulation.

**Safety** preparation should include roof access, ladders, elevated work, personal protective equipment, tools, material handling, hot surfaces, electrical control components, and other construction hazards.

The **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)** should be used during preparation to build retained safety knowledge because it is not allowed into the trade examination room.

The **Solar Water and Pool Heating Design and Installation Manual** can help connect multiple C-37A exam topics. Use it to review collector layouts, piping configurations, pumps, storage, controls, valves, circulation, heat-transfer concepts, and complete system operation.

Because this manual is not allowed into the exam room, active recall is useful. After reviewing a system diagram, close the book and explain the flow path and purpose of each major component from memory.

**Potable water** considerations should receive careful attention because the C-37A classification specifically covers systems used to heat water for potable uses.

Candidates should understand the relationship between the solar thermal system and the water-heating and storage components authorized by the classification.

**Heat transfer** is central to solar water heating. Understand how solar radiation becomes usable thermal energy and how that energy moves from the collector into the water-heating system.

**Freeze protection** can be important because collectors and piping may be exposed to outdoor temperatures. Candidates should understand general methods used to protect solar thermal components from damaging conditions.

**Insulation** helps reduce unwanted heat loss between the collector, storage system, and final water-heating application. Candidates should understand why solar thermal piping is insulated and how outdoor exposure affects system performance.

**Thermal expansion** should also be considered in systems exposed to elevated temperatures. Pipe movement, materials, supports, and system design can all affect installation.

A useful study schedule should divide preparation among Piping, System Design, Pumps and Storage Systems, Collectors, System Valves and Controls, and Safety.

## How does the Solar Water Heating Contractor (C-37A) exam work?

The Solar Water Heating Contractor (C-37A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
