## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-37A preparation plan should begin with complete **solar water heating system design**. Candidates should understand how collectors, piping, pumps, storage tanks, heat exchangers, valves, controls, and water-heating components work together rather than memorizing each part separately.

Follow the path of thermal energy through the system. Understand how solar energy is captured by the collectors, transferred into a working fluid or directly into water, circulated through piping, stored when necessary, and ultimately used to provide heated potable water.

**Piping** should receive substantial preparation time. Review pipe materials, fittings, joining methods, supports, routing, insulation, thermal expansion, valves, and connections.

The **Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018** should be used throughout preparation rather than only during the final days before the examination. Repeated use can make the organization of the code more familiar and improve search efficiency.

Practice locating code requirements by topic. Start with a question involving pipe material, identify the key term, use the index, and locate the applicable section. Repeat the process with fittings, supports, valves, water heaters, storage tanks, and potable water provisions.

**Definitions** can be important because plumbing terms may have specific code meanings. Understanding those definitions can help determine which requirements apply to a particular installation.

**Tables** should also receive regular attention. Important requirements involving materials, supports, sizing, or installation conditions may appear in table format rather than narrative text.

**Exceptions** can modify a general requirement. Candidates should read surrounding code language carefully so they do not overlook special conditions.

**Cross-references** may direct the reader to another section containing additional requirements. Practice following them instead of assuming the first section contains the complete answer.

**Collectors** are a central component of solar water heating. Candidates should understand collector function, orientation, mounting, circulation, arrangement, and how collectors transfer solar energy into the working medium.

Review how multiple collectors can be connected and how collector arrangement can influence flow and heat transfer.

**Pumps** should be studied in relation to circulation. Candidates should understand why pumps are used, how they move fluid through collectors and storage systems, and how poor circulation can affect performance.

**Storage tanks** are especially important because solar energy availability and hot-water demand may occur at different times. Candidates should understand how storage helps bridge that difference.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

**Heat exchangers** may be used to transfer thermal energy between separate fluid circuits. Candidates should understand their purpose and the relationship between the solar collector loop and potable water.

**System Valves and Controls** should include shutoff valves, check valves, balancing devices, temperature controls, differential controls, and other components used to manage circulation and system operation.

**Potable water** considerations deserve careful attention because the C-37A classification specifically covers systems used to heat water for potable uses.

Candidates should understand the relationship between the solar thermal system and the water heaters or storage tanks authorized within the C-37A scope.

**Heat transfer** is central to the trade. Understand how solar radiation becomes usable thermal energy and how that energy is moved from the collector into the water-heating system.

**Freeze protection** can be important because collectors and piping may be exposed to outdoor temperatures. Candidates should understand general methods used to protect solar thermal components from damaging conditions.

**Insulation** helps reduce unwanted heat loss between collectors, piping, storage tanks, and water-heating components. Candidates should understand why solar thermal piping is insulated and how environmental exposure can affect system performance.

**Thermal expansion** should also be considered in systems exposed to elevated temperatures. Pipe movement, materials, supports, and system design can all affect installation.

## How does the Solar Water Heating Contractor (C-37A) exam work?

The Solar Water Heating Contractor (C-37A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
