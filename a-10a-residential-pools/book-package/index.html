Exam Details

The Nevada A-10A Residential Pools Contractor examination contains 50 questions. Candidates receive 2 hours to complete the examination and must answer 35 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following areas:

  • Filtration/Circulation Systems: 8 questions
  • Maintenance and Operation: 7 questions
  • Accessories and Associated Trades: 6 questions
  • Pool and Spa Business Laws and Rules: 3 questions
  • Concrete, Shotcrete and Gunite: 8 questions
  • Design: 12 questions
  • Soils and Earthwork: 6 questions

Design is the largest examination category with 12 questions. Candidates should understand how the pool or spa is planned as a complete system, including dimensions, circulation, equipment, structural considerations, access, surrounding features, and the relationship between the vessel and site conditions.

Filtration/Circulation Systems and Concrete, Shotcrete and Gunite each account for 8 questions. These subjects are central to residential pool construction because the finished project depends on both a properly constructed shell and a circulation system capable of supporting safe and effective pool operation.

Maintenance and Operation accounts for 7 questions, while Accessories and Associated Trades and Soils and Earthwork each account for 6 questions. Candidates should also prepare for 3 questions involving Pool and Spa Business Laws and Rules.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes A-10A Residential Pools as a subclassification within the A-10 Commercial and Residential Pools classification.

The A-10A scope covers the construction, alteration, and repair of residential pools and spas used for swimming, therapy, or decoration, including appurtenant equipment.

The broad scope means candidates should understand more than surface repair or routine maintenance. Residential pool construction involves site evaluation, excavation, soil conditions, structural shells, concrete or shotcrete construction, circulation equipment, plumbing relationships, pool and spa accessories, surrounding construction, and safe operation.

Nevada also has specific requirements affecting contractors who perform residential pool and spa work. Residential pool contractors must understand that pool construction involves consumer-protection obligations in addition to general contractor licensing requirements.

A contractor performing residential pool work should also understand the importance of written agreements, clearly defined work, approved plans, project coordination, and compliance with applicable state and local requirements.

The A-10A examination is one part of the licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy applicable experience, application, business and law, financial, bonding, and other contractor licensing requirements before a license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada A-10A study plan should begin with residential pool design because Design is the largest examination category. Candidates should understand how the shape, dimensions, depth, circulation, equipment, structural shell, access, and site conditions work together as one complete system.

Filtration and Circulation Systems should receive substantial attention. Review pumps, filters, suction and return paths, circulation principles, equipment relationships, and the purpose of moving and treating water throughout the pool or spa.

Maintenance and Operation should include routine pool and spa care, circulation, filtration, equipment operation, water-related conditions, and troubleshooting concepts. The Pool and Spa Operator Handbook and Ultimate Pool Maintenance Manual can support this area.

Concrete, Shotcrete and Gunite should be studied as structural construction subjects. Candidates should understand substrate preparation, reinforcement coordination, application, placement, curing, quality control, and the importance of constructing a sound pool shell.

The Guide to Shotcrete and The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction can help candidates strengthen understanding of practical concrete and shotcrete construction before test day.

Soils and Earthwork should include excavation, grading, trench safety concepts, soil conditions, drainage, compaction, and the relationship between the site and the finished pool structure.

Pool shells, decks, piping, and surrounding improvements can all be affected by settlement or poor drainage. Candidates should understand why site preparation matters before structural work begins.

Accessories and Associated Trades should include the equipment and construction components that interact with the pool or spa. Candidates should understand coordination rather than studying each component in isolation.

Residential Spa Construction should receive dedicated study time because the A-10A classification includes residential spas. The ANSI/NSPI-3 reference can support study of permanently installed residential spa concepts.

Residential Inground Pool Standards can be reviewed using the ANSI/NSPI-5 reference included in the package. Because this material is not available during the trade examination, candidates should focus on understanding the concepts rather than relying on exam-room lookup.

The Uniform Swimming Pool, Spa and Hot Tub Code, 2018 should be used during every stage of study. Practice finding applicable provisions rather than reading only from beginning to end.

Use the index, definitions, chapter headings, and major pool and spa sections to develop navigation skills. Candidates should become familiar enough with the book to know where to begin searching when a code-based question appears.

More on test Information and Study Materials

A useful study exercise is to choose a subject such as circulation, residential spas, equipment, safety, or construction requirements and time how long it takes to locate the relevant section.

The study-only books should gradually be removed from practice sessions. Review the material, close the reference, and answer questions from memory. This helps build the retained knowledge needed when those books are not available in the trade examination.

Practice questions should be organized around the official exam subjects: Design, Filtration/Circulation, Maintenance and Operation, Concrete/Shotcrete/Gunite, Accessories and Associated Trades, Soils and Earthwork, and Pool and Spa Business Laws and Rules.

With 50 questions in two hours, pacing matters. Candidates should avoid spending too long on one code lookup. Answer familiar questions efficiently and return to more difficult items when possible.

How does the Residential Pools Contractor (A-10A) exam work?

The Residential Pools Contractor (A-10A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
