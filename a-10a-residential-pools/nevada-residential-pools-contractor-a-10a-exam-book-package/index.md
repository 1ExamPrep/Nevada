## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada A-10A study plan should begin with **residential pool design** because Design is the largest examination category. Candidates should understand how the shape, dimensions, depth, circulation, equipment, structural shell, access, and site conditions work together as one complete system.

**Filtration and Circulation Systems** should receive substantial attention. Review pumps, filters, suction and return paths, circulation principles, equipment relationships, and the purpose of moving and treating water throughout the pool or spa.

**Maintenance and Operation** should include routine pool and spa care, circulation, filtration, equipment operation, water-related conditions, and troubleshooting concepts. The Pool and Spa Operator Handbook and Ultimate Pool Maintenance Manual can support this area.

**Concrete, Shotcrete and Gunite** should be studied as structural construction subjects. Candidates should understand substrate preparation, reinforcement coordination, application, placement, curing, quality control, and the importance of constructing a sound pool shell.

The **Guide to Shotcrete** and **The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction** can help candidates strengthen understanding of practical concrete and shotcrete construction before test day.

**Soils and Earthwork** should include excavation, grading, trench safety concepts, soil conditions, drainage, compaction, and the relationship between the site and the finished pool structure.

Pool shells, decks, piping, and surrounding improvements can all be affected by settlement or poor drainage. Candidates should understand why site preparation matters before structural work begins.

**Accessories and Associated Trades** should include the equipment and construction components that interact with the pool or spa. Candidates should understand coordination rather than studying each component in isolation.

**Residential Spa Construction** should receive dedicated study time because the A-10A classification includes residential spas. The ANSI/NSPI-3 reference can support study of permanently installed residential spa concepts.

**Residential Inground Pool Standards** can be reviewed using the ANSI/NSPI-5 reference included in the package. Because this material is not available during the trade examination, candidates should focus on understanding the concepts rather than relying on exam-room lookup.

The **Uniform Swimming Pool, Spa and Hot Tub Code, 2018** should be used during every stage of study. Practice finding applicable provisions rather than reading only from beginning to end.

Use the index, definitions, chapter headings, and major pool and spa sections to develop navigation skills. Candidates should become familiar enough with the book to know where to begin searching when a code-based question appears.

## How does the Residential Pools Contractor (A-10A) exam work?

The Residential Pools Contractor (A-10A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
