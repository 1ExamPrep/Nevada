The A-10C scope covers the **repair and replacement of plaster, coping, and coating of pool decks on residential pools and spas**.

**Pool plaster** is a critical finished surface. Candidates should understand surface condition, preparation, repair areas, application considerations, and how water exposure and substrate condition can affect long-term performance.

**Coping** forms the finished transition around the top edge of a pool or spa. Repair work can involve removing damaged material, preparing the underlying area, maintaining alignment, and restoring a suitable finished edge.

**Pool deck coatings** require attention to surface preparation, substrate condition, cracking, drainage, compatibility, and environmental exposure. Candidates should understand why repairing the underlying condition is often as important as applying the new coating.

Although the A-10C classification focuses on plaster, coping, and deck coatings, the examination includes broader pool topics because repair work occurs within an existing pool or spa system. Circulation, operation, design, soil conditions, and associated trades can all influence repair performance.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada A-10C study plan should begin with the specific work authorized by the classification: **plaster, coping, and pool deck coatings**.

**Plaster Repair** should include evaluating existing surfaces, identifying damaged or deteriorated material, understanding preparation requirements, and recognizing conditions that may affect the durability of the completed repair.

Surface preparation is especially important. New material applied over a weak, contaminated, cracked, or unstable substrate may not perform properly. Candidates should understand why the existing condition must be evaluated before repair work begins.

**Coping Repair** should include removal of damaged pieces, preparation of the underlying area, alignment, replacement, joint considerations, and maintaining a suitable finished transition around the pool or spa.

**Pool Deck Coatings** should be studied in relation to cracking, drainage, surface preparation, compatibility, and environmental exposure. Candidates should understand how moisture, movement, and substrate condition can influence coating performance.

**Accessories and Associated Trades** is the largest examination category. Candidates should understand how pool repair work can interact with equipment, finishes, surrounding surfaces, and other trades even when those items are outside the narrow A-10C scope.

**Filtration and Circulation Systems** should receive substantial attention because they account for 10 questions. Review the basic path of water through the pool or spa and the purpose of pumps, filters, suction outlets, valves, piping, and return fittings.

Candidates should understand how poor circulation or filtration can affect pool conditions. Even when the repair work itself involves plaster, coping, or decking, the overall system can influence the environment in which those materials perform.

**Maintenance and Operation** should include general pool and spa operation, circulation, filtration, routine care, and recognition of conditions that may contribute to surface staining, scaling, deterioration, or other repair concerns.

**Design** should be studied as a systems topic. Candidates should understand how the pool shell, coping, deck, circulation system, access, drainage, and safety features work together.

**Soils and Earthwork** should include basic concepts related to settlement, soil movement, drainage, excavation, and backfill. Ground conditions can affect pool decks, shells, coping, and adjacent surfaces.

Cracks or movement in a deck or pool surface may be symptoms of a deeper condition. Candidates should understand the importance of evaluating the cause before performing a cosmetic repair.
