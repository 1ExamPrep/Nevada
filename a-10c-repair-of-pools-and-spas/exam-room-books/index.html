Exam Details

The Nevada A-10C Repair Of Pools And Spas Contractor examination contains 40 questions. Candidates are allowed 1.5 hours to complete the test and must answer 28 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Filtration/Circulation Systems: 10 questions
  • Maintenance and Operation: 8 questions
  • Accessories and Associated Trades: 11 questions
  • Pool and Spa Business Laws and Rules: 3 questions
  • Design: 5 questions
  • Soils and Earthwork: 3 questions

Accessories and Associated Trades is the largest category with 11 questions. Candidates should understand how pool repair work interacts with finishes, coping, decks, equipment, and other systems associated with residential pools and spas.

Filtration/Circulation Systems accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should understand the basic path of water through suction outlets, pumps, filters, heaters or treatment components, and return fittings, along with how circulation problems can affect pool operation and surface conditions.

Maintenance and Operation accounts for 8 questions. Candidates should be familiar with routine pool and spa operation, system care, circulation, water-related conditions, and maintenance concepts that can affect repair work.

Design accounts for 5 questions, while Pool and Spa Business Laws and Rules and Soils and Earthwork account for 3 questions each. These subjects help candidates understand how repair work fits into the complete residential pool or spa system.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes A-10C Repair Of Pools And Spas as a sub-subclassification under A-10 Commercial and Residential Pools.

The A-10C scope covers the repair and replacement of plaster, coping, and coating of pool decks on residential pools and spas.

Pool plaster is a critical finished surface. Candidates should understand surface condition, preparation, repair areas, application considerations, and how water exposure and substrate condition can affect long-term performance.

Coping forms the finished transition around the top edge of a pool or spa. Repair work can involve removing damaged material, preparing the underlying area, maintaining alignment, and restoring a suitable finished edge.

Pool deck coatings require attention to surface preparation, substrate condition, cracking, drainage, compatibility, and environmental exposure. Candidates should understand why repairing the underlying condition is often as important as applying the new coating.

Although the A-10C classification focuses on plaster, coping, and deck coatings, the examination includes broader pool topics because repair work occurs within an existing pool or spa system. Circulation, operation, design, soil conditions, and associated trades can all influence repair performance.

The A-10C trade examination is one part of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada A-10C study plan should begin with the specific work authorized by the classification: plaster, coping, and pool deck coatings.

Plaster Repair should include evaluating existing surfaces, identifying damaged or deteriorated material, understanding preparation requirements, and recognizing conditions that may affect the durability of the completed repair.

Surface preparation is especially important. New material applied over a weak, contaminated, cracked, or unstable substrate may not perform properly. Candidates should understand why the existing condition must be evaluated before repair work begins.

Coping Repair should include removal of damaged pieces, preparation of the underlying area, alignment, replacement, joint considerations, and maintaining a suitable finished transition around the pool or spa.

Pool Deck Coatings should be studied in relation to cracking, drainage, surface preparation, compatibility, and environmental exposure. Candidates should understand how moisture, movement, and substrate condition can influence coating performance.

Accessories and Associated Trades is the largest examination category. Candidates should understand how pool repair work can interact with equipment, finishes, surrounding surfaces, and other trades even when those items are outside the narrow A-10C scope.

Filtration and Circulation Systems should receive substantial attention because they account for 10 questions. Review the basic path of water through the pool or spa and the purpose of pumps, filters, suction outlets, valves, piping, and return fittings.

Candidates should understand how poor circulation or filtration can affect pool conditions. Even when the repair work itself involves plaster, coping, or decking, the overall system can influence the environment in which those materials perform.

Maintenance and Operation should include general pool and spa operation, circulation, filtration, routine care, and recognition of conditions that may contribute to surface staining, scaling, deterioration, or other repair concerns.

Design should be studied as a systems topic. Candidates should understand how the pool shell, coping, deck, circulation system, access, drainage, and safety features work together.

Soils and Earthwork should include basic concepts related to settlement, soil movement, drainage, excavation, and backfill. Ground conditions can affect pool decks, shells, coping, and adjacent surfaces.

Cracks or movement in a deck or pool surface may be symptoms of a deeper condition. Candidates should understand the importance of evaluating the cause before performing a cosmetic repair.

More on test Information and Study Materials

The Uniform Swimming Pool, Spa and Hot Tub Code, 2018 should be used regularly during preparation. Start by reviewing the table of contents and the major sections relevant to pools and spas.

Use the index to locate subjects such as circulation, equipment, safety, barriers, spas, and construction requirements. Over time, candidates should develop a general sense of where major topics are located.

Definitions should receive attention because code terminology can affect how a requirement is interpreted. Knowing the meaning of a technical term can make both the question and the applicable code section easier to understand.

A useful study exercise is to create a list of common A-10C subjects and time how long it takes to locate each one in the code. Repeat slower searches until navigation becomes more efficient.

Practice questions should be completed with the code nearby. First attempt the question from trade knowledge, then determine whether a lookup is necessary. This helps candidates develop judgment about when to use the reference.

Because the examination provides 90 minutes for 40 questions, pacing should be practiced. Spending too long on a single search can reduce the time available for easier questions.

If a question becomes difficult, moving forward and returning later may be more effective than searching the code for several minutes.

A useful preparation sequence is to begin with plaster, then move through coping, deck coatings, circulation, maintenance, design, soils, and code navigation. Mixed practice can then help candidates become comfortable switching among the different subjects tested.

How does the Repair of Pools and Spas Contractor (A-10C) exam work?

The Repair of Pools and Spas Contractor (A-10C) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
