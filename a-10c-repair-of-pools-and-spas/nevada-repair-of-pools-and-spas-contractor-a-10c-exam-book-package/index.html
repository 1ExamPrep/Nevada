The Nevada A-10C Repair Of Pools And Spas Contractor examination contains 40 questions. Candidates receive 1.5 hours to complete the examination and must answer 28 questions correctly to pass.

  • Pool and Spa Business Laws and Rules: 3 questions

Accessories and Associated Trades is the largest category with 11 questions. Candidates should prepare for the way pool repair work interacts with finishes, coping, decks, equipment, surrounding construction, and other systems associated with residential pools and spas.

Filtration/Circulation Systems accounts for 10 questions. Even though A-10C focuses on plaster, coping, and pool deck coatings, candidates should understand basic circulation-system operation because repair work occurs within an existing pool or spa system.

Maintenance and Operation accounts for 8 questions. Candidates should understand general pool operation, routine maintenance, circulation, equipment care, water-related conditions, and how operational issues can affect repaired surfaces.

Design accounts for 5 questions, while Pool and Spa Business Laws and Rules and Soils and Earthwork account for 3 questions each. These areas support a broader understanding of residential pool repair projects and the conditions that can affect long-term performance.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes A-10C Repair Of Pools And Spas as a sub-subclassification under A-10 Commercial and Residential Pools.

The A-10C scope covers the repair and replacement of plaster, coping, and coating of pool decks on residential pools and spas.

The scope is narrower than the A-10E Maintenance and Repair classification. A-10C centers on residential pool and spa surface and finish repair rather than the broader alteration and repair of pumps, filters, heaters, controls, lights, and other equipment included under other pool classifications.

Pool plaster is a critical finished surface. Candidates should understand preparation, repair conditions, removal of deteriorated material, application considerations, curing-related concepts, and conditions that may affect the performance of repaired plaster.

Coping forms the transition around the top edge of the pool or spa. Repair work can involve removing damaged material, preparing the underlying area, maintaining appropriate alignment, and restoring the finished edge.

Pool deck coatings require attention to substrate condition, preparation, surface integrity, drainage, cracking, and compatibility of repair materials. Existing deck conditions should be assessed before coating or repair work begins.

Swimming pool and spa contractors may also be subject to additional Nevada requirements related to residential pool and spa work. The A-10C trade exam is one part of the contractor licensing process, and applicants must complete the applicable qualification, application, business and law, and other licensing requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada A-10C study plan should begin with the work specifically authorized by the classification: plaster, coping, and pool deck coatings.

Plaster Repair preparation should include evaluating existing surfaces, identifying deteriorated material, understanding surface preparation, preparing repairs, and recognizing conditions that can affect adhesion or durability.

Candidates should understand that surface preparation is a major part of successful repair work. Applying a new finish over an unstable, contaminated, cracked, or poorly prepared surface can lead to premature failure.

Coping Repair should include removal of damaged pieces, preparation of the supporting area, alignment, replacement, joint considerations, and maintaining a suitable finished transition around the pool or spa.

Pool Deck Coatings should be studied in relation to substrate condition, cracks, surface preparation, drainage, coating compatibility, and exposure to water and outdoor conditions. Candidates should understand why deck preparation can be just as important as the coating itself.

Accessories and Associated Trades is the largest examination category with 11 questions. Candidates should understand how repair work may interact with other pool components and construction trades, even when those components are not the primary scope of the A-10C classification.

Filtration and Circulation Systems account for 10 questions. Review the basic circulation path through suction outlets, pumps, filters, heaters or treatment components, and return fittings. Understanding the system helps candidates recognize how pool operation can affect repaired surfaces and vice versa.

Maintenance and Operation accounts for 8 questions. Review routine pool care, circulation, filtration, water-condition concepts, equipment operation, and conditions that can contribute to surface staining, scaling, deterioration, or other repair concerns.

Design should include the relationship between the pool shell, coping, deck, drainage, circulation, access, and safety features. Repair work should fit the existing system rather than creating new performance problems.

Soils and Earthwork should include settlement, drainage, soil movement, and the ways ground conditions can affect pool decks, coping, shells, and adjacent surfaces.

Cracking can sometimes be a symptom rather than the entire problem. Candidates should understand the importance of evaluating underlying movement, drainage, or substrate conditions before performing a cosmetic repair.

The Uniform Swimming Pool, Spa and Hot Tub Code, 2018 should be part of every study session. Practice locating code subjects instead of only reading chapters in sequence.

Timed practice is also important. With 40 questions in 1.5 hours, candidates should not spend too long on a difficult code lookup. Answer familiar questions efficiently and return to more difficult items later when possible.

A useful study sequence is to begin with the A-10C scope, then move through plaster, coping, deck coatings, circulation, maintenance, design, soils, and code navigation. Finish with mixed practice so candidates become comfortable switching among subjects.

How does the Repair of Pools and Spas Contractor (A-10C) exam work?

The Repair of Pools and Spas Contractor (A-10C) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
