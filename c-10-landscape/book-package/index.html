Exam Details

The Nevada C-10 Landscape Contracting trade examination is part of the contractor licensing process administered through Nevada's contractor licensing system. Candidates become eligible to schedule the applicable trade examination after submitting the contractor license application and receiving authorization to test.

The examination is designed to evaluate trade knowledge relevant to landscape contracting. Candidates should prepare for practical subjects associated with landscape installation, irrigation, plants, soil, water management, construction methods, grading, drainage, materials, and jobsite work.

Irrigation should receive substantial attention during preparation. Landscape contractors frequently work with systems that must deliver water efficiently and consistently across planted areas. Candidates should understand the relationship between pressure, flow, pipe sizing, valves, sprinkler or emitter placement, zoning, and system operation.

Plant knowledge is also important. Preparation should include the characteristics and needs of trees, shrubs, vines, turf, and other landscape materials. Candidates should understand how plant selection relates to climate, water demand, exposure, soil conditions, space, and landscape function.

Landscape construction requires additional trade knowledge. Site preparation, grading, drainage, installation sequencing, materials, tools, and construction methods all contribute to successful landscape projects. Candidates should understand how these elements fit together from initial site work through final installation.

Water-efficient landscaping deserves particular attention in Nevada. The study package includes a dedicated waterwise landscaping reference to help candidates review plant selection and landscape practices that consider efficient water use.

Closed Book Test

The Nevada Landscape Contracting (C-10) examination is a closed-book test.

The following references are included for study but are not allowed inside the examination room:

  • Simplified Irrigation Design, 2nd Ed, 1995
  • Landscaping Principles and Practices, 2009
  • Waterwise Landscaping with Trees, Shrubs, and Vines
  • Landscape Construction, 2011

Closed-book testing requires a different preparation strategy from an open-book contractor examination. Candidates cannot depend on searching for answers during the test, so important concepts, formulas, terminology, installation practices, and field principles should become familiar before exam day.

Instead of simply reading each book from beginning to end, candidates should use active recall. Study a topic, close the reference, and explain the concept without looking at the page. Practice questions can then be used to determine whether the material has been retained.

Irrigation calculations should be practiced repeatedly until the process becomes familiar. Plant and soil concepts should be reviewed by category. Landscape construction subjects can be studied through real-world scenarios involving grading, drainage, materials, layout, and installation sequencing.

The closed-book format also makes time spent correcting missed practice questions especially valuable. Every missed question can identify a topic that needs more review before the examination.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada C-10 Landscape Contracting examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable licensing requirements in addition to studying for and completing the trade examination.

  1. Confirm the C-10 Landscape Contracting classification. Determine that the landscape work the business intends to perform falls within the Nevada C-10 classification.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor license application must identify the individual responsible for providing the required trade qualification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable work experience demonstrating landscape contracting knowledge and responsibilities related to the requested classification.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Provide the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the application and verifies the qualifying experience.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. After eligibility is established, the candidate can proceed with scheduling the required examination.
  7. Prepare for the C-10 trade examination. Study irrigation, landscape principles, plants, waterwise landscaping, construction methods, grading, drainage, and other trade subjects.
  8. Prepare for Business and Law. Nevada contractor applicants are also generally required to complete the applicable Business and Law examination unless an authorized waiver or exemption applies.
  9. Complete the required examinations. Take the examinations associated with the contractor license application.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Satisfy the other applicable requirements before the Nevada contractor license is issued.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-10 Landscape Contracting as a contractor license classification. The classification covers landscape contracting activities and includes work associated with landscape development and installation.

Within the authorized C-10 scope, Nevada also allows certain related landscape improvements. A C-10 license holder may perform specified nonload-bearing landscape construction in conjunction with landscape work, including limited brick or stone walkways, limited brick or stone patio areas, landscape retaining walls within the applicable height limit, and low-voltage landscape lighting within the applicable voltage limit.

The classification therefore requires candidates to understand both plant-oriented landscape work and construction-oriented landscape installation. Irrigation, drainage, grading, plant materials, soil, hardscape-related work, and landscape lighting can all interact on a complete project.

Applicants must qualify for the classification through the Nevada contractor licensing process. Examination eligibility is issued after the application is filed and qualifying experience is reviewed.

The C-10 trade examination is not the only licensing requirement. Applicants must also satisfy the other applicable application, qualification, business, financial, and licensing requirements before a contractor license can be issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-10 study plan should divide preparation into major landscape subjects and use the books to reinforce both practical knowledge and technical problem solving.

Irrigation Design should receive regular attention. Candidates should understand how pressure and flow affect system performance, how irrigation zones are organized, how pipe size relates to water movement, and how valves and distribution components work together.

Use Simplified Irrigation Design to practice calculations instead of only reading examples. Work through problems involving pressure, flow, system layout, and irrigation-system decisions until the process becomes familiar without consulting the book.

Plants and Landscape Principles should include trees, shrubs, vines, turf, soil conditions, plant establishment, maintenance, environmental requirements, and installation practices. Candidates should understand why a plant is appropriate for one location and unsuitable for another.

Plant selection should consider factors such as exposure, available space, soil, water requirements, growth characteristics, and landscape purpose. Learning these relationships is more useful than memorizing isolated plant facts.

Waterwise Landscaping is particularly relevant to landscape work in an arid state. Candidates should understand how plant selection, grouping, irrigation, and design decisions can influence water demand.

Study the relationships between trees, shrubs, vines, and site conditions. Consider how mature size, root systems, shade, sun exposure, irrigation needs, and placement can affect long-term landscape performance.

Landscape Construction should include site preparation, layout, grading, drainage, soil work, materials, installation sequencing, tools, and basic construction methods. Candidates should understand how to move from an undeveloped site to a completed landscape.

Grading and Drainage deserve dedicated study because poor water movement can damage plants, structures, and landscape improvements. Review slope concepts, drainage direction, surface preparation, and the importance of moving water appropriately through a site.

Hardscape-Related Knowledge should include the basic construction principles involved in walkways, patios, retaining features, and other landscape improvements within the classification's authorized scope. Candidates should understand preparation, materials, layout, drainage, and stability concepts.

Landscape Lighting should be reviewed within the limitations of the C-10 classification. Candidates should understand basic low-voltage landscape lighting concepts, placement, and coordination with other landscape systems.

Soils should be studied for texture, drainage, amendments, compaction, plant suitability, and preparation. Landscape success often depends on matching plant and irrigation decisions to actual soil conditions.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Because the examination is closed book, active recall should be used for every major subject. After studying irrigation, close the book and work problems. After studying plants, create questions about site suitability. After studying landscape construction, explain the correct sequence for preparing and installing a landscape feature.

Flashcards can be useful for terminology, but scenario-based practice is often more valuable for trade preparation. Ask what should happen when pressure is inadequate, drainage is poor, plant water needs differ, or a site requires grading before installation.

Practice questions should be reviewed carefully. The goal is not simply to count correct answers but to understand why an incorrect answer was chosen. Return to the appropriate reference, review the concept, and then attempt a similar question without the book.

A rotating study schedule can keep preparation balanced. One session might focus on irrigation, another on plants and waterwise landscaping, another on landscape construction, and another on soils, grading, and drainage.

As the examination approaches, candidates should gradually complete more study without the books open. This more closely reflects the closed-book testing environment and helps reveal which concepts have actually been retained.

How does the Landscape Contractor (C-10) exam work?

The Landscape Contractor (C-10) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
