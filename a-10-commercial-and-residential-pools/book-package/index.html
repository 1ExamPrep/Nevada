Exam Details

The Nevada A-10 Commercial and Residential Pools trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates authorized to test through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada A-10 Commercial and Residential Pools
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 75
  • Time Allowed: 2.5 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 53 correct answers

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Filtration/Circulation Systems: 12 questions
  • Maintenance and Operation: 12 questions
  • Accessories and Associated Trades: 8 questions
  • Pool and Spa Business Laws and Rules: 3 questions
  • Concrete, Shotcrete and Gunite: 15 questions
  • Design: 16 questions
  • Soils and Earthwork: 9 questions

Design is the largest examination category with 16 questions. Candidates should understand pool and spa layout, dimensions, circulation relationships, equipment considerations, structural concepts, accessibility of components, hydraulic relationships, and practical design factors associated with commercial and residential installations.

Concrete, Shotcrete and Gunite accounts for 15 questions. Candidates should review concrete placement, shotcrete application, reinforcement relationships, preparation, curing, finishing, construction quality, and practices used in pool shells and related structures.

Filtration/Circulation Systems and Maintenance and Operation each account for 12 questions. These categories require familiarity with pumps, filters, circulation, water movement, equipment, operational considerations, maintenance practices, and the systems used to keep pools and spas operating properly.

Soils and Earthwork represents nine questions. Candidates should understand excavation, site conditions, soil considerations, grading relationships, preparation, and other earthwork concepts that affect pool construction.

Accessories and Associated Trades accounts for eight questions. Candidates should review equipment and related construction activities that interact with the pool or spa installation.

Pool and Spa Business Laws and Rules accounts for three questions. Candidates should understand applicable regulatory concepts associated with Nevada pool and spa contracting.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the A-10 Commercial and Residential Pools classification. Passing the A-10 examination is one component of the complete Nevada contractor licensing process.

  1. Confirm the A-10 classification. Determine that Commercial and Residential Pools matches the contracting work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. Designate the person who will satisfy applicable experience and examination requirements.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare records showing relevant pool and spa construction, alteration, repair, equipment, excavation, concrete, circulation-system, and related experience.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor license application. Gather business information, qualifier information, experience documentation, financial information, and required supporting materials.
  5. Submit the application. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the applicant's qualifications before examination eligibility is established.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow required disclosure, fingerprint, and background procedures.
  7. Meet financial responsibility requirements. Provide the financial documentation required for the requested Nevada contractor license.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Candidates who must test can proceed after authorization is issued.
  9. Schedule the A-10 examination through PSI. Arrange the Commercial and Residential Pools trade examination after eligibility has been established.
  10. Prepare for the A-10 trade examination. Review design, concrete and shotcrete, filtration, circulation, maintenance, operation, earthwork, accessories, business rules, and code navigation.
  11. Prepare for Nevada contractor management requirements. Nevada applicants generally must also satisfy applicable business and law or contractor management examination requirements unless an authorized waiver applies.
  12. Complete required examinations. Satisfy applicable trade and management testing requirements.
  13. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Address applicable bonding, financial responsibility, consumer-protection requirements for residential pool and spa work, background procedures, and other Nevada State Contractors Board requirements.

State Requirements

The Nevada A-10 Commercial and Residential Pools classification authorizes the construction, alteration, and repair of commercial and residential pools and spas used for swimming, therapy, or decoration, including appurtenant equipment.

The classification includes several related sub-subclassifications covering residential pools, residential spas, pool and spa repair, fiberglass pools and spas, maintenance and repair of pools and spas, and qualifying water and gas service-line work associated with residential pools and spas.

Pool and spa work can involve layout, excavation, structural preparation, concrete or shotcrete construction, piping, circulation equipment, filtration, heaters, decks, finishes, controls, and other components necessary for a complete installation.

Residential pool and spa contractors in Nevada are also subject to additional consumer-protection requirements. These requirements operate alongside the general contractor licensing rules and are important for businesses performing residential pool and spa work.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document at least four years of qualifying experience within the applicable experience period required by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

Qualifying experience should relate directly to the requested classification. Applicants should maintain organized records describing the work performed, responsibilities held, systems constructed or repaired, and the period during which the experience was obtained.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong A-10 study strategy should reflect the examination weighting. Design and Concrete, Shotcrete and Gunite together account for 31 of the 75 questions, making those areas a major priority.

Prioritize design. Review pool and spa layout, dimensions, circulation relationships, construction planning, equipment considerations, and technical design principles.

Study concrete and shotcrete. Review pool-shell construction, concrete quality, shotcrete placement, reinforcement relationships, curing, finishing, and construction practices.

Strengthen filtration and circulation knowledge. Understand pumps, filters, circulation, piping, equipment relationships, and the movement of water through pool and spa systems.

Review maintenance and operation. Study equipment operation, routine maintenance, filtration, circulation, water-system management, and practical pool and spa service concepts.

Prepare for soils and earthwork. Review excavation, soil conditions, grading, preparation, and site-work principles that affect pool construction.

Study accessories and associated trades. Understand the relationship between the pool installation and associated components, systems, and construction activities.

Practice code navigation. Use the Uniform Swimming Pool, Spa and Hot Tub Code regularly so you can locate relevant requirements efficiently during the open-book examination.

Use timed practice. Candidates have 2.5 hours for 75 questions. Timed study helps develop a realistic exam pace and better decisions about when a code lookup is necessary.

How does the Commercial and Residential Pools Contractor (A-10) exam work?

The Commercial and Residential Pools Contractor (A-10) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board.
