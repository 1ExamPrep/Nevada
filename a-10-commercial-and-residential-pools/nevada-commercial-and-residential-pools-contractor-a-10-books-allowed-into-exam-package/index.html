Exam Details

The Nevada A-10 Commercial and Residential Pools trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates authorized to test through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada A-10 Commercial and Residential Pools
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 75
  • Time Allowed: 2.5 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 53 correct answers

The examination content is divided into several major subject areas:

  • Filtration/Circulation Systems: 12 questions
  • Maintenance and Operation: 12 questions
  • Accessories and Associated Trades: 8 questions
  • Pool and Spa Business Laws and Rules: 3 questions
  • Concrete, Shotcrete and Gunite: 15 questions
  • Design: 16 questions
  • Soils and Earthwork: 9 questions

Design is the largest individual category on the A-10 examination. Candidates should understand pool and spa layout, system relationships, equipment considerations, dimensional requirements, practical planning, and other design concepts used in residential and commercial installations.

Concrete, Shotcrete and Gunite is another major category. Candidates should review pool-shell construction, placement methods, reinforcement relationships, preparation, curing, finishing, and quality practices associated with concrete and shotcrete work.

Filtration/Circulation Systems requires knowledge of pumps, filters, piping, water movement, circulation patterns, system components, and equipment relationships. Candidates should understand how water moves through a pool or spa system and how the major components work together.

Maintenance and Operation focuses on the practical operation of pools and spas, including equipment performance, circulation, filtration, routine service, and other operational considerations.

Soils and Earthwork includes excavation, site preparation, grading relationships, soil conditions, and other factors that affect the construction and stability of a pool installation.

Accessories and Associated Trades covers related components and construction work that interact with pool and spa installations, while Pool and Spa Business Laws and Rules addresses applicable regulatory topics.

State Requirements

The Nevada A-10 Commercial and Residential Pools classification authorizes the construction, alteration, and repair of commercial and residential pools and spas used for swimming, therapy, or decoration, including appurtenant equipment.

The work can involve multiple construction disciplines and technical systems. Pool and spa projects may include excavation, structural work, concrete or shotcrete, piping, circulation equipment, filtration, accessories, finishes, and related components required for a complete installation.

Because the classification includes both commercial and residential work, candidates should be prepared for concepts that apply across different types of pools and spas. Commercial facilities and residential installations can present different design, operational, and regulatory considerations, but both are included within the A-10 examination scope.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document at least four years of qualifying experience within the applicable experience period required by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

Qualifying experience should relate directly to the classification requested. Applicants should maintain organized documentation describing the type of work performed, responsibilities held, systems constructed or repaired, and the time period during which the experience was obtained.

Residential pool and spa contracting in Nevada also carries consumer-protection requirements that operate alongside general contractor licensing rules. Contractors should understand the obligations that apply to residential projects as part of responsible business operation.

Passing the A-10 trade exam does not automatically issue a Nevada contractor license. Applicants must also satisfy applicable experience, management qualification, financial responsibility, bonding, background, and other requirements established by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong A-10 study plan should balance technical knowledge with code-navigation practice. The Uniform Swimming Pool, Spa and Hot Tub Code is a valuable exam-room tool, but candidates should also understand the underlying trade concepts covered by the test.

Prioritize design. Design is the largest section of the exam with 16 questions. Candidates should review planning, layout, dimensions, system relationships, equipment considerations, and practical design principles.

Study concrete, shotcrete, and gunite. The category accounts for 15 questions. Candidates should understand pool-shell construction, materials, reinforcement relationships, placement, curing, and general construction quality.

Review filtration and circulation. Pumps, filters, piping, water movement, and related equipment are central to pool and spa operation. Candidates should understand how these components interact within a complete system.

Review maintenance and operation. Study equipment operation, filtration, circulation, routine maintenance, and practical pool and spa management concepts.

Prepare for soils and earthwork. Review excavation, site preparation, soil conditions, grading, and other earthwork principles relevant to pool construction.

Review accessories and associated trades. Understand how pool installations interact with related equipment and construction components.

Study applicable rules. The examination includes a smaller section addressing pool and spa business laws and rules. Candidates should not overlook this category simply because it represents fewer questions.

Practice code navigation. Use the Uniform Swimming Pool, Spa and Hot Tub Code throughout your study process rather than waiting until the end. Learn where common topics are located and practice moving between sections quickly.

Use the index efficiently. The index can help identify the proper chapter or code section, but candidates should also become familiar with recurring terms and how the code organizes pool and spa subjects.

Practice timed lookups. During study sessions, set a reasonable time limit for locating a code provision. If a search takes too long, review how the subject is indexed and where it is located so future searches become faster.

Do not rely on the code for every answer. Questions involving trade knowledge may be answered more efficiently from understanding and experience. Use the approved reference when a specific code requirement, limitation, dimension, or installation rule needs to be confirmed.

Work according to the exam weighting. Design, concrete, shotcrete, filtration, circulation, maintenance, and operation account for a large portion of the examination. Your study schedule should reflect those priorities.

How does the Commercial and Residential Pools Contractor (A-10) exam work?

The Commercial and Residential Pools Contractor (A-10) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
