Exam Details

The Nevada C-41C Fire Detection Contractor examination contains 55 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the examination, and the minimum passing score is 39 correct answers.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • General Electrical Knowledge: 5 questions.
  • Wiring: 8 questions.
  • Grounding: 3 questions.
  • Power Supply: 5 questions.
  • Detectors: 15 questions.
  • Alarms Notification: 14 questions.
  • General Regulations: 5 questions.

Detectors and alarm notification make up the largest portions of the examination, so candidates should give those areas substantial study time. Wiring, power supply, electrical knowledge, grounding, and general regulations remain important and should also be included in a balanced preparation plan.

With 55 questions and two hours available, candidates have a little over two minutes per question on average. That makes time management and reference familiarity especially important. Candidates should avoid using the code books for every question when they already know the answer, while still being prepared to confirm detailed code requirements when needed.

Questions may require practical trade knowledge in addition to direct code lookup. Candidates should therefore combine reference study with a strong understanding of fire detection systems, electrical wiring, alarm devices, power requirements, and system operation.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-41C Fire Detection Contractor examination is an open-book test. The approved references included in this package are NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 edition and NFPA 72 - National Fire Alarm Code, 2013.

Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center. The references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used, while temporary tabs such as removable notes are not permitted.

The references may not contain writing, and additional loose or attached papers are not permitted. Candidates should therefore prepare their books in advance according to the applicable exam-room rules and use only approved reference markings.

The National Electrical Code should be used to prepare for electrical installation concepts, conductor and circuit requirements, grounding, power supplies, wiring methods, and other electrical provisions relevant to fire alarm systems.

NFPA 72 should be used for fire alarm-specific preparation, including detection devices, notification appliances, alarm signaling, system power, initiation, testing, inspection, maintenance, and other fire alarm requirements.

Open-book testing rewards familiarity. Candidates who know which reference applies to a question and where major subjects are located can use the books more efficiently than candidates seeing the material for the first time on exam day.

A good study routine is to practice identifying the subject first, choose either NFPA 70 or NFPA 72, locate the applicable provision, and then review surrounding language before selecting an answer. This helps build both technical understanding and navigation speed.

Exam preparation can begin while application documentation is being organized. Studying early gives candidates more time to become familiar with both the electrical code and fire alarm code before testing.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-41C study plan should follow the exam content outline and devote significant time to detectors and alarm notification, while still covering wiring, grounding, power supply, electrical fundamentals, and general regulations.

General Electrical Knowledge should include basic electrical terminology, circuit concepts, voltage, current, resistance, electrical equipment, and the practical principles needed to understand fire alarm wiring and system operation.

Wiring accounts for eight exam questions and should receive focused review. Candidates should understand wiring methods, conductor requirements, circuits, cable use, installation methods, and applicable National Electrical Code provisions related to fire alarm systems.

Grounding should include the purpose of grounding and bonding, applicable conductor concepts, equipment grounding, and electrical safety considerations. Practice locating grounding-related provisions in the NEC so you can confirm details efficiently during the exam.

Power Supply is another important area. Fire detection and alarm systems depend on reliable power, including primary and secondary sources. Candidates should understand how system power requirements relate to alarm operation, reliability, and code compliance.

Detectors represent the largest section of the exam with 15 questions. Preparation should include smoke detection, heat detection, initiating devices, device application, installation considerations, location concepts, system response, and related NFPA 72 provisions.

Candidates should understand that different detectors are suited to different conditions and applications. Study the basic operating principles and the factors that influence device selection and placement.

Alarm Notification represents 14 questions and is another major area. Candidates should review notification appliances, audible and visible signaling, alarm communication, appliance operation, circuits, and the requirements associated with alerting building occupants.

NFPA 72 should be used extensively for detector and notification preparation. Rather than simply reading chapters from beginning to end, practice locating information based on the wording of sample questions.

General Regulations account for five questions. Candidates should be familiar with the general regulatory concepts associated with fire detection contracting and understand the scope and responsibilities connected to the C-41C classification.

Timed reference drills can improve open-book performance. Select a topic such as notification appliances, smoke detectors, fire alarm circuits, or grounding and give yourself a limited amount of time to find the applicable information.

More on test Information and Study Materials

If a lookup takes too long, repeat it later. The purpose is not to memorize page numbers but to develop a mental map of each reference so you know where major topics are located.

Use the NEC primarily for electrical requirements and NFPA 72 for fire alarm system requirements. Some questions may involve both areas, so candidates should understand how electrical installation rules and fire alarm requirements work together.

Practice questions should also be used to identify weaker topics. If detector questions are difficult, dedicate additional time to NFPA 72. If wiring or grounding questions are challenging, return to the NEC and review the relevant electrical concepts.

Open-book preparation should balance knowledge and navigation. A candidate who understands the trade can quickly identify the subject of a question, while a candidate who knows the reference structure can confirm detailed requirements without wasting time.

Regular review is more useful than waiting until immediately before the examination. Repeated exposure to the terminology, code organization, and system concepts helps make the material more familiar and easier to apply under testing conditions.

How does the Fire Detection Contractor (C-41C) exam work?

The Fire Detection Contractor (C-41C) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
