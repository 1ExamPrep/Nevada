## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-7 study plan should include technical system knowledge, safety requirements, equipment terminology, reference navigation, and practice-oriented review.

**Elevator systems** should be a major area of preparation. Candidates should understand elevator components, hoistways, cars, doors, controls, drive systems, suspension systems, safety devices, machine spaces, pits, and related equipment.

**Escalator systems** should also be reviewed. Study components, operating systems, safety devices, controls, inspection concepts, and installation requirements relevant to escalators.

**Safety devices** are critical in elevation and conveyance work. Candidates should understand the purpose of safety systems and learn how technical standards organize requirements related to protection, stopping, operation, and failure conditions.

**Controls and signaling systems** are another important area. Elevators and other conveyance systems rely on controls, operating devices, signal systems, and safety circuits that work together to manage movement and protect users.

**Platform lifts** should be studied using ASME A18.1. These systems often serve accessibility needs and have requirements that differ from standard passenger elevators.

**Stairway chairlifts** also fall under ASME A18.1. Candidates should understand their equipment configurations, controls, operating requirements, safety features, and installation concepts.

**Inspection and testing** should be part of preparation. Elevation and conveyance systems require proper testing and inspection to confirm that safety devices, operating systems, and equipment function correctly.

When using ASME A17.1, practice identifying the equipment type first. Then locate the relevant section and review the requirement in context. Technical standards often contain multiple conditions or equipment-specific provisions that affect how a rule applies.

When using ASME A18.1, practice distinguishing between platform lift and stairway chairlift requirements. Knowing which section applies can reduce the time spent searching.

Reference navigation should be treated as a skill. Choose a topic, locate the correct section, close the book, and repeat the search later. Repetition can help build familiarity with the standard's structure.

Practice questions can help identify weak areas. When you miss a question, determine whether the issue came from limited technical knowledge, unfamiliar terminology, or difficulty locating the correct provision.

If a reference search takes too long, repeat it. The goal is to become familiar enough with the approved books that navigation feels routine instead of unfamiliar during the examination.

## How does the Elevation and Conveyance Contractor (C-7) exam work?

The Elevation and Conveyance Contractor (C-7) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
