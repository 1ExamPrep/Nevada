The C-7 classification covers the fabrication and installation of systems designed to transport persons, animals, and other chattels in elevators, conveyors, dumbwaiters, and escalators. The scope also includes associated controls, signal systems, devices, and equipment.

The classification includes several types of elevation and conveyance work. Test Information and Study Materials

C-7 exam preparation should include a combination of technical code study, system knowledge, safety review, and reference-navigation practice. Elevator and conveyance work involves multiple mechanical and electrical systems working together, so candidates should study the equipment as integrated systems rather than isolated components.

Elevator and escalator safety requirements should be reviewed using ASME A17.1. Candidates should become comfortable with terminology associated with elevator cars, hoistways, pits, machine spaces, doors, controls, suspension systems, safety devices, drive systems, and operating equipment.

Testing and inspection concepts are another important area. Elevation systems contain multiple safety components that require inspection, testing, and proper operation. Candidates should understand how the applicable standards organize requirements relating to system safety and performance.

Controls and operating systems should also be included in preparation. Modern elevator and conveyance systems use controls, signals, operating devices, and safety circuits that work together to regulate movement and protect passengers.

Platform lifts and stairway chairlifts should be studied using ASME A18.1. Candidates should understand that these systems have different configurations and applications than conventional elevators while still requiring detailed safety, installation, and operating standards.

Personnel hoists should be reviewed with ANSI A10.4 before test day. Because the book cannot enter the examination room, candidates should focus on understanding equipment requirements, safe operation, installation considerations, and related safety concepts.

Accessibility should be studied using ICC A117.1-2017. Elevators and lifts are often essential components of accessible building design, making familiarity with accessible controls, spaces, routes, and equipment important for contractors working with elevation systems.

Practice with the exam-room books should emphasize navigation. Choose a system component or safety topic, identify likely keywords, locate the corresponding section in the ASME reference, and review the complete requirement.

Do not rely only on memorizing section numbers. Understanding the structure of the standards can be more useful because exam questions may use different terminology than the wording you studied.

When reviewing a technical requirement, study the surrounding provisions as well. Elevator and conveyance standards often contain related conditions, exceptions, definitions, equipment-specific requirements, and referenced sections that affect how a rule applies.

Practice questions can help identify areas that require additional study. If a question involving an elevator safety device is difficult, return to the applicable ASME section and review both the device and the related system requirements.

How does the Elevation and Conveyance Contractor (C-7) exam work?

The Elevation and Conveyance Contractor (C-7) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
