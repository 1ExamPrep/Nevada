Exam Details

The Nevada A-19B Pipelines And Conduits For Gas Contractor examination contains 40 questions. Candidates are allowed 1.5 hours to complete the examination and must answer 28 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following areas:

  • Gas Piping: 15 questions
  • Welding: 6 questions
  • Safety: 6 questions
  • Paving: 4 questions
  • Excavation, Compaction and Grading: 6 questions
  • Nevada One Call: 3 questions

Gas Piping is the largest exam category with 15 questions. Candidates should understand pipeline materials, fittings, joining methods, installation practices, trench conditions, protective measures, and the overall construction process associated with underground gas systems.

Welding accounts for 6 questions. Preparation should include pipe preparation, alignment, welding procedures, equipment, joints, and the importance of producing suitable connections in pipeline construction.

Safety also accounts for 6 questions. Gas pipeline work can involve trenches, heavy equipment, welding, hot work, traffic, lifting, buried utilities, and changing jobsite conditions. Safety should therefore be studied as a core part of the trade.

Excavation, Compaction and Grading accounts for another 6 questions. Candidates should understand how trench preparation, boring, shoring, bedding, backfill, compaction, and final grading affect the completed pipeline installation.

Paving accounts for 4 questions, reflecting the need to restore disturbed surfaces after underground construction. Nevada One Call accounts for 3 questions and reinforces the importance of underground utility awareness before excavation begins.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes A-19B Gas as a subclassification under A-19 Pipeline and Conduits.

The scope covers the fabrication and installation of gas pipelines, including trenching, boring, shoring, backfilling, compacting, paving, surfacing, and application of protective coatings necessary to complete the pipeline installation.

The scope explains why the A-19B examination includes more than gas-pipe assembly. Pipeline work begins with planning and site preparation, moves through excavation and trench protection, continues through pipe installation and joining, and finishes with backfill, compaction, grading, and restoration.

Underground utility awareness is another important part of the work. Before excavation or boring begins, contractors must account for existing underground facilities and follow applicable utility-notification and marking procedures.

Gas pipeline construction can also involve welding, protective coatings, trench safety, heavy equipment, and restoration of paved surfaces. Candidates should understand how these activities connect to one another within the complete project.

The trade examination is only one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also complete the applicable application, qualification, business and law, and other licensing requirements before receiving the contractor credential.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada A-19B preparation plan should combine gas-pipeline trade knowledge with regular practice using NFPA 30.

Gas Piping should receive the largest share of study time because it accounts for 15 of the 40 exam questions. Candidates should understand pipeline materials, fittings, installation methods, protection, joining concepts, and how gas systems are incorporated into underground construction.

Study should also include the relationship between the pipeline and the trench surrounding it. A properly installed gas pipeline depends on appropriate excavation, bedding, support, backfill, and compaction.

Welding should receive focused preparation. Candidates should understand pipe preparation, alignment, welding equipment, joint considerations, procedures, and the importance of producing suitable connections.

Excavation, Compaction and Grading should be studied as part of the pipeline construction sequence. Candidates should understand trench preparation, boring, shoring, safe access, bedding, backfill placement, compaction, and final grade.

Underground construction requires more than simply opening and closing a trench. The condition of the soil, the support provided beneath and around the pipe, and the quality of the backfill can affect the completed installation.

Safety should be integrated throughout the study process. Pipeline projects can expose workers to trenches, equipment, traffic, buried facilities, suspended loads, welding operations, and other hazards.

Candidates should understand the importance of hazard recognition, safe equipment use, personal protective equipment, communication, and safe access to work areas.

Paving should be reviewed as part of site restoration. When a gas pipeline installation disturbs pavement, the completed work may require appropriate backfill, compaction, base preparation, asphalt placement, and surface finishing.

Nevada One Call should be reviewed as part of pre-excavation planning. Underground utility notification and marking are essential considerations before trenching or boring begins.

The NFPA 30 reference should be used regularly during preparation. Candidates should not wait until the final days before the exam to begin navigating the book.

Start by reviewing the table of contents and major subject areas. Then practice using the index to locate piping, storage, handling, and other relevant provisions.

Definitions should also be reviewed because specific terminology can affect how code requirements are interpreted. Understanding the language used in the reference can make lookups more efficient.

A project-sequence approach can help organize the broader A-19B material. Start with utility notification and site planning, continue through excavation and trench protection, then review gas-pipe installation, joining, welding, protective coatings, backfill, compaction, grading, and paving.

The approach connects individual exam categories to the way gas-pipeline projects progress in the field and can make the material easier to remember.

How does the Pipelines and Conduits for Gas Contractor (A-19B) exam work?

The Pipelines and Conduits for Gas Contractor (A-19B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
