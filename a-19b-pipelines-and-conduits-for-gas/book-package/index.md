## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada A-19B study plan should begin with **Gas Piping**, which represents 15 of the 40 examination questions.

Candidates should review pipeline materials, fittings, construction methods, joining, installation, protection, trench conditions, and the relationship between the pipe system and surrounding earthwork.

The **ASME B31.8** reference can support deeper study of gas transmission and distribution systems. Because this book is not available during the exam, candidates should use repeated review and practice questions to convert the material into working knowledge.

**Pipefitter's Handbook** can reinforce general piping knowledge involving fittings, dimensions, measurements, layout, and practical field concepts. These fundamentals can help candidates understand gas-piping questions more efficiently.

**Welding** should receive dedicated study time because it accounts for 6 questions. Candidates should understand pipe preparation, alignment, joint considerations, welding procedures, equipment, and workmanship.

Pipe Welding Procedures can be used to review the sequence and reasoning behind common pipeline welding operations. The goal should be understanding the process rather than memorizing isolated terms.

**Excavation, Compaction and Grading** also accounts for 6 questions. Candidates should understand trench preparation, boring, shoring, bedding, backfill, compaction, and final grade.

Pipeline performance depends on the conditions around the pipe. Poor trench preparation, unsuitable bedding, inadequate backfill, or insufficient compaction can affect the completed installation.

**Safety** should be studied alongside excavation and welding. Gas pipeline construction can expose workers to trench hazards, heavy equipment, moving vehicles, hot work, tools, suspended loads, and buried utilities.

OSHA preparation should include general construction hazard recognition, excavation protection, personal protective equipment, safe equipment operation, and access to trenches and work areas.

**Paving** accounts for 4 questions. Gas-pipeline projects may disturb existing paved surfaces, so candidates should understand the general sequence of backfill, compaction, base preparation, asphalt placement, rolling, and surface restoration.

The Hot Mix Asphalt Paving Handbook supports this portion of preparation by connecting paving principles to the restoration phase of an underground project.

**Nevada One Call** accounts for 3 questions. Candidates should understand why notification and utility marking occur before excavation and how underground facility awareness reduces the risk of damage.

## How does the Pipelines and Conduits for Gas Contractor (A-19B) exam work?

The Pipelines and Conduits for Gas Contractor (A-19B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
