Exam Details

The Nevada C-1I Chilled-Water Piping trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates authorized to test through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-1I Chilled-Water Piping
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 30
  • Time Allowed: 75 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 21 correct answers

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Piping Materials: 10 questions
  • Piping Installation: 10 questions
  • Valves, Controls, Pumps, and Equipment: 7 questions
  • Safety: 3 questions

Piping Materials represents one-third of the examination. Candidates should understand common piping materials, fittings, joining methods, compatibility, applications, supports, and code requirements related to chilled-water piping systems.

Piping Installation also accounts for 10 questions. Preparation should include layout, installation methods, supports, joining, routing, equipment connections, valves, system components, and requirements found in the Uniform Plumbing Code and Uniform Mechanical Code.

Valves, Controls, Pumps, and Equipment accounts for seven questions. Candidates should understand how pumps move chilled water through a system, how valves regulate and isolate flow, how controls affect system operation, and how equipment components work together within a chilled-water piping system.

Safety accounts for three questions. Candidates should understand common construction hazards, personal protective equipment, safe tool use, material handling, ladders, fall hazards, and other jobsite safety principles applicable to piping work.

With 30 questions and 75 minutes available, candidates have approximately two and a half minutes per question. Efficient reference use is important. Candidates should avoid spending excessive time searching through a code book when the answer can be determined from trade knowledge.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-1I Chilled-Water Piping examination is an open-book test. The approved exam-room references included in this package are:

  • Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018
  • Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018

Candidates should practice with both references throughout preparation. The Uniform Plumbing Code and Uniform Mechanical Code serve different purposes, and efficient exam performance depends in part on recognizing which book is most likely to contain the information needed for a particular question.

The Uniform Plumbing Code supports preparation involving piping materials, installation requirements, fittings, joints, water-system provisions, and related piping requirements.

The Uniform Mechanical Code supports preparation involving mechanical equipment, hydronic piping relationships, system components, mechanical installation requirements, and other provisions relevant to chilled-water piping.

References permitted in the examination may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam. Permanent tabs may be used. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed.

The following included books are not allowed into the C-1I examination:

  • Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd edition
  • Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters, 8th edition
  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)

The references should be used before test day to develop technical knowledge. Candidates should be prepared to apply refrigeration concepts, piping mathematics, and safety principles without having these books available during the examination.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-1I Chilled-Water Piping subclassification covers the fabrication, installation, repair, and removal of pipes, pumps, valves, and other equipment used in the transfer of chilled water for temperature control.

The C-1I scope is specifically focused on the chilled-water piping side of the system. The classification does not authorize the C-1I license holder to fabricate, install, repair, remove, or connect to a chiller.

The distinction is important when preparing for both the examination and actual licensed work. Candidates should understand piping distribution, pumps, valves, system components, and equipment used to move chilled water while recognizing the limits of the subclassification.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document at least four years of qualifying experience within the 10 years immediately preceding the application.

Qualifying experience should directly relate to the classification requested. Applicants should maintain organized documentation describing the work performed, systems installed or repaired, responsibilities held, and the period during which the experience was obtained.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification, financial responsibility review, bonding, background procedures, and other requirements administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board. Passing the C-1I trade examination alone does not automatically result in contractor license issuance.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-1I study plan should begin with the exam weighting. Piping Materials and Piping Installation each account for 10 questions, meaning those two areas alone represent 20 of the 30 questions on the examination.

Prioritize piping materials. Review common piping materials, fittings, joining methods, compatibility, supports, applications, and applicable mechanical and plumbing-code requirements.

Study piping installation thoroughly. Review layout, routing, support, joining, system connections, installation practices, and requirements found in the UPC and UMC.

Review pumps and valves. Understand how pumps create circulation, how valves regulate or isolate flow, and how these components affect system performance.

Study controls and equipment. Review the function of controls and other equipment associated with chilled-water transfer systems.

Practice piping mathematics. Use Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters to strengthen calculations involving measurements, dimensions, geometry, and piping layouts. Because the book is not allowed into the exam, candidates should practice until common calculations can be completed independently.

Strengthen refrigeration fundamentals. Use Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning to understand system components, heat-transfer concepts, pumps, controls, and the broader mechanical principles behind chilled-water systems.

Prepare for safety. Use OSHA during study to reinforce safe construction practices. Candidates should be prepared to answer safety questions without using OSHA during the examination.

Practice code navigation. Work directly with the Uniform Plumbing Code and Uniform Mechanical Code. Learn which reference applies to different types of questions and practice locating information within a limited amount of time.

Use timed practice. With 75 minutes for 30 questions, candidates should develop a steady pace and avoid lengthy reference searches.

How does the Chilled-Water Piping Contractor (C-1I) exam work?

The Chilled-Water Piping Contractor (C-1I) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
