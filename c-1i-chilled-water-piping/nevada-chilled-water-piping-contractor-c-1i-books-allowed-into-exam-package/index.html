Exam Details

The Nevada C-1I Chilled-Water Piping trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates authorized to test through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-1I Chilled-Water Piping
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 30
  • Time Allowed: 75 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 21 correct answers

The examination content is divided into the following areas:

  • Piping Materials: 10 questions
  • Piping Installation: 10 questions
  • Valves, Controls, Pumps, and Equipment: 7 questions
  • Safety: 3 questions

Piping Materials accounts for one-third of the exam. Candidates should review common piping materials, fittings, joining methods, compatibility, applications, and applicable requirements for chilled-water piping systems.

Piping Installation also represents 10 questions. Preparation should include routing, joining, supports, connections, layout, installation practices, valves, system components, and code requirements that affect chilled-water piping work.

Valves, Controls, Pumps, and Equipment accounts for seven questions. Candidates should understand the functions of pumps, flow-control devices, isolation valves, control components, and other equipment used to move and regulate chilled water through a system.

Safety accounts for three questions. Candidates should understand jobsite hazards, safe use of tools, personal protective equipment, lifting and material handling, fall hazards, and other safety principles associated with piping installation.

With 30 questions and 75 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two and a half minutes per question. Effective preparation should include timed reference-navigation exercises so candidates can locate code provisions without losing too much time.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-1I Chilled-Water Piping examination is an open-book test. The approved exam-room references included in this package are:

  • Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018
  • Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018

Candidates should use both books throughout preparation. The goal is to become familiar with the organization of each reference and learn which code is most likely to contain the information needed for a particular question.

The Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018 supports preparation involving piping materials, joints, fittings, installation requirements, water-system provisions, and related piping topics.

The Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018 supports preparation involving mechanical equipment, hydronic-system relationships, mechanical piping, system components, and other requirements relevant to chilled-water piping.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not permitted. Candidates should prepare their references before arriving for the exam and become familiar with the organization of the books through repeated study.

An open-book examination still requires strong trade knowledge. Candidates should not expect to search for every answer. Questions involving familiar piping methods, pumps, valves, controls, materials, or practical installation concepts may be answered more efficiently from technical knowledge.

A strong approach is to determine whether a question requires a specific code rule. When it does, identify whether the UPC or UMC is the more appropriate reference and then locate the relevant section. This method can reduce random searching and improve overall testing pace.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-1I Chilled-Water Piping subclassification covers the fabrication, installation, repair, and removal of pipes, pumps, valves, and other equipment used in the transfer of chilled water for temperature control.

The classification has an important limitation: a C-1I license does not authorize the holder to fabricate, install, repair, remove, or connect to a chiller. Candidates should understand this distinction when preparing for the examination and when evaluating the type of work authorized under the classification.

The scope is therefore centered on the chilled-water distribution side of a system. This includes piping, pumps, valves, and related components that transfer chilled water, rather than the chiller itself.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document at least four years of qualifying experience within the applicable experience period required by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

Experience should relate directly to the requested classification. Applicants should maintain organized records describing the systems worked on, responsibilities held, equipment involved, and the period during which the qualifying work was performed.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification, financial responsibility review, bonding, background procedures, and other licensing requirements. Passing the C-1I trade examination alone does not automatically result in contractor license issuance.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong C-1I preparation plan should reflect the weighting of the examination. Piping Materials and Piping Installation together account for 20 of the 30 questions, so those subjects should receive the greatest amount of study time.

Prioritize piping materials. Review commonly used piping materials, fittings, joints, joining methods, compatibility, supports, and the code requirements that affect their selection and installation.

Study piping installation. Review layout, routing, supports, connections, valves, installation methods, and relationships between piping and mechanical equipment.

Review pumps. Understand how pumps circulate chilled water and how pumping equipment relates to piping systems, valves, and controls.

Study valves and controls. Candidates should understand the purpose of isolation, regulation, and control components used within chilled-water distribution systems.

Review equipment relationships. Understand how piping connects with system components while remembering that the C-1I scope does not authorize connection to a chiller.

Prepare for safety. Review common piping and construction hazards, safe tool use, personal protective equipment, lifting, material handling, and general jobsite practices.

Practice UPC navigation. Use the Uniform Plumbing Code to locate requirements related to piping materials, fittings, joints, water systems, and installation practices.

Practice UMC navigation. Use the Uniform Mechanical Code to locate requirements involving mechanical systems, hydronic relationships, equipment, and mechanical piping.

Learn which code to use. One of the most useful skills on a multi-reference open-book exam is choosing the correct book before beginning a search. Candidates should practice identifying whether a question is primarily plumbing-code or mechanical-code based.

Use timed searches. With 75 minutes for 30 questions, reference lookups should be efficient. Practice locating code provisions within a short period so that one difficult question does not consume too much of the examination.

Balance code study with trade knowledge. The exam can include questions based on trade knowledge and general industry practices. Candidates should understand the technical concepts rather than relying entirely on the approved books.

How does the Chilled-Water Piping Contractor (C-1I) exam work?

The Chilled-Water Piping Contractor (C-1I) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
