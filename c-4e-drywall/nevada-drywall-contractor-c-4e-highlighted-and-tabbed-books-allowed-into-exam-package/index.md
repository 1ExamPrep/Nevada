## State Requirements

Nevada contractor licensing is administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board. A business seeking a contractor license must have an individual who is qualified to oversee the applicable construction work and business activities associated with the requested classification.

The trade qualifying individual generally must have at least **four full years of applicable experience within the 15 years immediately before the application is filed**. Qualifying experience may be at the journeyman, supervising employee, foreman, or contractor level in the classification requested.

Accepted education or training may be used toward a portion of the experience requirement when it satisfies Nevada's applicable rules, but education does not automatically replace the complete experience requirement.

Nevada also requires applicants to satisfy applicable financial responsibility and contractor bond requirements. The amount of the required contractor bond is determined during the licensing process based on factors established for the applicant and license.

The qualifying individual generally must pass both the Nevada Business and Law examination and the trade examination specific to the requested classification unless the individual qualifies for an examination waiver.

Candidates cannot simply schedule a Nevada contractor examination independently before completing the required application steps. Examination eligibility is provided after the contractor application has been submitted and the applicable experience requirement has been reviewed.

## How does the Drywall Contractor (C-4E) exam work?

The Drywall Contractor (C-4E) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
