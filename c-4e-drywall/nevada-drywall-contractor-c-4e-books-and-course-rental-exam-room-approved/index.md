## Exam Details

The Nevada C-4E Drywall trade examination is administered by PSI for the Nevada State Contractors Board. The examination contains **20 questions**, requires a minimum passing score of **14 correct answers**, and allows **1 hour** for completion.

The official C-4E content outline divides the examination into four subject areas:

- **Installation — 6 questions:** The portion addresses knowledge associated with installing drywall and gypsum wallboard systems using accepted trade practices and methods.
- **Taping and Finishing — 6 questions:** Candidates should understand the procedures, materials, and techniques associated with taping, coating, and finishing gypsum wallboard surfaces.
- **Materials — 4 questions:** The section evaluates familiarity with materials used in drywall construction and their appropriate applications.
- **Special Applications and Accessories — 4 questions:** Candidates should be prepared for questions involving specialized drywall applications and related components or accessories.

Installation and Taping and Finishing together account for 12 of the 20 questions, making these two areas an important part of C-4E exam preparation. Materials and Special Applications and Accessories account for the remaining eight questions.

The examination may include questions based on the listed reference materials as well as general industry practices and trade knowledge. For code-related questions, PSI bases the examination on the specific code edition identified in the official content outline. For the C-4E examination, that code is the **2018 International Building Code**.

Nevada's Contractor Management Survey is a separate Business and Law examination. The CMS contains **60 scored questions** with a minimum passing score of **45 correct answers**. Candidates receive 120 minutes for the scored questions. The examination also includes three non-scored items with an additional six minutes.

The CMS content outline covers Licensing, Estimating and Bidding, Lien Law, Financial Management, Tax Laws, Labor Laws, Project Management, Contracts, Risk Management, and Environmental and Safety. The **Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada** is the approved reference for the open-book CMS examination.

## Open Book Test

The Nevada C-4E Drywall examination is an **open-book test**. PSI specifically identifies the **International Building Code, 2018** as the reference permitted inside the examination center for this trade exam.

Candidates are responsible for bringing their approved reference to the examination center. PSI permits approved references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination session. Permanent tabs may be used to organize an approved book. Temporary tabs, including Post-It-style tabs, are not permitted and must be removed before the examination begins.

PSI does not permit candidates to write in or highlight their references during the examination session. Additional papers, whether loose or attached to the book, are also prohibited. Preparing the approved reference before the exam is therefore an important part of an open-book study strategy.

The included rental International Building Code, 2018 is highlighted and tabbed to support organized studying and faster reference navigation. The goal is to help you become familiar with the layout of the code and reduce unnecessary searching when you need to locate information.

The CMS Business and Law examination is also open book. Candidates may use the **Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada** during that examination. The trade and Business and Law exams use different approved references, so preparation should include practice navigating the correct book for each test.

## Licensing Steps

Obtaining a Nevada contractor license involves an application and qualification process through the Nevada State Contractors Board. The C-4E examination is one component of that process.

1. **Select the appropriate classification.** Candidates seeking authorization for the work covered by the Nevada C-4E Drywall classification should apply for the classification that matches their intended scope of contracting work.
2. **Identify the qualifying individual.** Nevada allows one individual to serve as both the management and trade qualifier, or different individuals may serve in those roles.
3. **Document qualifying trade experience.** The trade qualifying individual must satisfy the experience requirements established by the Nevada State Contractors Board for the requested classification.
4. **Submit the contractor license application.** The application and required supporting documents are filed with the Nevada State Contractors Board.
5. **Receive examination eligibility.** The Board issues examination eligibility after the application has been submitted and applicable experience has been verified.
6. **Schedule the required examinations.** Nevada has contracted with PSI to administer its contractor licensing examinations.
7. **Prepare for the C-4E and CMS examinations.** Review the official subject areas, study applicable trade knowledge, and practice navigating the approved exam-room references.
8. **Complete the required examinations.** The management qualifier must satisfy the CMS requirement, and the trade qualifier must satisfy the applicable C-4E trade examination requirement unless an approved waiver applies.
9. **Complete remaining Board requirements.** Contractor licensing also involves other requirements established by the Nevada State Contractors Board, including applicable financial and licensing documentation.

## State Requirements

The Nevada C-4E Drywall subclassification authorizes the **installation and coating of gypsum wallboard and sheathing, including the installation of a system of suspended ceilings made of gypsum wallboard**. The classification is part of Nevada's C-4 Painting and Decorating classification structure.

The trade qualifying individual generally must have at least **four full years of qualifying experience** within the **15 years immediately preceding the filing of the application**. Qualifying experience may be obtained as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the specific classification requested.

Nevada does not impose a general educational degree requirement for contractor licensing. Training received through an accredited college, university, or equivalent program accepted by the Board may be used to satisfy up to three years of the required experience when appropriately documented.

The Board accepts specific forms of documentation to establish trade experience. Depending on the applicant's circumstances, this may include Certification of Work Experience forms, a current master certification issued by a governmental agency in a substantially similar discipline, proof of transferable military experience and training, or qualifying licensure by endorsement.

The trade qualifier must be a bona fide owner, officer, member, manager, or employee of the company and must be actively engaged in the contracting business. Nevada contractor licenses belong to the licensed business entity rather than personally to the qualifying individual.

Applicants must also demonstrate financial responsibility and satisfy the additional application requirements established by the Nevada State Contractors Board. Passing an examination alone does not create or issue a contractor license.

## Test Information and Study Materials

The C-4E examination allows one hour for 20 questions, giving candidates an average of approximately three minutes per question. That makes time management and efficient reference navigation important parts of preparation. An open-book format can be helpful, but it does not eliminate the need to understand the trade material before testing.

Begin your C-4E preparation with the official content outline. Installation and Taping and Finishing contain six questions each and together represent 60% of the listed exam questions. Materials and Special Applications and Accessories contain four questions each. A balanced study plan should cover all four areas while placing appropriate study time on the larger categories.

Use the International Building Code as an active study tool instead of waiting until exam day to open it. Become familiar with the table of contents, index, chapters, code organization, tables, and major sections. When a practice question involves building-code requirements, locate the applicable information in the book. Repeated reference work can help you recognize where information is likely to be found.

The highlighted and tabbed rental reference supports this process by helping organize important areas of the book. Permanent tabs can provide visual landmarks, while highlighting can help relevant provisions stand out during review. The greatest value comes from practicing with the organized reference repeatedly so its layout becomes familiar.

Trade knowledge deserves equal attention. PSI notes that trade examinations may contain questions based on general industry practices in addition to information found in the listed references. Candidates should therefore review drywall installation, taping and finishing, materials, accessories, and special applications rather than relying exclusively on the building code.

For the Business and Law examination, study the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada while following the CMS content outline. Financial Management is the largest listed CMS category with 12 scored questions, followed by Licensing with 10 questions. Other areas include estimating, Nevada lien law, taxes, labor law, contracts, project management, risk management, and environmental and safety requirements.

Build your study schedule around consistent practice. Shorter, repeated study sessions can help you reinforce terminology, work through trade-focused material, and practice using the references under realistic time limits. As your exam date approaches, spend additional time on weaker subjects and continue practicing reference navigation.
