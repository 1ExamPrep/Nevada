Exam Details

The Nevada C-4E Drywall trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who have received examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-4E Drywall
  • Number of Questions: 20
  • Time Allowed: 1 hour
  • Minimum Passing Score: 14 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The current C-4E examination outline is divided into four technical subject areas:

  • Installation: 6 questions
  • Taping and Finishing: 6 questions
  • Materials: 4 questions
  • Special Applications and Accessories: 4 questions

Installation and Taping and Finishing are the two largest categories, with six questions each. Together they account for 12 of the 20 questions, so candidates should give both subjects substantial attention during preparation.

Installation study should include gypsum wallboard handling, layout, measuring, cutting, fastening, attachment methods, joints, openings, ceilings, sheathing, and general procedures used to produce a properly installed drywall system.

Taping and Finishing preparation should include joint treatment, tape, joint compounds, corner treatments, finishing sequences, surface preparation, and the procedures used to create completed gypsum board surfaces.

The Materials category contains four questions. Candidates should become familiar with gypsum wallboard products, sheathing, fasteners, compounds, trims, accessories, and other materials associated with drywall construction.

Special Applications and Accessories also contains four questions. Preparation should include suspended gypsum wallboard ceilings, specialty installations, trims, accessories, assemblies, and other conditions that go beyond basic wallboard installation.

Because the exam contains only 20 questions, each answer matters. Candidates should prepare across all four content areas instead of relying solely on practical installation experience or concentrating only on the largest categories.

Framing relationships, board installation, gypsum materials, fastening, finishing methods, ceiling systems, and accessory applications should become familiar before exam day.

Open-book testing still requires preparation. With one hour to complete 20 questions, candidates benefit from knowing which answers can be reached from trade knowledge and when a precise International Building Code lookup is appropriate.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-4E Drywall subclassification covers the installation and coating of gypsum wallboard and sheathing, including installation of suspended ceiling systems made of gypsum wallboard.

The classification is specialized and should not be treated as unrestricted authority for unrelated construction trades. Contractors must operate within the classification and scope for which their Nevada license is issued.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must have at least four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the specific classification requested.

The qualifying experience generally must have been obtained within the 15 years immediately preceding the application. Applicants should maintain clear documentation showing the nature of the work performed and how it relates to drywall contracting.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes financial responsibility and applicable bonding requirements. These obligations are separate from passing the C-4E examination and must be satisfied as part of the overall licensing process.

The contractor license is issued to the business entity, while qualifying individuals satisfy applicable management and trade qualification requirements. One person may serve in both roles when qualified, or the company may designate separate qualifying individuals.

How does the Drywall Contractor (C-4E) exam work?

The Drywall Contractor (C-4E) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
