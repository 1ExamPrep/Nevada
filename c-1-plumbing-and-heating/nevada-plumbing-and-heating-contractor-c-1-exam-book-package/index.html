Candidates should practice with the approved code books long before exam day. With five different exam-room references and 120 questions, it is important to recognize which book is most likely to contain the information needed for a particular topic.

Approved reference books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used. Temporary tabs are not permitted, and candidates should not add written notes or loose or attached papers to approved exam references.

State Requirements

Nevada's C-1 Plumbing and Heating Contracting classification is a broad specialty license. A person holding the primary C-1 classification may perform work authorized under the C-1 subclassifications.

The scope includes boilers and hot-water or steam piping with related fittings, valves, gauges, pumps, radiators, tanks, fuel-oil lines, chimneys, flues, and associated work. It also includes fire sprinkler systems and insulation of pipes and ducts.

C-1 work also extends to plumbing systems providing safe water, sanitary waste and venting, roof drainage, hydronic heating systems within the applicable classification scope, landscape irrigation, potable-water heating systems including solar equipment, and gas supply systems.

The classification further reaches into sheet metal fabrication and installation, heating and evaporative cooling systems, gas pipes and vents, water heaters, chilled-water piping, firefighter breathing-air systems, and industrial piping used to transport water, steam, gas, chemicals, food, and other substances.

Because the primary C-1 classification covers such a wide range of work, the examination is considerably broader than a plumbing-only test. Candidates should prepare for both plumbing and mechanical construction along with specialized subjects such as fire sprinklers, industrial piping, hydronics, refrigeration-related systems, welding, and safety.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-1 study plan should divide preparation into major systems rather than attempting to read every reference from beginning to end.

Drain, Waste, and Vent Systems should receive substantial attention. Review drainage sizing, slope, vents, fixture units, cleanouts, traps, interceptors, and sanitary-system requirements using the Uniform Plumbing Code.

Water Supply preparation should include sizing, pressure, materials, valves, backflow protection, supports, distribution, and system protection. Candidates should practice locating applicable UPC tables quickly.

Gas Systems should be studied with NFPA 54 and NFPA 58. Learn how natural gas and LP-Gas systems differ and practice selecting the correct reference before beginning a lookup.

Fire Sprinklers should be studied with NFPA 13. Focus on sprinkler systems, piping, components, spacing concepts, and installation requirements relevant to the C-1 examination.

Hydronics and Boilers should include heating-system components, pumps, valves, piping, heat transfer, controls, and boiler-related knowledge. The UMC and Modern Hydronic Heating reference can be used together during preparation.

Chilled Water Piping accounts for 6 questions. Candidates should review piping materials, valves, pumps, supports, insulation considerations, and system-operation concepts associated with chilled-water distribution.

Plumbing Mathematics and Isometric Analysis accounts for 8 questions. Practice pipe calculations, dimensions, elevations, geometry, offsets, areas, volumes, and plan interpretation until the steps become familiar.

Industrial Piping should include system materials, valves, equipment, supports, joining methods, and piping used for water, steam, gas, chemicals, food, and other industrial substances.

Safety should be learned before test day because the OSHA reference is not allowed into the examination. Use the study reference to strengthen knowledge of jobsite hazards, protective equipment, tools, excavation, and general construction safety.

Welding knowledge can support industrial and mechanical piping preparation. Review joint preparation, welding methods, equipment, workmanship, and safe procedures using Modern Welding.

Heating, Cooling, and Refrigeration concepts should also be part of the study plan because the C-1 classification includes heating and air-circulation work. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning can support this broader mechanical review.

Reference navigation should be practiced under timed conditions. Choose a question, identify whether it belongs in the UPC, UMC, NFPA 13, NFPA 54, or NFPA 58, and then locate the applicable provision. The ability to choose the correct book quickly can save as much time as knowing how to use the index.

More on test Information and Study Materials

With 120 questions in five hours, pacing matters. Candidates should avoid spending excessive time on one code lookup. If a question is taking too long, move forward and return later if time remains.

How does the Plumbing and Heating Contractor (C-1) exam work?

The Plumbing and Heating Contractor (C-1) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
