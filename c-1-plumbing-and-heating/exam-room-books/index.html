Open Book Test

The Nevada C-1 Plumbing and Heating Contractor examination is an open-book test.

The books included in this package and allowed into the examination are:

  • Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018

  • NFPA 13: Installation of Sprinkler Systems, 2019

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need for preparation. With 120 questions in five hours, candidates have an average of two and a half minutes per question. Searching multiple books without a clear idea of where to look can quickly consume valuable time.

A practical strategy is to identify the subject first and then select the appropriate reference. Plumbing questions often lead to the Uniform Plumbing Code. Mechanical, heating, hydronic, and related questions may lead to the Uniform Mechanical Code. Fire sprinkler questions may require NFPA 13, natural gas questions may require NFPA 54, and LP-Gas questions may require NFPA 58.

Candidates should practice code navigation before exam day. Use the table of contents, index, chapter structure, definitions, and tables repeatedly until the organization of each reference becomes familiar.

State Requirements

Nevada's C-1 Plumbing and Heating Contracting classification is broad and covers work across multiple plumbing and mechanical specialties.

The scope includes plumbing systems that provide safe water and sanitary drainage, waste, and vent systems. It also includes roof drainage, gas supply systems, water-heating systems, hydronic heating, and related piping work within the classification.

The C-1 scope also includes boilers and related piping, fire sprinkler systems, insulation of pipes and ducts, sheet metal work, heating and cooling systems, gas pipes and vents, chilled-water piping, industrial piping, and firefighter breathing-air systems.

The broad scope is reflected in the examination. Candidates may encounter questions involving plumbing code, mechanical systems, fire protection, gas systems, hydronics, piping, calculations, and system components.

The C-1 trade exam is only one part of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Applicants must also complete the applicable qualification, application, business and law, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-1 study plan should organize the exam into systems and connect each system with its most relevant reference.

Drain, Waste, and Vent Systems should receive substantial attention because this is the largest individual category. Candidates should review drainage sizing, slope, vents, fixture units, cleanouts, traps, interceptors, and sanitary plumbing requirements using the Uniform Plumbing Code.

Water Supply should include sizing, pressure, materials, valves, distribution, backflow protection, supports, and system protection. Practice using UPC tables and locating applicable requirements quickly.

Gas Systems should be studied with NFPA 54 and NFPA 58 nearby. Candidates should understand the difference between natural gas and LP-Gas systems and know which code applies to each.

Fire Sprinklers should be reviewed with NFPA 13. Candidates should become familiar with sprinkler system components, piping, installation concepts, and the organization of the standard.

Hydronics, Boilers, and Chilled Water Piping should be studied with the Uniform Mechanical Code. Review piping systems, valves, controls, pumps, equipment, supports, heat transfer, and system operation.

Water Heaters and Venting should include installation, combustion air, venting, temperature and pressure protection, fuel-gas considerations, and related code provisions.

Traps and Interceptors account for 8 questions. Candidates should understand trap function, seal protection, interceptor applications, installation requirements, and related UPC provisions.

Plumbing Mathematics and Isometric Analysis also account for 8 questions. Candidates should practice measurements, pipe calculations, elevations, areas, volumes, offsets, and interpretation of piping layouts.

Code books can provide tables and values, but candidates should still be comfortable performing the calculations. Efficient mathematics can save time that can then be used for more difficult reference lookups.

Storm Drainage should include roof drainage, conductors, sizing, drainage pathways, and related code requirements.

General Plumbing Requirements should be reviewed throughout preparation rather than treated as a separate subject. Many general provisions apply across water, waste, vent, fixture, and piping questions.

A useful study method is to create five groups of practice questions: UPC, UMC, NFPA 13, NFPA 54, and NFPA 58. This helps build the habit of identifying the correct reference before beginning a search.

After candidates become comfortable selecting the correct book, practice should become more mixed. Randomly combine plumbing, mechanical, sprinkler, natural gas, and LP-Gas questions so the process more closely resembles the actual examination.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Timed navigation drills can also improve efficiency. Select a topic, choose the reference, locate the section or table, and record how long the lookup takes. Repeat slower subjects until navigation becomes more natural.

The table of contents and index are useful, but candidates should also learn the overall structure of each book. Knowing which chapter contains a major subject can be faster than beginning with the index every time.

Definitions should also be reviewed because technical terminology can affect the meaning of a question. Understanding how a code defines a term can help candidates interpret both the question and the applicable requirement.

Because the exam contains 120 questions, pacing should be part of every practice session. Candidates should avoid spending too much time on a single code search. If a question becomes difficult, moving forward and returning later can help protect overall exam time.

How does the Plumbing and Heating Contractor (C-1) exam work?

The Plumbing and Heating Contractor (C-1) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
