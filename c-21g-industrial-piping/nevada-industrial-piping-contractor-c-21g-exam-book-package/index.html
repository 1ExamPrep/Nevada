Exam Details

The Nevada C-21G Industrial Piping trade examination evaluates knowledge associated with piping and equipment used in industrial applications. Because the classification can involve water, steam, gas, chemicals, food, and other industrial substances, candidates should expect their preparation to extend beyond ordinary plumbing installation.

Industrial piping candidates should understand how piping systems are assembled, supported, connected, repaired, and integrated with equipment. Preparation should include piping materials, fittings, valves, joints, supports, system components, mechanical-code requirements, plumbing-code requirements, welding, process-piping principles, trade mathematics, and construction safety.

The Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018 should be studied for mechanical-system provisions, equipment, piping, ventilation, installation requirements, clearances, and other technical requirements that may apply to industrial piping work.

The Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018 should be studied for applicable piping materials, fittings, installation methods, testing requirements, system components, and plumbing-code provisions relevant to piping systems.

Supplemental books help candidates build the knowledge needed for questions that cannot simply be researched from the exam-room codes. ASME B31.3 strengthens understanding of process piping, Modern Welding supports welding preparation, and Mathematics for Plumbers and Pipefitters reinforces the calculations commonly encountered in piping work.

The Facility Piping Systems Handbook provides additional background on industrial and facility piping arrangements, while Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning supports broader mechanical-system knowledge. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 helps candidates review common construction-safety requirements and hazards.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada C-21G Industrial Piping examination is one part of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable qualification and licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the trade exam.

  1. Confirm the C-21G classification. Determine that the industrial piping work the business intends to perform falls within the scope of the classification.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor application must identify the individual who provides the applicable trade qualification.
  3. Prepare experience documentation. Organize the work history and supporting information needed to demonstrate applicable industrial piping experience.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Submit the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. Nevada reviews the application and experience before examination eligibility is established.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. Once authorized, the qualifying individual can schedule the applicable contractor examinations.
  7. Prepare for the C-21G trade examination. Study piping, process piping, welding, calculations, mechanical systems, construction safety, and approved code references.
  8. Practice with the UPC and UMC. Use both approved references throughout preparation to build faster and more confident code-navigation habits.
  9. Complete applicable examinations. Satisfy the trade and Business and Law examination requirements unless an authorized waiver applies.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish all other applicable requirements before a Nevada contractor license is issued.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-21G Industrial Piping classification covers the fabrication, installation, repair, and removal of piping and equipment used for the transmission of water, steam, gas, chemicals, food, and other substances for industrial uses.

The broad scope can apply to piping systems in manufacturing, processing, industrial, and commercial facilities. Candidates should therefore prepare for systems that may differ significantly in materials, operating conditions, joining methods, equipment, and safety requirements.

Industrial piping work can involve welded systems, mechanical joints, pumps, valves, equipment connections, supports, specialized piping materials, and process-related components. Understanding how these elements work together is an important part of trade preparation.

The Nevada contractor licensing process also includes requirements beyond passing the C-21G examination. Applicants must complete the applicable qualification, application, business, financial, and licensing requirements before a contractor license can be issued.

The trade exam should therefore be treated as an important part of a larger licensing process rather than the only requirement for becoming a Nevada Industrial Piping contractor.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-21G study plan should combine code navigation with practical industrial piping knowledge. Candidates should understand materials, valves, fittings, supports, equipment connections, joining methods, system layout, testing concepts, welding, calculations, and safe work practices.

Uniform Mechanical Code Preparation should focus on locating mechanical requirements efficiently. Practice with piping, equipment, clearances, ventilation, installation provisions, definitions, and applicable technical tables.

Uniform Plumbing Code Preparation should focus on piping-related provisions, materials, fittings, testing, installation methods, and system requirements. Candidates should become comfortable using both the index and chapter organization.

Process Piping should receive dedicated study through ASME B31.3. Review terminology, materials, fabrication, joining, examination, testing, and the general principles associated with piping systems used in industrial facilities.

Because ASME B31.3 is not allowed inside the examination center, candidates should practice recalling major concepts without keeping the book open. Read a section, close the reference, and answer practice questions before checking the material again.

Welding preparation should include common welding processes, joint preparation, positions, equipment, terminology, basic inspection concepts, and safe work practices. Industrial piping systems frequently rely on welded connections, making welding knowledge valuable to the trade.

Trade Mathematics should be practiced regularly. Candidates should become comfortable solving measurement, fraction, decimal, geometry, elevation, offset, and other pipefitting problems without depending on the study book during the exam.

Facility Piping study should focus on system layout, valves, equipment connections, supports, piping materials, and how multiple piping systems operate within industrial facilities.

Mechanical Systems preparation can be strengthened through Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning. Review pumps, controls, heat transfer, piping, equipment, and system-operation principles that can support broader industrial piping knowledge.

Safety should be studied using OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Industrial piping work can involve welding, heavy materials, ladders, elevated work, tools, equipment, material handling, and other construction hazards.

For the two exam-room codes, practice timed lookups. Read a question, identify the main technical issue, decide whether the UMC or UPC is the better reference, and locate the applicable provision.

If a lookup takes too long, record the subject and repeat it later. Familiarity grows through repetition, and frequently encountered topics should gradually become easier to locate.

When reviewing an answer, pay attention to definitions, tables, exceptions, and related sections. Technical code questions may depend on more than a single sentence.

How does the Industrial Piping Contractor (C-21G) exam work?

The Industrial Piping Contractor (C-21G) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
