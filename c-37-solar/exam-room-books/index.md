## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-37 preparation strategy should begin with **System Design** because it represents 15 of the 45 examination questions. Candidates should understand how solar thermal collectors, piping, pumps, storage, valves, controls, and heat-transfer components work together.

Review complete system layouts rather than memorizing isolated components. Candidates should be able to follow the movement of heat from the collector through the circulation system and into storage or the final heating application.

**Piping** is another major subject with 10 questions. This is one of the most important areas for Uniform Plumbing Code navigation. Candidates should become familiar with pipe materials, fittings, supports, joining methods, routing, and installation requirements.

Practice locating plumbing requirements by subject. For example, begin with a question about pipe material and use the index to find the appropriate section. Repeat the same exercise with fittings, supports, valves, and connections.

The **Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018** should be used throughout preparation rather than only during the final days before the examination. Regular use helps make the structure of the book more familiar.

**Definitions** can be especially important. A question may depend on the specific code meaning of a plumbing term, and locating the definition can help determine which section applies.

**Tables** should also receive attention. Important plumbing requirements may appear in tables, and candidates should practice reading headings, notes, conditions, and related references carefully.

**Exceptions** can modify a general rule. Candidates should read enough surrounding code language to identify exceptions or special conditions before selecting an answer.

**Cross-references** may direct the candidate to another section containing additional requirements. Practice following these references instead of assuming the first section contains the complete answer.

**Collectors** represents 7 questions. Candidates should understand the basic purpose of solar thermal collectors and how they transfer solar energy into a working fluid or thermal system.

Review collector arrangement, mounting concepts, orientation, circulation, and the relationship between collector placement and system performance.

**Pumps and Storage Systems** represents 5 questions. Candidates should understand how pumps circulate fluids through a solar thermal system and how storage allows collected energy to be used when needed.

Review circulation principles, pump placement, storage tanks, heat exchangers, and the relationship between system demand and stored thermal energy.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

**System Valves and Controls** accounts for 3 questions. Review the purpose of shutoff valves, check valves, balancing devices, temperature controls, differential controls, and other system components that regulate operation.

**Safety** represents 5 questions. Candidates should prepare for practical jobsite safety involving ladders, elevated work, roof access, tools, piping, hot surfaces, electrical control components, lifting, and other installation hazards.

Because this package contains only the exam-room-approved Uniform Plumbing Code, candidates should make sure their broader study plan also covers system design, collectors, pumps, storage, controls, and safety.

**Heat transfer** is a useful core concept for solar thermal preparation. Candidates should understand how energy collected from sunlight is transferred into a working fluid and then moved to storage or another heating application.

**System balance** should also be studied. Proper fluid movement through collectors can affect system performance, so candidates should understand the basic purpose of balanced piping and controlled circulation.

**Freeze protection** is another practical area worth reviewing. Solar thermal components can be exposed to outdoor conditions, so system design must account for environmental temperatures and appropriate protection methods.

**Insulation** is important because heat can be lost through exposed piping. Candidates should understand why solar thermal piping is insulated and how heat loss can affect system performance.

**Expansion** should be considered in systems exposed to high temperatures. Hot piping can expand, so supports, materials, and system design must account for thermal movement.

A useful study schedule is to divide preparation according to the exam outline. Spend the most time on System Design and Piping, followed by Collectors, Pumps and Storage Systems, Safety, and System Valves and Controls.

Once each area becomes familiar, move into mixed-question practice. The actual exam can shift quickly from a piping requirement to a system-design question or collector concept.

Timed lookup drills are especially useful because candidates have approximately two minutes per question. Choose a plumbing topic and practice locating the relevant UPC section within a limited amount of time.

Do not depend on finding every answer in the book. Some questions will be better answered through retained trade knowledge, while the UPC should be used for targeted confirmation of code-based requirements.

## How does the Solar Contractor (C-37) exam work?

The Solar Contractor (C-37) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
