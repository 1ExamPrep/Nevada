Nevada Contractor License: 15 Questions People Ask, Answered

Every Nevada contractor license takes the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam at PSI, four years of experience and a bond. Here are straight answers to the 15 questions people search most. Each answer is short, plain and checked against the official board and exam sources.

nevada contractor licensenscbbusiness and lawcms

Key takeaways

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the open-book Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam at PSI.
  • The NSCB application fee is $300; PSI charges $95 for one exam or $140 for a trade exam with the CMS.
  • The CMS has 60 scored questions in 120 minutes, and 45 right (75 percent) passes.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license.

Updated November 23, 2026 · prices checked against the 1 Exam Prep store

Nevada requires a contractor license from the Nevada State Contractors Board (NSCB) for almost all construction work for others. Every applicant passes the open-book Business and Law exam, called the CMS, plus a trade exam for the classification, both at PSI. Most licenses need four years of experience in the last 15 and a bond. Here are answers to the 15 questions Nevadans search most.

Do you need a contractors license in Nevada?

Yes, for nearly all construction work you perform for others, including remodeling, roofing, plumbing, electrical and landscaping. The Nevada State Contractors Board issues the license, and it covers the business that bids the work. A narrow handyman exemption applies to small jobs that need no building permit, so check the NSCB rules before relying on it. Bidding or advertising without a license is itself a violation in Nevada, not only doing the work.

Does Nevada require a general contractor license?

Yes. General building contractors hold a Class B license, such as B General Building or B-2 Residential and Small Commercial. Each one takes the CMS exam, the B trade exam, four years of experience in the last 15, a bond and a monetary limit. Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam. Since March 2026, Nevada also issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.

How do I get a Nevada contractor license?

Follow five steps:

  1. Pick your classification, such as B-2, C-1 Plumbing and Heating or C-2 Electrical.
  2. Gather proof of four years of experience in the last 15 and a financial statement.
  3. Apply to the NSCB and pay the $300 application fee.
  4. When the Board sends your exam eligibility letter, book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI.
  5. Pass both, post the bond the Board sets, and the license is issued.

How much does it cost to get a contractors license in Nevada?

The fixed costs are small next to the bond. The NSCB application fee is $300. PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken together with the CMS. The Board then sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 based on your experience and finances, and you pay a bond company a yearly premium on that amount. Add study materials, which for an open-book exam usually means the manual and the code books for your trade.

How long does it take to get a contractor license in Nevada?

Plan on two to three months from application to license. NSCB review of the experience and financial statement takes the longest, often several weeks. Once the eligibility letter arrives, most candidates study for 3 to 6 weeks and book both exams on the same day. PSI gives scores right after the exam, so the last step is posting the bond.

How hard is it to get a contractors license in Nevada?

The experience and paperwork stop more applicants than the exams do. You need four years of experience in the last 15 that the Board can verify, plus a financial statement it accepts. The exams are manageable with preparation: the CMS is open book, and you get three attempts before the application is void. Candidates who fail usually run out of time looking things up, not knowledge.

Is the Nevada contractors license test open book?

Partly. The Business and Law (CMS) exam is open book, and the only reference allowed is the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada. Trade exams are closed book except for the code references named in the PSI content outline for your classification. That is why exam-room book packages are sold as allowed in the exam: they hold only the references PSI permits.

How many questions are on the Nevada CMS exam?

The CMS has 60 scored questions in 120 minutes, and you need 45 right, or 75 percent, to pass. That is two minutes a question with the manual open. A highlighted and tabbed manual turns most lookups into one flip, which matters more than memorizing. Trade exam lengths vary by classification and are listed in the PSI bulletin.

How do I become a licensed contractor in Nevada with an LLC?

Form the LLC or corporation first, then apply for the license in the business's name. The license goes to the entity, and the person who qualifies it, the qualified employee or officer, sits for the exams. The Board reviews the entity's financial statement when it sets the bond and the monetary limit, so keep the business books in order before you apply.

What is the monetary limit on a Nevada license?

Every Nevada license carries a monetary limit, the largest single job you may bid. The Board sets it from your experience and financial statement when it approves the license. You can ask the Board to raise it later as your finances grow. Bidding above your limit is a violation, so check it before you price a large job.

Does Nevada accept a California contractors license?

Not automatically. A California license does not let you work in Nevada. You apply to the NSCB for a Nevada license, but the Board can waive the trade exam for contractors licensed in a state it has a reciprocity agreement with, and California is one it has worked with. Check the NSCB reciprocity rules for your trade before you book exams. Expect the Nevada business requirements, bond and monetary limit to apply either way.

Do electricians need a state license in Nevada?

No state license, but a local card. Nevada has no state electrician license. Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site, issued by Electrical Safety Professionals after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage. Cards are valid for 3 years. Electrical contractors also need a C-2 license from the NSCB.

Do plumbers need a license in Nevada?

Plumbing contractors need an NSCB license, such as C-1 Plumbing and Heating or C-1D Plumbing, with the CMS exam. Individual journeyman plumber cards are issued by local boards, not by the Contractors Board. In Reno, the journeyman plumbing exam is open book and written to the 2024 Uniform Plumbing Code.

When do you need a contractors license in Nevada?

Before you bid, advertise or sign a contract for construction work for others. Nevada treats offering the work without a license the same way it treats doing it. If you plan to grow into a new trade, such as adding roofing to a remodeling business, you also need that classification on your license before you take the job.

How do I keep my Nevada license active?

Renew with the NSCB before the license expires, keep the bond in force and report changes to the business, such as a new qualified employee or address. A lapsed license means you cannot bid or work until it is reinstated. For electricians, an ID card expired for more than a year means taking the ICC exam again.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to a few more questions people ask about the exam and license.

How many tries do I get at the PSI exams?

Three attempts before the application is void.

What is the B-7 Restricted License?

A restricted license the NSCB has issued since March 2026 for jobs up to $7,000.

Can I use NASCLA for the Nevada B license?

Yes. Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam; the CMS is still required.

Who licenses contractors in Nevada?

The Nevada State Contractors Board (NSCB) issues every contractor license in the state.

Who gives the Nevada contractor exams?

PSI gives both the Business and Law (CMS) exam and the trade exams. The CMS is open book.

Does every Nevada contractor need a bond?

Yes. The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 for each license, based on the license and the applicant.
