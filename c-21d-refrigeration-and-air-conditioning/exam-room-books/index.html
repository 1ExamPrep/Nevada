Exam Details

The Nevada C-21D Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Maintenance Contractor examination contains 60 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the test and must answer 42 questions correctly to pass.

The examination is focused on the knowledge needed to maintain and repair existing refrigeration and air-conditioning systems. Candidates should be prepared for technical questions involving system operation, troubleshooting, refrigeration components, controls, electrical devices, piping, airflow, testing, service procedures, and applicable mechanical code requirements.

A strong understanding of the refrigeration cycle is essential. Candidates should know how the compressor, condenser, metering device, and evaporator work together and how pressure, temperature, and refrigerant condition change through the system.

Troubleshooting is another major area of preparation. Candidates should understand how symptoms such as insufficient cooling, icing, high or low pressures, compressor problems, airflow issues, control failures, and electrical problems can point toward different causes.

Controls and electrical components should also be reviewed. Relays, contactors, thermostats, capacitors, motors, pressure switches, transformers, and safety controls are commonly involved in refrigeration and air-conditioning maintenance work.

With 60 questions in two hours, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes per question. Efficient preparation should therefore include both technical study and regular practice navigating the Uniform Mechanical Code.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-21D Maintenance as a subclassification under C-21 Refrigeration and Air-Conditioning.

The classification covers the repair and replacement of parts of existing air-conditioning and refrigeration units or systems. This can include compressors, motors, relays, coils, heat exchangers, and related equipment used to keep existing systems operating properly.

The scope is maintenance-oriented rather than design-oriented. C-21D work focuses on servicing existing systems and replacing components while remaining within the original system design.

The classification does not include work that alters the design of an existing system. It also does not include certain major equipment replacements, including complete condenser assemblies on remote systems and air handlers.

The distinction is important for exam preparation because the C-21D classification emphasizes service, diagnostics, maintenance, repair, component replacement, and system testing rather than complete HVAC installation or redesign.

The C-21D trade examination is one part of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, and other state requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-21D preparation plan should combine strong troubleshooting knowledge with regular use of the Uniform Mechanical Code.

The Refrigeration Cycle should be one of the first technical subjects reviewed. Candidates should understand compression, condensation, expansion, and evaporation and how refrigerant pressure and temperature change through each stage.

Compressors are central to refrigeration and air-conditioning systems. Candidates should understand compressor function, pressure relationships, lubrication, electrical operation, overheating, abnormal operating conditions, and common failure symptoms.

Condensers should be studied in relation to heat rejection, airflow, coil cleanliness, fan operation, ambient temperature, and system pressure. Restricted airflow or dirty coils can significantly affect system performance.

Evaporators should be reviewed in relation to heat absorption, airflow, refrigerant condition, icing, condensate management, and overall system capacity.

Metering Devices should be studied according to their role in controlling refrigerant flow and creating the pressure difference needed for the refrigeration cycle. Candidates should understand how improper operation can affect evaporator performance and system pressures.

Refrigerant System Troubleshooting should include recognition of abnormal suction and discharge pressures, airflow restrictions, refrigerant restrictions, improper charge, component problems, and other conditions that can reduce system performance.

Controls and Electrical Components should include thermostats, relays, contactors, capacitors, transformers, pressure switches, solenoid valves, motors, and safety controls.

Maintenance technicians should understand how to distinguish between an electrical-control problem and a refrigeration-system problem. A compressor that does not start, for example, can result from several different electrical, control, or mechanical causes.

Motors and Blowers should be reviewed in relation to airflow, electrical operation, bearings, capacitors, rotation, speed, and mechanical condition. Poor airflow can affect comfort, equipment performance, coil temperatures, and refrigeration pressures.

Coils and Heat Exchangers should be studied in relation to heat transfer, airflow, cleanliness, operating condition, and maintenance. Dirty or damaged heat-transfer surfaces can affect both efficiency and system performance.

Piping and Tubing should include line function, materials, fittings, supports, insulation, leaks, restrictions, routing, and service practices. Candidates should understand how piping conditions can influence refrigerant flow, oil return, and pressure drop.

Testing and Diagnosis should include evaluation of pressure, temperature, airflow, electrical measurements, component operation, and overall system behavior. Troubleshooting should follow a systematic process rather than guessing which component needs replacement.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Preventive Maintenance is also important. Candidates should understand routine inspection of filters, coils, motors, electrical connections, condensate systems, refrigerant lines, controls, and other components that can affect system reliability.

The Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018 should be used as the primary code-navigation reference during preparation. Candidates should learn where major refrigeration and mechanical subjects are located and how the code is organized.

Start by reviewing the table of contents and major chapters. Then practice locating subjects involving refrigeration equipment, machinery spaces, ventilation, mechanical equipment, piping, ducts, and other topics relevant to maintenance work.

Definitions should also be reviewed because technical terminology can affect how a code requirement is interpreted. Knowing the code definition of a term can help candidates understand both the question and the applicable section.

A useful practice method is to create a list of common C-21D topics and time how long it takes to locate each one in the UMC. Repeat slower searches until navigation becomes more efficient.

Practice questions should be approached from technical knowledge first. If the question asks about a specific mechanical rule, then use the UMC to confirm the applicable requirement. This helps candidates learn when a code lookup is useful.

Because the exam contains 60 questions in two hours, pacing should be practiced. Candidates should avoid spending too much time on one difficult code search. If necessary, move on and return to the question later.

A service-oriented study sequence can help organize preparation. Start with the refrigeration cycle, then move through compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, controls, motors, airflow, piping, testing, and troubleshooting.

The approach connects individual technical topics to the way a refrigeration or air-conditioning system is actually diagnosed and serviced in the field.

How does the Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Contractor (C-21D) exam work?

The Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Contractor (C-21D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
