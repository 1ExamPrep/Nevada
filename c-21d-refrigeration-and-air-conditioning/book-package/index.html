Exam Details

The Nevada C-21D Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Maintenance Contractor examination contains 60 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the examination and must answer 42 questions correctly to pass.

The exam is designed around the knowledge required to maintain and repair existing refrigeration and air-conditioning systems. Preparation should include refrigeration principles, troubleshooting, mechanical equipment, controls, electrical components, piping, ventilation, system testing, equipment service, and safety.

Candidates should be comfortable with the basic refrigeration cycle and the way major components interact. A service technician must be able to recognize whether a problem is related to the compressor, condenser, evaporator, metering device, airflow, controls, electrical components, refrigerant circuit, or another part of the system.

Troubleshooting skills are particularly important for the C-21D classification. Candidates should understand how symptoms such as insufficient cooling, high or low pressures, compressor problems, poor airflow, icing, electrical failures, and control malfunctions can point toward different causes.

Because the examination allows two hours for 60 questions, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes per question. Efficient preparation should therefore combine strong technical knowledge with fast use of the approved mechanical code.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-21D Maintenance as a subclassification under C-21 Refrigeration and Air-Conditioning.

The classification authorizes the repair and replacement of parts of existing air-conditioning and refrigeration units or systems, including compressors, motors, relays, coils, heat exchangers, and other associated equipment.

The scope is specifically directed toward maintaining and repairing existing systems. It does not authorize replacement of complete condenser assemblies on remote systems, replacement of air handlers, or work that alters the design of an existing system.

This makes C-21D different from a broader installation classification. Candidates should prepare for service-oriented work such as diagnostics, maintenance, repair, component replacement, system testing, and recognition of operating problems rather than full HVAC system redesign.

Understanding the limits of the classification is important. A maintenance contractor may work extensively within an existing system while still being restricted from performing work that changes the original system design or replaces certain major assemblies.

The C-21D examination is only one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy the applicable qualification, application, business and law, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-21D study plan should focus heavily on troubleshooting and maintenance. The classification is built around repairing and replacing components in existing systems, so candidates should be able to evaluate symptoms and connect them to likely equipment or system problems.

The Refrigeration Cycle should be one of the first areas reviewed. Candidates should understand compression, condensation, expansion, and evaporation and how pressure, temperature, and refrigerant condition change throughout the cycle.

Compressors are central to refrigeration and air-conditioning systems. Review compressor function, operating conditions, electrical components, lubrication concepts, overheating, abnormal pressures, and common causes of compressor failure.

Condensers should be studied in relation to heat rejection, airflow, coil condition, fan operation, ambient temperature, and pressure. Candidates should understand how restricted airflow or dirty surfaces can affect system performance.

Evaporators should be reviewed in relation to heat absorption, airflow, coil temperature, refrigerant condition, icing, and drainage. Problems in the evaporator section can produce symptoms that resemble refrigerant or airflow issues.

Metering Devices should be studied according to their role in controlling refrigerant flow and creating the pressure difference needed for the refrigeration cycle. Candidates should understand how improper operation can affect superheat, evaporator performance, and system pressures.

Refrigerant System Troubleshooting should include recognition of abnormal high-side and low-side pressures, undercharge, overcharge, airflow restrictions, refrigerant restrictions, contamination, and component problems.

Controls and Electrical Components are another major part of maintenance work. Candidates should review relays, contactors, thermostats, capacitors, motors, pressure switches, transformers, safety controls, and basic control circuits.

A technician should understand how to separate an electrical failure from a refrigeration-system failure. A compressor that does not run, for example, can result from control issues, electrical problems, safety-device operation, or compressor failure.

Motors and Blowers should be studied in relation to airflow, electrical operation, rotation, bearings, capacitors, speed, and mechanical condition. Poor airflow can affect both comfort and refrigeration-system pressures.

Heat Exchangers and heating components should be reviewed where they are part of existing HVAC systems. Candidates should understand basic operating principles, inspection concerns, airflow effects, and maintenance considerations.

Piping and Tubing should include refrigerant line function, fittings, supports, insulation, leaks, restrictions, and proper service practices. The condition of piping can affect pressure drop, refrigerant flow, oil return, and overall system performance.

More on test Information and Study Materials

System Testing should include pressure and temperature evaluation, electrical measurements, airflow observations, leak detection concepts, and the process of comparing actual system behavior with expected operation.

Preventive Maintenance should be considered alongside repair work. Cleaning coils, checking filters, inspecting electrical connections, evaluating motors, examining condensate systems, and monitoring system operation can help identify issues before larger failures occur.

The Troubleshooting and Servicing Modern Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Systems reference should be used to develop a systematic diagnostic approach. Instead of replacing components based only on symptoms, candidates should practice confirming the cause of a problem through observation and testing.

The Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd edition provides the technical foundation needed to understand why a system behaves the way it does. Candidates should connect theory to service work so pressure, temperature, airflow, and electrical readings have practical meaning.

Safety must also be part of preparation. Refrigeration and air-conditioning maintenance may involve electrical circuits, pressurized refrigerant systems, ladders, tools, moving machinery, hot surfaces, sharp sheet metal, and elevated work locations.

Because the OSHA reference is not allowed in the examination, candidates should move from reading to active recall. Review a safety topic, close the book, and practice explaining the correct procedure without looking at the text.

The Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018 should be handled differently because it is permitted during the exam. Practice using the table of contents, index, definitions, refrigeration chapters, equipment provisions, and ventilation sections under timed conditions.

A useful study method is to work through troubleshooting questions without the technical books nearby, then use the UMC only when the question requires a specific mechanical code provision. This better reflects the balance between retained technical knowledge and open-book navigation.

Time management should also be practiced. With 60 questions in two hours, candidates should avoid spending several minutes searching for one answer. If a lookup becomes difficult, continue with the exam and return later when possible.

How does the Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Contractor (C-21D) exam work?

The Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Contractor (C-21D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
