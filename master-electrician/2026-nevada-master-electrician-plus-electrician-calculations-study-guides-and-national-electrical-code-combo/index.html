Licensing Steps

Nevada electrical licensing steps depend on the contractor classification or credential being pursued. A typical Nevada contractor licensing path includes identifying the correct license classification, reviewing Nevada State Contractors Board requirements, completing the contractor license application, submitting experience documentation, demonstrating financial responsibility, receiving exam eligibility, scheduling the required examinations with PSI, preparing with NEC-based study materials, passing the required exams, and completing final licensing steps with the Board.

The first step is choosing the proper classification. Electrical work may fall under C-2 Electrical Contracting or a related electrical sub-classification, depending on the scope of work. Selecting the correct classification is important because the trade examination, experience review, and license scope are tied to the classification requested.

Applicants should also understand that Nevada contractor licensing is business-focused. The licensing process may involve qualifying individual information, business entity information, experience certification, financial documentation, bonding, fees, and compliance with Board requirements. State Requirements

Nevada contractor licensing is administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board. Applicants seeking an electrical contractor license must satisfy the Board’s requirements for the license classification they are pursuing. These requirements can include experience, application review, examinations, financial responsibility, bonding, and business compliance items.

Nevada requires each applicant to pass a business and law examination or Construction Management Survey and a trade examination specific to the requested license classification, unless the applicant qualifies for a waiver or endorsement pathway. The trade examination for an electrical classification focuses on electrical work, code knowledge, safety, installation requirements, and related trade concepts.

Nevada may allow certain applicants licensed in substantially equivalent states to qualify through licensure by endorsement. Under qualifying circumstances, the Board may waive the state-specific trade examination requirement and experience certification process requirement. Local requirements may still apply. Cities, counties, or other local authorities may require permits, inspections, business licensing, registrations, or local credentials before electrical work can be performed. Candidates should review both state contractor licensing requirements and local requirements for the area where they plan to work.

How does the Master Electrician (ICC G16) exam work?

The Master Electrician (ICC G16) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
  • Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
  • ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
  • Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
