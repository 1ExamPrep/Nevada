Exam Details

Nevada’s Master-level electrician credentialing is supported through the Nevada Electrical Qualifications Card Program. Program guidance explains that the International Code Council (ICC) offers National Electrician exams used to obtain Nevada Journeyman/Master electrical cards, with testing available through Pearson VUE testing centers and online via ICC PRONTO. The program notice also lists multiple NEC-edition exam options (including 2017 NEC and 2020 NEC exam titles), and it states that 2023 NEC electrical exam testing became available later as listed in the program update.

For Master-level testing, Nevada candidates commonly use the ICC National Standard Master Electrician exam series (often identified with the “Master Electrician” national standard exam code used for the NEC edition you select when scheduling). The ICC outline for the National Standard Master Electrician exam describes:

  • 100 multiple-choice questions
  • Open book format
  • 5-hour time limit

The outline also breaks down the exam by content area and weighting. Those percentages are a study roadmap, because they show where the exam puts its emphasis:

  • General Knowledge and Plan Reading: 12%
  • Services and Service Equipment: 16%
  • Feeders: 4%
  • Branch Circuits and Conductors: 16%
  • Wiring Methods and Materials: 19%
  • Equipment and Devices: 10%
  • Control Devices: 3%
  • Motors and Generators: 8%
  • Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions: 12%

This is exactly why practice exams are so effective. When your practice matches the exam’s weighting, your prep becomes more efficient. You spend more time on the highest-value categories—services, branch circuits, wiring methods, and special occupancies—while still maintaining broad coverage across all areas.

Open Book Test

Yes—Nevada’s Master-level testing through the ICC National Standard Master Electrician exam is open book. The ICC exam outline explicitly lists the National Standard Master Electrician exam as Open book—5-hour time limit.

Open book is a real advantage, but only when you’ve trained the right way. The test isn’t asking whether you own a code book—it’s testing whether you can use your references efficiently while the clock is running. Strong candidates don’t try to look up everything. They use references with intention: confirm what matters, avoid wandering searches, and keep momentum.

What open-book success looks like in practice:

  • Fast topic recognition: identify what the question is truly testing (service equipment, grounding concepts within service setup, conductor rules, wiring method limitations, motor requirements, or special occupancy provisions).
  • Efficient navigation: go directly to the likely article or table, confirm the requirement, and move on.
  • Exception awareness without overthinking: focus on what the question actually includes and avoid chasing exceptions that don’t apply.
  • Time protection: keep moving. A perfect search on one hard question can cost easy points on the next five.

Test Information and Study Materials

The National Standard Master Electrician exam is long enough to reward careful work—and demanding enough to punish slow searches. With 100 questions across a 5-hour session, your pace matters. The goal is to create a repeatable workflow you can rely on all the way through the final questions.

How to use the 12 practice exams for real improvement:

  • Start with a baseline exam. Take one practice exam early under realistic conditions. Your first score is less important than what it reveals: where do you lose points, and where do you lose time?
  • Build a “miss list” tied to the outline. Tag every missed question to a content bucket (services, branch circuits, wiring methods, special occupancies, motors, equipment/devices, plan reading).
  • Fix the cause, not just the answer. Misses usually come from one of three causes: you didn’t know the rule, you misread the question, or you couldn’t find the reference quickly enough. Each cause needs a different fix.
  • Repeat lookups until they get fast. Open-book success improves dramatically when your lookup time drops. Re-run missed questions and practice going straight to the right place.
  • Rotate focus areas. Don’t stay in one comfort zone. Services, branch circuits, wiring methods, and special occupancies carry heavy weight—cycle them consistently.

How to use the 2 full final exams:

  • Save them for the end. Finals are most valuable after you’ve already improved with multiple practice rounds and targeted review.
  • Simulate the full session. Take each final timed and uninterrupted. That’s how you build test stamina and pacing discipline.
  • Use results as your final study map. Your finals should identify the last gaps to tighten: a category you still miss, a question type that slows you down, or a reference lookup that takes too long.

Category-focused prep tips (matched to the ICC outline):

More on test Information and Study Materials

  • Services and service equipment (16%): Train precision. These questions often hinge on details, definitions, and correct application steps, so careful reading and confident code navigation are essential.
  • Branch circuits and conductors (16%): Practice both rule recognition and the ability to confirm conductor/overcurrent logic without getting stuck in a long search.
  • Wiring methods and materials (19%): Expect limitations, installation rules, and what’s permitted vs. prohibited. These are common “easy misses” when qualifiers are overlooked.
  • Special occupancies/equipment/conditions (12%): The questions reward the candidate who reads carefully and confirms the correct section quickly—especially when the scenario adds a specific condition.
  • Motors and generators (8%): Stay organized. Use a consistent process for identifying what the question is asking before you chase details.
  • Plan reading and general knowledge (12%): Practice here often builds quick points, especially when you train yourself to avoid second-guessing.

A practical open-book strategy to follow during study:

  • Answer what you know first. Don’t turn every question into a deep lookup. Protect your time for the questions that truly need verification.
  • Use keywords to navigate. Train yourself to recognize the keyword that points to the correct NEC location (service equipment, feeder, branch circuit, wiring method, motor, special occupancy).
  • Keep momentum. The exam is long. Consistent points across the entire session often beats perfect certainty on a few questions.

FAQ Section

Is the Nevada Master Electrician exam open book?

Yes. The ICC National Standard Master Electrician exam outline lists the exam as open book with a five-hour time limit.

How many questions are on the National Standard Master Electrician exam?

The ICC outline lists 100 multiple-choice questions.

How long is the exam session?

The ICC outline lists a five-hour time limit.

What topics does the exam emphasize most?

The ICC outline shows high weight in Wiring Methods and Materials, Services and Service Equipment, and Branch Circuits and Conductors, with additional weight in General Knowledge/Plan Reading and Special Occupancies/Equipment/Conditions.

What references are listed for the exam?

The ICC outline lists the National Electrical Code (NEC) as a primary reference and Ugly’s Electrical References (any edition) as an allowed reference (not required).

How are Nevada electrician exams scheduled?

Program guidance for the Nevada Electrical Qualifications Card Program states that ICC National Electrician exams used for Nevada Journeyman/Master electrical cards are available through Pearson VUE testing centers and online via ICC PRONTO.

How should I use the 2 full final exams?

Use them late in your prep as full dress rehearsals. Take each final timed and uninterrupted, then use your results to target the last weak areas before exam day.

What’s the fastest way to improve open-book performance?

Timed repetition. Practice questions force you to identify keywords, go directly to the right reference location, confirm the requirement efficiently, and move on. Over time your lookup speed improves and your answers become more consistent.

How does the Master Electrician (ICC G16) exam work?

The Master Electrician (ICC G16) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
  • Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
  • ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
  • Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
