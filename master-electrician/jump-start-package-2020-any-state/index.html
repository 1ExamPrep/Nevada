Exam Details

Master electrician exams are designed to measure readiness for higher-level electrical work, supervision, and code compliance decision-making. While exam structure varies by jurisdiction, master-level testing commonly blends:

  • NEC application questions where you must locate, confirm, and apply the correct requirement using the code book.
  • Knowledge-based questions that assess trade understanding without relying on the code text in front of you.
  • Calculations and problem solving that require clean setups, unit awareness, and accuracy under time pressure.
  • Scenario interpretation where small details matter (exceptions, conditions, and table usage).

The package supports both code-driven practice and “no-book” readiness by emphasizing active training. The included study guide and online kit are built around simulated exams and timed work so you can practice finding answers efficiently, strengthen weak areas, and develop a repeatable method for handling questions quickly.

What changes at the master level is not only what you’re tested on, but how you’re expected to think. Master testing often focuses on correct interpretation, code intent, and accurate application in realistic scenarios—exactly where candidates can lose points if they rely on memory instead of confirming requirements.

Open Book Test

Many master electrician exams include open-book or code-book-related questions that require you to use the NEC efficiently. Open-book questions reward candidates who can move quickly through the code, verify details precisely, and apply the rule exactly as written. This is where the National Electrical Code 2020 Paperback with Tabs becomes a practical advantage: the tabs help you reach the correct area faster, reducing wasted time and keeping your focus on interpretation and application.

Open-book success is a skill. It’s not about flipping until something looks familiar—it’s about building a reliable workflow:

  • Identify the target concept behind the question (wiring methods, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, load work, etc.).
  • Navigate to the correct chapter/article quickly using the book’s structure and your tabs.
  • Find the exact section that answers the question, not just a nearby topic.
  • Check tables, notes, and exceptions before committing.
  • Apply the requirement to the scenario and eliminate answer choices that conflict with the code language.

The 2020 Master Electrician Study Guide is designed to reinforce this workflow by forcing you to locate answers in the time limit. This kind of timed practice is one of the most effective ways to improve performance on code-based questions because it turns “knowing where it is” into a habit.

At the same time, many master exams also include “no book” (closed-book style) questions. The study guide and online course are designed to prepare you for both types of questions, so you don’t become dependent on the code book for everything. The goal is to be strong in both modes: quick and accurate with the NEC, and confident with essential trade knowledge when the code book isn’t part of the moment.

Licensing Steps

Master electrician licensing is set by the state or local authority, but the path often follows a similar progression. Here’s a practical overview of the steps most candidates move through as they plan for the master exam:

  1. Confirm eligibility based on documented experience, apprenticeship history, or required years of work at the journeyman level.
  2. Gather documentation that supports your work history, supervision records, or other required proof.
  3. Submit an application to the licensing authority or exam administrator.
  4. Receive approval to test and schedule your exam date.
  5. Prepare with the correct NEC edition and a structured practice plan that includes code lookups, calculations, and simulated exams.
  6. Take the exam with a timing strategy and a clear workflow for both code-book and no-book questions.
  7. Complete final licensing requirements after passing (fees, paperwork, and any jurisdiction-specific steps).

The Jump Start Package supports the most critical stage for most candidates: the focused preparation period between approval and exam day. A consistent routine—built around timed practice and active review—helps you avoid last-minute cramming and makes test day feel more familiar.

How does the Master Electrician (ICC G16) exam work?

The Master Electrician (ICC G16) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
  • Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
  • ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
  • Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
