## Exam Details

The Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor examination is administered by PSI for the Nevada State Contractors Board. Applicants must first submit a contractor-license application and have their experience reviewed. After the Board determines that the applicant is eligible, an examination eligibility letter is issued with scheduling instructions.

The C-2 Electrical Contractor trade examination contains 80 questions. Candidates receive four hours to complete the exam and must answer at least 56 questions correctly to meet the minimum passing score.

The content outline includes General Electrical Knowledge, Service, Feeders and Branch Circuits, Grounding and Bonding, Conductors and Cables, Raceways and Boxes, Special Occupancies and Equipment, Electrical Power, Motors, Low Voltage, Lighting, Illuminated Signs, Fire Detection and Alarm Systems, Safety Information, Transmission Lines, and Overcurrent Protection.

Conductors and Cables and Safety Information are among the larger categories, with eight questions assigned to each area. Service, Feeders and Branch Circuits, Grounding and Bonding, and Raceways and Boxes each account for seven questions.

General Electrical Knowledge, Electrical Power, and Motors each account for six questions. Lighting and Transmission Lines each account for five questions, while Overcurrent Protection accounts for four questions. The remaining questions cover low voltage, illuminated signs, fire detection and alarm systems, and special occupancies and equipment.

General electrical knowledge may involve terminology, theory, calculations, practical installation principles, and recognized trade practices. Service and feeder questions may require candidates to calculate loads, size conductors, identify disconnect requirements, select overcurrent protection, and apply grounding and bonding provisions.

Conductors and cable questions can involve ampacity, temperature limitations, adjustment factors, insulation types, permitted uses, physical protection, and installation requirements. Raceways and boxes may require familiarity with conduit systems, raceway fill, box fill, fittings, enclosures, and support requirements.

Motor questions may address conductor sizing, overload protection, branch-circuit protection, controllers, disconnects, and multiple-motor installations. Electrical power questions may include voltage, current, resistance, power, transformers, and related mathematical relationships.

Safety questions may involve recognized jobsite hazards, protective practices, equipment safety, and electrical work procedures. Transmission-line questions may address conductors, equipment, and systems associated with electrical distribution beyond standard building wiring.

The simulated practice exams included with this package help candidates become familiar with exam-style wording and time management. Practice results can reveal weaknesses in calculations, electrical theory, NEC navigation, safety knowledge, or question interpretation.

## Open Book Test

The Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing the approved reference materials listed in the current PSI examination bulletin.

The current bulletin identifies the National Electrical Code or National Electrical Code Handbook, 2017 Edition, and NFPA 72, National Fire Alarm Code or Handbook, 2010 or 2013 Edition, as approved references for the C-2 examination.

The included course and NEC are based on the 2023 edition. Candidates should also obtain and prepare the exact code editions approved for their official Nevada examination. Code language, tables, article organization, and technical requirements can change between editions.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Handwritten notes are not permitted. Candidates may not write in, highlight, underline, or index their references during the test.

Permanent tabs are allowed. Temporary tabs, removable flags, and Post-It-style markers are not permitted. Loose papers and additional attached pages are also prohibited.

The NEC tabs included with this package support organized study navigation in the 2023 NEC. Candidates preparing the approved exam-room code book should use only permanent tabs that comply with the current PSI rules.

An open-book test still requires extensive preparation. Four hours may appear generous, but candidates must answer 80 technical questions across a broad examination outline. Searching the entire code book for every answer can consume valuable time.

Students should become familiar with the NEC table of contents, index, Article 100 definitions, chapter structure, article numbering, tables, exceptions, informational notes, and annexes. They should also practice reading complete code sections because a condition, exception, or table note may affect the final answer.

## Licensing Steps

Begin by determining that the Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor classification matches the scope of work the business plans to perform. The C-2 classification covers the installation of electrical wires, fixtures, appliances, apparatuses, raceways, and conduits.

Identify the individuals who will serve as the management qualified individual and the trade qualified individual. One person may serve in both roles, or the business may designate separate individuals for management and trade qualification.

The management qualified individual is responsible for passing the Nevada Business and Law, or CMS, examination. The trade qualified individual is responsible for demonstrating the required electrical experience and passing the C-2 trade examination unless a waiver applies.

Gather documentation showing at least four full years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the requested classification. The experience must have been obtained within the 15 years immediately before the application is filed.

Qualifying experience can be documented through certification-of-work-experience forms and a detailed resume of experience. Accepted education or training may be used toward part of the experience requirement, subject to the Nevada State Contractors Board’s limits and approval.

Complete the Nevada contractor-license application and submit the required business, ownership, financial, background, and qualifying-party information. The Board begins processing after the application is complete and submitted with the required payment.

The Board reviews the trade experience and other licensing qualifications. After approval, the applicant receives an examination eligibility letter with instructions for registering and scheduling through PSI.

Prepare for both required examination portions. Use this package for NEC study, trade knowledge, calculations, and simulated electrical practice. Study the Nevada construction business and law material separately for the CMS examination.

Before test day, review the current PSI bulletin for approved references, identification requirements, testing-center procedures, calculator policies, and prohibited items. Prepare all reference books before arriving at the examination center.

After passing the required examinations, complete the remaining financial-responsibility, bond, background, and license-issuance requirements. Passing the examinations does not automatically activate the contractor license.

## State Requirements

Nevada electrical contracting is regulated by the Nevada State Contractors Board. The statewide contractor classification associated with unrestricted electrical contracting is C-2 Electrical Contractor.

The contractor license belongs to the business entity rather than the qualifying individual. Licenses may be issued to individuals, partnerships, corporations, limited liability companies, and joint ventures that satisfy the Board’s requirements.

The business must have a qualified individual who possesses the experience, knowledge, and authority needed to oversee the work performed under the license. The qualifier must be a genuine owner, officer, member, manager, or employee who is actively involved in the contracting business.

The trade qualified individual must document at least four years of qualifying experience obtained within the previous 15 years. The experience must be at the journeyman, foreman, supervisory, or contractor level in the classification requested.

Accepted training from an accredited college, university, or equivalent program approved by the Board may satisfy up to three years of the experience requirement. Transferable military training or a qualifying master certification may also support eligibility when accepted by the Board.

Nevada normally requires the qualifying individual to pass both the trade examination and the Business and Law examination unless a waiver applies. Licensure by endorsement may allow the Board to waive the trade exam and experience certification for qualifying applicants licensed in an approved state with substantially equivalent requirements.

The contractor applicant must also demonstrate financial responsibility. The Board may require financial statements, bank verification, background disclosures, fingerprints, and other documents as part of the licensing review.

A contractor bond must be filed and continuously maintained in the amount established for the license. The license may also include a monetary limit governing the size of projects the contractor is authorized to undertake.

Contractors and subcontractors must be properly licensed before submitting bids or performing work that falls within Nevada’s contractor-licensing requirements. Permits, inspections, local business licensing, and code compliance remain separate responsibilities after the state contractor license is issued.

## How does the Master Electrician (ICC G16) exam work?

The Master Electrician (ICC G16) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

- Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
- Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
- ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
- Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
