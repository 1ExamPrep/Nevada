Open Book Test

The Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor exam is an open book test. The Nevada contractor candidate bulletin identifies approved references and states that candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the examination center. Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination session. References may not be written in before or during the exam, and candidates may not bring loose or attached additional papers with approved references.

Permanent tabs are allowed under the Nevada PSI rules. Temporary tabs, including removable notes such as Post-it notes, are not allowed and must be removed before the examination begins. Licensing Steps

The Nevada licensing path begins with identifying the credential needed. A person who wants to operate as an electrical contractor in Nevada generally works through the Nevada State Contractors Board and applies for the appropriate classification, such as C-2 Electrical Contractor. A person seeking an individual master or journeyman credential should also check the requirements of the local jurisdiction where the work will be performed.

For Nevada contractor licensing, the applicant submits the contractor license application to the Nevada State Contractors Board. The Board reviews the application and verifies the applicant’s qualifications and experience. Nevada contractor applicants are generally tested through PSI after the Board determines eligibility.

Once approved, the applicant receives an eligibility letter and follows the PSI scheduling instructions. The candidate schedules the required examination portion or portions, reports to the examination center with proper identification, and brings only the approved references allowed for that exam. Nevada PSI rules address reference condition, tabs, highlighting, underlining, additional papers, and testing-room security.

After the examination is completed, the score is given immediately. PSI forwards passing results to the Nevada State Contractors Board for processing. If a candidate does not pass, Nevada rules allow three attempts before the application becomes void. After a third failed attempt, the applicant must reapply after the required waiting period.

State Requirements

Nevada electrical contractor licensing is regulated by the Nevada State Contractors Board. The Board licenses contractors by classification and requires applicants to follow the state application, experience verification, examination, and licensing process. Contractor applicants must pass the required examination portions unless a waiver or endorsement pathway applies.

The Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor classification covers the installation of electrical wires, fixtures, appliances, apparatuses, raceways, and conduits. Electrical contractor applicants should understand that the state contractor license is a business and contracting credential. It is separate from any local individual journeyman or master electrician credential that may be required by a city, county, or local authority.

Nevada contractor exams are administered by PSI. Candidates do not schedule the examination until the Nevada State Contractors Board determines eligibility. The state also provides licensure by endorsement options under certain circumstances when an applicant is licensed in a state that Nevada considers substantially equivalent.

Local requirements matter in Nevada. A contractor license may be necessary for contracting activity, but local jurisdictions may also require permits, registrations, inspections, or individual trade credentials. Candidates should follow both the Nevada State Contractors Board requirements and the local requirements that apply where the electrical work will be performed.

How does the Master Electrician (ICC G16) exam work?

The Master Electrician (ICC G16) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
  • Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
  • ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
  • Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
