Exam Details

Nevada contractor licensing examinations are administered through the Nevada State Contractors Board’s testing program, and PSI is the testing vendor used for the state’s examination system. Nevada requires each applicant to pass a general business and law (CMS) exam and a trade exam specific to the license classification being pursued. After your application is filed and your experience is verified, Nevada issues an eligibility letter so you can schedule your examinations through PSI.

From a study standpoint, that structure matters because it means most candidates are preparing for two different types of testing:

  • Business/management concepts tested on the CMS exam.
  • Trade knowledge and code application tested on the electrical trade exam for your classification.

The CMS examination is designed to confirm that contractors understand business and law fundamentals relevant to contracting work. PSI’s Nevada contractor bulletin describes the CMS exam as a scored exam with a fixed time limit and a required passing score, and it notes important retake rules: candidates who test unsuccessfully must wait before retaking, and after a third failed attempt the application becomes void and you must reapply with the Board.

For the electrical trade exam portion, Nevada publishes exam content outlines by classification. Those outlines identify the topics emphasized on the exam and clarify what references (if any) may be used in the testing center. This combo is built to help you perform on the trade side by strengthening NEC reasoning, code-rule recognition, and the calculations habits that keep you moving under time limits.

Closed Book Test

Nevada’s exam rules are not “one-size-fits-all.” In general, Nevada’s Contractors Board explains that the CMS (business and law) exam is open book, while trade exams are closed book with limited exceptions for certain code references depending on the specific exam content outline.

The exam style is important because it changes how you should prepare:

  • You can’t rely on slow searching. Even when certain code references are permitted, the exam is still designed to test whether you know how to apply the rules—not whether you can flip pages for a long time.
  • You need strong rule recognition. The fastest points come from reading a question and immediately recognizing the code concept it’s testing.
  • You need clean calculation setup. In contractor-level exams, math questions often reward consistent process more than advanced math skill.

How to study for a closed-book trade exam that may allow limited code references:

  • Learn the “why,” not just the sentence. When you understand the purpose behind a rule, you’re less likely to get trapped by small wording changes.
  • Practice with a two-pass method. Answer from knowledge first, then verify using the NEC during study time. This builds recall and strengthens accuracy.
  • Train table confidence weekly. Many questions are won or lost on table decisions—right table, right column, right conditions of use.
  • Build a repeatable math workflow. If your setup is consistent, your speed and confidence improve naturally.

Even when the exam is not fully open book, the NEC 2023 paperback is still a powerful part of preparation. It teaches you the structure of the code, how rules are written, and how tables and exceptions actually work—so you can perform without depending on last-minute lookups.

  1. Apply with the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  2. Schedule your exams through PSI using your eligibility letter.

Test Information and Study Materials

Master-level electrical exams rarely fail people because of “hard math” or “mystery code.” Most missed points come from predictable patterns—and the good news is those patterns are fixable with the right practice routine.

The four most common score killers:

  • Misreading the question. One word (location, equipment type, condition of use) can change the entire rule path.
  • Missing an exception or definition. Many questions are won or lost by whether you remembered to check what changes under special conditions.
  • Wrong table or wrong column. Table errors are common, but they disappear quickly when you practice them intentionally.
  • Calculation rework. Most wrong answers start with a setup mistake, not a math mistake.

How this combo helps you fix those issues:

  • Master Study Guide: strengthens scenario interpretation and trains you to identify the deciding detail before committing to an answer.
  • Calculations Study Guide: builds a consistent workflow so math problems don’t slow you down or cause restarts.
  • NEC 2023 Paperback: improves code fluency so rules, tables, and exceptions feel familiar and logical instead of overwhelming.

A calculations workflow that protects points:

  • Write the givens first: voltage, phase, load type, and any constraints stated in the question.
  • Keep units visible: volts, amps, watts, VA—unit discipline prevents avoidable mistakes.
  • Use one consistent step order: setup → formula → substitute → compute → sanity check.
  • Sanity-check the result: ask “Does this number make sense for the scenario?” before finalizing.

A practical weekly study plan (built for working electricians):

  • Day 1: Scenario practice
    Work a focused set from the Nevada master study guide. After the set, review every miss and label the cause: misread question, missed qualifier, missed exception/definition, wrong table, or wrong rule path.
  • Day 2: Calculations focus
    Work calculation sets with clean steps and visible units. Consistency first—speed follows naturally.
  • Day 3: NEC skill session
    Choose one high-value category and practice it deeply: services/feeders/branch circuits, grounding and bonding concepts, wiring methods, overcurrent protection logic, motors/equipment, and special conditions.
  • Day 4: Mixed exam-mode set
    Combine scenario questions and calculations under a timer. Practice moving on from slow questions and returning later to protect pacing.
  • Day 5: Review and weak-spot cleanup
    Rework missed questions and drill the biggest pattern until it becomes reliable.

FAQ Section

What’s included in this Nevada combo?

The package includes the 2023 Nevada Master Electrician Study Guide, the 2023 Electrician Calculations Study Guide, and the National Electrical Code 2023 Paperback.

Does Nevada require both a business exam and a trade exam?

Yes. Nevada requires each applicant to pass the general business and law (CMS) exam and a trade exam specific to the classification of license being pursued.

Is the Nevada CMS exam open book?

Yes. Nevada states the CMS exam is open book and allows the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada.

Are Nevada trade exams open book?

Nevada states trade exams are generally closed book, with limited exceptions for certain code references depending on the exam content outline for the specific exam.

Who administers Nevada contractor license exams?

Nevada contractor licensing exams are administered through PSI as part of the Nevada State Contractors Board examination program.

If I fail, can I retake the exam?

Retesting is allowed, but Nevada and PSI describe required waiting periods after unsuccessful attempts and note that if an exam is failed on the third attempt the application becomes void and you must reapply with the Board.

Why include a calculations study guide in a master electrician combo?

Because calculations affect pacing and accuracy. A consistent setup routine helps reduce unit mistakes, prevent rework, and improve speed on multi-step problems—especially under time limits.

Will this combo guarantee I pass?

No. Exam outcomes depend on your preparation and performance. This combo is designed to strengthen the skills electrical exams reward—code application, disciplined calculations, and steady exam habits—so you can prepare with structure and confidence.

How does the Master Electrician (ICC G16) exam work?

The Master Electrician (ICC G16) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
  • Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
  • ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
  • Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
