Exam Details

Nevada contractor licensing is regulated by the Nevada State Contractors Board, with examinations administered through PSI. Nevada requires each applicant to pass a general business and law exam (often referenced as the CMS exam) and a trade exam that matches the license classification you are applying for. After you file your application and your experience is verified, you receive an exam eligibility letter that allows you to schedule your exam.

For the C-2 Electrical Contractor trade exam (Nevada Class C specialty), PSI’s published outline lists the following:

  • Number of questions: 80
  • Minimum passing score: 56
  • Time allowed: 4 hours

PSI’s published content outline for C-2 includes these topic areas and item counts:

  • General Electrical Knowledge: 6
  • Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits: 7
  • Grounding and Bonding: 7
  • Conductors and Cables: 8
  • Raceways and Boxes: 7
  • Special Occupancies and Equipment: 5
  • Electrical Power: 6
  • Motors: 6
  • Low Voltage: 2
  • Lighting: 5
  • Illuminated Signs: 2
  • Fire Detection and Alarm Systems: 2
  • Safety Information: 8
  • Transmission Lines: 5
  • Overcurrent Protection: 4

Because the outline is broad, the best prep strategy is not “read a chapter once.” It’s to build repeatable skill across the areas that show up repeatedly: services and feeders, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, overcurrent protection, motors, and special conditions—while training yourself to slow down enough to catch exceptions and table notes.

Open Book Test

Nevada’s exam rules depend on the exam type. The Nevada Contractors Board states that the CMS (business and law) exam is open book, and that trade exams are generally closed book with exceptions for certain code references listed in the content outline.

For the C-2 Electrical Contractor exam specifically, PSI’s outline states: This examination is OPEN BOOK. Open-book does not mean easy. It means the exam rewards the electrician who can:

  • Recognize the topic quickly so you know where to look
  • Confirm the exact wording instead of answering from memory
  • Check exceptions automatically before committing
  • Use tables carefully (including notes and conditions)
  • Maintain a steady pace across a wide range of topics

The goal is to spend your time reading the right rule carefully, not flipping through the book.

Licensing Steps

Nevada’s contractor licensing process includes a clear sequence: apply, get approved to test, pass required exams, and complete remaining licensing requirements. While details can vary by classification, a practical exam-centered path looks like this:

  1. Select the correct license classification for the work you intend to perform (for many electrical contractors, this is C-2).
  2. Submit your contractor license application and ensure your experience documentation is complete and verifiable.
  3. Receive your eligibility letter after experience is verified, then schedule exams through PSI.
  4. Pass the CMS (business and law) exam and the required trade exam for your classification.
  5. Complete any remaining Board requirements for license issuance and maintain ongoing compliance for your contracting business.

PSI’s Nevada contractor bulletin also explains important retake rules and timelines that affect planning. Candidates who test unsuccessfully must wait before retaking a portion, and if an exam is failed on the third attempt, the application becomes void and you must reapply with the Board. That’s why a structured study plan matters: it protects your timeline and keeps your momentum moving.

State Requirements

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates contractor licensing statewide and determines eligibility for examination. The Board’s published exam guidance also highlights key requirements that shape how candidates plan their timeline:

  • Exams required: Nevada requires both a general business and law (CMS) exam and a trade exam specific to your classification.
  • Eligibility process: An eligibility letter is provided after your application is filed and experience is verified.
  • Background and identity controls: Nevada’s contractor bulletin includes required security procedures for testing and describes Board-directed eligibility standards (including fingerprint-based criminal history checks as part of the licensing process).

Test Information and Study Materials

The C-2 exam outline is broad on purpose. It tests whether you can apply code rules across many areas, not just one specialty. The best way to prepare is to turn studying into skill training with a repeatable cycle:

  • Practice questions
  • Locate the supporting code section
  • Read the rule and exceptions carefully
  • Correct the misunderstanding
  • Repeat until consistent

A practical weekly routine

  • 2–3 code sessions per week: Work practice sets and prove every answer by locating the rule in the NEC. If there’s an exception, read it. If there’s a table, read the notes.
  • 1 calculation/technical session per week: Focus on motors, services/feeders, conductor decisions, overcurrent protection logic, and any table-driven questions. Train clean setup and careful units.
  • 1 timed mixed session weekly: Mix topics under a timer so you build pacing and reduce the tendency to rush late in the exam.
  • CMS lane (separate): Keep business & law study separate so it doesn’t become last-minute cramming.

High-value topics to drill for the Nevada C-2 outline

  • Services, feeders, and branch circuits: questions often combine rules, protection, and real-world scenarios.
  • Grounding and bonding: detail-heavy and easy to miss without disciplined review.
  • Raceways and boxes: installation rules and fill concepts reward careful reading.
  • Overcurrent protection: coordination between conductors and devices is a common source of close-answer questions.
  • Motors and electrical power: rule application plus table accuracy matters here.
  • Special occupancies/equipment and safety: added requirements and exceptions show up regularly.

How to use the tabs effectively

  • Tab for speed, read for accuracy: tabs get you to the right neighborhood quickly; points come from reading the exact rule and applying it precisely.
  • Build repeat routes: if you keep landing in the same sections during practice, that’s a good sign. Repetition builds speed and confidence.
  • Make exceptions automatic: treat “check exceptions” as a required step, not an optional one.
  • Track misses by section: write down the topic and the code section tied to every missed question, then revisit weekly until it becomes a strength.

How does the Master Electrician (ICC G16) exam work?

The Master Electrician (ICC G16) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
  • Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
  • ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
  • Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
