Exam Details

Nevada contractor examinations are administered through PSI for the Nevada State Contractors Board. Nevada state law requires contractors to be licensed and regulated by the Nevada State Contractors Board, and eligibility for examination is determined by the Board. After the Board accepts an applicant’s qualifications, the candidate receives eligibility information and scheduling instructions for the required examination.

The Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor classification covers the installation of electrical wires, fixtures, appliances, apparatuses, raceways, and conduits. The published C-2 Electrical Contractor examination outline lists 80 questions, a minimum passing score of 56, and 3.5 hours of testing time. The subject outline includes General Electrical Knowledge, Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits, Grounding and Bonding, Conductors and Cables, Raceways and Boxes, Special Occupancies and Equipment, Electrical Power, Motors, Low Voltage, Lighting, Illuminated Signs, Fire Detection and Alarm Systems, Safety Information, Transmission Lines, and Overcurrent Protection.

The content outline shows why a balanced study plan matters. Electrical candidates cannot rely on only one subject area. Services and feeders require careful code reading. Calculation questions require step-by-step practice and the ability to choose the correct method before working the math.

Nevada also requires contractor candidates to complete the business and law or Contractor Management Survey portion when applicable. The CMS examination is separate from the trade examination and focuses on licensing, estimating and bidding, lien law, financial management, tax laws, labor laws, project management, contracts, risk management, and environmental and safety topics. Candidates pursuing a contractor license should prepare for both the trade and business portions required by their classification.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor examination is identified as an open book examination under the published C-2 Electrical Contractor outline. Open book testing does not make the exam easy. It means candidates must know how to use approved references efficiently, read code language carefully, understand table notes and exceptions, and apply requirements under time pressure.

The published C-2 outline states that the National Electrical Code or National Electrical Code Handbook is allowed in the examination center. It also states that reference material may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed, but must otherwise be unmarked, may not be written in, and may not contain additional loose or attached papers. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references because the references are not provided at the examination center.

The Nevada State Contractors Board also explains that trade exams are generally closed book except for certain code references, and candidates must refer to the specific exam content outline to determine which reference materials may be used. For the C-2 Electrical Contractor outline, the NEC reference is listed as allowed. Candidates should follow the current PSI and Board instructions for the exact reference edition, tabs, markings, highlighting, indexing, and exam-room rules tied to their scheduled exam.

Licensing Steps

Nevada electrical contractor licensing is handled through the Nevada State Contractors Board. A practical licensing path begins by identifying the correct classification for the work being performed. For broad electrical work, the C-2 Electrical Contractor classification covers electrical wires, fixtures, appliances, apparatuses, raceways, and conduits. Candidates should confirm that the classification matches the work they plan to perform before beginning the application process.

The licensing process begins with completing the Nevada State Contractors Board application process and submitting the required documentation. The Board determines whether the applicant qualifies to register for examination. After the Board accepts the applicant’s qualifications, the candidate receives a Candidate Eligibility letter and examination instructions. PSI then administers the examination through its computer-based testing program.

Candidates should complete the required examination portion or portions, follow PSI scheduling rules, bring proper identification to the examination center, and comply with the examination security procedures. After the candidate passes the examination, PSI forwards the results to the Board for processing. The Board then continues the licensing process according to its requirements.

Nevada contractor licensing may also involve business documents, financial information, qualifying individual details, fingerprints, insurance, bonding, application fees, license fees, and other Board requirements. The exact requirements depend on the license application and classification. Candidates should follow the Board’s current application instructions for their license type.

The study portion of the licensing path should be treated as a structured project. Candidates should review the examination content outline, study the 2026 NEC, practice calculations, work through code navigation, review safety and trade knowledge areas, and use flash cards for repeated reinforcement. Consistent preparation helps candidates approach Nevada electrical contractor and master-level electrical study with more organization and confidence.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada electrical exam study plan should include NEC study, calculation practice, trade knowledge review, reference navigation, safety review, and timed question work. Electrical exams can cover a wide range of subjects, so candidates should avoid spending all study time on only one topic. A balanced routine helps build confidence across services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors, raceways, boxes, motors, low voltage, lighting, fire detection, safety, transmission lines, overcurrent protection, and calculations.

For NEC study, start by learning the structure of the code book. Review article organization, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Practice moving from a question to the correct article and reading the surrounding language carefully. Many electrical exam questions are missed because a candidate finds a related section but overlooks a condition, exception, table note, or specific wording.

For calculations, write out each step. Electrical calculation mistakes often happen when a candidate skips a conversion, uses the wrong table, applies the wrong value, or confuses branch circuit, feeder, service, motor, transformer, raceway fill, or box fill requirements. The 2026 Electrician Calculations Study Guide gives candidates a dedicated way to practice these skills repeatedly.

For open book preparation, use the NEC actively during study. Practice locating sections, reading tables, and confirming answers in the code book. Then practice some questions without immediately opening the code book so you can build recognition. The stronger your recall becomes, the more time you can save during questions that require deeper code lookup.

For flash card study, use short sessions throughout the week. Flash cards can help reinforce formulas, definitions, code concepts, and common electrical terms. They are useful before work, during breaks, after a longer study block, or as final review. Flash cards should not replace full study sessions, but they help strengthen recall and recognition.

How does the Master Electrician (ICC G16) exam work?

The Master Electrician (ICC G16) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
  • Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
  • ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
  • Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
