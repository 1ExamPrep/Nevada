Licensing Steps

The Nevada General Engineering Contractor examination is one component of the contractor licensing process. Candidates must satisfy the applicable licensing requirements in addition to passing the trade examination.

  1. Confirm the General Engineering classification. Determine that the fixed works, infrastructure, utility, civil, pipeline, or engineering construction activities the business intends to perform fall within the appropriate Nevada classification.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor license application must designate the individual responsible for providing the required trade qualification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable work history demonstrating experience related to the requested General Engineering classification.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Provide the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. Nevada reviews the application and qualifying experience before examination eligibility is established.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. Once approved to test, the qualifying individual can proceed with scheduling the required contractor examinations.
  7. Prepare for the General Engineering trade examination. Study piping, concrete, paving, excavation, grading, welding, steel, safety, utility construction, and the other applicable subjects.
  8. Practice with approved references. Use WTS-22 and WTS-23, ASME B31.8, and NFPA 30 during study so their organization becomes familiar before the examination.
  9. Complete the applicable examinations. Satisfy the examination requirements associated with the license application unless an authorized waiver applies.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish the other applicable steps required before a Nevada contractor license is issued.

State Requirements

Nevada classifies a Class A license as General Engineering contracting. The principal business associated with the classification involves fixed works requiring specialized engineering knowledge and skill.

General Engineering contracting can include work associated with major infrastructure, utilities, transportation, pipelines, excavation, grading, paving, water systems, sewer systems, industrial projects, foundations, and other fixed works that require engineering construction knowledge.

The broad scope of General Engineering work is reflected in the examination. Candidates should be prepared to apply technical knowledge across several disciplines rather than concentrating on a single trade.

The Nevada contractor licensing process also requires more than an examination score. Applicants must complete the applicable application and qualification requirements, and the qualifying individual must demonstrate the experience appropriate to the requested contractor classification.

Candidates should treat exam preparation and licensing preparation as related but separate responsibilities. The books in this package support the trade examination, while the contractor licensing application involves additional requirements outside of the examination itself.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada General Engineering study plan should separate subjects that can be researched from approved references during the exam from subjects that must be learned through advance preparation.

Gas and Liquid Fuel Piping deserves significant attention because it represents an important portion of the examination. Candidates should understand basic system concepts and practice navigating ASME B31.8 and NFPA 30 for technical requirements.

Water and Piping preparation should include water systems, pipe installation, underground utility construction, sewer concepts, and related civil construction knowledge. The Nevada water pollution control fact sheets should be reviewed alongside broader piping study materials.

Concrete is another major examination subject. Study should include concrete materials, placement, reinforcement, curing, consolidation, field practices, quantities, and construction quality. These topics require preparation beyond the three references included with this package.

Paving preparation should include asphalt paving operations, surface preparation, placement, compaction, materials, and field construction methods.

Excavation, Compaction and Grading should include trenching, soil handling, equipment, backfill, compaction, slopes, grading operations, and underground utility protection.

Safety should be reviewed as an independent study area. General Engineering projects frequently involve heavy equipment, excavation, traffic exposure, lifting, welding, piping, and other hazards that require strong construction-safety knowledge.

Steel and Welding preparation should include structural components, steel handling, welding terminology, joint preparation, procedures, and common field practices.

Electrical Transmission Lines should also be included in the study plan. Candidates should understand general transmission and utility construction concepts associated with the examination outline.

Nevada One-Call preparation should include utility-location and excavation-notification concepts. Underground utility protection is particularly relevant to General Engineering work involving excavation and pipeline construction.

For the three exam-room references, practice should be timed. Start with a technical question, identify which reference applies, locate the relevant section, and confirm the answer. Repeating these exercises can help reduce unnecessary searching during the examination.

Candidates should not expect every General Engineering question to be answered directly from one of the three books. The exam includes numerous construction subjects that require trade knowledge developed before test day.

A balanced preparation schedule might alternate between code-reference navigation and closed-reference trade review. One study session could focus on ASME B31.8 navigation, while another focuses on concrete or excavation concepts without relying on a book. This helps build both lookup skills and technical recall.

How does the General Engineering Contractor (A) exam work?

The General Engineering Contractor (A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
