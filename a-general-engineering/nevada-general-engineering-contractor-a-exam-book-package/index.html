Licensing Steps

The Nevada General Engineering trade examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the trade exam.

  1. Determine that the General Engineering classification fits the intended work. Review the type of infrastructure, utility, civil, or engineering construction the business plans to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor application must designate the person responsible for providing the applicable trade qualification.
  3. Prepare experience documentation. Organize qualifying work history that demonstrates experience related to the requested General Engineering classification.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Provide the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. Examination eligibility is established through the Nevada contractor licensing process.
  6. Prepare for the General Engineering examination. Study piping, concrete, paving, excavation, grading, welding, steel, safety, Nevada One Call, and the other subjects included in the examination outline.
  7. Complete applicable examinations. Satisfy the examinations required for the contractor license application unless an authorized waiver applies.
  8. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish all other applicable requirements before the contractor license is issued.

State Requirements

Nevada's General Engineering contractor classification applies to large-scale engineering and infrastructure construction rather than conventional building construction alone. Work associated with the classification can involve highways, roads, utilities, pipelines, grading, paving, water systems, sewer systems, industrial facilities, power-related infrastructure, foundations, and other engineering projects.

The examination reflects this broad scope by testing technical knowledge across multiple civil and industrial construction areas. Candidates should be comfortable moving between topics such as concrete placement, pipeline systems, welding, excavation, paving, reinforcing steel, utility protection, and safety.

The qualifying individual is responsible for establishing the trade qualification associated with the application. The Nevada contractor licensing process also includes requirements beyond the trade exam, including application review and other applicable licensing requirements.

Passing the trade examination does not by itself create a contractor license. Candidates should prepare for the examination while also keeping the overall licensing process moving forward.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada General Engineering study strategy should follow the examination outline. Concrete is one of the largest subject areas, so candidates should dedicate meaningful study time to concrete materials, reinforcing steel, placement, curing, quality, and construction practices.

Paving and excavation, compaction, and grading also make up significant portions of the examination. Review asphalt paving operations, site preparation, excavation safety, trenching, backfill, grading methods, compaction, underground utilities, and equipment practices.

Gas and liquid fuel piping preparation should include regular use of ASME B31.8 and NFPA 30. Practice identifying whether a question involves gas transmission and distribution or flammable and combustible liquid requirements before choosing a reference.

Water and piping preparation should include ductile iron pipe, concrete pipe, pipe installation, excavation, fittings, and general utility construction practices. Welding preparation should include pipe welding terminology, procedures, joint preparation, and common field methods.

Safety should be reviewed through OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 even though the OSHA reference is not allowed into the exam. Study excavation hazards, fall protection, equipment safety, personal protective equipment, material handling, and other common heavy-construction hazards.

Nevada One Call should be studied for utility-location and excavation-notification concepts. Because this reference is not available during the exam, candidates should learn the key responsibilities and procedures before test day.

Electrical transmission line preparation should include the concepts supported by the Lineman’s and Cableman’s Handbook. Candidates should also review steel erection, piles, traffic control, post-tensioning, and reinforcing bars as part of a balanced study routine.

For study-only books, use active recall. Review a section, close the book, answer practice questions without assistance, and return to the reference only after attempting the question. This helps convert study material into knowledge that can be used during the closed-reference portions of the exam.

For the three approved exam-room references, practice timed navigation. Identify the subject, select the correct reference, locate the applicable section, and confirm the answer without spending excessive time searching.

How does the General Engineering Contractor (A) exam work?

The General Engineering Contractor (A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
