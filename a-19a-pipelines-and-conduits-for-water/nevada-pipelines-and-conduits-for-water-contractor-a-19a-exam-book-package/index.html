Exam Details

The Nevada A-19A Pipelines and Conduits for Water trade examination is part of the contractor licensing process administered through the Nevada State Contractors Board. Nevada contracts with PSI to administer contractor licensing examinations.

The A-19A trade examination contains 40 questions. Candidates are allowed 1.5 hours to complete the examination. The minimum passing score is 28 correct answers, which represents 70% of the examination.

The published examination content is centered on water pipeline work and the construction activities that support pipeline installation. Major study areas include water piping, safety, paving, excavation, compaction and grading, and Nevada One Call requirements.

Water Piping represents the largest portion of the examination. Candidates should devote substantial study time to pipe materials, ductile iron installation, joints, fittings, trench preparation, bedding, alignment, handling, backfill, and general water pipeline construction methods.

Excavation, compaction, and grading are also important because water pipelines are normally installed through coordinated earthwork operations. Candidates should understand trenching methods, soil considerations, excavation equipment, shoring, bedding, backfill, compaction, grading, and safe work procedures.

Safety preparation should emphasize the construction hazards that frequently arise during underground utility projects. Excavations, heavy equipment, trench access, materials, lifting, traffic exposure, tools, and working around existing utilities all require careful planning and compliance with applicable safety standards.

Paving questions can involve restoration of surfaces disturbed during pipeline construction. Candidates should understand asphalt placement, compaction, joints, preparation, and basic paving procedures. Nevada One Call questions focus on preventing damage to existing underground facilities before and during excavation.

Closed Book Test

The Nevada Pipelines and Conduits for Water Contractor (A-19A) examination is a closed-book test. The reference books in this package are used for pre-exam study and are not intended to be relied upon during the examination.

Closed-book testing changes the way candidates should prepare. Instead of spending most study time learning where information is located, candidates should build enough understanding to recognize construction problems and select the correct answer from memory and trade knowledge.

Begin by studying one technical subject at a time. For water piping, review pipe materials, installation methods, fittings, joints, bedding, backfill, and common field procedures. For excavation, study trench construction, grading, compaction, soil considerations, and equipment. For safety, focus on the OSHA requirements most closely connected with underground construction.

After reviewing the individual subjects, move into mixed-topic practice. This helps develop the ability to switch quickly between water piping, excavation, safety, paving, and utility-damage prevention without depending on a reference book.

Memorization alone should not replace understanding. Many contractor examination questions are easier to answer when the candidate understands why an installation method, safety procedure, or construction sequence is used. Field experience combined with structured study can help reinforce this type of practical reasoning.

Licensing Steps

Nevada contractor licenses are administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board. Applicants seeking the A-19A Pipelines and Conduits for Water classification must first submit the appropriate contractor license application to the Board.

The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the application and verifies the experience of the qualifying individual. Candidates cannot independently schedule the required licensing examinations before receiving examination eligibility from the Board.

After the application has been filed and the qualifying individual's experience has been verified, the Nevada State Contractors Board issues examination eligibility information. The qualifying individual can then schedule the required examinations with PSI.

Nevada generally requires the qualifying individual to pass the Contractor Management Survey, commonly referred to as the CMS or Business and Law examination, together with the trade examination required for the requested classification, unless an applicable examination waiver is approved.

For the A-19A classification, the trade examination is the Pipelines and Conduits for Water examination. The CMS examination covers Nevada contractor business and law subjects and is separate from the technical A-19A examination.

After the required examinations and other licensing requirements are completed, the application continues through the Nevada State Contractors Board review process. Passing an examination alone does not automatically create or issue a contractor license. Applicants must complete the full licensing process and satisfy the requirements applicable to the requested license.

State Requirements

The A-19A Pipelines and Conduits for Water classification falls within Nevada's general engineering contractor classifications. It is a specialized pipeline classification focused on water pipeline installation.

The authorized scope includes fabrication and installation of water pipelines together with trenching, boring, shoring, backfilling, compacting, paving, surfacing, and protective coating work necessary to complete the installation of the pipes and conduits.

The scope makes excavation and restoration knowledge particularly important. A water pipeline contractor may need to open a trench, maintain safe working conditions, prepare bedding, install the pipe, complete connections, backfill and compact the excavation, and restore a disturbed paved or surfaced area.

Applicants should pursue the classification that matches the work their business intends to perform. The A-19A designation is specifically centered on pipelines and conduits for water and should not be treated as a general authorization for unrelated contracting work.

Test Information and Study Materials

Water piping should receive the greatest share of preparation time. Use the Installation Guide for Ductile Iron Pipe to study how pipe is transported, handled, positioned, joined, supported, bedded, and backfilled. Review common fittings and installation terminology so practical questions can be recognized quickly.

Pipe and Excavation Contracting should be used to connect the pipe itself with the larger construction process. Review trench layout, excavation methods, equipment, bedding, backfill, compaction, grading, and the sequence of underground utility work.

Excavation safety deserves dedicated study because trench failures and underground utility strikes can create serious hazards. Use OSHA to review protective systems, competent-person responsibilities, access and egress, spoil placement, heavy-equipment hazards, and other requirements associated with excavation work.

Nevada One Call preparation should focus on safe excavation around existing underground facilities. Candidates should understand the purpose of utility notification and marking systems and the responsibilities involved in identifying and protecting buried lines before excavation begins.

Study the marking guidelines so common colors, markings, and field communication practices are familiar. Utility damage prevention is not simply an administrative step. It is an essential part of planning safe pipeline excavation.

Concrete pipe study should include handling, bedding, line and grade, joint assembly, backfill, and installation practices. Understanding how proper support and installation affect pipe performance can help candidates answer practical construction questions rather than relying on isolated facts.

Paving preparation should include asphalt placement and compaction, surface preparation, joints, equipment, and restoration. Pipeline trenches frequently require repair of roads or other paved areas after underground work is complete, making surface restoration a natural part of the A-19A scope.

As the examination approaches, shift from reading toward recall and problem solving. Because the test is closed book, practice answering questions without immediately consulting a reference. After answering, use the books to check your reasoning and study any topic you missed.

Create a review cycle around the major subject areas: water piping, excavation and grading, safety, paving, and Nevada One Call. Revisit weaker categories more frequently while continuing to review stronger subjects so information remains fresh.

How does the Pipelines and Conduits for Water Contractor (A-19A) exam work?

The Pipelines and Conduits for Water Contractor (A-19A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board.
