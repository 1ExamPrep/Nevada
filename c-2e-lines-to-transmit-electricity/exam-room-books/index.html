Exam Details

The Nevada Lines to Transmit Electricity (C-2E) trade examination contains 40 questions. Candidates are allowed 1.5 hours to complete the examination, and the minimum passing score is 28 correct answers.

The examination covers the following subject areas:

  • Transformers: 6 questions
  • Electrical Knowledge: 5 questions
  • Conductors: 5 questions
  • Overhead Lines: 4 questions
  • Underground Lines: 4 questions
  • Equipment and Safety: 4 questions
  • Grounding Methods: 3 questions
  • Service Drops and Laterals: 3 questions
  • Poles and Structures: 3 questions
  • Electric Supply Stations: 3 questions

Transformers represent the largest individual subject area. Candidates should understand transformer purpose, operating principles, connections, electrical characteristics, equipment relationships, and the role transformers play in distributing electrical power.

Electrical knowledge and conductors also account for a significant portion of the examination. Preparation should include voltage, current, resistance, power, conductor properties, ampacity concepts, conductor identification, installation conditions, and other basic electrical principles.

Overhead line preparation should include poles, structures, anchors, guys, conductor placement, line equipment, supports, clearances, and other practical aspects of electrical distribution work. Underground line preparation should include cable systems, service laterals, protection, routing, underground equipment, and installation considerations.

Grounding methods, service drops, poles, structures, and electric supply stations are additional areas that should not be overlooked. The relatively compact 40-question examination makes every subject meaningful to the overall result.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-2E Lines to Transmit Electricity examination is an open-book test with a specific approved reference.

The exam-room book included in this package is:

  • NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 edition

The National Electrical Code should be used regularly during preparation. Candidates should learn how the code is organized rather than depending entirely on random searches during the examination.

Practice with the index, table of contents, article structure, definitions, tables, and cross-references. If a question concerns grounding, begin with the appropriate grounding terminology or article. If the question concerns conductors, services, overcurrent protection, or equipment, identify the subject before searching.

With 40 questions and 1.5 hours, candidates have slightly more than two minutes per question on average. Spending too much time searching for a single answer can make it difficult to maintain a comfortable testing pace.

For this reason, candidates should develop a two-part strategy: answer questions they understand from trade knowledge and use the National Electrical Code when a specific requirement needs confirmation.

When a likely code provision is located, read enough surrounding text to identify exceptions, tables, definitions, and cross-references. Electrical-code questions can depend on details that are easy to miss when only one sentence is reviewed.

  1. Confirm the C-2E classification. Make sure the work the business intends to perform falls within the Nevada Lines to Transmit Electricity classification.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor license application must designate the person responsible for providing the applicable trade qualification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable work history involving electrical distribution, overhead or underground lines, transformers, conductors, poles, service equipment, or related systems.

  9. Complete applicable examinations. Satisfy the examination requirements associated with the contractor license application unless an authorized waiver applies.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish all other applicable requirements before the Nevada contractor license is issued.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-2E Lines to Transmit Electricity classification covers the installation, alteration, and repair of overhead or underground secondary lines that transmit electricity.

The scope includes work involving poles, towers, anchors, guys, transformers, substations, circuit breakers, and other related hardware, equipment, and systems. Candidates should therefore prepare for a trade that combines electrical knowledge with utility-style line construction.

Overhead systems can involve structural supports, conductors, transformers, service equipment, clearances, and hardware. Underground systems can involve cable routing, service laterals, protection, equipment, and other installation considerations.

Grounding and bonding are also important because electrical distribution systems must be installed and maintained with system safety in mind. Candidates should understand both the practical purpose of grounding and the code requirements that govern applicable installations.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-2E preparation plan should combine National Electrical Code navigation with practical electrical distribution knowledge. Candidates should understand the trade first and use the NEC to confirm detailed requirements.

Transformers should receive significant attention because they make up the largest individual exam category. Review transformer function, voltage relationships, connections, equipment considerations, and how transformers fit into electrical distribution systems.

Electrical Knowledge should include voltage, current, resistance, power, basic electrical relationships, electrical terminology, and practical calculations. These fundamentals support nearly every other subject on the examination.

Conductors should be studied for size, material, insulation, ampacity, installation conditions, connections, identification, and application in overhead and underground systems. Candidates should be familiar with the NEC provisions that apply to conductors and services.

Overhead Lines should include line construction, poles, structures, anchors, guys, conductors, transformers, hardware, and service drops. Candidates should understand how each component functions as part of an electrical distribution system.

Underground Lines should include underground conductors, service laterals, cable protection, routing, installation conditions, and underground distribution equipment.

Equipment and Safety should include electrical equipment, working conditions, protective practices, system hazards, and safe construction principles. Candidates should recognize the risks associated with energized systems, elevated work, underground utilities, and heavy electrical equipment.

Grounding Methods should receive dedicated NEC practice. Review grounding and bonding terminology, grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding, grounded conductors, and the purpose of grounding in electrical safety.

Service Drops and Laterals should be studied for overhead and underground service configurations, conductor routing, protection, clearances, and applicable code requirements.

Poles and Structures should include supports, anchors, guys, hardware, equipment placement, and practical line-construction concepts.

Electric Supply Stations should include transformers, substations, circuit breakers, switching equipment, grounding, and related components used in electrical distribution.

For NEC practice, begin each question by identifying the main subject. Avoid opening the book and scanning without a plan. Choose a keyword, locate it in the index, move to the applicable article, and review the relevant section.

Definitions are particularly important in electrical-code work. A technical term can have a precise meaning that affects the correct answer, so candidates should use the definitions when necessary.

Tables should also receive regular practice. Many electrical requirements depend on conductor size, ampacity, equipment ratings, or other values that may be presented in table form.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Timed navigation is one of the best ways to prepare for an open-book electrical examination. Give yourself a realistic amount of time to locate each answer and note which subjects consistently take too long.

If grounding lookups are slow, dedicate a study session to grounding articles and terminology. If conductor questions are difficult, focus on conductor-related articles and tables. If service questions are challenging, practice locating service requirements repeatedly.

Candidates should also complete portions of their preparation without using the NEC. The examination includes practical electrical and line-construction knowledge that may be faster to answer from understanding than through a lengthy code search.

A balanced approach combines electrical theory, line-construction knowledge, practice questions, and reference navigation. The National Electrical Code is most useful when candidates already understand the problem and know approximately where to look for confirmation.

How does the Lines to Transmit Electricity Contractor (C-2E) exam work?

The Lines to Transmit Electricity Contractor (C-2E) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
