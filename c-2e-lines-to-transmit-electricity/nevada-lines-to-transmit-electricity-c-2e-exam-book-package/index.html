Exam Details

The Nevada Lines to Transmit Electricity (C-2E) examination contains 40 questions, allows 1.5 hours for completion, and requires a minimum passing score of 28 correct answers.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Transformers: 6 questions
  • Electrical Knowledge: 5 questions
  • Conductors: 5 questions
  • Overhead Lines: 4 questions
  • Underground Lines: 4 questions
  • Equipment and Safety: 4 questions
  • Grounding Methods: 3 questions
  • Service Drops and Laterals: 3 questions
  • Poles and Structures: 3 questions
  • Electric Supply Stations: 3 questions

Transformers represent the largest single subject area. Candidates should understand transformer operation, connections, equipment relationships, electrical characteristics, and how transformers fit into secondary electrical distribution systems.

Electrical knowledge and conductors are also major areas. Preparation should include basic electrical theory, voltage, current, resistance, power, conductor characteristics, ampacity concepts, conductor identification, installation considerations, and related calculations.

Overhead and underground lines require different construction methods and safety considerations. Candidates should understand poles, supports, guys, anchors, line clearances, conductor installation, underground cable systems, service laterals, protection, and the practical differences between above-ground and below-ground distribution work.

Open-book testing still requires efficient preparation. With 40 questions in 1.5 hours, candidates have limited time to search. Knowing whether a question involves grounding, conductors, services, equipment, or another NEC subject helps narrow the search before opening the book.

Use the NEC index, table of contents, article organization, definitions, tables, and cross-references during practice. Candidates who understand the structure of the code can generally locate technical requirements more efficiently than candidates who rely on random page searches.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada C-2E trade examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Candidates must complete the applicable contractor licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the examination.

  1. Confirm the C-2E classification. Make sure the electrical distribution work the business intends to perform falls within the Lines to Transmit Electricity classification.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor license application must identify the individual who will provide the applicable trade qualification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable work history related to overhead and underground secondary electrical lines, transformers, poles, structures, conductors, substations, and related electrical systems.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Submit the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. Examination eligibility is determined through the Nevada contractor licensing process.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. Once approved to test, the qualifying individual can schedule the applicable contractor examinations.
  7. Prepare for the C-2E trade exam. Study transformers, electrical knowledge, conductors, overhead lines, underground lines, grounding, services, poles, electric supply stations, and safety.
  8. Practice with the NEC. Use the 2017 National Electrical Code throughout preparation to develop efficient code-navigation habits.
  9. Complete applicable examinations. Satisfy the examinations required for the contractor license application unless an authorized waiver applies.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish the other applicable requirements before the Nevada contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-2E study plan should follow the examination content outline and combine NEC navigation with practical line-construction knowledge.

Transformers deserve substantial preparation because they represent six questions. Candidates should understand transformer purpose, operation, connections, electrical characteristics, installation considerations, and the role transformers play within distribution systems.

Electrical Knowledge should include voltage, current, resistance, power, electrical relationships, basic calculations, and practical electrical theory. Candidates should be comfortable applying these concepts rather than simply memorizing formulas.

Conductors should be studied for materials, sizes, ampacity concepts, installation conditions, insulation, identification, connections, and application in overhead and underground systems.

Overhead Lines should include poles, structures, conductors, anchors, guys, clearances, equipment placement, and line-construction practices. The Lineman’s and Cableman’s Handbook can help reinforce practical understanding in this area.

Underground Lines should include cable installation, routing, protection, service laterals, underground equipment, excavation considerations, and installation methods appropriate to secondary distribution systems.

Equipment and Safety should be studied through both trade references and OSHA material. Candidates should understand safe work practices around electrical equipment, elevated structures, tools, vehicles, excavation, and energized systems.

Grounding Methods should receive focused NEC preparation. Study grounding and bonding terminology, grounding conductors, electrodes, equipment grounding, and how grounding supports system safety.

Service Drops and Laterals should include the differences between overhead service conductors and underground service conductors, routing, clearances, protection, and applicable electrical requirements.

Poles and Structures should be studied for installation, support, hardware, anchors, guys, equipment placement, and general structural considerations associated with electrical line construction.

Electric Supply Stations should include substations, switching equipment, transformers, circuit breakers, grounding, and related components that support electrical distribution.

For the National Electrical Code, practice timed lookups. Start with a question, identify the electrical subject, select an index term, locate the applicable article or table, and confirm the answer. Repeating this process can help reduce unnecessary search time during the examination.

For the study-only references, use active recall. Review a subject from the National Electrical Safety Code, Lineman’s and Cableman’s Handbook, or OSHA, then close the book and answer practice questions without consulting the reference.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Because those books cannot be used during testing, candidates should focus on understanding concepts rather than memorizing page locations. The ability to recognize safe line-construction practices, transformer concepts, utility hardware, and electrical distribution principles from memory is more valuable than knowing where a topic appears in a study book.

Practice questions should be used to identify weak areas. If transformer questions are difficult, spend additional time on transformer theory and equipment. If grounding searches take too long, increase NEC navigation practice. If overhead-line questions are challenging, return to the line-work references and review poles, guys, conductors, and distribution equipment.

How does the Lines to Transmit Electricity Contractor (C-2E) exam work?

The Lines to Transmit Electricity Contractor (C-2E) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
