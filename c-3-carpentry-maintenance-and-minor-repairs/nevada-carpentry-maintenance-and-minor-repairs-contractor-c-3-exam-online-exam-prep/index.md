## State Requirements

Nevada designates C-3 as the specialty classification for Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs. The full classification allows work associated with the C-3 subclassifications.

Carpentry and Repairs includes form work, framing and rough carpentry used to construct framed structures, installation and repair of framing-system components, subflooring, siding, exterior staircases and railings, roof decking, truss members, sheathing, doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, and related structural and finishing carpentry work.

Finish Carpentry includes installation, repair, and finishing of cabinets, sash casing, door casing, wooden flooring, baseboards, countertops, and other millwork within the authorized scope.

Insulation and Weather Stripping includes installation of building insulation and application of weather stripping and caulking used to control the effects of weather. The classification also includes firestop-system installation within the applicable scope.

Overhead Doors includes fabrication, erection, and installation of guides, counterweights, tracks, weather stripping, manual and automatic controls, and other equipment used in overhead-door systems.

Drywall includes installation, taping, and finishing of drywall, gypsum wallboard panels and assemblies, sheathing, acoustical tile, and sheet-metal studs used for ceiling framing and nonstructural partitions.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must demonstrate four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor within the applicable period before the application.

Nevada contractor applicants must also address financial responsibility and the monetary limit assigned to the license. Contractor bonding and other final requirements must be completed as part of the licensing process.

## How does the Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) exam work?
