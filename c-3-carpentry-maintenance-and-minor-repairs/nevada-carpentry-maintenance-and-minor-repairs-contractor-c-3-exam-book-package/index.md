## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-3 study plan should reflect the broad nature of the classification. Candidates should avoid concentrating exclusively on one type of carpentry work and instead build competence across rough carpentry, finish carpentry, structural components, maintenance, repairs, drywall, truss work, and safety.

**Build a strong carpentry foundation.** Use Carpentry and Building Construction to review framing, floors, walls, roofs, sheathing, layouts, measurements, materials, fastening, tools, doors, windows, and general construction techniques.

**Strengthen finish carpentry knowledge.** Use Finish Carpenter's Manual to review detailed installation work involving trim, cabinets, doors, windows, millwork, fitting, and finishing techniques.

**Review truss handling and bracing.** Use the BCSI guide to study the handling, installation, restraint, and bracing of metal plate connected wood trusses. These topics can be important when working with framed roof and floor systems.

**Study drywall and gypsum systems.** Use the Gypsum Construction Handbook to review materials, installation relationships, finishing, framing coordination, and common gypsum construction practices.

**Prepare for safety questions.** Use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 to reinforce knowledge involving fall hazards, ladders, scaffolds, protective equipment, tools, and jobsite safety. Since OSHA is not allowed into the exam, this material should be learned before test day.

**Practice IBC navigation.** Work directly with the International Building Code during preparation. Practice locating relevant provisions involving framing, structural construction, exterior walls, roofs, openings, stairs, guards, and other related topics.

**Use timed practice.** With 56 questions in 120 minutes, candidates should develop a steady pace. Timed exercises can help improve decisions about when to answer directly and when to use the IBC.

**Review weak areas by subject.** If practice results show repeated errors in framing, trusses, drywall, finish carpentry, safety, or code navigation, dedicate additional study sessions to that area rather than repeatedly reviewing stronger subjects.

## How does the Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) exam work?

The Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
