State Requirements

Nevada designates C-3 as the specialty classification for Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs. A contractor holding the full C-3 classification may perform work authorized for the C-3 subclassifications.

Carpentry and Repairs includes form work, framing and rough carpentry required to construct framed structures, installation and repair of framing-system components, subflooring, siding, exterior staircases and railings, roof decking, truss members, sheathing, doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, and related structural and finishing work.

Finish Carpentry includes installation, repair, and finishing of cabinets, sash casing, door casing, wooden flooring, baseboards, countertops, and other millwork within the scope of the classification.

Insulation and Weather Stripping includes installation of building insulation and application of weather stripping and caulking used for control of weather effects. The scope also includes firestop-system installation.

Overhead Doors includes fabrication, erection, and installation of guides, counterweights, tracks, weather stripping, automatic and manual controls, and related equipment associated with overhead-door operation.

Drywall includes installation, taping, and finishing of drywall, gypsum wallboard panels and assemblies, sheathing, acoustical tile, and sheet-metal studs used for ceiling framing and nonstructural partitions.

The standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway generally requires a qualifying individual to establish four years of relevant experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor within the applicable period preceding the application.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes financial responsibility and contractor bonding requirements. The license is issued to the business entity, while the qualifying individual satisfies the applicable trade or management qualification role.

How does the Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) exam work?

The Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
