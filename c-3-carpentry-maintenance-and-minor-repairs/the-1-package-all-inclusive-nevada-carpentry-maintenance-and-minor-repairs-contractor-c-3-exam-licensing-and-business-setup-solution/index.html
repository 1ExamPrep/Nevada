Exam Details

The Nevada C-3 Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs trade examination is administered through PSI for applicants who have received examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-3 Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs Contractor
  • Number of Questions: 56
  • Time Allowed: 120 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 40 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The C-3 examination covers a broader range of work than the C-3A Carpentry and Repairs subclassification. Candidates should be prepared for questions involving rough and finish carpentry, construction safety, wood trusses, drywall and gypsum systems, doors and windows, insulation and weather stripping, overhead doors, materials, installation practices, and building-code requirements.

Carpentry preparation should include framing systems, form work, floor and wall framing, roof framing, structural components, sheathing, subflooring, stairs, railings, materials, fasteners, measurements, layout, doors, windows, cabinets, trim, flooring, countertops, and other millwork.

The C-3 classification also incorporates specialty work beyond traditional framing and finish carpentry. Candidates should understand insulation and weather-stripping concepts, including firestop systems, as well as the components and installation principles associated with overhead doors.

Drywall preparation should include installation, taping, and finishing of gypsum wallboard, panels, sheathing, acoustical tile, and nonstructural sheet-metal studs used for ceilings and partitions. The Gypsum Construction Handbook provides focused study support for these subjects.

Wood-truss knowledge is another important part of preparation. The BCSI guide supports study involving handling, installation, restraint, bracing, and jobsite practices for metal plate connected wood trusses. Candidates should understand both proper installation principles and the safety considerations associated with truss work.

Nevada contractor applicants generally must also satisfy the Contractor Management Survey requirement. The included Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada supports preparation for contractor management, business, licensing, and law topics that are separate from the C-3 technical trade examination.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-3 Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs trade examination is open book with respect to the approved code reference. The International Building Code, 2018 is the technical reference included with this package for exam-room code navigation.

Candidates should become comfortable using the International Building Code before test day. Effective preparation includes learning how to use the index, table of contents, chapter structure, numbered sections, tables, exceptions, and cross-references so that relevant requirements can be located efficiently during the timed exam.

The Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA), Carpentry and Building Construction, BCSI truss guide, Gypsum Construction Handbook, and Finish Carpenter's Manual are study references and are not permitted in the C-3 trade examination room.

This makes preparation a combination of code navigation and learned trade knowledge. Candidates can use the IBC for approved code lookups during the examination, but carpentry methods, safety practices, truss handling, gypsum construction, finish work, insulation concepts, and other technical information must be studied before test day.

Open-book testing does not remove the need for strong subject knowledge. With 56 questions and 120 minutes, candidates benefit from knowing whether a question can be answered directly from trade knowledge or whether an IBC lookup is necessary. Repeated timed practice can help improve this decision-making process.

State Requirements

Nevada designates C-3 as the specialty classification for Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs. A contractor holding the full C-3 classification may perform work authorized under the C-3 subclassifications.

The C-3A Carpentry and Repairs scope includes form work, framing, rough carpentry, installation and repair of individual framing-system components, subflooring, siding, exterior staircases and railings, roof decking, truss members, sheathing, doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, and other structural and finish carpentry work.

The C-3B Finish Carpentry scope includes installation, repair, and finishing of cabinets, sash casing, door casing, wooden flooring, baseboards, countertops, and other millwork. Finish Carpentry under this classification does not include ceramic tile, marble, or artificial or cultured stone installation.

The C-3C Insulation and Weather Stripping scope includes installation of building insulation as well as weather stripping and caulking used for the internal control of weather effects. The scope also includes installation of firestop systems.

The C-3D Overhead Doors scope includes fabrication, erection, and installation of guides, counterweights, tracks, weather stripping, automatic and manual controls, and other equipment associated with the efficient operation of overhead doors.

The C-3E Drywall scope includes installation, taping, and finishing of drywall, gypsum wallboard panels and assemblies, sheathing, acoustical tile, and sheet-metal studs used for ceiling framing and nonstructural partitions.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document four years of relevant experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor within the applicable period required by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

Nevada contractor licensing also evaluates financial responsibility and establishes a monetary limit for the license. Bonding and other final licensing requirements must be completed before the contractor can operate under the issued license.

How does the Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) exam work?

The Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
