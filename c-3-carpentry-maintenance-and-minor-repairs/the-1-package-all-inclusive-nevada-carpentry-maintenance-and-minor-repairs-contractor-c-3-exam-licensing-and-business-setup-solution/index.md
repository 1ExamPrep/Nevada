## Open Book Test

The Nevada C-3 Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs trade examination is open book with respect to the approved code reference. The International Building Code, 2018 is the technical reference included with this package for exam-room code navigation.

Candidates should become comfortable using the International Building Code before test day. ## State Requirements

Nevada designates C-3 as the specialty classification for Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs. A contractor holding the full C-3 classification may perform work authorized under the C-3 subclassifications.

The C-3A Carpentry and Repairs scope includes form work, framing, rough carpentry, installation and repair of individual framing-system components, subflooring, siding, exterior staircases and railings, roof decking, truss members, sheathing, doors, windows, cabinets, fixtures, and other structural and finish carpentry work.

The C-3B Finish Carpentry scope includes installation, repair, and finishing of cabinets, sash casing, door casing, wooden flooring, baseboards, countertops, and other millwork. Finish Carpentry under this classification does not include ceramic tile, marble, or artificial or cultured stone installation.

The C-3C Insulation and Weather Stripping scope includes installation of building insulation as well as weather stripping and caulking used for the internal control of weather effects. The scope also includes installation of firestop systems.

The C-3D Overhead Doors scope includes fabrication, erection, and installation of guides, counterweights, tracks, weather stripping, automatic and manual controls, and other equipment associated with the efficient operation of overhead doors.

The C-3E Drywall scope includes installation, taping, and finishing of drywall, gypsum wallboard panels and assemblies, sheathing, acoustical tile, and sheet-metal studs used for ceiling framing and nonstructural partitions.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document four years of relevant experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor within the applicable period required by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

Nevada contractor licensing also evaluates financial responsibility and establishes a monetary limit for the license. Bonding and other final licensing requirements must be completed before the contractor can operate under the issued license.

## How does the Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) exam work?

The Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
