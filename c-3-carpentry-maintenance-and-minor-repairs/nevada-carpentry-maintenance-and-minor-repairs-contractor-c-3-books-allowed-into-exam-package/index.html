Exam Details

The Nevada C-3 Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs trade examination is administered through PSI for eligible candidates pursuing the classification through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-3 Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 56
  • Time Allowed: 120 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 40 correct answers

The C-3 examination covers the full Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs classification rather than only one narrow area of carpentry. With 56 questions and two hours available, candidates need to maintain a steady pace. Reference lookups should be deliberate and efficient. Spending too much time searching through the code for a single question can reduce the time available for the rest of the examination.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-3 Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs classification incorporates several related areas of carpentry and repair work. The full C-3 classification includes work associated with C-3A Carpentry and Repairs, C-3B Finish Carpentry, C-3C Insulation and Weather Stripping, C-3D Overhead Doors, and C-3E Maintenance and Minor Repairs.

Because the full C-3 classification spans multiple areas, candidates should prepare for a broader scope than they would encounter when pursuing only one subclassification. Technical knowledge may involve structural carpentry, finish carpentry, maintenance, repair work, installation activities, and related building systems.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document at least four years of qualifying experience within the 10 years immediately preceding the application.

Experience should directly relate to the classification being requested. Applicants should maintain organized records describing the work performed, responsibilities held, and the time period during which the qualifying experience was obtained.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification, financial responsibility review, background procedures, bonding, and other requirements administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board. Passing the C-3 trade examination alone does not automatically result in license issuance.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-3 preparation plan should combine practical carpentry knowledge with efficient International Building Code navigation. Because the full C-3 classification covers multiple areas of construction and repair, candidates should avoid concentrating on only one subject.

Review rough carpentry. Study floor framing, wall framing, roof systems, sheathing, subflooring, structural components, measurements, layouts, fastening, and related construction methods.

Review finish carpentry. Prepare for doors, windows, trim, cabinets, fixtures, millwork, fitting, alignment, measurements, and detail-oriented installation concepts.

Study repair and maintenance concepts. The full C-3 classification includes maintenance and minor repair work, so candidates should be comfortable evaluating existing conditions and understanding appropriate repair approaches.

Review truss-related concepts. Candidates should understand the fundamentals of handling, installing, restraining, and bracing wood truss systems as part of broader framing preparation.

Include drywall and gypsum-system study. Review basic materials, installation relationships, framing coordination, and common repair or finishing concepts.

Prepare for construction safety. Candidates should understand common jobsite hazards, safe tool use, fall protection, ladders, material handling, personal protective equipment, and general safety practices.

Practice IBC navigation. Use the International Building Code to locate requirements connected with structural systems, roofs, exterior walls, stairs, guards, openings, doors, windows, and related construction areas.

Use timed code searches. With 56 questions in 120 minutes, candidates should become comfortable finding information without spending excessive time on a single lookup.

Know when not to search. Open-book testing rewards efficient reference use. Candidates should answer familiar technical questions directly and save code navigation for questions that require a precise rule or provision.

Review weak areas separately. If practice reveals repeated mistakes in framing, repairs, finish carpentry, code navigation, drywall, trusses, or safety, candidates should dedicate additional study time to those topics instead of repeatedly reviewing stronger subjects.

How does the Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) exam work?

The Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
