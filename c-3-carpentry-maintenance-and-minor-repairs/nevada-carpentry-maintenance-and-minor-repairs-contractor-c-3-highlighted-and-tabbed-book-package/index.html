Exam Details

The Nevada C-3 Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates authorized to test through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-3 Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 56
  • Time Allowed: 120 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 40 correct answers

The full C-3 classification incorporates several related areas of carpentry, maintenance, and minor repair work. Candidates should prepare for a broad technical range rather than concentrating on only one area of construction.

Study should include framing systems, floors, walls, roofs, sheathing, structural components, wood trusses, doors and windows, finish carpentry, drywall and gypsum systems, maintenance, repair procedures, materials, tools, measurements, safety, and building-code navigation.

Because the examination contains 56 questions to be completed in two hours, pacing is important. Candidates should practice recognizing which questions can be answered directly from trade knowledge and which require a lookup in the International Building Code.

Repeated navigation practice can reduce the amount of time spent searching during the exam. Candidates should become comfortable with the IBC chapter structure, definitions, tables, index, exceptions, and cross-references before test day.

  1. Confirm the C-3 classification. Determine that Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs matches the type of contracting work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. Designate the individual who will satisfy the applicable technical experience and examination requirements.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare records demonstrating relevant carpentry, maintenance, repair, and related construction work.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor license application. Gather business information, qualifier details, experience documentation, financial information, and required supporting materials.

  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow required fingerprint, disclosure, and background procedures.
  7. Meet financial responsibility requirements. Provide the applicable financial information required during the licensing process.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Qualified applicants can proceed with scheduling after authorization.
  9. Schedule the C-3 examination through PSI. Arrange the trade examination once eligibility has been established.
  10. Prepare for the trade examination. Study framing, carpentry, finish work, trusses, drywall, repairs, maintenance, safety, and IBC navigation.
  11. Prepare for Nevada Business and Law. Nevada contractor applicants generally must also satisfy the applicable management qualification requirement unless an approved waiver applies.
  12. Complete applicable examinations. Meet the required trade and management testing requirements.
  13. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Address applicable bonding, financial responsibility, background, and other Nevada State Contractors Board requirements.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-3 Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs classification incorporates several related subclassifications. The full C-3 classification includes work associated with C-3A Carpentry and Repairs, C-3B Finish Carpentry, C-3C Insulation and Weather Stripping, C-3D Overhead Doors, and C-3E Maintenance and Minor Repairs.

Because the full classification includes multiple areas, candidates should prepare for a broader range of trade knowledge than would be required for only one subclassification. Preparation can involve structural carpentry, finish carpentry, insulation and weather-related work, overhead-door concepts, maintenance, repair procedures, and related building systems.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document at least four years of qualifying experience within the 10 years immediately preceding the application.

Qualifying experience should directly relate to the classification being requested. Applicants should maintain organized records describing the work performed, responsibilities held, and the period during which the experience was obtained.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification, financial responsibility review, bonding, background procedures, and other requirements administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board. Passing the C-3 trade examination alone does not automatically result in license issuance.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-3 study plan should reflect the broad nature of the classification. Candidates should build a balanced foundation across structural carpentry, finish work, truss systems, drywall, repair activities, safety, and code navigation.

Build broad carpentry knowledge. Use Carpentry and Building Construction to review floors, walls, roofs, framing, sheathing, measurements, layouts, materials, tools, fasteners, doors, windows, siding, and general construction techniques.

Develop finish carpentry skills. Use Finish Carpenter's Manual to reinforce doors, windows, cabinets, trim, millwork, fitting, alignment, measurement, and detail-oriented installation concepts.

Review truss handling and bracing. Use the BCSI guide to study handling, installation, restraint, and bracing principles associated with metal plate connected wood trusses.

Study drywall and gypsum systems. The Gypsum Construction Handbook supports review of materials, installation relationships, finishing concepts, and coordination with framing systems.

Prepare for safety questions. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports review of ladders, scaffolds, falls, tools, personal protective equipment, and other construction hazards. Because OSHA is not permitted in the trade exam, candidates should learn these concepts before test day.

Practice International Building Code navigation. Work with the IBC repeatedly and practice finding code requirements associated with structural systems, roofs, exterior walls, openings, stairs, guards, doors, windows, and related construction subjects.

Use timed study sessions. With 56 questions in 120 minutes, candidates should develop a steady testing pace and practice deciding whether a code lookup is necessary.

Review weaknesses by topic. Candidates should identify repeated mistakes in framing, finish carpentry, trusses, drywall, safety, repair concepts, or code navigation and devote additional preparation time to those areas.

How does the Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) exam work?

The Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
