State Requirements

The Nevada C-3 Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs classification incorporates several related carpentry and repair subclassifications. The full C-3 classification includes work associated with C-3A Carpentry and Repairs, C-3B Finish Carpentry, C-3C Insulation and Weather Stripping, C-3D Overhead Doors, and C-3E Maintenance and Minor Repairs.

Because the full C-3 classification spans multiple areas, candidates should prepare for a broader technical scope than someone pursuing only one C-3 subclassification. Preparation may include structural carpentry, finish work, maintenance procedures, minor repairs, insulation-related concepts, overhead-door work, and other construction activities covered by the classification.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document at least four years of qualifying experience within the 10 years immediately preceding the application.

Qualifying experience should directly relate to the requested classification. Applicants should maintain clear records describing the type of work performed, responsibilities held, and the period during which the qualifying experience was obtained.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification, financial responsibility review, bonding, background procedures, and other requirements administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board. Passing the C-3 trade exam alone does not automatically result in license issuance.

Because this product contains only the approved exam-room book, candidates should use their separate technical study materials to prepare for trade concepts that cannot be answered through code lookups alone.

Review rough carpentry. Study floor systems, wall framing, roof framing, sheathing, structural components, layouts, measurements, fastening, and general construction methods.

Review finish carpentry. Prepare for doors, windows, trim, cabinets, fixtures, fitting, alignment, measurements, and detail-oriented installation work.

Study maintenance and repairs. The full C-3 classification includes maintenance and minor repair activities, so candidates should be comfortable evaluating existing conditions and understanding appropriate repair approaches.

Review truss-related concepts. Candidates should understand fundamental handling, installation, restraint, and bracing concepts related to wood truss systems.

Include drywall and gypsum-system preparation. Review common materials, installation relationships, framing coordination, finishing, and repair concepts.

Prepare for construction safety. Study safe work practices involving falls, ladders, tools, personal protective equipment, material handling, and other common jobsite hazards.

Practice IBC navigation. Use the highlighted and tabbed International Building Code to locate requirements related to structural systems, roofs, exterior walls, openings, stairs, guards, doors, windows, and other relevant topics.

Use timed code searches. Candidates have 120 minutes to complete 56 questions. Practice finding code provisions within a limited amount of time so reference use remains efficient.

Learn when not to use the book. Familiar trade questions should generally be answered directly. The IBC should be used when a specific code requirement needs to be located or confirmed.

Review weak subjects separately. If practice shows repeated mistakes in framing, repairs, finish carpentry, drywall, truss concepts, safety, or code navigation, dedicate additional study time to those areas before test day.

How does the Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) exam work?

The Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
