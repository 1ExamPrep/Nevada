Exam Details

The Nevada C-3 Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs trade examination is administered through PSI for eligible candidates pursuing the classification through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-3 Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 56
  • Time Allowed: 120 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 40 correct answers

The full C-3 examination covers a broad range of carpentry, maintenance, and repair knowledge. Candidates should prepare for concepts involving structural carpentry, finish work, framing systems, trusses, drywall, doors and windows, repair procedures, maintenance activities, construction safety, and building-code requirements.

With 56 questions in two hours, candidates should develop a steady testing pace. Open-book testing can be beneficial, but only when the candidate knows where to look and does not spend excessive time searching the reference for every answer.

The International Building Code should be used regularly during preparation. Candidates should become familiar with the chapter structure, index, definitions, tables, exceptions, and cross-references so code-related questions can be handled efficiently.

Technical study should also extend beyond the IBC. Many trade questions require practical understanding of carpentry methods, materials, installation procedures, truss systems, gypsum construction, repair work, and safety practices that should be learned before test day.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the C-3 Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs classification. Passing the trade exam is one part of the complete contractor licensing process.

  1. Confirm the C-3 classification. Determine that Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs matches the work the contracting business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. Designate the individual who will satisfy the applicable experience and examination requirements.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare records showing relevant carpentry, maintenance, repair, installation, and related construction work.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor license application. Gather business information, qualifier information, experience documentation, financial records, and required supporting materials.
  5. Submit the application. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the applicant's qualifications before examination eligibility is established.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow required fingerprint, disclosure, and background procedures.
  7. Meet financial responsibility requirements. Provide the applicable financial information required during the licensing process.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Qualified applicants can proceed with scheduling after authorization.
  9. Schedule the C-3 examination through PSI. Arrange the trade exam after eligibility has been established.
  10. Prepare for the C-3 trade examination. Review carpentry, framing, trusses, drywall, finish work, maintenance, repairs, safety, and IBC navigation.
  11. Prepare for Nevada Business and Law. Use the included Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada to study for the applicable management examination requirement.
  12. Complete applicable examinations. Satisfy the required trade and management testing requirements unless an authorized waiver applies.
  13. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Address applicable bonding, financial responsibility, background, and other Nevada State Contractors Board requirements.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-3 Carpentry, Maintenance, and Minor Repairs classification incorporates several related areas of work. The full C-3 classification includes activities associated with C-3A Carpentry and Repairs, C-3B Finish Carpentry, C-3C Insulation and Weather Stripping, C-3D Overhead Doors, and C-3E Maintenance and Minor Repairs.

Because the full classification spans multiple areas, candidates should prepare for a wider technical range than someone pursuing only one C-3 subclassification. Study should include both structural and finish carpentry, repair work, maintenance concepts, building components, and related construction practices.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document at least four years of qualifying experience within the 10 years immediately preceding the application.

Qualifying experience should directly relate to the requested classification. Applicants should maintain organized documentation describing the work performed, responsibilities held, and the period during which the experience was obtained.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification, financial responsibility review, background procedures, bonding, and other requirements administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board. Passing the C-3 trade examination alone does not automatically result in license issuance.

How does the Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) exam work?

The Carpentry, Maintenance and Minor Repairs Contractor (C-3) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
