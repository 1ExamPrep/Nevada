The C-17F scope covers the erection and installation of studs made of sheet metal for the framing of ceilings and nonstructural partitioning walls.

The classification is focused on nonstructural sheet metal framing. Candidates should understand practical wall and ceiling framing systems that use light-gauge metal studs and tracks.

Typical work can involve partition layout, top and bottom tracks, vertical studs, openings, ceiling framing, fasteners, alignment, and coordination with gypsum board and other finish systems.

Because the classification involves ceilings and nonstructural partition walls, candidates should understand how metal framing fits into complete building assemblies without confusing the work with structural steel framing.

Building-code knowledge is also important because wall and ceiling systems may be affected by requirements involving fire resistance, materials, assemblies, and construction methods. The International Building Code, 2018 is the exam-room reference used for these code-based topics.

The C-17F trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-17F preparation plan should focus primarily on Metal Framing Installation because this subject represents 16 of the 20 examination questions.

Begin with the basic components of a nonstructural metal framing system. Candidates should understand the purpose of studs, tracks, ceiling framing members, fasteners, openings, and common connection points.

Layout is a fundamental part of the trade. Candidates should understand how partition locations are established, how top and bottom tracks are aligned, and how stud spacing is determined before installation begins.

Stud installation should include placement within tracks, spacing, orientation, alignment, fastening, and coordination with openings and surrounding construction.

Track installation should include proper positioning, attachment, alignment, and the relationship between tracks and vertical studs.

Openings should receive dedicated preparation. Doorways, access openings, and other penetrations can require additional framing considerations, and candidates should understand how nonstructural metal framing is arranged around these areas.

Ceiling framing is directly included in the C-17F scope. Candidates should understand how sheet metal studs and related components are used to frame ceilings and how those systems connect with surrounding walls and structural surfaces.

Fastening should be studied in relation to the framing material and completed assembly. Candidates should understand how secure connections affect alignment, stability, and installation quality.

Measurement and alignment are important practical skills. Inaccurate layout can affect wall position, door openings, ceiling framing, and subsequent finish work.

Coordination should also be part of preparation. Nonstructural framing often interfaces with doors, gypsum board, mechanical systems, electrical work, and other building components.

Code Requirements represents 4 questions, so candidates should also practice locating information in the International Building Code efficiently.

The International Building Code, 2018 should be used throughout preparation rather than only during the final days before the examination. Repeated use helps candidates become more familiar with how the book is organized.

Definitions can be important because building-code terms may have a specific meaning. Knowing where to find definitions can help candidates understand a question and locate the relevant section.

Tables should receive regular attention. Important requirements involving assemblies, materials, or construction conditions may appear in table format rather than narrative text.

Exceptions can modify a general requirement. Candidates should read enough surrounding code language to determine whether an exception applies.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Cross-references may direct the reader to another section containing additional requirements. Practice following those references rather than assuming the first section contains the entire answer.

Wall assemblies should be a regular part of IBC navigation practice. Candidates should understand how nonstructural framing fits into complete wall systems and how code provisions can affect the assembly.

Ceiling assemblies should also be reviewed. Since ceiling framing is included in the C-17F scope, candidates should know how to locate applicable building-code provisions involving ceilings and related construction.

Fire resistance is another useful area of study. Many partition and ceiling assemblies are part of rated construction, and candidates should understand how code requirements can apply to the complete system.

Materials should also be reviewed. Candidates should be able to recognize how the code addresses materials used in wall and ceiling construction and how those requirements may affect installation.

A useful study schedule is to divide preparation between Metal Framing Installation and Code Requirements while giving substantially more time to Metal Framing Installation because it represents 80 percent of the examination questions.

Begin with topic-based study sessions covering layout, tracks, studs, openings, ceiling framing, fastening, alignment, wall assemblies, fire resistance, and IBC navigation.

Once individual topics become familiar, move into mixed-question practice. The actual examination can shift from a practical framing question to a code question without warning.

Timed code-navigation drills can improve open-book performance. Choose a subject such as wall assemblies, ceiling construction, fire resistance, or materials and locate the applicable IBC provision within a limited period.

Because candidates have approximately three minutes per question on average, they should avoid using the code unnecessarily for questions they can answer confidently from retained trade knowledge.

Active recall can also strengthen practical framing knowledge. Review a framing procedure, close your notes, and explain the sequence from memory, including layout, track placement, stud installation, fastening, alignment, and completion.

Practice questions should initially be grouped by topic so weak areas are easier to identify. Later, move into mixed practice covering framing installation and code requirements together.

When a practice question is missed, identify the reason. The issue may involve weak framing knowledge, unfamiliar terminology, slow code navigation, or difficulty interpreting the building-code requirement.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Correcting the underlying weakness is more valuable than memorizing a single answer. The goal is to build a working understanding of nonstructural sheet metal framing that can be applied to unfamiliar questions.

Time management should also be part of preparation. Answer familiar installation questions efficiently, use the International Building Code for targeted confirmation, mark difficult questions, and return to them after completing items that can be answered more quickly.

How does the Studs of Sheet Metal Contractor (C-17F) exam work?

The Studs of Sheet Metal Contractor (C-17F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
