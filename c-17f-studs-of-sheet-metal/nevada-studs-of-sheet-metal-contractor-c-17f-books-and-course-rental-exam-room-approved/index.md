## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-17F study plan should place the greatest emphasis on **Metal Framing Installation** because that subject accounts for 16 of the 20 trade examination questions.

Begin with the major components of a nonstructural metal framing system. Candidates should understand the purpose and relationship of studs, tracks, fasteners, openings, and ceiling framing members.

**Layout** is one of the fundamental skills involved in sheet metal framing. Candidates should understand how partition locations are established and how top and bottom tracks are aligned before studs are installed.

Accurate measurements are important because errors in layout can affect walls, doors, openings, ceilings, and subsequent finish work.

**Stud installation** should include placement within tracks, orientation, alignment, spacing concepts, fastening, and coordination with nearby construction.

**Track installation** should include positioning, alignment, attachment, and the relationship between the tracks and completed wall system.

**Openings** deserve separate study. Candidates should understand how framing layouts change around doorways and other penetrations and how those conditions affect surrounding studs and tracks.

**Ceiling framing** is specifically included in the C-17F scope. Review the principles of overhead metal framing and how ceiling members coordinate with surrounding surfaces and partition systems.

**Fastening** should be understood as part of proper installation. Candidates should recognize how secure connections support alignment and the intended performance of a nonstructural framing system.

**Alignment** is another important practical subject. A properly framed wall or ceiling depends on accurate layout and consistent positioning of individual members.

**Coordination with other trades** can also affect sheet metal framing. Partitions and ceilings may interact with doors, gypsum board, mechanical work, electrical systems, and other construction components.

The **International Building Code, 2018** should be used regularly during preparation for Code Requirements. Candidates should become comfortable moving through the highlighted and tabbed book rather than waiting until test day to use the reference extensively.

**Definitions** can influence how a code requirement applies. Practice locating unfamiliar terms before moving deeper into the code.

**Tables** may contain important information about materials or assemblies. Candidates should practice reading headings, notes, and conditions carefully.

**Exceptions** may change how a general requirement applies. Always read the surrounding language before selecting an answer.

**Cross-references** can direct candidates to other sections containing additional provisions. Practice following those references so the process becomes familiar.

## How does the Studs of Sheet Metal Contractor (C-17F) exam work?

The Studs of Sheet Metal Contractor (C-17F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
