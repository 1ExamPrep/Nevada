## State Requirements

Nevada identifies **C-17F** as the **Studs of Sheet Metal** subclassification.

The scope covers the **erection and installation of studs made of sheet metal for the framing of ceilings and nonstructural partitioning walls**.

The classification is centered on nonstructural sheet metal framing. Candidates should understand wall layout, floor and ceiling tracks, vertical studs, ceiling framing, openings, fastening, alignment, and coordination with surrounding construction.

The C-17F scope should not be confused with structural steel framing. The work centers on sheet metal studs used in ceilings and nonstructural partitions rather than structural framing systems.

Building-code knowledge remains important because the completed wall and ceiling assemblies may be subject to code requirements involving fire resistance, materials, construction methods, and assembly performance. The International Building Code, 2018 serves as the exam-room reference for those code-related topics.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-17F study plan should place the greatest emphasis on **Metal Framing Installation** because it represents 16 of the 20 examination questions.

Begin with the fundamental components used in nonstructural sheet metal framing. Candidates should understand the function of studs, tracks, ceiling framing members, fasteners, and supporting components used to create walls and ceiling systems.

**Layout** should be a core study topic. Review how partition locations are established, how floor and ceiling tracks align, how openings affect the layout, and how inaccurate measurements can affect the completed wall system.

**Stud installation** should include proper placement within tracks, orientation, spacing concepts, alignment, fastening, and coordination with doors, openings, and surrounding construction.

**Track installation** should include positioning, alignment, attachment, and the relationship between track placement and the finished wall or ceiling.

**Openings** deserve separate attention. Candidates should understand how framing changes around doorways and other penetrations and how surrounding members work together to maintain the intended partition configuration.

**Ceiling framing** is specifically included within the C-17F scope. Study how sheet metal framing components are used overhead, how they connect with surrounding construction, and how alignment affects the finished ceiling assembly.

**Fastening** is another practical subject. Candidates should understand the purpose of secure attachments and how fastening influences alignment and installation quality.

The **Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th Edition** is especially useful for studying relationships between metal framing and gypsum systems. Review wall assemblies, partition systems, ceiling conditions, framing members, attachment methods, and related construction details.

Because the Gypsum Construction Handbook is not allowed into the exam room, use active recall while studying. After reviewing an installation detail, close the book and describe the sequence from memory.

The **Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016** reference can reinforce measurements, layout, framing terminology, wall construction, ceilings, openings, and general building principles.

Although the C-17F classification focuses specifically on sheet metal studs, broader framing knowledge can help candidates understand how nonstructural framing fits into the complete construction process.

**Code Requirements** represents 4 questions, so candidates should also spend time practicing with the International Building Code, 2018.

The highlighted and tabbed format can help candidates quickly recognize important areas during study, but familiarity with the code structure remains essential. Practice moving between the index, chapters, tables, definitions, exceptions, and cross-references.

**Cross-references** may direct candidates to another section for additional requirements. Use them during practice so following a reference becomes routine.

**Wall assemblies** should be part of regular IBC study. Candidates should understand how nonstructural partitions relate to code requirements and complete wall systems.

**Ceiling assemblies** should also receive attention because sheet metal ceiling framing falls within the C-17F scope.

**Fire-resistance requirements** are an important area to understand in relation to both the IBC and gypsum systems. The performance of a rated assembly can depend on the full combination of framing, gypsum products, fastening, and installation details.

**Materials** should be reviewed from both practical and code perspectives. Candidates should understand the basic purpose of sheet metal studs, tracks, gypsum products, and fasteners within nonstructural wall and ceiling systems.

## How does the Studs of Sheet Metal Contractor (C-17F) exam work?

The Studs of Sheet Metal Contractor (C-17F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
