Exam Details

The Nevada C-17F Studs of Sheet Metal examination contains 20 questions. Candidates are allowed 1 hour to complete the examination and must answer 14 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Code Requirements: 4 questions
  • Metal Framing Installation: 16 questions

Metal Framing Installation represents 16 of the 20 examination questions, making it the primary area of study. Candidates should understand practical methods involving sheet metal studs, tracks, partition walls, ceilings, openings, fasteners, alignment, layout, and coordination with gypsum systems.

Code Requirements accounts for 4 questions. Candidates should be comfortable locating relevant provisions in the International Building Code, 2018 and applying them to nonstructural metal framing conditions.

Because the test contains only 20 questions, each question represents a meaningful portion of the total score. Candidates should study both practical installation knowledge and code navigation rather than relying too heavily on only one area.

With one hour available for 20 questions, candidates have approximately three minutes per question on average. This provides some time for targeted code lookups, but broad searches through the International Building Code can still use valuable examination time.

State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-17F as the Studs of Sheet Metal subclassification.

The C-17F scope covers the erection and installation of studs made of sheet metal for the framing of ceilings and nonstructural partitioning walls.

The classification is focused on nonstructural sheet metal framing. Candidates should understand practical wall and ceiling framing systems using light-gauge metal studs and tracks.

Typical work can involve partition layout, top and bottom tracks, vertical studs, openings, ceiling framing, fasteners, alignment, and coordination with gypsum board and other finish systems.

Because the classification involves nonstructural partitioning walls and ceiling framing, candidates should understand how metal framing relates to complete building assemblies without treating the work as structural steel framing.

Code knowledge is also important because wall and ceiling systems may be affected by building-code requirements involving materials, fire resistance, and assembly construction. The International Building Code, 2018 is the exam-room reference used to support these code-based topics.

The C-17F trade examination is one component of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-17F preparation plan should place the greatest emphasis on Metal Framing Installation because this category represents 16 of the 20 examination questions.

Begin with the basic components of a nonstructural metal framing system. Candidates should understand the purpose of studs, tracks, framing around openings, ceiling framing members, fasteners, and common connection points.

Layout is a fundamental part of metal framing work. Candidates should understand how partitions are located, how top and bottom tracks are aligned, and how stud spacing is established before installation begins.

Stud installation should include placement within tracks, spacing, orientation, alignment, fastening, and coordination with openings and surrounding construction.

Track installation should include proper positioning at floors and ceilings, alignment, attachment, and the relationship between tracks and vertical studs.

Openings should receive dedicated study. Doorways, access openings, and other penetrations can require additional framing considerations, and candidates should understand how metal framing is arranged around these conditions.

Ceiling framing is directly included in the C-17F scope. Review how sheet metal studs and related components are used to frame ceilings and how those systems connect with surrounding walls and structural surfaces.

Fastening should be studied in relation to the framing material and completed assembly. Candidates should understand the purpose of secure connections and how fastening affects alignment and stability.

Gypsum construction is an important supporting subject because nonstructural sheet metal framing frequently serves as the framework for gypsum wallboard and ceiling systems.

The Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th Edition should be used to study metal studs, tracks, wall systems, ceilings, attachment, and rated assemblies. Because this book is not allowed into the examination room, focus on understanding and retaining the concepts rather than memorizing page locations.

After reviewing a gypsum wall or ceiling assembly, close the book and explain the installation sequence from memory. Active recall can help strengthen knowledge that must be available without the study book during the examination.

The Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016 reference can help reinforce general framing concepts, measurements, layout, wall construction, ceiling relationships, openings, and construction terminology.

Although the C-17F classification specifically addresses sheet metal studs, broader construction knowledge can help candidates understand how nonstructural metal framing fits into the complete building process.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Code Requirements represents 4 questions, making efficient use of the International Building Code important even though practical installation is the larger portion of the examination.

Use the International Building Code, 2018 to practice locating wall, ceiling, fire-resistance, materials, and related construction requirements.

Definitions should receive attention because code questions may depend on the precise meaning of a construction term. Knowing where to locate definitions can also help narrow the search for an unfamiliar question.

Tables can contain important information that may not be repeated in narrative text. Practice reading table headings, notes, conditions, and references carefully.

Exceptions can change how a general requirement applies. Candidates should read the surrounding code language instead of stopping at the first sentence that appears relevant.

Cross-references may direct candidates to another section containing additional requirements. Practice following these references so the complete code relationship is understood.

Fire-resistance should be connected to both IBC study and gypsum construction study. Many partition and ceiling systems are part of assemblies that must satisfy fire-resistance requirements.

Candidates should understand that the performance of an assembly can depend on the combination of framing, gypsum products, fastening, and installation details rather than on one component by itself.

Material knowledge should include the basic characteristics and purposes of sheet metal studs, tracks, gypsum products, fasteners, and related framing accessories.

Measurement and alignment are practical skills that affect installation quality. Candidates should understand how inaccurate layout can affect wall position, openings, ceiling framing, and subsequent finish work.

Coordination is also important. Nonstructural walls and ceilings often interact with doors, finishes, mechanical systems, electrical work, and other building components.

A useful study schedule is to divide preparation between Metal Framing Installation and Code Requirements while giving substantially more time to Metal Framing Installation because it represents 80 percent of the examination questions.

Begin with topic-based study sessions covering layout, tracks, studs, openings, ceiling framing, fastening, gypsum systems, fire resistance, and IBC navigation.

Once individual subjects become familiar, move into mixed practice. The actual examination can shift from a practical framing question to a code question without warning.

Timed code-navigation drills can improve open-book performance. Choose a subject such as wall assemblies, ceiling construction, fire resistance, or materials and locate the relevant IBC provision within a limited amount of time.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Because candidates have approximately three minutes per question on average, they should avoid searching the code unnecessarily for questions that can be answered confidently from retained trade knowledge.

When a practice question is missed, identify why. The issue may involve weak framing knowledge, unfamiliar terminology, slow code navigation, confusion about gypsum-system relationships, or difficulty interpreting the question.

Correcting the underlying weakness is more valuable than memorizing a single answer. The goal is to build a working understanding of nonstructural metal framing that can be applied to unfamiliar exam questions.

How does the Studs of Sheet Metal Contractor (C-17F) exam work?

The Studs of Sheet Metal Contractor (C-17F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
