Exam Details

The Nevada C-17F Studs of Sheet Metal examination contains 20 questions. Candidates are allowed 1 hour to complete the examination and must answer 14 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following categories:

  • Code Requirements: 4 questions
  • Metal Framing Installation: 16 questions

Metal Framing Installation represents the majority of the C-17F examination. Candidates should spend most of their trade-study time on practical installation knowledge involving sheet metal studs, tracks, wall layout, nonstructural partitions, ceilings, openings, fastening, alignment, and coordination with surrounding building systems.

Code Requirements represents 4 questions. The International Building Code, 2018 is particularly important for this portion of the examination. Candidates should become comfortable locating provisions involving wall systems, ceilings, materials, fire-resistance requirements, definitions, tables, and related construction provisions.

With 20 questions in one hour, candidates have approximately three minutes per question on average. Efficient code navigation can help preserve time, but practical metal framing knowledge remains essential because most questions focus on installation rather than direct code lookup.

State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-17F as the Studs of Sheet Metal subclassification.

The C-17F scope covers the erection and installation of studs made of sheet metal for the framing of ceilings and nonstructural partitioning walls.

The classification is focused on nonstructural sheet metal framing. Candidates should understand the difference between this work and structural steel framing. C-17F work centers on light-gauge sheet metal framing used for walls and ceilings rather than structural members that carry primary building loads.

Typical work can involve floor and ceiling tracks, vertical studs, partition layout, openings, ceiling framing, fasteners, alignment, and coordination with gypsum board and other building components.

Building-code knowledge is important because wall and ceiling systems may be affected by requirements involving materials, fire resistance, assemblies, and construction methods. The International Building Code, 2018 supports these code-based portions of preparation.

The C-17F trade examination is one component of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also complete the applicable application, qualification, business, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong C-17F study plan should focus first on Metal Framing Installation because it represents 16 of the 20 trade examination questions.

Candidates should understand the basic components of nonstructural metal framing, including studs, tracks, fasteners, ceiling framing members, and related connection points.

Layout is a key area of preparation. Review how partition locations are established, how floor and ceiling tracks are aligned, and how accurate measurements affect the completed wall system.

Stud installation should include placement, spacing concepts, orientation, alignment, fastening, and coordination with openings and adjacent construction.

Track installation should include positioning, alignment, attachment, and its relationship to the completed partition or ceiling system.

Openings should receive focused review. Doorways and other penetrations can change framing layouts, and candidates should understand how surrounding metal framing is arranged to accommodate them.

Ceiling framing is specifically included in the C-17F scope. Candidates should study how sheet metal framing components are installed overhead and coordinated with adjacent walls and building surfaces.

Fastening and alignment are practical installation subjects that affect the quality of the finished system. Candidates should understand how connections and layout work together to produce properly positioned framing.

The Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th Edition can help candidates connect metal framing with the wall and ceiling systems it supports. Review partition assemblies, ceiling systems, metal studs, tracks, fastening methods, and related installation details.

The Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016 reference can reinforce measurements, layout, general framing, wall construction, openings, tools, and building terminology.

Because those two trade references are not allowed into the trade examination room, candidates should use them to build retained knowledge. After studying a procedure, close the book and explain the installation sequence from memory.

The International Building Code, 2018 should be used regularly for Code Requirements preparation. Candidates should become comfortable with the book's structure as well as the highlighting and tabs provided in the rental package.

Definitions can help determine how a code requirement applies. Candidates should know how to locate definitions quickly when a term is unclear.

Tables can contain important construction information that may not appear elsewhere in the same form. Practice reading table headings, notes, and conditions carefully.

Exceptions can modify general code requirements. Candidates should review surrounding provisions before choosing an answer.

How does the Studs of Sheet Metal Contractor (C-17F) exam work?

The Studs of Sheet Metal Contractor (C-17F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
