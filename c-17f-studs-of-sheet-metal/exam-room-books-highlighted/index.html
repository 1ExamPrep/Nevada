State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-17F as the Studs of Sheet Metal subclassification.

The C-17F scope covers the erection and installation of studs made of sheet metal for the framing of ceilings and nonstructural partitioning walls.

The classification is focused on nonstructural sheet metal framing rather than structural steel framing. Candidates should understand the difference between light-gauge framing used for partitions and ceilings and structural framing systems designed to carry building loads.

Typical C-17F work can involve floor and ceiling tracks, vertical studs, partition layout, openings, ceiling framing, fasteners, alignment, and coordination with gypsum board and related finish systems.

Building-code knowledge remains important because completed wall and ceiling systems may be affected by provisions involving fire resistance, materials, construction methods, and assembly performance. The International Building Code, 2018 supports preparation for those code-related subjects.

The C-17F trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-17F study plan should begin with Metal Framing Installation because it represents 16 of the 20 examination questions.

Candidates should understand the basic components used in nonstructural sheet metal framing, including studs, tracks, ceiling framing members, fasteners, openings, and related connection points.

Layout is an important starting point. Candidates should understand how partition locations are established, how top and bottom tracks are aligned, and how inaccurate measurements can affect the completed wall system.

Stud installation should include placement within tracks, orientation, spacing concepts, alignment, fastening, and coordination with openings and surrounding construction.

Track installation should include positioning, attachment, alignment, and the relationship between track placement and the finished wall or ceiling.

Openings should receive dedicated study because doorways and other penetrations can require changes in framing layout. Candidates should understand how metal framing is arranged around these conditions.

Ceiling framing is specifically part of the C-17F scope. Study how sheet metal framing components are used overhead and how those components relate to surrounding walls and building surfaces.

Fastening should also be reviewed. Candidates should understand how connections affect the alignment, stability, and quality of nonstructural framing systems.

Measurement and alignment are practical areas that can affect several parts of an installation. Incorrect layout can affect wall location, door openings, ceiling systems, and later finish work.

Coordination is another important part of the trade. Nonstructural framing often interfaces with doors, gypsum board, mechanical systems, electrical work, and other building components.

The International Building Code, 2018 is especially important for the Code Requirements portion of the examination. Candidates should practice locating relevant provisions instead of waiting until test day to become familiar with the book.

The highlighted and tabbed format can make this practice more efficient by giving candidates a visual structure for major subjects. However, candidates should still become comfortable using the book's normal navigation tools.

Definitions can be important because code terms may have specific meanings. Understanding those definitions can help determine which section applies to a question.

Tables should receive regular attention because important requirements may be presented in table format rather than in surrounding text.

Exceptions can modify general provisions. Candidates should read the complete requirement and any applicable exceptions before selecting an answer.

Wall assemblies should be included in code-navigation practice. Candidates should understand how nonstructural partitions fit into broader building-code requirements.

Ceiling assemblies should also receive attention because ceiling framing falls directly within the C-17F classification scope.

Fire-resistance requirements are another important area. Candidates should understand that the performance of an assembly can depend on the complete system rather than one component alone.

Materials should be reviewed from both practical and code perspectives. Candidates should understand the basic role of sheet metal studs, tracks, fasteners, and related components within nonstructural walls and ceilings.

A practical study schedule should give most study time to Metal Framing Installation while still including regular IBC navigation sessions for Code Requirements.

Start with separate study sessions for layout, tracks, studs, openings, ceiling framing, fastening, wall assemblies, fire resistance, and materials. After these areas become familiar, move into mixed practice.

Timed code-navigation drills can be especially useful. Select a topic such as wall assemblies, ceilings, fire resistance, or materials and practice locating the relevant provision within a limited period.

The tabs and highlighting can support these drills by making the organization of the book easier to recognize. The goal is not simply to rely on markings, but to use them as part of a broader navigation strategy.

Because candidates have approximately three minutes per question on average, they should avoid unnecessary code searches for practical framing questions they can answer confidently from retained knowledge.

Active recall is useful for installation topics. Review a framing procedure, close the study material, and explain the sequence from memory, including layout, track placement, stud installation, fastening, alignment, and completion.

When a practice question is missed, identify the cause. The issue may involve weak framing knowledge, unfamiliar terminology, slow IBC navigation, or difficulty interpreting a code provision.

Correcting the underlying weakness is more useful than memorizing a single answer. Strong preparation comes from understanding the trade and knowing how to use the International Building Code efficiently when a lookup is needed.

How does the Studs of Sheet Metal Contractor (C-17F) exam work?

The Studs of Sheet Metal Contractor (C-17F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
