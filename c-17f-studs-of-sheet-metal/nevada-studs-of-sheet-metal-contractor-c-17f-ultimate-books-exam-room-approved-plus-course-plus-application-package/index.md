## Exam Details

The Nevada C-17F Studs of Sheet Metal Contractor examination is administered through PSI as part of the Nevada State Contractors Board licensing process. Nevada requires contractor applicants to submit their application and receive examination eligibility before scheduling the applicable trade examination.

The C-17F trade examination contains **20 questions**. Candidates receive **1 hour** to complete the examination and must answer **14 questions correctly** to pass.

The examination content is divided into two primary areas:

- **Code Requirements:** 4 questions.
- **Metal Framing Installation:** 16 questions.

Metal Framing Installation represents 80 percent of the trade examination. Candidates should therefore spend most of their preparation time developing practical knowledge of nonstructural metal framing rather than relying entirely on code lookups.

Important preparation areas include wall layout, top and bottom tracks, vertical studs, stud spacing, framing around openings, ceiling framing, fasteners, attachment methods, alignment, connections, and coordination with surrounding construction.

The four Code Requirements questions require candidates to understand how the International Building Code applies to wall and ceiling assemblies, fire-resistance requirements, building materials, definitions, tables, exceptions, and related construction provisions.

Because the test contains only 20 questions, each question represents a significant percentage of the final score. Candidates should prepare carefully for both practical installation and code-navigation subjects.

## State Requirements

Typical trade knowledge may include layout, tracks, studs, wall intersections, openings, ceiling framing, fasteners, attachment, alignment, and coordination with gypsum board and related building components.

The C-17F classification should not be confused with structural steel framing. The scope concerns studs of sheet metal used for ceilings and nonstructural partitioning walls rather than structural steel building frames.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-17F study plan should give most of its attention to Metal Framing Installation because that subject represents 16 of the 20 examination questions.

Begin with the primary components used in nonstructural sheet metal framing. Candidates should understand how studs and tracks work together, how walls are laid out, and how the framing system is positioned before installation begins.

**Stud installation** should include spacing, orientation, alignment, placement within tracks, fastening, and coordination with openings and adjacent construction. Candidates should understand how consistent installation affects the completed wall assembly.

**Track installation** should include layout, positioning, attachment, alignment, and the relationship between tracks and vertical framing members. Proper track placement establishes the boundaries of the wall and influences the alignment of the studs.

**Openings** deserve dedicated preparation. Doors, access panels, and other wall interruptions may require additional framing or altered stud placement. Candidates should understand the practical principles used to frame these conditions.

**Ceiling framing** is specifically included within the C-17F classification. Review the relationship between metal framing members, support conditions, alignment, attachment, and coordination with surrounding wall and ceiling systems.

**Fasteners and connections** should also be studied. Candidates should understand why proper attachment matters and how fastening methods contribute to alignment and stability within a nonstructural framing assembly.

**Measurement and layout** are essential trade concepts. Improper layout can affect wall location, openings, ceiling framing, and the installation of finish materials that follow the framing work.

The four Code Requirements questions make International Building Code navigation an important secondary part of preparation. Practice finding information involving wall assemblies, ceilings, fire resistance, construction materials, definitions, and related provisions.

The IBC index is useful when the exact section is unknown. Identify the principal term in the question and use the index to narrow the search. Once the section is found, read enough surrounding text to understand whether an exception, table, note, or cross-reference affects the requirement.

Definitions should also receive attention because building-code terminology can have a specific meaning. A candidate who understands the defined term can often identify the correct code path more quickly.

Timed practice is especially useful for a 20-question examination. Candidates have approximately three minutes per question on average, but practical framing questions may be answered more quickly, leaving additional time for targeted code lookups.
