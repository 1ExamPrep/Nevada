## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada B-2 preparation strategy combines construction knowledge with efficient use of the approved exam-room references. Candidates should understand the building process well enough to recognize which code book is most likely to contain the answer.

**Sitework** preparation should include grading, excavation, drainage, soil conditions, trenches, compaction, and foundation preparation. These subjects create the conditions on which the rest of the structure depends.

**Concrete** should include footings, foundation walls, slabs, reinforcement, formwork, placement, finishing, curing, and structural provisions. Candidates should practice recognizing when ACI 318 is the most appropriate reference and when the IBC or IRC provides the governing building requirement.

**Masonry** should include brick, block, stone, mortar, wall systems, structural relationships, openings, and coordination with foundations and framing.

**Carpentry** should include floors, walls, roofs, sheathing, openings, headers, stairs, and construction sequencing. Candidates should practice using the IRC for residential framing topics and the IBC for broader building requirements.

**Thermal and Moisture Protection** should include roofing, flashing, weather barriers, waterproofing, drainage, and insulation. These systems protect the building enclosure from moisture and environmental exposure.

**Doors and Windows** should be reviewed in relation to rough openings, structural support, installation, flashing, dimensions, and weather protection.

**Drywall and Finishes** should include gypsum systems, interior wall and ceiling construction, framing coordination, fastening, finishing, and related building practices.

**Plan Reading** should be practiced regularly. Candidates should be able to interpret floor plans, foundation plans, elevations, sections, details, schedules, symbols, dimensions, and construction notes.

**Estimating** should include dimensions, areas, volumes, quantities, and basic material takeoffs. Candidates should understand how plan information connects to the amount of work and material required in the field.

The **International Building Code** should be studied by subject area. Practice locating construction types, structural requirements, fire and life safety provisions, means of egress, foundations, and broader building topics.

The **International Residential Code** should be used frequently for one- and two-family dwelling subjects such as foundations, floors, walls, roofs, stairs, openings, and weather protection.

**ACI 318-14** should be used for structural concrete preparation. Candidates should become familiar with its organization so reinforcement and structural concrete provisions can be located more quickly.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

A useful study technique is to separate practice questions by reference at first. Complete a set of IBC questions, then IRC questions, then ACI 318 questions. This helps reinforce the purpose and organization of each book.

Once candidates become comfortable with each reference, the questions should be mixed together. Mixed practice more closely resembles the actual exam because candidates must decide which book applies before starting a lookup.

The highlighted and tabbed format can support faster review of important sections, but candidates should not depend only on tabs. Knowing how to use the table of contents, index, definitions, chapter structure, tables, notes, and exceptions remains essential.

Definitions deserve attention because code terminology can affect the meaning of a question. Understanding the exact language used in the references can help candidates identify the correct section and avoid misreading a requirement.

Tables should also receive regular practice. Candidates should read headings, notes, footnotes, exceptions, and limitations carefully instead of stopping as soon as a familiar value appears.

Timed navigation drills can improve speed. Select a subject such as stairs, foundations, concrete reinforcement, wall framing, or means of egress and practice locating the applicable provision within a limited amount of time.

With 80 questions in three hours, pacing is important. Candidates should avoid spending too long on a single difficult search while easier questions remain unanswered.

If a question becomes time-consuming, moving forward and returning later may be more effective than continuing to search. The approved books are tools for confirming requirements, but strong trade knowledge and time management remain important.

## How does the Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) exam work?

The Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
