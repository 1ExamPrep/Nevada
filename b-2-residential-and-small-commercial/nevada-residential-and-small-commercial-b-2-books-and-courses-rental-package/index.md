## State Requirements

Nevada's **B-2 Residential and Small Commercial** classification covers construction and remodeling of houses and other structures that support, shelter, or enclose persons, animals, or property and that do not extend more than **three stories above ground and one story below ground**.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada B-2 study plan should follow the sequence of a building project. Begin with **sitework**, then move through foundations, concrete, masonry, framing, enclosure, openings, interior finishes, safety, plans, and estimating.

**Sitework** preparation should include grading, excavation, drainage, trenching, soil conditions, compaction, underground utility awareness, and foundation preparation.

**Concrete** should include footings, foundation walls, slabs, reinforcement, formwork, placement, consolidation, curing, and structural requirements. ACI 318 should be used regularly for structural concrete preparation.

**Masonry** preparation should include brick, block, stone, mortar, wall systems, layout, and the relationship between masonry and other structural components.

**Carpentry** should include floor framing, wall framing, roof framing, sheathing, openings, headers, stairs, and construction sequencing. Residential framing questions may often lead candidates to the IRC.

**Thermal and Moisture Protection** should include roofing, waterproofing, flashing, weather barriers, drainage, and insulation. These systems protect the building envelope and help prevent moisture-related failures.

**Doors and Windows** should include rough openings, structural support, installation, flashing, weather protection, and coordination with surrounding wall systems.

**Drywall and Finishes** should include gypsum products, wall and ceiling systems, framing coordination, fastening, finishing, and common installation practices.

**Safety** should be studied independently because OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 is not allowed into the trade exam. Candidates should develop retained knowledge of fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, excavation hazards, personal protective equipment, tools, electrical hazards, and material handling.

**Plan Reading** should include floor plans, foundation plans, elevations, sections, details, schedules, symbols, dimensions, and notes. Candidates should practice following information from one drawing to another.

**Estimating** should include dimensions, areas, volumes, quantities, and basic material takeoffs. The ability to read drawings accurately is essential for both exam questions and real-world general contracting.

The **International Building Code**, **International Residential Code**, and **ACI 318** should be used regularly during preparation. Candidates should practice identifying the correct reference before beginning a lookup.

A useful study method is to separate questions by reference at first, then mix them later. This helps candidates build familiarity with the purpose and organization of each exam-room book.

The study-only references should be used to build knowledge that can be recalled without those books during the exam. Review a topic, close the reference, and answer practice questions from memory.

## How does the Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) exam work?

The Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
