## State Requirements

Nevada defines the **B-2 Residential and Small Commercial** classification as the construction and remodeling of houses and other structures that support, shelter, or enclose persons, animals, or other property and that do not extend more than **three stories above the ground and one story below the ground**.

B-2 is a general building classification. Projects performed under a general building license involve coordination of multiple unrelated construction trades when building or remodeling a structure is the primary purpose of the project.

The B-2 scope is more limited by building size than the unrestricted general B classification. Candidates should understand the three-story-above-ground and one-story-below-ground limitation when evaluating which projects fit the classification.

A B-2 contractor should also understand that holding a general building classification does not automatically authorize self-performance of every specialty trade. Electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and fire-protection work require the applicable specialty authority when performed directly.

The Nevada State Contractors Board also expects qualifying experience for B-2 to demonstrate actual building-construction experience rather than experience limited solely to isolated remodeling or specialty trade work.

The trade examination is one part of the licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy applicable experience, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before the contractor license is issued.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada B-2 study plan should follow the sequence of a building project. Start with **sitework**, then move through foundations, concrete, masonry, structural framing, exterior protection, openings, interior finishes, safety, plans, and estimating.

**Sitework** preparation should include excavation, grading, drainage, soil preparation, compaction, trenches, underground utilities, and Nevada One Call. Candidates should understand how site conditions influence foundations and building performance.

**Concrete** should include formwork, reinforcement, placement, consolidation, curing, finishing, slabs, footings, and structural concrete requirements. Use ACI 318 for code-based structural concrete study and The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction for practical field preparation.

**Masonry** should include brick, block, stone, mortar, walls, reinforcement, layout, tools, and installation practices. Candidates should understand the relationship between masonry construction and structural or weather-resistant building assemblies.

**Carpentry** should receive substantial attention. Review floor framing, wall framing, roof framing, openings, headers, stairs, sheathing, trusses, fastening, layout, and construction sequencing.

The BCSI truss guide should be used to strengthen understanding of truss handling, installation, temporary restraint, and bracing. Because this book is not available during the exam, important principles should be reinforced through active recall.

**Thermal and Moisture Protection** should include weather barriers, flashing, insulation, roofing, waterproofing, drainage, and protection of building assemblies from moisture intrusion.

**Doors and Windows** should include rough openings, installation, flashing, structural coordination, weather protection, and applicable code requirements.

**Drywall and Finishes** should include gypsum products, framing coordination, fastening, finishing, ceilings, partitions, and common installation practices.

**Safety** should be studied independently because OSHA is not allowed into the trade examination. Candidates should build retained knowledge involving fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, excavation hazards, electrical safety, personal protective equipment, tools, and material handling.

**Plan Reading** should include floor plans, foundation plans, framing plans, elevations, sections, details, schedules, dimensions, notes, and symbols. Candidates should practice tracing construction relationships from one drawing sheet to another.

**Estimating** should include basic quantity takeoffs, dimensions, areas, volumes, material quantities, and interpreting drawings accurately enough to determine construction needs.

The approved **IBC** and **IRC** should be used throughout preparation. Rather than reading each code from beginning to end, practice locating targeted requirements through chapter structure, tables of contents, indexes, and key sections.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

Use the **ACI 318-14** reference for structural concrete questions. Candidates should become familiar enough with the document to recognize where reinforcement, structural member, and concrete requirements are generally located.

A practical study method is to sort practice questions into three groups: IBC, IRC, and ACI. Once candidates are comfortable choosing the correct reference, mix the questions together to improve exam-style decision making.

Study-only books should gradually be removed from practice sessions. Review a topic, close the book, and answer questions from memory. This is particularly important for OSHA, carpentry, masonry, excavation, drywall, trusses, Nevada One Call, and field concrete knowledge.

Time management matters. With 80 questions in three hours, candidates should avoid spending too long on a single code search. Answer familiar questions efficiently and return to more difficult code lookups when possible.

## How does the Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) exam work?

The Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
