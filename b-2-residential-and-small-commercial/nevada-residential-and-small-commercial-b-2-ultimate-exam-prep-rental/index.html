Exam Details

The Nevada B-2 Residential and Small Commercial General Building Contractor examination is administered through PSI for candidates who receive examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada B-2 Residential and Small Commercial General Building Contractor
  • Number of Questions: 80
  • Time Allowed: 3 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 56 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination covers the following major construction areas:

  • Sitework: 4 questions
  • Concrete: 10 questions
  • Masonry: 10 questions
  • Metals: 5 questions
  • Carpentry: 12 questions
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection: 9 questions
  • Doors and Windows: 3 questions
  • Drywall and Finishes: 4 questions
  • Safety: 10 questions
  • General Code, Plan Reading and Estimating: 10 questions
  • Nevada One Call: 3 questions

Carpentry is the largest individual examination category with 12 questions. Candidates should devote substantial study time to floor framing, wall framing, roof framing, sheathing, trusses, openings, fastening, structural wood construction, and practical carpentry methods.

Concrete, Masonry, Safety, and General Code, Plan Reading and Estimating each represent significant portions of the exam. Candidates should be ready to connect practical building knowledge with code provisions, site safety, construction plans, dimensions, quantities, and estimating concepts.

Thermal and Moisture Protection is another important section. Study should include roofing, weather protection, insulation-related concepts, flashing, moisture control, and exterior protection methods relevant to residential and small commercial construction.

Metals, Sitework, Drywall and Finishes, Doors and Windows, and Nevada One Call are smaller categories but should not be overlooked because every correct answer contributes toward the passing score.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the B-2 Residential and Small Commercial classification. Passing the trade examination is one part of the complete Nevada contractor licensing process.

  1. Confirm the B-2 classification. Determine that Residential and Small Commercial matches the work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the Trade Qualified Individual. Designate the person who will establish the applicable experience and trade qualification.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare records demonstrating relevant residential and small commercial construction experience.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor application. Organize the required business, qualifier, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the application before examination eligibility is issued.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow the required fingerprint and background procedures.
  7. Provide applicable financial information. Nevada evaluates financial responsibility as part of contractor licensing.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Eligible candidates may proceed with the required examinations after the applicable review.
  9. Schedule the B-2 examination through PSI. Arrange testing after receiving authorization.
  10. Prepare for the B-2 examination. Review sitework, concrete, masonry, metals, carpentry, thermal and moisture protection, doors and windows, drywall, safety, code, plans, estimating, and Nevada One Call.
  11. Prepare for Nevada Business and Law. Use the included Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada for Contractor Management Survey preparation.
  12. Complete applicable examinations. Meet trade and management qualification requirements unless an applicable waiver is approved.
  13. Complete final licensing requirements. Address applicable financial responsibility, bonding, and remaining Nevada State Contractors Board requirements.

State Requirements

The Nevada B-2 Residential and Small Commercial classification covers qualifying construction and remodeling of houses and other structures that support, shelter, or enclose persons, animals, or other property, provided those structures remain within the size limitations established for the classification.

The classification applies to structures that do not extend more than three stories above ground and one story below ground. Candidates should understand this limitation when evaluating the type of work authorized under the B-2 classification.

The B-2 scope is broad, which is why the examination includes multiple construction disciplines. Contractors should be prepared to understand framing, concrete, masonry, drywall, weather protection, structural requirements, sitework, plans, estimating, and other general building subjects.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual generally must establish qualifying experience appropriate to the requested classification. Applicants should maintain organized documentation describing the work performed and the period during which the experience was gained.

Nevada contractor licensing also distinguishes between management and trade qualification. One individual may satisfy both roles when all applicable requirements are met, or separate qualified individuals may be designated.

The Trade Qualified Individual establishes the applicable experience and trade qualification for B-2. The Management Qualified Individual generally satisfies the Nevada Business and Law requirement unless an applicable waiver is approved.

Financial responsibility, background requirements, contractor bonding, and other licensing conditions are handled separately from the trade examination. Passing the B-2 examination alone does not complete the contractor licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada B-2 study plan should reflect the breadth of the examination and the weighting of the major categories. Candidates should focus heavily on Carpentry, Concrete, Masonry, Safety, General Code, Plan Reading and Estimating, and Thermal and Moisture Protection while still reviewing every exam subject.

Practice code navigation. Work repeatedly with the International Building Code, International Residential Code, and ACI 318-14. Practice locating specific requirements instead of simply reading large sections of the books.

Strengthen carpentry knowledge. Review framing systems, structural wood components, floors, walls, roofs, trusses, sheathing, openings, fastening, and general carpentry procedures.

Study concrete and masonry. Connect practical construction knowledge from the study references with structural and code requirements found in the approved exam-room books.

Prepare Safety for recall. OSHA is not allowed into the trade examination, so candidates should learn construction-safety concepts before test day.

Review sitework and excavation. Use Pipe and Excavation Contracting to strengthen understanding of trenching, earthwork, excavation, and related site conditions.

Review drywall and finishes. Use the Gypsum Construction Handbook to reinforce gypsum products, installation methods, finishing, and related construction knowledge.

Prepare for Nevada One Call. Study Suggested Marking Guidelines and The Nevada One Call Law carefully because the reference is not allowed into the exam and the subject appears directly on the examination.

Practice plan reading and estimating. Work with dimensions, quantities, construction details, project interpretation, and general estimating concepts.

Use timed practice. Candidates have three hours for 80 questions. Timed preparation can help improve pacing and make it easier to decide when to use an approved code book and when to answer directly from trade knowledge.

How does the Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) exam work?

The Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
