Exam Details

The Nevada B-2 Residential and Small Commercial General Building Contractor examination is administered through PSI for candidates who receive examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada B-2 Residential and Small Commercial General Building Contractor
  • Number of Questions: 80
  • Time Allowed: 3 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 56 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination covers a broad range of general building subjects:

  • Sitework: 4 questions
  • Concrete: 10 questions
  • Masonry: 10 questions
  • Metals: 5 questions
  • Carpentry: 12 questions
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection: 9 questions
  • Doors and Windows: 3 questions
  • Drywall and Finishes: 4 questions
  • Safety: 10 questions
  • General Code, Plan Reading and Estimating: 10 questions
  • Nevada One Call: 3 questions

Carpentry is the largest individual examination category. Candidates should review framing, structural wood construction, floor systems, wall framing, roof framing, trusses, sheathing, openings, fastening, and practical construction methods.

Concrete and Masonry are also major subjects. Preparation should include concrete placement, reinforcement, forms, structural requirements, masonry units, mortar, block and brick construction, and applicable building-code requirements.

Safety is an important part of B-2 preparation. Candidates should study construction hazards, personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, excavation safety, fall protection, tools, material handling, and other OSHA-related concepts.

General Code, Plan Reading and Estimating requires candidates to connect building-code knowledge with practical project interpretation. Candidates should be comfortable reading construction information, identifying applicable code requirements, understanding dimensions and details, and applying estimating principles.

Open Book Test

The Nevada B-2 Residential and Small Commercial examination is an open-book test with specific approved code references permitted in the examination room.

The approved exam-room references included with this package are:

  • International Building Code, 2018
  • International Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings, 2018
  • ACI 318-14: Building Code Requirements for Structural Concrete and Commentary

Candidates should become familiar with all three approved references before examination day. The International Building Code supports general building requirements, structural provisions, materials, occupancy-related requirements, fire and life-safety provisions, and other construction topics.

The International Residential Code is particularly important for residential construction. Candidates should practice locating requirements involving foundations, floors, walls, roofs, framing, openings, stairs, weather protection, and other residential construction elements.

ACI 318-14 supports structural concrete preparation. Candidates should practice locating requirements involving concrete materials, reinforcement, structural provisions, and related concrete construction requirements.

The following books are included for study but are not allowed into the B-2 trade examination:

  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)

Because these study-only references cannot be used during the trade exam, candidates should use them to build practical construction knowledge and recall. Strong familiarity with carpentry, masonry, concrete, excavation, drywall, trusses, safety, and Nevada One Call requirements can reduce unnecessary searching during the examination.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the B-2 Residential and Small Commercial classification. The licensing process includes trade qualification, application review, examinations, financial requirements, background procedures, and final license requirements.

  1. Confirm the B-2 classification. Determine that Residential and Small Commercial matches the work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. Designate the person who will establish the applicable experience and trade qualification.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare records demonstrating relevant residential and small commercial construction experience.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor application. Organize the required business, qualifier, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the application and establishes examination eligibility.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow required fingerprint and background procedures.
  7. Address financial responsibility. Provide applicable financial information required during contractor licensing.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Eligible candidates may proceed with the B-2 and applicable management examinations.
  9. Schedule the B-2 examination through PSI. Arrange testing after authorization is issued.
  10. Prepare for the B-2 trade examination. Review sitework, concrete, masonry, metals, carpentry, moisture protection, doors and windows, drywall, safety, code, plans, estimating, and Nevada One Call.
  11. Prepare for Nevada Business and Law. Use the included Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada for Contractor Management Survey preparation.
  12. Complete applicable examinations. Meet trade and management examination requirements unless an approved waiver applies.
  13. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Address applicable bonding, financial, and final Board requirements.
  14. Establish the business entity. Business Formation for an LLC or corporation is included with The 1 Package.
  15. Obtain the EIN. EIN Filing with the IRS is included for the contracting business.

State Requirements

The B-2 Residential and Small Commercial classification covers the construction and remodeling of houses and other structures that support, shelter, or enclose persons, animals, or other property and that do not extend more than three stories above ground and one story below ground.

The broad scope makes B-2 a general building classification requiring knowledge across multiple construction trades. Candidates should understand structural construction, materials, building-code requirements, safety, site conditions, framing, concrete, masonry, drywall, weather protection, and project interpretation.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must establish qualifying experience appropriate to the classification requested. Experience documentation should clearly describe the type of work performed and demonstrate responsibility in residential or small commercial construction.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes Business and Law requirements. The Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada included with this package supports preparation for the Contractor Management Survey examination.

Financial responsibility, background requirements, bonding, and a monetary limit are separate parts of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Passing the B-2 trade examination does not by itself complete the licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive B-2 preparation plan should give significant attention to Carpentry, Concrete, Masonry, Safety, General Code, Plan Reading and Estimating, and Thermal and Moisture Protection while still reviewing every examination category.

Practice code navigation. Work repeatedly with the International Building Code, International Residential Code, and ACI 318-14. Learn how the books are organized so code searches can be targeted rather than random.

Strengthen carpentry knowledge. Review framing systems, structural wood components, floors, walls, roofs, trusses, sheathing, openings, and practical building procedures.

Study concrete and masonry. Connect structural code requirements with practical knowledge from the concrete and masonry study references.

Prepare safety for recall. OSHA is not allowed into the trade examination, so candidates should learn construction-safety principles before testing.

Review plan reading and estimating. Practice interpreting building information, dimensions, construction details, quantities, and general estimating concepts.

Use timed practice. Candidates have three hours for 80 questions. Timed practice can help develop a steady pace and improve decisions about when an approved code reference should be consulted.

How does the Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) exam work?

The Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
