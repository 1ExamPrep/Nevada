## State Requirements

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada B-2 preparation plan should follow the sequence of a building project while connecting each phase to the approved code most likely to govern it.

**Sitework** should include grading, excavation, drainage, soil conditions, compaction, trenching, foundation preparation, and underground utility awareness. Even when a particular field reference is not available during the exam, candidates should understand how proper site preparation affects the rest of the building.

**Concrete** should include footings, walls, slabs, reinforcement, formwork, placement, curing, and structural requirements. ACI 318 should be used for structural concrete provisions, while the IBC and IRC can help connect those requirements to the building as a whole.

**Masonry** should include brick, block, stone, mortar, structural walls, layout, reinforcement concepts, and coordination with the rest of the building. Candidates should understand how masonry systems relate to foundations, openings, roof framing, and moisture protection.

**Carpentry** should include wall framing, floor framing, roof framing, sheathing, openings, headers, stairs, fastening, and construction sequencing. For residential questions, the IRC is often a primary reference.

**Thermal and Moisture Protection** should include roofing, weather barriers, flashing, waterproofing, insulation, drainage, and methods used to protect the building enclosure from moisture intrusion.

**Doors and Windows** should include rough openings, installation, flashing, dimensions, structural coordination, and weather protection. Candidates should understand how these components fit into framed wall assemblies.

**Drywall and Finishes** should include interior wall and ceiling systems, gypsum materials, fastening, finishing, and coordination with framing and other building components.

**Plan Reading** should be practiced regularly. Candidates should know how to read floor plans, foundation plans, elevations, sections, details, schedules, notes, dimensions, and symbols.

**Estimating** should include basic quantity takeoffs, dimensions, areas, volumes, material quantities, and interpretation of drawings. Candidates should be able to connect a drawing to the materials and construction work required in the field.

The **International Building Code** should be studied by major subject area rather than from beginning to end. Practice locating occupancy-related requirements, construction types, fire and life safety provisions, means of egress, structural sections, and other broad building topics.

The **International Residential Code** should be used for residential topics such as foundations, floors, walls, roofs, stairs, guards, openings, weather protection, and one- and two-family dwelling construction.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

**ACI 318-14** should be used for structural concrete study. Candidates should become familiar with how the book is organized so reinforcement, concrete members, development, and structural provisions can be located efficiently.

A practical preparation method is to sort questions into three groups: IBC, IRC, and ACI 318. Practice choosing the correct book before starting a lookup. Once that becomes comfortable, combine the questions so the study session more closely resembles the actual exam.

Definitions should receive attention in all three references. Technical terms can change the meaning of a question, and understanding the exact language used in the codes can help candidates recognize the correct section.

Tables are also important. Candidates should practice identifying when a table is likely to provide the required information and how to read headings, notes, footnotes, and limitations accurately.

Timed navigation practice can improve efficiency. Choose a topic such as stairs, wall framing, concrete reinforcement, foundations, or means of egress and record how long it takes to locate the applicable provision.

Repeat slower searches until navigation improves. The goal is not to memorize every code section, but to build enough familiarity that candidates know where to start.

Because the exam contains 80 questions in three hours, pacing should be part of preparation. Candidates should avoid spending several minutes on one code search while easier questions remain unanswered.

If a question becomes difficult, moving forward and returning later may be more effective than continuing to search. Good exam pacing depends on knowing when to use the books and when to answer from construction knowledge.

## How does the Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) exam work?

The Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
