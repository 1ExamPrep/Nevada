## State Requirements

Nevada's **B-2 Residential and Small Commercial** classification covers construction and remodeling of houses and other structures that support, shelter, or enclose persons, animals, or property and that do not extend more than **three stories above ground and one story below ground**.

The B-2 classification is a general building classification within those size limits. Candidates should understand the full construction process rather than concentrating on only one specialty trade.

A B-2 contractor may coordinate multiple phases of work, including site preparation, foundations, structural framing, masonry, roofing, weather protection, doors, windows, drywall, finishes, and other construction activities needed to complete a building.

General building authority does not automatically mean every specialty trade can be self-performed. Electrical, plumbing, HVAC, fire protection, and other specialty work require the applicable authority when performed directly.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada B-2 study plan should follow the sequence of a building project. Begin with **sitework and foundations**, then move through concrete, masonry, framing, enclosure, openings, interior finishes, safety, plan reading, and estimating.

**Sitework** preparation should include grading, excavation, drainage, trenches, soil conditions, compaction, and preparation for foundation work. Candidates should understand how poor site preparation can affect the building above it.

**Concrete** should include footings, foundations, slabs, reinforcement, formwork, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, and structural requirements. Use ACI 318 for reinforced structural concrete provisions and the Contractor's Guide for practical field study.

**Masonry** preparation should include brick, block, stone, mortar, walls, layout, reinforcement concepts, and coordination with other building systems.

**Carpentry** should include floors, walls, roofs, sheathing, openings, headers, stairs, fastening, framing layout, and construction sequencing. Residential framing questions may often lead candidates to the IRC.

**Wood Trusses** should be studied for handling, installation, restraint, and bracing. The BCSI guide is included for study so candidates can strengthen understanding of truss-related field practices.

**Thermal and Moisture Protection** should include roofing, flashing, weather barriers, waterproofing, insulation, drainage, and methods used to protect the building enclosure from moisture.

**Doors and Windows** should be reviewed in relation to openings, structural coordination, installation, flashing, weather protection, and applicable code requirements.

**Drywall and Finishes** should include gypsum materials, wall and ceiling systems, framing coordination, fastening, finishing, and common installation practices.

**Safety** should be studied independently because OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 is not allowed into the exam room. Candidates should develop retained knowledge involving fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, excavation hazards, electrical safety, tools, material handling, and personal protective equipment.

**Plan Reading** should include floor plans, foundation plans, elevations, sections, details, schedules, symbols, dimensions, and notes. Candidates should practice moving between drawing sheets and understanding how the information relates to field construction.

**Estimating** should include basic dimensions, areas, volumes, quantities, and material takeoffs. Careful interpretation of drawings is important because an estimating mistake can begin with a simple measurement or plan-reading error.

The **IBC**, **IRC**, and **ACI 318** should be used throughout preparation. Candidates should practice choosing the correct reference before beginning a lookup.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

A useful exercise is to separate practice questions by reference at first. Work through a group of IBC questions, then IRC questions, then ACI 318 questions. Once the structure of each book becomes more familiar, mix the questions together.

Highlighted and tabbed books can support this process by providing an organized physical reference during study. Candidates should still learn how the books are structured so they can navigate beyond only the sections they have used most often.

Tables, definitions, chapter headings, and indexes should all receive attention. Many code questions require more than locating a section number; candidates must also read table headings, notes, exceptions, and limitations correctly.

Timed practice is important. With 80 questions in three hours, spending too long on one difficult code search can reduce the time available for easier questions. Candidates should practice moving forward and returning to difficult items when needed.

## How does the Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) exam work?

The Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
