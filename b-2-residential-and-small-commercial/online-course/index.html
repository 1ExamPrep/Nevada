Exam Details

The Nevada B-2 Residential and Small Commercial General Building Contractor examination is administered through PSI for candidates who receive examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada B-2 Residential and Small Commercial General Building Contractor
  • Number of Questions: 80
  • Time Allowed: 3 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 56 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination covers the following major construction areas:

  • Sitework: 4 questions
  • Concrete: 10 questions
  • Masonry: 10 questions
  • Metals: 5 questions
  • Carpentry: 12 questions
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection: 9 questions
  • Doors and Windows: 3 questions
  • Drywall and Finishes: 4 questions
  • Safety: 10 questions
  • General Code, Plan Reading and Estimating: 10 questions
  • Nevada One Call: 3 questions

Carpentry is the largest individual examination category with 12 questions. Candidates should devote significant study time to framing systems, floor construction, wall framing, roof framing, trusses, sheathing, openings, fastening, structural wood components, and general carpentry methods.

Concrete and Masonry each contain 10 questions. Concrete preparation should include reinforcement, forms, placement, structural requirements, and quality construction practices. Masonry study should include brick, block, stone, mortar, layout, construction methods, and related building-code requirements.

Safety also contains 10 questions. Candidates should review fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, excavations, personal protective equipment, tools, material handling, and other construction hazards covered by OSHA-related preparation.

General Code, Plan Reading and Estimating contains 10 questions. Candidates should practice reading construction information, interpreting dimensions and details, locating code provisions, estimating quantities, and connecting project plans with applicable building requirements.

Thermal and Moisture Protection contains 9 questions. Preparation should include roofing, weather protection, flashing, moisture control, insulation-related concepts, and other methods used to protect residential and small commercial buildings from environmental exposure.

Sitework, Metals, Doors and Windows, Drywall and Finishes, and Nevada One Call make up smaller portions of the exam but remain important. Candidates should avoid concentrating only on the largest categories because every question can contribute toward the passing score.

Open Book Test

The Nevada B-2 Residential and Small Commercial examination is an open-book test with specific approved references permitted in the examination room.

The approved exam-room references used for this preparation are:

  • International Building Code, 2018
  • International Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings, 2018
  • ACI 318-14: Building Code Requirements for Structural Concrete and Commentary

The International Building Code supports preparation involving general building requirements, structural provisions, construction materials, occupancy-related requirements, fire and life-safety provisions, and other building-code topics relevant to small commercial and general building work.

The International Residential Code is especially important for residential construction. Candidates should become comfortable locating requirements involving foundations, floor systems, wall construction, roofs, framing, openings, stairs, weather protection, and other residential building subjects.

ACI 318-14 supports structural concrete preparation. Candidates should practice finding requirements involving concrete materials, reinforcement, structural provisions, and related concrete construction requirements.

The following books are used for study but are not allowed into the B-2 trade examination:

  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
  • Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016
  • Modern Masonry - Brick, Block, Stone, Clois E. Kicklighter, 10th Edition
  • Pipe and Excavation Contracting
  • Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th Edition
  • Suggested Marking Guidelines and The Nevada One Call Law
  • BCSI: Guide to Good Practice for Handling, Installing, Restraining, and Bracing of Metal Plate Connected Wood Trusses, 2025
  • The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction, 4th Edition

Because these books cannot be used during the trade exam, candidates should use them to build technical knowledge before test day. The stronger the candidate's recall in carpentry, masonry, safety, concrete, excavation, drywall, trusses, and Nevada One Call, the less time may be needed searching for answers during the examination.

With 80 questions and three hours available, candidates have a little more than two minutes per question on average. Efficient reference navigation is therefore important. A productive strategy is to answer familiar questions from trade knowledge and use the approved references for questions requiring a precise code provision, table, or structural requirement.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the B-2 Residential and Small Commercial classification. Passing the trade examination is one part of the overall contractor licensing process.

  1. Confirm the B-2 classification. Determine that Residential and Small Commercial matches the type of work the contracting business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. Designate the individual who will establish the applicable experience and trade qualification.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Organize records demonstrating relevant residential and small commercial construction experience.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor application. Assemble the required business, qualifier, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application to the Nevada State Contractors Board. The Board reviews the application before examination eligibility is issued.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow the required fingerprint and background procedures.
  7. Provide applicable financial information. Financial responsibility is evaluated as part of the Nevada licensing process.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Eligible candidates may proceed with required testing.
  9. Schedule the B-2 trade examination through PSI. Arrange testing after receiving authorization.
  10. Prepare for the B-2 examination. Study sitework, concrete, masonry, metals, carpentry, moisture protection, doors and windows, drywall, safety, code, plans, estimating, and Nevada One Call.
  11. Prepare for Nevada Business and Law. Nevada contractor applicants generally must satisfy the applicable management examination requirement unless an approved waiver applies.
  12. Complete applicable examinations. Meet the trade and management qualification requirements.
  13. Complete final licensing requirements. Address applicable bonding, financial, and remaining Board requirements before license issuance.

State Requirements

The Nevada B-2 Residential and Small Commercial classification covers qualifying construction and remodeling of houses and other structures that support, shelter, or enclose persons, animals, or other property, within the limits established for the classification.

The classification applies to structures that do not extend more than three stories above ground and one story below ground. Candidates should understand this scope when preparing for both the examination and the responsibilities associated with the license.

The broad B-2 scope explains why the examination includes many construction disciplines. Candidates are expected to understand how framing, concrete, masonry, building codes, sitework, safety, moisture protection, drywall, and project interpretation fit together in residential and small commercial construction.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must establish qualifying experience appropriate to the requested classification. Applicants should maintain organized records that clearly describe the type of construction work performed and the period during which the experience was obtained.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification, financial responsibility, background requirements, contractor bonding, and other conditions separate from the B-2 trade examination. Passing the trade test alone does not complete the licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada B-2 study plan should reflect both the breadth and weighting of the examination. Carpentry deserves significant attention, followed by Concrete, Masonry, Safety, General Code, Plan Reading and Estimating, and Thermal and Moisture Protection.

Practice code navigation. Work regularly with the International Building Code, International Residential Code, and ACI 318-14. Practice locating exact requirements instead of reading entire chapters without a specific purpose.

Strengthen carpentry knowledge. Review floor systems, wall framing, roofs, trusses, sheathing, openings, fastening, and other structural wood construction topics.

Study concrete and masonry. Connect practical trade knowledge with applicable structural and building-code requirements.

Review safety for recall. OSHA is not allowed into the trade examination, so candidates should learn the safety principles before testing rather than relying on reference searches.

Prepare for sitework and excavation. Review excavation, trenching, site preparation, and related earthwork concepts.

Study drywall and finishes. Review gypsum materials, installation methods, finishing procedures, and related construction practices.

Prepare for Nevada One Call. Study the Nevada One Call material carefully because it is a specific examination category and the reference is not allowed into the exam room.

Practice plan reading and estimating. Work with dimensions, project details, quantities, construction information, and general estimating concepts.

Use timed practice. Work through practice-oriented questions under conditions that encourage a steady pace. This can help candidates develop better judgment about when to search an approved reference and when to answer directly from experience and study.

How does the Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) exam work?

The Residential and Small Commercial Contractor (B-2) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
