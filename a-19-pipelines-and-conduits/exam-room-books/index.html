Exam Details

The Nevada A-19 Pipeline and Conduits Contractor examination contains 60 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the examination and must answer 42 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following areas:

  • Gas Piping: 15 questions
  • Water Piping: 15 questions
  • Welding: 6 questions
  • Safety: 8 questions
  • Paving: 5 questions
  • Excavation, Compaction and Grading: 8 questions
  • Nevada One Call: 3 questions

Gas Piping is one of the two largest sections. Candidates should understand gas-pipeline materials, fittings, joining methods, installation procedures, protective methods, trench considerations, and other construction concepts associated with gas transmission and distribution work.

Water Piping also accounts for 15 questions. Preparation should include pipe materials, installation methods, trench preparation, fittings, joining, bedding, backfilling, compaction, and related underground water-pipeline work.

Safety accounts for 8 questions and should receive substantial preparation. Pipeline and conduit construction can involve excavation hazards, heavy equipment, traffic, buried utilities, lifting, tools, hot work, and changing jobsite conditions.

Excavation, Compaction and Grading also accounts for 8 questions. Candidates should understand how proper earthwork supports the pipeline installation from trench preparation through backfill and surface restoration.

Welding, Paving, and Nevada One Call complete the examination outline and reflect the broad field responsibilities associated with underground pipeline and conduit work.

State Requirements

Nevada classifies A-19 Pipeline and Conduits within General Engineering contracting.

The scope includes the fabrication and installation of electrical conduits and pipelines, together with trenching, boring, shoring, backfilling, compacting, paving, surfacing, and application of protective coatings necessary to complete the installation.

The classification also encompasses work associated with water pipelines, gas pipelines, and underground electrical conduits. This broad scope explains why the A-19 examination includes several different construction disciplines rather than focusing only on one type of pipe.

Pipeline and conduit work requires coordination between earthwork, pipe installation, joining, safety, underground utility awareness, and surface restoration. Candidates should understand how each part of the process affects the final installation.

Excavation is especially important because pipelines and conduits are installed underground. Contractors must understand trench preparation, safe access, shoring concepts, bedding, backfill, and compaction along with the technical requirements of the pipe system itself.

Surface restoration is also part of the classification. When trenching disturbs pavement or other finished surfaces, the work may require proper compaction and paving before the project is complete.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada A-19 preparation plan should combine broad pipeline and conduit knowledge with efficient use of the NFPA 30 reference.

Gas Piping should receive substantial attention because it accounts for 15 questions. Candidates should understand the basic construction sequence for underground gas piping, including trench preparation, materials, fittings, joining, protection, and installation considerations.

Candidates should also understand how gas-piping work differs from water-piping work. The system purpose, materials, joining methods, testing considerations, and protective requirements can vary depending on the type of pipeline involved.

Water Piping also accounts for 15 questions. Preparation should include pipe materials, fittings, trench conditions, bedding, joining, backfilling, compaction, and installation practices.

Water-pipeline work can involve different types of pipe and fittings. Candidates should understand how installation requirements change depending on the material and site conditions.

Excavation, Compaction and Grading should be studied as part of the pipeline installation process rather than a separate subject. A successful underground installation begins with proper site preparation and continues through trenching, bedding, backfill, compaction, and final grade.

Improper earthwork can affect the performance of the installed pipeline. Poor bedding, inadequate backfill, or insufficient compaction can contribute to settlement, movement, or damage.

Safety should also receive significant study time. Pipeline construction can involve trenches, excavation equipment, traffic, buried utilities, suspended loads, tools, welding, and other hazards.

Candidates should understand the importance of hazard recognition, personal protective equipment, safe access, excavation protection, and communication around heavy equipment and active work zones.

Welding accounts for 6 questions. Candidates should understand pipe preparation, alignment, welding procedures, equipment, workmanship, and the purpose of producing sound pipeline connections.

Paving accounts for 5 questions. Pipeline and conduit projects frequently disturb streets, driveways, or other paved areas. Candidates should understand the general sequence of restoring excavated areas through backfill, compaction, base preparation, and asphalt placement.

Nevada One Call accounts for 3 questions. Underground utility awareness is a critical part of excavation work. Candidates should understand the importance of notification and utility marking before trenching or boring begins.

The NFPA 30 reference should be used throughout study sessions so candidates become comfortable with its physical organization. The more familiar the book becomes, the less time may be needed for lookups during the exam.

More on test Information and Study Materials

One useful exercise is to create a list of common NFPA 30 topics and practice locating each one repeatedly. Candidates can track how long each search takes and focus additional practice on the slowest areas.

Practice questions should also be combined with code navigation. Attempt a question first from trade knowledge, decide whether NFPA 30 is needed, and then use the reference only when a code lookup adds value.

The method helps develop judgment. Some questions can be answered from technical understanding, while others may require confirmation of a specific code provision.

Candidates should also practice managing time. With 60 questions in two hours, spending excessive time on one reference search can affect the entire examination. Move past difficult questions when necessary and return later.

A project-sequence study method can help organize the broader A-19 material. Begin with Nevada One Call and site planning, move into excavation and trench protection, then review pipe materials, joining and welding, protection, backfill, compaction, and paving.

The sequence reflects the way underground pipeline and conduit projects progress in the field and can make the exam categories easier to connect.

Because this package contains only the exam-room-approved reference, candidates should make sure their broader study plan also covers the trade knowledge needed for gas piping, water piping, excavation, safety, welding, paving, and utility notification.

How does the Pipelines and Conduits Contractor (A-19) exam work?

The Pipelines and Conduits Contractor (A-19) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
