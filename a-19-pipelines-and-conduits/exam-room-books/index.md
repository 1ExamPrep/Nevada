## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada A-19 preparation plan should combine broad pipeline and conduit knowledge with efficient use of the NFPA 30 reference.

**Gas Piping** should receive substantial attention because it accounts for 15 questions. Candidates should understand the basic construction sequence for underground gas piping, including trench preparation, materials, fittings, joining, protection, and installation considerations.

Candidates should also understand how gas-piping work differs from water-piping work. The system purpose, materials, joining methods, testing considerations, and protective requirements can vary depending on the type of pipeline involved.

**Water Piping** also accounts for 15 questions. Preparation should include pipe materials, fittings, trench conditions, bedding, joining, backfilling, compaction, and installation practices.

Water-pipeline work can involve different types of pipe and fittings. Candidates should understand how installation requirements change depending on the material and site conditions.

**Excavation, Compaction and Grading** should be studied as part of the pipeline installation process rather than a separate subject. A successful underground installation begins with proper site preparation and continues through trenching, bedding, backfill, compaction, and final grade.

Improper earthwork can affect the performance of the installed pipeline. Poor bedding, inadequate backfill, or insufficient compaction can contribute to settlement, movement, or damage.

**Safety** should also receive significant study time. Pipeline construction can involve trenches, excavation equipment, traffic, buried utilities, suspended loads, tools, welding, and other hazards.

Candidates should understand the importance of hazard recognition, personal protective equipment, safe access, excavation protection, and communication around heavy equipment and active work zones.

**Welding** accounts for 6 questions. Candidates should understand pipe preparation, alignment, welding procedures, equipment, workmanship, and the purpose of producing sound pipeline connections.

**Paving** accounts for 5 questions. Pipeline and conduit projects frequently disturb streets, driveways, or other paved areas. Candidates should understand the general sequence of restoring excavated areas through backfill, compaction, base preparation, and asphalt placement.

**Nevada One Call** accounts for 3 questions. Underground utility awareness is a critical part of excavation work. Candidates should understand the importance of notification and utility marking before trenching or boring begins.

The **NFPA 30** reference should be used throughout study sessions so candidates become comfortable with its physical organization. The more familiar the book becomes, the less time may be needed for lookups during the exam.

## How does the Pipelines and Conduits Contractor (A-19) exam work?

The Pipelines and Conduits Contractor (A-19) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
