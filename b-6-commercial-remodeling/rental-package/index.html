Exam Details

The Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling Contractor trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates authorized to test through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling Contractor
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 80
  • Time Allowed: 3 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 56 correct answers

The examination is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Concrete: 4 questions
  • Masonry: 4 questions
  • Metals: 3 questions
  • Carpentry: 7 questions
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection: 10 questions
  • Doors and Windows: 5 questions
  • Drywall and Finishes: 17 questions
  • Tile: 10 questions
  • Safety: 10 questions
  • Plan Reading and Estimating: 10 questions

Drywall and Finishes is the largest individual exam category with 17 questions. Candidates should review gypsum systems, wall and ceiling assemblies, finishing methods, materials, installation practices, and common commercial interior applications.

Thermal and Moisture Protection, Tile, Safety, and Plan Reading and Estimating each account for 10 questions. These categories represent a major portion of the examination and should receive significant study time.

Carpentry accounts for seven questions, while Doors and Windows accounts for five. Concrete and Masonry each contain four questions, and Metals contains three.

The broad exam structure makes organized preparation especially useful. Candidates should divide their study time according to exam weighting while still reviewing all categories.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the B-6 Commercial Remodeling classification. Passing the B-6 trade examination is one part of the complete contractor licensing process.

  1. Confirm the B-6 classification. Determine that Commercial Remodeling matches the type of contracting work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. Designate the person who will satisfy applicable experience and examination requirements.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare records describing relevant commercial remodeling work, project responsibilities, supervision, materials, and trades.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor license application. Gather business information, qualifier information, experience documentation, financial information, and required supporting materials.
  5. Submit the application. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the applicant's qualifications before examination eligibility is established.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow required disclosure, fingerprint, and background procedures.
  7. Meet financial responsibility requirements. Provide applicable financial information required during the Nevada licensing process.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Candidates who must test can proceed with scheduling after authorization.
  9. Schedule the B-6 examination through PSI. Arrange the Commercial Remodeling trade examination after eligibility has been established.
  10. Prepare for the trade exam. Study drywall, finishes, tile, moisture protection, safety, plan reading, estimating, carpentry, concrete, masonry, metals, doors, windows, and code navigation.
  11. Prepare for Nevada contractor management requirements. Use the included Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada as part of business and law preparation.
  12. Complete required examinations. Satisfy applicable trade and management testing requirements unless an approved waiver applies.
  13. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Address applicable bonding, financial responsibility, background, and other Nevada State Contractors Board requirements.

State Requirements

The Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling subclassification authorizes remodeling and improvement of qualifying interior spaces in structures that support, shelter, or enclose persons, animals, or other property.

The classification includes important limitations. A B-6 contractor is not authorized under this classification to install, remove, or modify structural members, mechanical systems, fire protection systems, or other life-safety systems.

The limitations matter in both exam preparation and actual contracting work. Commercial remodeling can involve many interior trades, but work that crosses into structural or life-safety systems may require an appropriately licensed contractor or specialty trade.

Typical knowledge areas include drywall, finishes, tile, moisture protection, doors, windows, carpentry, metals, concrete, masonry, plan reading, and estimating. Candidates should understand how these materials and systems interact within a commercial remodeling project.

For the standard Nevada licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document at least four years of qualifying experience within the applicable experience period required by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

Passing the B-6 trade examination does not automatically issue a Nevada contractor license. Applicants must also satisfy applicable experience, management qualification, financial responsibility, bonding, background, and other licensing requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive B-6 study plan should follow the examination weighting. Drywall and Finishes deserves significant attention because it is the largest section, while Tile, Safety, Thermal and Moisture Protection, and Plan Reading and Estimating are also major categories.

Prioritize drywall and finishes. Review gypsum systems, interior walls and ceilings, finishing methods, materials, and installation practices.

Study tile installation. Review substrates, materials, setting systems, layout, joint considerations, and ceramic, glass, and stone tile methods.

Prepare for safety. Use the included OSHA reference to study personal protective equipment, fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, tools, and common construction hazards.

Practice plan reading and estimating. Candidates should be comfortable interpreting plans, dimensions, quantities, materials, and basic estimating information.

Review concrete and masonry. Use the study-only technical references to build knowledge while practicing navigation in the approved ACI exam-room references.

Practice code navigation. Become familiar with the highlighted and tabbed International Building Code, ACI 318-14, and ACI 530/530.1-13 so the organization of each reference is familiar before exam day.

Use timed practice. With 80 questions and three hours available, candidates should develop a steady pace and avoid spending excessive time on individual reference lookups.

Use your 6 months of course access strategically. Organize study by exam category, review weaker areas, practice reference navigation, and return to difficult subjects throughout the preparation period.

How does the Commercial Remodeling Contractor (B-6) exam work?

The Commercial Remodeling Contractor (B-6) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
