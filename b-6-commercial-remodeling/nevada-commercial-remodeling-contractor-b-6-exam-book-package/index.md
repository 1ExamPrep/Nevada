## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong B-6 preparation strategy should reflect the examination weighting. Drywall and Finishes is the largest individual section, while Thermal and Moisture Protection, Tile, Safety, and Plan Reading and Estimating each contain 10 questions.

**Prioritize drywall and finishes.** Review gypsum board systems, interior assemblies, installation, finishing techniques, materials, and common commercial interior construction practices.

**Study tile installation.** Review substrate preparation, materials, setting methods, layout, installation practices, and ceramic, glass, and stone tile systems.

**Review thermal and moisture protection.** Understand moisture control, waterproofing relationships, insulation concepts, sealants, and other protective systems relevant to interior remodeling.

**Prepare for safety.** Use OSHA during study to reinforce fall protection, personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, tools, electrical hazards, material handling, and common commercial construction safety practices.

**Practice plan reading and estimating.** Candidates should be comfortable interpreting plans, dimensions, quantities, materials, and basic estimating information used in remodeling projects.

**Review carpentry, doors, and windows.** Study framing relationships, interior carpentry, openings, door and window installation concepts, hardware relationships, and finish work.

**Study concrete and masonry.** Practice navigating ACI 318-14 and ACI 530/530.1-13 while using the additional technical references to build practical understanding of concrete and masonry construction.

**Practice building-code navigation.** Become comfortable using the International Building Code, especially for occupancy, egress, interior construction, fire-resistance, accessibility, and other topics connected with commercial remodeling.

**Use timed practice.** Candidates have three hours to answer 80 questions. Efficient code navigation and disciplined pacing are important during a multi-reference examination.

## How does the Commercial Remodeling Contractor (B-6) exam work?

The Commercial Remodeling Contractor (B-6) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
