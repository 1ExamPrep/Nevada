About this Commercial Remodeling Contractor (B-6) package

Prepare for the Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling Contractor examination with an online exam prep course designed around the technical subjects, code references, and trade knowledge used for this Nevada contractor classification. The course helps candidates organize their preparation across commercial remodeling topics including concrete, masonry, metals, carpentry, thermal and moisture protection, doors and windows, drywall and finishes, tile, safety, plan reading, and estimating.

The Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling Contractor classification is regulated by the Nevada State Contractors Board. The classification focuses on the remodeling and improvement of interior spaces in commercial and other qualifying structures. Its scope includes interior remodeling work in structures that support, shelter, or enclose persons, animals, or other property, including taller structures and certain buildings connected to structures exceeding three stories.

The B-6 classification has important scope limitations. It does not authorize the license holder to install or modify structural members, mechanical systems, fire protection systems, or other life-safety systems. Candidates should understand these limitations as part of preparing for the license and for operating within the authority of the classification.

The exam-preparation references include the International Building Code, 2012; ACI 318-14: Building Code Requirements for Structural Concrete and Commentary; and ACI 530/530.1-13: Building Code Requirements and Specification for Masonry Structures and Companion Commentaries. These are the principal code references permitted in the current B-6 trade examination room.

Additional study references include Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA); Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th Edition; Modern Masonry - Brick, Block, Stone by Clois E. Kicklighter, 10th Edition; ANSI A108/A118/A136.1:2017, American National Standard Specifications for the Installation of Ceramic Tile; Builders Guide to Floors, 1997; Setting Tile, 1995, supplied as a used reference; Handbook for Ceramic, Glass, and Stone Tile Installation, 2017; and The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction, 4th Edition.

The study-only references are not allowed into the B-6 trade examination room. Candidates should use them to build technical understanding before test day while practicing efficient lookup skills with the code books that are allowed during the examination.

Exam Details

The Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling Contractor examination is administered through PSI for applicants who have received examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling Contractor
  • Number of Questions: 80
  • Time Allowed: 3 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 56 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The current examination content outline contains the following subject areas:

  • Concrete: 4 questions
  • Masonry: 4 questions
  • Metals: 3 questions
  • Carpentry: 7 questions
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection: 10 questions
  • Doors and Windows: 5 questions
  • Drywall and Finishes: 17 questions
  • Tile: 10 questions
  • Safety: 10 questions
  • Plan Reading and Estimating: 10 questions

Drywall and Finishes is the largest individual examination category with 17 questions. Candidates should devote significant preparation time to gypsum wallboard, finishing systems, assemblies, installation methods, partitions, ceilings, surface preparation, and related commercial interior finishing concepts.

Thermal and Moisture Protection, Tile, Safety, and Plan Reading and Estimating each contain 10 questions. Together, these subjects account for a large portion of the examination and should be treated as major study areas rather than secondary topics.

Tile preparation can include substrate preparation, installation methods, setting materials, grout, layout, tolerances, ceramic tile standards, and industry installation practices. The ANSI tile standards, tile installation handbook, and Setting Tile reference help support study of these subjects.

Plan Reading and Estimating preparation should include interpreting construction drawings, identifying dimensions and materials, understanding symbols and details, performing quantity calculations, and evaluating project information commonly used in commercial remodeling.

Safety also contains 10 questions. Candidates should prepare for OSHA-related construction topics involving personal protective equipment, fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, tools, electrical hazards, material handling, and other jobsite safety requirements.

Carpentry contains seven questions, while Doors and Windows contains five. Concrete and Masonry each contain four questions, and Metals contains three. Although these categories are smaller, they remain important because every correct answer contributes toward the required score of 56.

Open Book Test

The Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling Contractor trade examination is an open-book test, but only specific code references are approved for use inside the examination center.

The approved exam-room references are:

  • International Building Code, 2012
  • ACI 318-11 or ACI 318-14: Building Code Requirements for Structural Concrete and Commentary
  • ACI 530/530.1-11 or ACI 530/530.1-13: Building Code Requirements and Specification for Masonry Structures and Companion Commentaries

The package study list uses ACI 318-14 and ACI 530/530.1-13, both of which fall within the approved editions for the current B-6 examination.

Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved references to the testing center. References may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Temporary tabs such as Post-it notes are not allowed.

Approved books may not contain candidate-added writing before or during the examination. Loose papers and additional attached materials are also prohibited. Candidates should prepare their exam-room references in advance and become comfortable navigating them before test day.

The International Building Code is especially important for commercial remodeling code questions. Candidates should practice locating provisions involving occupancy, construction types, interior finishes, means of egress, accessibility-related building provisions, fire-resistance concepts, materials, and other code topics relevant to commercial interiors.

ACI 318 supports structural concrete code knowledge, while ACI 530/530.1 supports masonry-related code requirements. Even though the B-6 scope does not authorize modifying structural members, the examination still includes concrete and masonry questions that require familiarity with applicable code concepts.

The remaining study references are not allowed into the trade examination room. These materials should therefore be used for knowledge-building and recall rather than exam-day lookup.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the B-6 Commercial Remodeling classification. Applicants must satisfy the applicable experience, application, examination, financial, background, and final licensing requirements before a license can be issued.

  1. Confirm the B-6 classification. Determine that Commercial Remodeling matches the type of interior remodeling work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor applicant must designate a person who meets the experience and examination qualifications for the B-6 classification.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare work-experience documentation showing relevant commercial remodeling and construction experience within the required period.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor application. Submit the required business, qualifier, financial, and supporting information to the Nevada State Contractors Board.
  5. Complete applicable fingerprint and background requirements. Follow the Board's required background procedures for the individuals identified in the application.
  6. Provide required financial information. Nevada evaluates financial responsibility and uses applicable financial documentation when establishing the monetary limit for the license.
  7. Receive examination eligibility. After the Board reviews the applicant's qualifications, approved candidates may proceed with testing.
  8. Schedule the B-6 examination through PSI. Use the examination eligibility information to arrange the trade examination.
  9. Prepare for the B-6 trade examination. Study drywall and finishes, tile, safety, thermal and moisture protection, estimating, carpentry, concrete, masonry, metals, doors and windows, and approved code navigation.
  10. Complete the Contractor Management Survey requirement. Nevada contractor applicants generally must also pass the Business and Law examination unless an applicable waiver applies.
  11. Complete final licensing requirements. Satisfy the bond, financial, and other requirements established by the Nevada State Contractors Board before license issuance.

State Requirements

The Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling classification is limited to remodeling and improvement of interior spaces. Its scope includes qualifying commercial structures, including structures exceeding three stories and certain buildings of fewer than three stories that are connected to taller structures.

The classification specifically does not authorize a B-6 contractor to install or modify structural members, mechanical systems, fire protection systems, or other life-safety systems. Contractors must remain within the legal scope of the license and use appropriately licensed specialty contractors when work falls outside B-6 authority.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual must document a minimum of four years of relevant work experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the specific classification requested.

The required experience must have been obtained within the 15 years immediately preceding the filing of the application. The total documented work experience must equal at least four full years, or 1,460 days.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes financial-responsibility requirements. The Nevada State Contractors Board considers financial information when determining the monetary limit assigned to a contractor license.

A contractor bond is required before license issuance. The Board determines the bond amount as part of the approval process based on factors including the type of license, monetary limit, financial responsibility, experience, and other licensing considerations.

The Nevada contractor license is issued to the business entity, while the qualifying individual is the person who satisfies the required experience and examination qualifications and performs the duties assigned to the qualifier under Nevada contractor law.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada B-6 study plan should reflect the exam weighting instead of giving every subject the same amount of time. Drywall and Finishes is the largest category, followed by several 10-question areas that together make up a substantial portion of the test.

Prioritize Drywall and Finishes. With 17 questions, this category deserves the largest share of focused study. Review gypsum systems, partitions, ceilings, finishing methods, installation practices, surface preparation, and commercial interior finish concepts.

Study Tile as a major category. Tile represents 10 questions. Use the ANSI standards, tile handbook, and Setting Tile to reinforce substrates, setting materials, installation methods, layout, grout, and accepted industry practices.

Prepare thoroughly for Safety. Safety also contains 10 questions. Review OSHA construction requirements for ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, fall protection, tools, material handling, electrical hazards, and general workplace safety.

Practice Plan Reading and Estimating. Ten questions are dedicated to this area. Review dimensions, symbols, plans, quantities, measurements, materials, and estimating techniques associated with commercial remodeling work.

Review Thermal and Moisture Protection. The category contains 10 questions. Study materials and systems used to control water, moisture, air movement, and environmental exposure in commercial interior remodeling applications.

Build strong carpentry knowledge. Carpentry contains seven questions. Review framing, layout, materials, fasteners, doors, trim, floor systems, and common interior construction methods.

Practice code navigation with the approved books. Use the 2012 International Building Code, ACI 318-14, and ACI 530/530.1-13 regularly so their organization becomes familiar before examination day.

Use non-exam-room books for recall. The OSHA, gypsum, masonry, tile, flooring, and concrete references should be studied with the goal of understanding the material rather than locating it during testing.

Practice under timed conditions. Candidates have three hours for 80 questions. Timed practice can help develop a steady pace and improve decisions about when a code lookup is worthwhile.
