Exam Details

The Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling Contractor examination is administered through PSI for candidates authorized to test through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling Contractor
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 80
  • Time Allowed: 3 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 56 correct answers

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Concrete: 4 questions
  • Masonry: 4 questions
  • Metals: 3 questions
  • Carpentry: 7 questions
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection: 10 questions
  • Doors and Windows: 5 questions
  • Drywall and Finishes: 17 questions
  • Tile: 10 questions
  • Safety: 10 questions
  • Plan Reading and Estimating: 10 questions

Drywall and Finishes is the largest individual category with 17 questions. Candidates should understand gypsum systems, wall and ceiling assemblies, materials, installation practices, finishing methods, and common commercial interior construction concepts.

Thermal and Moisture Protection, Tile, Safety, and Plan Reading and Estimating each account for 10 questions. Together, these categories represent a large share of the B-6 examination and should receive substantial attention during preparation.

Carpentry accounts for seven questions and can involve framing relationships, interior carpentry, openings, finish work, and other remodeling concepts. Doors and Windows accounts for five questions, while Concrete and Masonry each contain four questions.

Metals accounts for three questions. Although it is the smallest individual section, candidates should still review it because every correct answer contributes toward the required passing score.

ACI 318-14 supports concrete-related questions involving structural concrete, reinforcement, materials, construction requirements, and related technical provisions. Candidates should become familiar with its layout and major subject divisions before test day.

ACI 530/530.1-13 supports masonry-related questions involving materials, reinforcement, structural masonry requirements, specifications, and construction provisions.

The highlighting and tabs are intended to make the books easier to navigate, but candidates should still study the references in advance. A tab is most useful when the candidate already understands what subject it points to and when that reference is likely to apply.

A productive open-book strategy starts by identifying the type of question. A commercial building-code question may require the International Building Code, a concrete question may require ACI 318-14, and a masonry question may require ACI 530/530.1-13. Choosing the correct book first can save valuable examination time.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the B-6 Commercial Remodeling classification. Passing the B-6 trade examination is one part of the overall Nevada contractor licensing process.

  1. Confirm the B-6 classification. Determine that Commercial Remodeling matches the work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. Designate the person who will satisfy the applicable experience and examination requirements.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare records describing relevant commercial remodeling work, responsibilities, project types, materials, trades, and supervision experience.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor license application. Gather business information, qualifier information, experience documentation, financial information, and other required materials.
  5. Submit the application. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the applicant's qualifications before examination eligibility is established.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow required disclosure, fingerprint, and background procedures.
  7. Meet financial responsibility requirements. Provide the applicable financial information required during the licensing process.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Candidates required to test can proceed with scheduling after authorization.
  9. Schedule the B-6 examination through PSI. Arrange the Commercial Remodeling trade examination after eligibility has been established.
  10. Prepare for the trade examination. Review drywall, finishes, tile, moisture protection, safety, plan reading, estimating, carpentry, concrete, masonry, metals, doors, windows, and code navigation.
  11. Complete applicable management qualification requirements. Nevada contractor licensing includes management qualification in addition to the trade examination.
  12. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Satisfy applicable financial, bonding, background, and other Nevada State Contractors Board requirements before license issuance.

State Requirements

The Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling subclassification authorizes remodeling and improvement of qualifying interior spaces in structures that support, shelter, or enclose persons, animals, or other property.

The classification includes important limitations. A B-6 contractor is not authorized under this classification to install, remove, or modify structural members, mechanical systems, fire protection systems, or other life-safety systems.

The limitations are important for both exam preparation and licensed practice. Commercial remodeling projects frequently involve multiple construction disciplines, and candidates should understand where the B-6 scope ends and where other appropriately licensed trades may be required.

Typical knowledge areas for the classification include drywall, finishes, tile, moisture protection, carpentry, doors, windows, metals, concrete, masonry, plan reading, and estimating. Candidates should understand how these materials and systems are used within commercial interior remodeling projects.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document at least four years of qualifying experience within the applicable experience period required by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

Applicants should maintain organized documentation describing the commercial remodeling work performed, responsibilities held, projects completed, supervision experience, and the time period during which qualifying work was obtained.

Passing the B-6 trade examination alone does not automatically issue a Nevada contractor license. Applicants must also satisfy applicable experience, management qualification, financial responsibility, bonding, background, and other licensing requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong B-6 preparation strategy should balance trade knowledge with efficient use of the highlighted and tabbed references. The exam includes many subjects that cannot be answered solely through reference lookup, so candidates should prepare broadly while learning how to use the approved books effectively.

Prioritize drywall and finishes. Prioritize drywall and finishes is the largest examination category. Review gypsum products, wall and ceiling assemblies, installation practices, finishing methods, materials, and common commercial interior applications.

Review thermal and moisture protection. Study insulation relationships, waterproofing, sealants, moisture control, and other protective systems used in commercial remodeling.

Study tile. Understand substrate preparation, setting materials, layout, installation practices, and finish considerations for ceramic, glass, and stone tile systems.

Prepare for safety. Candidates should understand construction hazards, fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, tools, and material handling even though OSHA is not one of the exam-room-approved B-6 references.

Practice plan reading and estimating. Candidates should be comfortable reading plans, interpreting dimensions, calculating quantities, identifying materials, and working through basic estimating information.

Review carpentry, doors, and windows. Study interior framing relationships, openings, hardware, installation concepts, finish work, and related remodeling practices.

Use the International Building Code throughout preparation. Become familiar with topics such as occupancy, egress, interior finishes, accessibility, construction types, and fire-resistance.

Practice with ACI 318-14. Use the tabs and highlighted sections to build familiarity with concrete-related requirements and learn where key subjects are located.

Practice with ACI 530/530.1-13. Become comfortable finding masonry materials, reinforcement, structural requirements, and specification provisions.

Use timed reference exercises. With 80 questions and three hours available, candidates should practice identifying the correct reference and locating the applicable material without spending too much time on a single lookup.

Learn the tabs before test day. The best time to become familiar with highlighted sections and tab locations is during preparation, not during the examination. Repeated use builds familiarity and can make navigation more natural.

How does the Commercial Remodeling Contractor (B-6) exam work?

The Commercial Remodeling Contractor (B-6) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
