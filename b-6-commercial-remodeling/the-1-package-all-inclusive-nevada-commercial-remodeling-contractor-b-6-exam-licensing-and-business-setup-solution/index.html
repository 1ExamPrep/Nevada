Exam Details

The Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling Contractor trade examination is administered through PSI for applicants who receive examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling Contractor
  • Number of Questions: 80
  • Time Allowed: 3 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 56 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The B-6 examination covers the following major subject areas:

  • Concrete: 4 questions
  • Masonry: 4 questions
  • Metals: 3 questions
  • Carpentry: 7 questions
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection: 10 questions
  • Doors and Windows: 5 questions
  • Drywall and Finishes: 17 questions
  • Tile: 10 questions
  • Safety: 10 questions
  • Plan Reading and Estimating: 10 questions

Drywall and Finishes is the largest individual category, with 17 questions. Candidates should devote substantial study time to gypsum wallboard, commercial partitions, ceilings, assemblies, finishing methods, installation practices, and related interior construction concepts.

Thermal and Moisture Protection, Tile, Safety, and Plan Reading and Estimating each contain 10 questions. Together these four categories account for half of the examination and should be treated as major preparation areas.

Tile study can include substrates, setting materials, mortar, grout, layout, tolerances, installation procedures, and accepted ceramic, glass, and stone tile practices. The ANSI tile standards, Handbook for Ceramic, Glass, and Stone Tile Installation, and Setting Tile provide focused preparation for this work.

Plan Reading and Estimating should include drawing interpretation, measurements, quantities, material takeoffs, dimensions, details, symbols, and project information used in commercial remodeling. Candidates should be comfortable moving between drawings and construction concepts while working under time limits.

Concrete, Masonry, Metals, Carpentry, and Doors and Windows contribute the remaining questions. These areas should not be neglected because correct answers across the smaller categories can make an important difference in reaching the required score.

Open Book Test

The Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling Contractor trade examination is open book, but only approved references may be brought into the examination room.

The principal approved exam-room references included with this package are:

  • International Building Code, 2012
  • ACI 318-14: Building Code Requirements for Structural Concrete and Commentary
  • ACI 530/530.1-13: Building Code Requirements and Specification for Masonry Structures and Companion Commentaries

Candidates should practice using these books before test day. Efficient navigation of the International Building Code can support questions involving construction requirements, interior finishes, building materials, means of egress, and other code-related commercial remodeling subjects.

ACI 318-14 supports concrete-code preparation, while ACI 530/530.1-13 supports masonry-code preparation. Although concrete and masonry are smaller examination categories, candidates should still know how to locate relevant provisions efficiently when necessary.

The following books are study references and are not allowed into the B-6 trade examination: Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA); Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th Edition; Modern Masonry - Brick, Block, Stone; ANSI A108/A118/A136.1:2017; Builders Guide to Floors; Setting Tile; Handbook for Ceramic, Glass, and Stone Tile Installation; and The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction.

The study-only books should be used to build knowledge before exam day. Candidates should understand drywall systems, tile installation, practical masonry, flooring, concrete construction, and OSHA safety well enough to answer trade-knowledge questions without consulting those references during testing.

State Requirements

The Nevada B-6 Commercial Remodeling classification applies to qualifying commercial interior remodeling and improvement work. The classification is designed for work involving interior spaces in structures covered by Nevada's B-6 scope.

The B-6 classification does not authorize the contractor to install or modify structural members, mechanical systems, fire protection systems, or other life-safety systems. Work outside the B-6 scope must be performed under the appropriate contractor classification.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must document at least four years of relevant experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the requested classification.

The qualifying experience must generally fall within the 15 years immediately preceding the application. Applicants should maintain organized experience documentation demonstrating their work within the commercial remodeling trade.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes financial-responsibility requirements. Financial information is considered when establishing the monetary limit under which the licensed contractor may operate.

A contractor bond is required before final license issuance. The applicable bond amount is established during the licensing process. Contractors must also maintain the legal and administrative requirements associated with their Nevada business and contractor license.

How does the Commercial Remodeling Contractor (B-6) exam work?

The Commercial Remodeling Contractor (B-6) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • Nevada accepts a passing NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the B trade exam, and since March 2026 issues a B-7 Restricted License for jobs up to $7,000.
