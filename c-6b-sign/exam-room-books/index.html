State Requirements

Nevada identifies C-6B as a specialty sign contractor subclassification focused on electrical sign work.

The scope includes the fabrication, installation, and erection of electrical signs and the installation of related wiring.

Candidates should understand both the physical construction of sign assemblies and the electrical systems used to power or illuminate them. This combination makes the C-6B examination broader than a purely electrical or purely fabrication-based test.

Electrical preparation should include sign wiring, branch circuits, grounding, bonding, overcurrent protection, disconnecting means, conductors, and other requirements that can apply to electrical signs and outline lighting.

Sign-code preparation should include installation, support, placement, structural relationships, and other requirements associated with complete sign assemblies.

Practical preparation should also include neon systems, sheet metal fabrication, and jobsite safety because those subjects appear on the examination even though the corresponding study books are not part of this books-allowed-into-exam package.

The C-6B trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, financial, business, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-6B preparation plan should begin with the two books included in this package because they account for 28 of the 50 examination questions.

The Uniform Sign Code represents 15 questions and is the largest single category. Candidates should learn where to find requirements involving sign construction, installation, supports, location, structural relationships, and other sign-specific provisions.

The National Electrical Code represents 13 questions. Candidates should review sign and outline lighting requirements as well as general electrical provisions that can apply to electrical sign systems.

Do not limit NEC preparation to one sign-specific article. Sign installations may involve branch circuits, conductors, wiring methods, grounding, bonding, overcurrent protection, disconnects, and electrical equipment covered elsewhere in the code.

Electrical sign circuits should receive focused review. Candidates should understand how power reaches the sign, how conductors are protected, and how electrical equipment connects to the overall installation.

Grounding and bonding are also important because sign assemblies may include metal components and electrical equipment that must be properly integrated into the electrical system.

Disconnecting means should be included in study because electrical sign systems require a safe method of disconnecting power for service and maintenance.

Sign and outline lighting provisions should be reviewed carefully. Candidates should understand the relationship between specialized sign requirements and broader NEC rules.

For the Uniform Sign Code, begin by learning the organization of the book. Use the table of contents and index to locate sign types, construction requirements, support conditions, placement rules, and other relevant provisions.

Definitions matter in both books. A question may use terminology with a specific code meaning, and checking the definition can help determine which section applies.

Tables should be reviewed carefully. Important requirements may be contained in tables rather than repeated in paragraph text.

Exceptions can change how a general requirement applies. Candidates should read enough surrounding text to identify exceptions, limitations, or special conditions before selecting an answer.

Cross-references can lead to additional requirements in another article, section, or chapter. Practice following these references instead of assuming the first section contains the complete answer.

Timed navigation drills can help improve open-book performance. Select a topic and locate the applicable requirement in either the NEC or Uniform Sign Code under a set time limit.

More on test Information and Study Materials

A useful drill is to identify the correct book before opening either reference. Ask whether the question is primarily electrical or primarily related to sign construction and placement. This simple step can reduce unnecessary searching.

Because the exam includes 10 Neon questions, candidates should also develop retained knowledge of neon sign systems. Study neon tubing, transformers, electrodes, fabrication concepts, electrical relationships, installation practices, and common terminology.

The 6 Sheet Metal questions require practical knowledge of metal fabrication. Candidates should understand measurement, layout, cutting, bending, forming, joining, fastening, and common tools associated with sheet metal work.

The 6 Safety questions require retained knowledge of construction safety. Review electrical hazards, fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, power tools, material handling, and general jobsite safety practices.

Because this package contains only the exam-room-approved references, candidates should make sure their broader preparation covers neon, sheet metal, and safety through other study resources and trade experience.

A balanced study schedule can divide preparation into five areas matching the examination outline: National Electrical Code, Uniform Sign Code, Neon, Sheet Metal, and Safety.

Begin by studying each subject separately. This makes it easier to identify weak areas and build confidence before moving into mixed practice.

Once individual topics become familiar, use mixed practice questions that shift among electrical code, sign code, neon, fabrication, and safety.

When a practice question is missed, determine whether the problem was lack of trade knowledge, slow reference navigation, misunderstanding of a code term, or difficulty interpreting the question.

Correcting the underlying issue is more useful than memorizing a single answer. This builds stronger preparation for unfamiliar questions on test day.

Time management should be practiced regularly. With 50 questions in two hours, candidates should avoid spending too long on one difficult lookup while easier questions remain unanswered.

A practical approach is to answer familiar questions first, use the NEC or Uniform Sign Code for targeted confirmation when necessary, mark difficult items, and return to them after completing faster questions.

How does the Sign Contractor (C-6B) exam work?

The Sign Contractor (C-6B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
