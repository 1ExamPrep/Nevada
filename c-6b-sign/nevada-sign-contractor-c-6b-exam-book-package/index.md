## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-6B preparation plan should begin with the **Uniform Sign Code** because it represents 15 of the 50 examination questions. Become familiar with the organization of the code and practice locating requirements involving sign construction, support, placement, installation, and related conditions.

The **National Electrical Code** should receive equally serious attention. With 13 questions assigned to the NEC, candidates should be comfortable navigating electrical requirements efficiently.

Review the NEC provisions associated with **electric signs and outline lighting**, but do not limit preparation to one article. Electrical sign questions can also involve general wiring methods, grounding and bonding, branch circuits, overcurrent protection, disconnecting means, conductors, and equipment.

Use the NEC index and table of contents regularly. When a question presents a specific electrical term, identify that term first and then locate the most likely article or section.

**Definitions** can be important in both code books. A question may depend on the exact meaning of a term such as a sign component, electrical device, support condition, or installation method.

**Neon** represents 10 questions and should receive a dedicated portion of the study schedule. Review neon tubing, electrodes, transformers, electrical relationships, fabrication, installation, troubleshooting concepts, and terminology associated with neon sign systems.

Because Neon Techniques is not allowed into the exam room, candidates should use active recall. Read a topic, close the book, and explain the components or procedure from memory before checking the reference again.

**Sheet Metal** accounts for 6 questions. Sign contractors should understand practical fabrication concepts involving layout, measuring, cutting, forming, bending, joining, and fastening sheet metal.

Review the tools used for different metalworking tasks and understand how fabricated sheet metal components can be incorporated into sign cabinets, housings, panels, enclosures, or supports.

**Safety** also accounts for 6 questions. Sign work can involve electrical hazards, elevated work, ladders, scaffolds, lifting, cutting tools, sharp materials, power tools, and installation around traffic or other jobsite conditions.

Use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 to build retained safety knowledge. Since the OSHA book is not available during the trade examination, candidates should practice recognizing common hazards and appropriate protective measures without relying on reference lookup.

A useful preparation structure is to divide study into five blocks matching the examination outline: National Electrical Code, Uniform Sign Code, Neon, Sheet Metal, and Safety.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

Begin with isolated study sessions so weak subjects are easier to identify. Later, transition into mixed practice where questions shift among code navigation, neon, fabrication, and safety.

Timed code-navigation drills can be especially valuable for the NEC and Uniform Sign Code. Select a subject, identify which reference should contain the answer, and locate the applicable provision within a limited amount of time.

The goal is not to memorize every section of either code. Instead, candidates should understand how the books are organized and how to narrow a search quickly.

Tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references should be read carefully. The complete answer may depend on information located beyond the first sentence of a section.

For practical sign questions, connect the technical knowledge to the full installation process. Think about fabrication, electrical components, assembly, support, erection, wiring, testing, and final completion of the sign.

When studying neon systems, connect components to the electrical requirements that apply to complete sign installations. This helps combine trade knowledge with code preparation rather than treating the subjects as separate.

When studying sheet metal, think about how a fabricated cabinet or enclosure must work with electrical components, sign faces, supports, and field installation conditions.

Time management should be incorporated into practice. With 50 questions in two hours, candidates should avoid spending too much time on one difficult code lookup while easier questions remain unanswered.

A practical approach is to answer familiar trade questions efficiently, use the NEC or Uniform Sign Code for targeted confirmation, flag difficult items, and return to them after completing questions that can be answered more quickly.

## How does the Sign Contractor (C-6B) exam work?

The Sign Contractor (C-6B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
