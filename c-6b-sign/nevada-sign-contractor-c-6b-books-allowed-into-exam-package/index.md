## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-6B preparation plan should begin with the two books included in this package because they account for **28 of the 50 examination questions**.

The **Uniform Sign Code** represents 15 questions and is the largest single category. Candidates should learn where to find requirements involving sign construction, installation, supports, location, structural relationships, and other sign-specific provisions.

The **National Electrical Code** represents 13 questions. Candidates should review sign and outline lighting requirements as well as general electrical provisions that can apply to electrical sign systems.

Do not limit NEC preparation to one sign-specific article. Sign installations may involve branch circuits, conductors, wiring methods, grounding, bonding, overcurrent protection, disconnects, and electrical equipment covered elsewhere in the code.

**Electrical sign circuits** should receive focused review. Candidates should understand how power reaches the sign, how conductors are protected, and how electrical equipment connects to the overall installation.

**Grounding and bonding** are also important because sign assemblies may include metal components and electrical equipment that must be properly integrated into the electrical system.

**Disconnecting means** should be included in study because electrical sign systems require a safe method of disconnecting power for service and maintenance.

**Sign and outline lighting** provisions should be reviewed carefully. Candidates should understand the relationship between specialized sign requirements and broader NEC rules.

For the Uniform Sign Code, begin by learning the organization of the book. Use the table of contents and index to locate sign types, construction requirements, support conditions, placement rules, and other relevant provisions.

**Definitions** matter in both books. A question may use terminology with a specific code meaning, and checking the definition can help determine which section applies.

**Tables** should be reviewed carefully. Important requirements may be contained in tables rather than repeated in paragraph text.

**Exceptions** can change how a general requirement applies. Candidates should read enough surrounding text to identify exceptions, limitations, or special conditions before selecting an answer.

**Cross-references** can lead to additional requirements in another article, section, or chapter. Practice following these references instead of assuming the first section contains the complete answer.

Timed navigation drills can help improve open-book performance. Select a topic and locate the applicable requirement in either the NEC or Uniform Sign Code under a set time limit.

## How does the Sign Contractor (C-6B) exam work?

The Sign Contractor (C-6B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
