Exam Details

The Nevada C-2G Photovoltaics trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who receive examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-2G Photovoltaics Contractor
  • Number of Questions: 75
  • Time Allowed: 150 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 56 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The C-2G examination content is divided into seven technical areas:

  • Photovoltaic Theory: 5 questions
  • Electrical Interconnection: 15 questions
  • Permitting, Inspection, and Utility Interconnections: 5 questions
  • Photovoltaic Components: 15 questions
  • Photovoltaic Installation: 10 questions
  • Estimating, Calculations, and Design: 20 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions

Estimating, Calculations, and Design is the largest individual category with 20 questions. Candidates should devote substantial preparation time to electrical calculations, photovoltaic system sizing concepts, conductor-related calculations, equipment relationships, system design principles, and the numerical reasoning required to evaluate solar-electric systems.

Electrical Interconnection contains 15 questions. Preparation should include the electrical relationships between photovoltaic equipment and the electrical system, connection methods, conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, disconnecting means, and applicable National Electrical Code requirements.

Photovoltaic Components also contains 15 questions. Candidates should understand photovoltaic modules, cells, batteries, inverters, conductors, disconnects, overcurrent devices, mounting-related equipment, and other components used in photovoltaic systems.

Photovoltaic Installation contains 10 questions. Candidates should review installation procedures, equipment placement, system configuration, wiring methods, mounting considerations, inspection concerns, and practical procedures involved in photovoltaic work.

Photovoltaic Theory, Permitting, Inspection and Utility Interconnections, and Safety each contain five questions. Although these sections contain fewer questions, they remain important parts of the examination and should be included in a complete study plan.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-2G Photovoltaics Contractor trade examination is an open-book test. The approved exam-room reference included with this package is:

  • NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 Edition

The 2017 National Electrical Code is the primary approved reference used during the C-2G examination. Candidates should become comfortable using the index, Article structure, definitions, tables, cross-references, and photovoltaic-related code requirements before test day.

Effective open-book preparation is not simply about knowing that the NEC is available. Candidates should practice locating relevant requirements efficiently because the examination contains 75 questions and allows 150 minutes. Spending too long searching for individual answers can make pacing difficult.

The following included books are study references and are not allowed into the C-2G examination:

  • Photovoltaic Systems, 3rd Edition, 2011
  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
  • Ugly's Electrical References

Photovoltaic Systems should be used to build knowledge involving solar-electric theory, photovoltaic modules, batteries, inverters, system design, installation, interconnection, and other practical photovoltaic concepts.

OSHA should be used to strengthen construction-safety knowledge involving ladders, fall protection, personal protective equipment, electrical hazards, tools, material handling, and other jobsite considerations.

Ugly's Electrical References can support preparation involving formulas, electrical calculations, conductor information, common electrical relationships, and other practical electrical concepts. Because it cannot be used during the examination, candidates should use it during study to strengthen calculation skills and technical recall.

Licensing Steps

  1. Confirm the C-2G classification. Determine that Photovoltaics matches the type of contracting work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the Trade Qualified Individual. Designate the person who will establish the required photovoltaic trade experience and qualification.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare records demonstrating relevant experience within the requested C-2G classification.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor application. Organize required business, qualifier, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the application and determines examination eligibility.

  7. Provide applicable financial information. Nevada evaluates financial responsibility as part of the licensing process.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Eligible candidates may proceed with the required examinations after Board review.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-2G Photovoltaics classification authorizes the installation, alteration, and repair of photovoltaic cells, batteries, and inverters used in the conversion of solar energy into electricity.

The scope does not authorize the installation, alteration, or repair of electrical wires beyond the service panel. Candidates should understand this boundary when preparing for the classification and when evaluating the type of work a C-2G license authorizes.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual generally must demonstrate at least four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the requested classification.

The qualifying experience generally must have been obtained within the 15 years immediately preceding the filing of the application. Applicants should maintain organized records describing the photovoltaic work performed and the period during which the experience was gained.

Nevada contractor licensing distinguishes between trade and management qualification. One individual may fulfill both roles when the applicable requirements are satisfied, or separate individuals may be designated.

The Trade Qualified Individual establishes the applicable experience and trade qualification for C-2G. The Management Qualified Individual generally satisfies the Nevada Business and Law requirement unless an applicable waiver is approved.

Financial responsibility, background requirements, contractor bonding, and other licensing requirements are separate from the technical examination. Passing the C-2G trade exam alone does not complete the Nevada contractor licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-2G study plan should reflect the examination weighting. Estimating, Calculations, and Design deserves the greatest emphasis, followed by Electrical Interconnection and Photovoltaic Components.

Prioritize Calculations and Design. The category contains 20 questions. Review electrical formulas, system relationships, sizing concepts, conductor considerations, equipment relationships, and photovoltaic design principles.

Study Electrical Interconnection. Review how photovoltaic systems connect electrically, including conductors, protection, grounding, disconnecting means, and applicable NEC requirements.

Know Photovoltaic Components. Study modules, cells, batteries, inverters, conductors, protective equipment, disconnects, and other system components.

Review Installation. Understand equipment placement, system configuration, wiring methods, mounting considerations, and practical photovoltaic installation procedures.

Prepare for Permitting and Utility Interconnections. Review the general relationship among photovoltaic projects, inspection, permitting, and utility interconnection processes represented on the examination.

Study Safety for recall. OSHA cannot be used during testing, so candidates should understand applicable construction and electrical safety concepts before examination day.

Practice NEC navigation. Use the 2017 National Electrical Code regularly throughout preparation. Practice locating photovoltaic and electrical requirements under timed conditions.

Use timed practice. Candidates have 150 minutes for 75 questions. Timed preparation can help develop a steady pace and improve decisions about when an NEC lookup is necessary.

How does the Photovoltaics Contractor (C-2G) exam work?

The Photovoltaics Contractor (C-2G) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
