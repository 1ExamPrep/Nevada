Exam Details

The Nevada C-2G Photovoltaics Contractor examination contains 75 questions. Candidates receive 150 minutes to complete the test and must answer 56 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is organized into the following areas:

  • Photovoltaic Theory: 5 questions
  • Electrical Interconnection: 15 questions
  • Permitting, Inspection, and Utility Interconnections: 5 questions
  • Photovoltaic Components: 15 questions
  • Photovoltaic Installation: 10 questions
  • Estimating, Calculations, and Design: 20 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions

Estimating, Calculations, and Design is the largest exam category with 20 questions. Candidates should be prepared to work with electrical values, system sizing concepts, conductor considerations, photovoltaic array relationships, equipment selection, and other calculations associated with solar-electric systems.

Electrical Interconnection accounts for 15 questions. Candidates should understand how photovoltaic systems connect to electrical equipment and how code requirements affect conductors, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, disconnects, wiring methods, and system interfaces.

Photovoltaic Components also accounts for 15 questions. Preparation should include modules, arrays, inverters, batteries, conductors, disconnecting means, overcurrent devices, mounting systems, and associated electrical equipment.

Photovoltaic Installation accounts for 10 questions and should include practical knowledge of system layout, mounting, wiring, equipment placement, electrical connections, and installation procedures.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-2G Photovoltaics Contractor examination is an open-book test.

The NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 edition is the exam-room-approved reference included in this package. The highlighting and tabs are intended to help candidates navigate the NEC more efficiently during preparation and when using the book during the timed exam.

The other references included in this package are for preparation only. Photovoltaic Systems, 3rd Edition, 2011, Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926, and Ugly's Electrical References are not allowed into the exam room.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need to know the material. With 75 questions in 150 minutes, candidates have an average of about two minutes per question. Searching the NEC from the beginning for every answer can consume too much time.

A strong strategy is to learn the photovoltaic and electrical concepts first, then use the NEC when a specific code requirement needs confirmation. Candidates should become familiar with the structure of the NEC, including chapters, articles, definitions, sections, tables, and the index.

The highlighted and tabbed format can help reduce navigation time. During study, practice using the tabs to reach major subject areas and then locate specific provisions within the code. Over time, repeated navigation can make the physical layout of the NEC more familiar.

  6. Prepare for the C-2G trade exam. Study photovoltaic theory, components, installation, calculations, design, electrical interconnections, permitting, utility interconnections, and safety.

  9. Complete the required examinations. Take the applicable trade and business examinations associated with the license application.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish the additional requirements associated with contractor license issuance.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-2G Photovoltaics classification covers the installation, alteration, and repair of photovoltaic cells, batteries, and inverters used in the conversion of solar energy into electricity.

The classification does not include installation, alteration, or repair of electrical wiring beyond the service panel. Candidates should understand the scope of the classification and the type of electrical work included within photovoltaic contracting.

Photovoltaic work combines solar-energy knowledge with electrical-system knowledge. A contractor must understand how photovoltaic modules produce electricity, how arrays are configured, how batteries and inverters interact with the system, and how the equipment interfaces with electrical wiring and protective devices.

This makes the National Electrical Code particularly important. Candidates should understand code requirements affecting conductors, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, disconnects, overcurrent protection, equipment installation, and photovoltaic interconnections.

The Nevada contractor licensing process also includes application, qualification, examination, and other requirements beyond the C-2G trade exam. Candidates should prepare for the trade examination as one part of the complete licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-2G study plan should combine photovoltaic theory, calculation practice, system design, electrical code navigation, installation knowledge, and safety review.

Estimating, Calculations, and Design deserves the largest share of preparation because it contains 20 questions. Candidates should work through calculations repeatedly so they become comfortable identifying the needed values, selecting the appropriate method, and completing problems efficiently.

Electrical Interconnection should be studied with the highlighted and tabbed NEC nearby. Review grounding and bonding, conductors, wiring methods, overcurrent protection, disconnects, equipment connections, and the way photovoltaic systems interface with electrical systems.

Photovoltaic Components should include modules, arrays, batteries, inverters, conductors, disconnecting means, protective devices, mounting hardware, and other equipment used in a photovoltaic system.

Candidates should understand not only the name of each component but also its function. Knowing where equipment fits within the energy path from photovoltaic module to electrical system can make installation and calculation questions easier to understand.

Photovoltaic Installation should include equipment placement, mounting, wiring, conductor routing, connections, protection, and practical system layout.

Photovoltaic Theory should include voltage, current, power, series and parallel relationships, module and array behavior, and other solar-electric fundamentals.

Permitting, Inspection, and Utility Interconnections should include an understanding of how completed photovoltaic systems move through inspection and connection processes and how electrical requirements affect system integration.

Safety should be reviewed using OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Photovoltaic work can involve electrical hazards, rooftops, ladders, fall exposure, tools, lifting, material handling, and active construction environments.

The highlighted and tabbed 2017 NEC should be used throughout preparation rather than saved only for code-specific questions. Candidates should become familiar with the physical location of important sections and learn how to move from a keyword or subject to the applicable code provision.

One effective navigation exercise is to select a list of common photovoltaic and electrical topics and time how long it takes to find each one in the NEC. Repeat the exercise until commonly tested areas become easier to locate.

The tabs should support that process rather than replace knowledge of the code structure. Candidates should still understand how chapters, articles, sections, tables, definitions, and the index work together.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Highlighting can help identify important language once a section is found. During study, candidates can review highlighted provisions repeatedly so the language and location become more familiar.

The study-only books should be used differently. Photovoltaic Systems can help build conceptual solar knowledge, Ugly's Electrical References can support formulas and calculations, and OSHA can strengthen construction safety preparation.

Because these three books cannot be used during the exam, candidates should practice answering related questions without opening them after the initial study phase. This helps turn reference material into working knowledge.

Practice questions should also be completed under timed conditions. Attempt each question first, decide whether an NEC lookup is necessary, and only then use the approved code book. This can help candidates develop better judgment about when to search and when to answer from knowledge.

Missed questions should be reviewed by topic. If a candidate repeatedly misses calculation questions, spend additional time with Ugly's and photovoltaic calculation material. If code questions take too long, focus more heavily on NEC navigation.

A balanced preparation schedule might alternate between photovoltaic theory, calculations, code navigation, installation, components, and safety. Rotating subjects can help keep knowledge fresh while building familiarity across the entire exam outline.

How does the Photovoltaics Contractor (C-2G) exam work?

The Photovoltaics Contractor (C-2G) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
