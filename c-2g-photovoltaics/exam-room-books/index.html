Exam Details

The Nevada C-2G Photovoltaics Contractor examination contains 75 questions. Candidates are allowed 150 minutes to complete the examination and must answer 56 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following areas:

  • Photovoltaic Theory: 5 questions
  • Electrical Interconnection: 15 questions
  • Permitting, Inspection, and Utility Interconnections: 5 questions
  • Photovoltaic Components: 15 questions
  • Photovoltaic Installation: 10 questions
  • Estimating, Calculations, and Design: 20 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions

Estimating, Calculations, and Design is the largest individual examination category with 20 questions. Candidates should be prepared to work with electrical values, photovoltaic array characteristics, conductor calculations, equipment sizing concepts, and other system-design considerations.

Electrical Interconnection accounts for 15 questions and is an important area for NEC navigation. Candidates should understand how photovoltaic systems connect to electrical systems and how requirements involving conductors, disconnects, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and equipment affect an installation.

Photovoltaic Components also accounts for 15 questions. Candidates should understand the functions of photovoltaic modules, arrays, inverters, batteries, conductors, disconnecting means, protective equipment, and related system components.

Photovoltaic Installation accounts for 10 questions and focuses on practical system installation principles. Candidates should understand equipment placement, wiring, mounting, connections, protection, and general installation procedures.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-2G Photovoltaics Contractor examination is an open-book test.

The NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 edition is the exam-room-approved reference included in this package. Candidates should bring their approved reference to the testing center and become familiar with its organization before exam day.

An open-book examination should not be approached as an opportunity to search for every answer. With 75 questions and 150 minutes, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes per question. A lengthy search for every code-related answer can consume valuable examination time.

The strongest preparation strategy is to learn the photovoltaic concepts first and use the NEC strategically. Candidates should know whether a question relates to conductors, equipment, grounding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, disconnecting means, photovoltaic systems, or another major electrical topic before beginning a code search.

Regular navigation practice can improve speed. Candidates can create sample questions during study sessions and time themselves locating the applicable NEC article, section, table, or definition. Repeating this process can build familiarity with the structure of the book.

Candidates should also understand the difference between knowing a concept and locating a requirement. Some exam questions may be answered from trade knowledge, while others may require confirmation of a specific electrical code rule. Effective candidates learn when a lookup is useful and when it may unnecessarily slow them down.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-2G Photovoltaics classification covers the installation, alteration, and repair of photovoltaic cells, batteries, and inverters used in the conversion of solar energy into electricity.

The classification does not include installation, alteration, or repair of electrical wires beyond the service panel. Candidates should understand the scope of the classification as part of their overall licensing preparation.

Photovoltaic contracting combines solar-energy knowledge with electrical knowledge. A photovoltaic contractor must understand how energy produced by photovoltaic modules moves through system components and interfaces with electrical equipment and wiring.

This is why the National Electrical Code plays an important role in preparation. Photovoltaic equipment must be installed in a manner consistent with applicable electrical requirements involving system components, conductors, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, disconnects, wiring methods, and interconnections.

Nevada also requires contractor applicants to complete the applicable state application and qualification process before they are authorized to schedule required examinations. The trade exam is therefore one component of the overall licensing process rather than a stand-alone credential.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-2G preparation plan should combine photovoltaic trade knowledge with frequent practice navigating the 2017 National Electrical Code.

Photovoltaic Theory should include basic solar-electric principles, voltage, current, power, series and parallel relationships, photovoltaic module behavior, and the way environmental conditions can affect system output.

Electrical Interconnection should receive significant attention because it accounts for 15 questions. Candidates should understand how photovoltaic systems connect to electrical systems and how electrical code provisions affect those connections.

Study conductors, wiring methods, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, equipment connections, and disconnecting means in conjunction with NEC navigation. Candidates should know both the purpose of these requirements and how to locate applicable provisions in the code.

Photovoltaic Components also accounts for 15 questions. Candidates should understand modules, arrays, inverters, batteries, disconnects, conductors, protective devices, and associated equipment as parts of a complete photovoltaic installation.

Rather than memorizing component names alone, candidates should understand what each component does, where it fits within the system, and how it interacts with other equipment.

Estimating, Calculations, and Design is the largest examination category with 20 questions. Candidates should practice calculations repeatedly so formulas and problem-solving steps become familiar before test day.

Electrical calculations should be practiced alongside NEC navigation. When a calculation depends on a code requirement, candidates should know how to locate the applicable information and then apply it correctly.

Photovoltaic Installation should include system layout, mounting, equipment placement, wiring, conductors, connections, protection, and practical installation procedures.

Permitting, Inspection, and Utility Interconnections should include understanding how photovoltaic installations move through inspection and connection processes and how electrical requirements affect system approval and integration.

Safety remains an important part of photovoltaic preparation. Solar work can involve electrical energy, elevated surfaces, ladders, rooftops, lifting, tools, and active construction environments. Candidates should understand fundamental safe-work concepts in addition to electrical code requirements.

For NEC navigation, begin by learning the overall structure of the code. Candidates should understand how chapters, articles, sections, tables, and definitions are organized rather than depending entirely on the index.

Next, practice locating photovoltaic-related requirements. Repeatedly navigating to commonly used photovoltaic and electrical topics can build familiarity and reduce lookup time during the examination.

More on test Information and Study Materials

The NEC index can still be valuable. Candidates should practice searching for a key term, following the index reference, and then reading enough surrounding code language to determine whether the section actually answers the question.

Definitions are also important. Electrical terminology can affect how a question is interpreted, and code definitions can provide context for requirements elsewhere in the NEC.

Timed navigation exercises can be especially useful. Choose ten electrical or photovoltaic topics and give yourself a limited amount of time to locate the applicable code information. Track which searches take too long and repeat those topics later.

Practice questions can then be combined with code navigation. Attempt the question first, decide whether a lookup is necessary, locate the code requirement if needed, and review why the correct answer applies.

Candidates should avoid using the NEC as a substitute for photovoltaic knowledge. The exam includes theory, components, installation, calculations, design, safety, and other topics that require understanding beyond simply locating code language.

A balanced study plan might devote separate sessions to calculations, electrical interconnection, photovoltaic components, installation, theory, and NEC navigation. Candidates can then combine those areas through timed practice sets.

Since the exam contains 75 questions in 150 minutes, pacing should be part of preparation. Candidates should practice moving past difficult questions and returning to them later rather than spending excessive time on a single code search.

How does the Photovoltaics Contractor (C-2G) exam work?

The Photovoltaics Contractor (C-2G) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
