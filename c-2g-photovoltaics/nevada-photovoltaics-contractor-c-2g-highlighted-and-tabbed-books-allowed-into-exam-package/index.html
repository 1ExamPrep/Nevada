Exam Details

The Nevada C-2G Photovoltaics Contractor examination contains 75 questions. Candidates receive 150 minutes to complete the examination and must answer 56 questions correctly to pass.

The examination is organized into the following subject areas:

  • Photovoltaic Theory: 5 questions
  • Electrical Interconnection: 15 questions
  • Permitting, Inspection, and Utility Interconnections: 5 questions
  • Photovoltaic Components: 15 questions
  • Photovoltaic Installation: 10 questions
  • Estimating, Calculations, and Design: 20 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions

Estimating, Calculations, and Design is the largest exam category with 20 questions. Candidates should practice electrical calculations, system sizing concepts, conductor considerations, photovoltaic array relationships, equipment selection, and design-related problem solving.

Electrical Interconnection accounts for 15 questions and is an important area for NEC navigation. Candidates should understand how photovoltaic equipment connects to electrical systems and how the code addresses conductors, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, disconnects, wiring methods, and system connections.

Photovoltaic Components also accounts for 15 questions. Preparation should include modules, arrays, inverters, batteries, conductors, disconnecting means, protective equipment, and related photovoltaic system components.

Photovoltaic Installation accounts for 10 questions and focuses on practical system installation knowledge, including equipment placement, mounting, wiring, connections, protection, and overall system layout.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-2G Photovoltaics classification covers the installation, alteration, and repair of photovoltaic cells, batteries, and inverters used in the conversion of solar energy into electricity.

The classification does not include installation, alteration, or repair of electrical wiring beyond the service panel. Candidates should understand the scope of the classification as part of their preparation for both the examination and the licensing process.

Photovoltaic contracting combines solar-energy knowledge with electrical-system knowledge. A contractor must understand how electricity is generated by photovoltaic modules, how modules are arranged into arrays, how batteries and inverters interact with the system, and how photovoltaic equipment interfaces with electrical wiring and protective devices.

This makes the National Electrical Code an essential reference for preparation. Candidates should understand how code requirements apply to conductors, grounding and bonding, disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, equipment, wiring methods, and photovoltaic interconnections.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes requirements beyond the C-2G examination. Candidates should view the trade exam as one step within the broader contractor licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-2G study strategy should combine technical photovoltaic knowledge with repeated use of the highlighted and tabbed National Electrical Code.

Photovoltaic Theory should include voltage, current, power, series and parallel relationships, module behavior, array characteristics, and basic solar-electric principles.

Candidates should understand how electrical values change when photovoltaic modules are connected in different arrangements. This foundation can make calculation and system-design questions easier to interpret.

Electrical Interconnection should receive significant attention because it accounts for 15 questions. Candidates should understand how photovoltaic systems interface with electrical distribution equipment and how code provisions affect those connections.

Use the highlighted and tabbed NEC to review conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, disconnects, equipment requirements, and system connections. The goal is not simply to locate a section but to understand what the requirement means in a photovoltaic installation.

Photovoltaic Components should include modules, arrays, batteries, inverters, conductors, disconnects, protective devices, mounting systems, and related equipment.

Candidates should understand the role of each component within the complete system. Knowing how electricity moves from the photovoltaic source through conversion and connection equipment can help organize both technical knowledge and code study.

Photovoltaic Installation should include mounting, equipment placement, wiring, conductor routing, system layout, protection, connections, and installation practices.

Estimating, Calculations, and Design deserves the largest share of preparation because it contains 20 questions. Candidates should repeatedly practice calculations until the problem-solving steps become familiar.

Calculation practice should be connected to NEC navigation when applicable. Candidates should become comfortable identifying when a code value or requirement is needed and locating that information efficiently in the approved reference.

Permitting, Inspection, and Utility Interconnections should include understanding the relationship between photovoltaic installations, inspections, electrical requirements, and utility connections.

Safety should remain part of the study plan even though this package focuses on the exam-room-approved NEC. Photovoltaic work can involve electrical hazards, rooftops, ladders, elevated work, lifting, tools, and active construction environments.

One of the best ways to use a highlighted and tabbed NEC is through timed navigation exercises. Choose a photovoltaic or electrical topic, start a timer, and locate the applicable article, section, table, or definition. Repeat difficult searches until they become faster.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Candidates should also learn the overall organization of the NEC. Understanding chapters, articles, definitions, sections, and tables can be more useful than depending only on tabs.

Tabs should serve as navigation aids rather than substitutes for knowledge. Too much dependence on a tab label can make it harder to locate related subjects that appear elsewhere in the code.

Highlighting can help once the correct section is reached. During study sessions, repeatedly review highlighted requirements so important language becomes familiar and easier to recognize under exam conditions.

The NEC index is another useful tool. Candidates should practice selecting an appropriate keyword, following the index entry to the code section, and then reading the surrounding language carefully enough to determine whether the section actually answers the question.

Definitions should also be part of preparation. Electrical terminology can affect how a question is interpreted, and the NEC definitions can provide useful context for requirements throughout the code.

Practice questions should be combined with the highlighted and tabbed NEC. Try answering from knowledge first, decide whether a code lookup is needed, and then use the book strategically. This can help candidates improve judgment about when to search.

If a candidate understands the technical concept but consistently takes too long to locate the code section, additional navigation practice should become a priority. If the code section is easy to find but the question itself is confusing, more technical photovoltaic study may be needed.

A balanced study schedule might rotate between photovoltaic theory, electrical interconnection, components, installation, calculations, design, and NEC navigation. This approach helps candidates build both technical understanding and exam-room reference skills.

Because the C-2G examination is timed, candidates should also practice moving past difficult questions and returning to them later. Efficient time management can be just as important as knowing how to find a particular code section.

How does the Photovoltaics Contractor (C-2G) exam work?

The Photovoltaics Contractor (C-2G) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
