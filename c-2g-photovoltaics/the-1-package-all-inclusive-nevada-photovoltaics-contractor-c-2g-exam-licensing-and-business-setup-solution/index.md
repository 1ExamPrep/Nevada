## Exam Details

The Nevada C-2G Photovoltaics trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who receive examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

- **Exam:** Nevada C-2G Photovoltaics Contractor
- **Number of Questions:** 75
- **Time Allowed:** 150 minutes
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 56 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The C-2G examination content is divided into seven technical areas:

- **Photovoltaic Theory:** 5 questions
- **Electrical Interconnection:** 15 questions
- **Permitting, Inspection, and Utility Interconnections:** 5 questions
- **Photovoltaic Components:** 15 questions
- **Photovoltaic Installation:** 10 questions
- **Estimating, Calculations, and Design:** 20 questions
- **Safety:** 5 questions

**Estimating, Calculations, and Design** is the largest individual category with 20 questions. Candidates should devote substantial preparation time to electrical calculations, photovoltaic system sizing concepts, conductor-related calculations, equipment relationships, system design principles, and the numerical reasoning required to evaluate solar-electric systems.

**Electrical Interconnection** contains 15 questions. Preparation should include the electrical relationships between photovoltaic equipment and the electrical system, connection methods, conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, disconnecting means, and applicable National Electrical Code requirements.

**Photovoltaic Components** also contains 15 questions. Candidates should understand photovoltaic modules, cells, batteries, inverters, conductors, disconnects, overcurrent devices, mounting-related equipment, and other components used in photovoltaic systems.

**Photovoltaic Installation** contains 10 questions. Candidates should review installation procedures, equipment placement, system configuration, wiring methods, mounting considerations, inspection concerns, and practical procedures involved in photovoltaic work.

Photovoltaic Theory, Permitting, Inspection and Utility Interconnections, and Safety each contain five questions. Although these sections contain fewer questions, they remain important parts of the examination and should be included in a complete study plan.

## State Requirements

The Nevada C-2G Photovoltaics classification authorizes the installation, alteration, and repair of photovoltaic cells, batteries, and inverters used in the conversion of solar energy into electricity.

The scope does not authorize the installation, alteration, or repair of electrical wires beyond the service panel. Candidates should understand this boundary when preparing for the classification and when evaluating the type of work a C-2G license authorizes.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual generally must demonstrate at least four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the requested classification.

The qualifying experience generally must have been obtained within the 15 years immediately preceding the filing of the application. Applicants should maintain organized records describing the photovoltaic work performed and the period during which the experience was gained.

Nevada contractor licensing distinguishes between trade and management qualification. One individual may fulfill both roles when the applicable requirements are satisfied, or separate individuals may be designated.

The Trade Qualified Individual establishes the applicable experience and trade qualification for C-2G. The Management Qualified Individual generally satisfies the Nevada Business and Law requirement unless an applicable waiver is approved.

Financial responsibility, background requirements, contractor bonding, and other licensing requirements are separate from the technical examination. Passing the C-2G trade exam alone does not complete the Nevada contractor licensing process.

## How does the Photovoltaics Contractor (C-2G) exam work?

The Photovoltaics Contractor (C-2G) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
