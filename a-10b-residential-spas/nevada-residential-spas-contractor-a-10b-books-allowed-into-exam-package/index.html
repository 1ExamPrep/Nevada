State Requirements

Nevada recognizes A-10B Residential Spas as a sub-subclassification within A-10 Commercial and Residential Pools.

The authorized scope covers the design, construction, and repair of residential spas, including appurtenant equipment.

This means candidates should understand residential spa projects as complete systems. Site preparation, structural shells, concrete or shotcrete construction, circulation systems, pumps, filters, fittings, equipment, surrounding surfaces, and related construction coordination all contribute to a successful installation.

Design is a major part of the A-10B scope and examination. Candidates should understand how dimensions, equipment location, access, circulation components, structural conditions, and surrounding construction affect the completed spa.

Soils and Earthwork are also important because residential spas may involve excavation and site preparation. Drainage, settlement, soil conditions, and compaction can affect the structure and surrounding surfaces.

Concrete, Shotcrete and Gunite knowledge is important because the spa shell must be constructed to support long-term performance. Candidates should understand the relationship between structural work, reinforcement, placement, curing, and quality construction.

The A-10B trade examination is one part of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, bonding, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada A-10B study plan should begin with Design, the largest examination category. Candidates should understand how the spa is planned as a complete system rather than treating the shell, equipment, circulation, and site conditions as unrelated subjects.

Spa Layout should include the relationship between the vessel, seating, access, surrounding surfaces, equipment, circulation components, and site conditions. Candidates should understand how design choices affect both construction and operation.

Filtration and Circulation Systems should include the movement of water through suction points, pumps, filters, treatment components, and return fittings. Candidates should understand the purpose of circulation and the function of the major equipment components.

Maintenance and Operation should include routine spa care, equipment operation, circulation, filtration, and recognition of conditions that can affect spa performance.

Concrete, Shotcrete and Gunite should be studied as structural construction subjects. Candidates should understand reinforcement relationships, shell construction, placement concepts, curing, and quality considerations.

Soils and Earthwork should include excavation, drainage, settlement, soil conditions, and site preparation. Poor site conditions can affect the spa shell, surrounding deck, piping, and related improvements.

Accessories and Associated Trades should be reviewed as part of the complete spa system. Equipment, finishes, surrounding surfaces, utilities, and other construction components must work together.

The Uniform Swimming Pool, Spa and Hot Tub Code, 2018 should be used during every stage of preparation. Start by reviewing the table of contents and identifying the major sections relevant to residential spas.

Use the index for targeted navigation practice. Select subjects such as circulation, equipment, safety, spas, construction requirements, and related provisions, then locate them repeatedly until the search process becomes familiar.

Definitions should receive careful attention. Code terminology can affect the meaning of both the exam question and the provision being applied. Knowing the terms can make navigation more efficient.

When reviewing a code section, candidates should read the surrounding text carefully. Notes, exceptions, related subsections, and limitations can affect how a requirement applies.

A useful study exercise is to create short lookup drills. Choose a residential spa topic, locate the applicable code section, and explain the requirement in your own words.

Practice questions should be organized by the major examination categories: Design, Concrete/Shotcrete/Gunite, Maintenance and Operation, Accessories and Associated Trades, Soils and Earthwork, Filtration/Circulation, and Pool and Spa Business Laws and Rules.

How does the Residential Spas Contractor (A-10B) exam work?

The Residential Spas Contractor (A-10B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
