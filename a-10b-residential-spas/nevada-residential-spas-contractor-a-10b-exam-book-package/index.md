## State Requirements

Nevada recognizes **A-10B Residential Spas** as a sub-subclassification within A-10 Commercial and Residential Pools.

The authorized scope covers the **design, construction, and repair of residential spas, including any appurtenant equipment**.

The scope means candidates should understand more than one construction activity. Residential spa projects can involve excavation and site preparation, structural shells, concrete or shotcrete construction, circulation systems, pumps, filters, fittings, equipment, surrounding surfaces, and related construction coordination.

Design is an important part of the classification. Candidates should understand how spa size, depth, layout, equipment location, circulation components, access, and site conditions can affect construction and operation.

Residential spa contractors also operate within Nevada's special requirements for residential pool and spa work. Contracts, disclosures, consumer protection requirements, permits, and other licensing responsibilities can apply to residential spa projects.

Residential pool and spa contractors may also be subject to additional bond requirements for consumer protection. The amount and form of any required bond are determined through the Nevada contractor licensing process.

The A-10B trade examination is one part of the licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy applicable experience, application, financial, bond, business and law, and other licensing requirements before the license is issued.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada A-10B study plan should begin with **Design**, the largest examination category. Candidates should understand how the spa shell, dimensions, seating, circulation, equipment, surrounding construction, access, and site conditions function together.

**Filtration and Circulation Systems** should include the movement of water through suction points, pumps, filtration equipment, treatment components, and return fittings. Candidates should understand the purpose of circulation and how equipment components work together.

**Maintenance and Operation** should include routine spa care, circulation, filtration, equipment operation, water-related conditions, and troubleshooting concepts. The Pool and Spa Operator Handbook and Ultimate Pool Maintenance Manual can support this preparation.

**Concrete, Shotcrete and Gunite** should receive significant study time. Candidates should understand structural shell construction, reinforcement coordination, material placement, finishing, curing, and the importance of quality application.

The **Guide to Shotcrete** can help candidates review the materials and processes involved in shotcrete construction, while **The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction** supports broader concrete field practices.

**Soils and Earthwork** should include excavation, drainage, soil conditions, compaction, settlement, and site preparation. A spa structure depends on suitable site conditions and proper preparation before structural work begins.

**Accessories and Associated Trades** should be reviewed as part of the complete spa system. Candidates should understand how equipment, surrounding surfaces, utilities, finishes, and related construction interact with the spa.

The **ANSI/NSPI-3 residential spa standard** should be used to strengthen knowledge of permanently installed residential spa design and construction concepts. Because it is not allowed into the trade examination, candidates should focus on understanding and retaining the information.

The **Uniform Swimming Pool, Spa and Hot Tub Code, 2018** should be used throughout preparation. Candidates should practice finding spa-related sections, definitions, equipment provisions, circulation requirements, and safety rules.

Use the index and table of contents for targeted navigation exercises. Select a topic, find the applicable section, and then explain the requirement in your own words. This helps develop both reference familiarity and trade understanding.

Definitions deserve careful attention because spa terminology can affect how a question or code requirement is interpreted. Knowing the exact meaning of technical terms can make code navigation faster.

Practice questions should be organized around the official exam categories. Begin with individual subjects, then move to mixed practice once the major areas become familiar.

## How does the Residential Spas Contractor (A-10B) exam work?

The Residential Spas Contractor (A-10B) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
