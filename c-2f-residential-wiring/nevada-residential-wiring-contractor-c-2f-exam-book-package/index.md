## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-2F study plan should begin with **Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits**, the largest exam category. Candidates should review service equipment, service conductors, disconnecting means, panelboards, feeder conductors, branch circuits, circuit ratings, load relationships, and common residential applications.

**General Electrical Knowledge** should include electrical principles, terminology, voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, and the practical concepts needed to interpret NEC requirements.

**Grounding and Bonding** deserves substantial attention. Candidates should understand grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding, grounded conductors, service bonding, and the purpose of providing an effective fault-current path.

Grounding terminology can be confusing, so candidates should practice distinguishing between the grounded conductor, grounding electrode conductor, equipment grounding conductor, and bonded metal components.

**Conductors and Cables** should include conductor materials, insulation types, ampacity, temperature ratings, cable assemblies, conductor identification, allowable uses, and sizing considerations.

NEC table navigation is particularly important for conductor questions. Candidates should practice locating conductor ampacity tables, adjustment and correction requirements, conductor-size information, and notes that affect the final answer.

**Overcurrent Protection** should include circuit breakers, fuses, conductor protection, equipment protection, branch-circuit relationships, and the purpose of limiting current during overloads and fault conditions.

**Electrical Power** should include common electrical formulas and basic calculations. Ugly's Electrical References can be used during preparation to reinforce calculation methods and electrical relationships.

Because Ugly's Electrical References is not allowed into the exam, candidates should gradually practice formulas and calculations without relying on the book nearby.

**Low Voltage** should be included in preparation because the C-2F exam contains questions in this area. Candidates should understand common residential low-voltage concepts and the differences between these systems and conventional power wiring.

**Fire and Intrusion Detection and Alarm Systems** should also receive review. Candidates should understand basic residential alarm wiring relationships and the role of these systems within residential electrical work.

**Safety Information** should be studied carefully because OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 is not available in the exam room. Candidates should review electrical hazard awareness, personal protective equipment, ladders, tools, fall protection, temporary conditions, and general construction safety.

**Raceways and Enclosures** should include conduit and raceway types, boxes, fittings, protection from damage, support, installation, and conductor-fill concepts.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

The **2017 National Electrical Code** should be used during every study session. Candidates should become familiar with Article 100 definitions, Chapter 2 wiring and protection, Chapter 3 wiring methods and materials, common equipment articles, and applicable tables.

Practice questions should be used to strengthen both trade knowledge and NEC navigation. When a question is missed, identify whether the problem came from misunderstanding the electrical concept, choosing the wrong NEC article, misreading a table, or making a calculation error.

A useful navigation method is to use the NEC index when the correct article is not immediately known. Search for the technical term used in the question, locate the referenced article or section, and then read enough surrounding text to understand the complete requirement.

Candidates should avoid stopping at the first familiar number or phrase. NEC sections can include exceptions, related subsections, table notes, and conditions that affect how a requirement applies.

A balanced study sequence is to begin with services, feeders, and branch circuits, followed by grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, general electrical knowledge, overcurrent protection, raceways and enclosures, safety, electrical power, low voltage, and alarm-system topics.

Finish preparation with mixed timed practice. The C-2F examination moves between different electrical subjects, so candidates should become comfortable switching quickly between service questions, conductor sizing, grounding, safety, raceways, and electrical calculations.

## How does the Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) exam work?

The Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
