Exam Details

The Nevada C-2F Residential Wiring trade examination is administered through PSI for qualifying candidates pursuing the classification through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-2F Residential Wiring
  • Number of Questions: 50
  • Time Allowed: 2 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 35 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination is organized around the following major electrical subjects:

  • General Electrical Knowledge: 9 questions
  • Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits: 10 questions
  • Grounding and Bonding: 7 questions
  • Conductors and Cables: 7 questions
  • Overcurrent Protection: 4 questions
  • Electrical Power: 2 questions
  • Low Voltage: 2 questions
  • Fire and Intrusion Detection and Alarm Systems: 2 questions
  • Safety Information: 4 questions
  • Raceways and Enclosures: 3 questions

Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits is the largest individual category with 10 questions. Candidates should devote substantial study time to residential service requirements, feeder sizing, branch-circuit requirements, residential loads, conductor protection, required circuits, and the relationship between service equipment and downstream circuits.

General Electrical Knowledge contains nine questions. Preparation should include electrical theory, terminology, formulas, calculations, current and voltage relationships, resistance, power, and other practical concepts that support the rest of the exam.

Grounding and Bonding and Conductors and Cables each contain seven questions. Candidates should understand conductor sizing, ampacity, insulation, cable applications, grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding requirements, and the purpose of effective fault-current paths.

Overcurrent Protection and Safety Information each contain four questions. Candidates should review circuit breakers, fuses, conductor protection, electrical hazards, fall protection, personal protective equipment, ladders, tools, and other construction-safety topics.

Raceways and Enclosures contains three questions. Electrical Power, Low Voltage, and Fire and Intrusion Detection and Alarm Systems each contain two questions. These are smaller portions of the exam, but they should still be included in a complete preparation plan because every correct answer contributes toward the passing score.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-2F Residential Wiring examination is an open-book test. The approved exam-room reference included with this package is:

  • NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 Edition

Candidates should use the National Electrical Code throughout preparation instead of waiting until the final stage of study to begin navigating it. Effective open-book testing depends on knowing how the NEC is organized and where different types of electrical requirements are generally located.

Useful NEC navigation practice includes services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor ampacity, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, boxes, raceways, enclosures, equipment, residential loads, receptacles, and other residential electrical conditions.

Candidates should practice using the NEC index, Article organization, definitions, tables, exceptions, notes, and cross-references. The goal is not simply to know that the answer is somewhere in the book, but to develop enough familiarity to find the correct section efficiently during a timed examination.

The following included study references are not allowed into the C-2F examination:

  • Ugly's Electrical References
  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)

Ugly's Electrical References should be used during preparation to reinforce electrical formulas, calculations, conductor information, common electrical relationships, and practical electrical concepts. Because it cannot be used during the examination, candidates should work toward understanding the calculation methods rather than depending on the book for every formula.

OSHA should be used to strengthen safety knowledge involving fall protection, ladders, personal protective equipment, electrical hazards, tools, material handling, and other construction-site conditions. Since OSHA cannot be used during testing, candidates should develop strong recall of the safety concepts represented on the exam.

With 50 questions and two hours available, candidates have an average of approximately 2.4 minutes per question. Efficient time management is important. A strong strategy combines electrical knowledge with targeted NEC navigation rather than researching every answer from the beginning.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-2F Residential Wiring subclassification covers qualifying installation, alteration, and repair of electrical wires, fixtures, appliances, and apparatuses whose electrical current does not exceed 600 volts on new or existing structures that do not exceed more than three stories above the ground.

Candidates should understand the authorized scope when deciding whether the C-2F classification matches the projects they intend to perform. Contractor licenses are classification-specific, and work must remain within the scope authorized by the license held.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual generally must establish four years of qualifying experience within the 15 years immediately preceding the application.

Qualifying experience can include applicable work performed as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor. Experience documentation should describe the residential electrical work performed and the period during which that work was completed.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification. The Management Qualified Individual generally satisfies the Contractor Management Survey and Nevada Business and Law requirement unless an approved waiver applies.

The licensing process also includes requirements separate from the technical exam, such as financial responsibility, background procedures, contractor bonding, and completion of other conditions established by the Nevada State Contractors Board.

Passing the C-2F trade examination does not by itself complete contractor licensing. Candidates should approach technical preparation, Business and Law study, application documentation, financial requirements, and final licensing steps as separate parts of the same licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-2F preparation plan should reflect the weighting of the examination. Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits deserves substantial attention, followed closely by General Electrical Knowledge, Grounding and Bonding, and Conductors and Cables.

Prioritize services, feeders, and branch circuits. Review residential service requirements, feeder calculations, branch-circuit sizing, required circuits, conductor protection, loads, and equipment relationships.

Strengthen general electrical knowledge. Review formulas, terminology, electrical theory, current, voltage, resistance, power, and common calculation methods. Ugly's Electrical References can support this stage of preparation.

Practice grounding and bonding. Review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding requirements, grounded conductors, and fault-current paths.

Review conductors and cables. Study conductor sizing, ampacity, cable types, insulation, wiring applications, and applicable NEC requirements affecting residential systems.

Prepare overcurrent protection. Review circuit breakers, fuses, conductor protection, and the relationship between loads, conductors, and overcurrent protective devices.

Study safety for recall. OSHA is not allowed into the C-2F trade examination, so safety principles should become familiar before test day.

Review low voltage and alarm systems. Although these are smaller categories, candidates should understand the basic concepts represented by the examination.

Practice raceways and enclosures. Review raceways, boxes, enclosures, conductor fill, installation methods, and protection of wiring systems.

Practice NEC navigation under timed conditions. Learn to locate specific requirements quickly enough to keep the overall exam pace under control.

Use timed practice questions. With 50 questions in two hours, candidates should build a consistent pace and avoid spending excessive time on any single code lookup or calculation.

Review missed questions by category. If repeated mistakes occur in grounding, conductors, service calculations, branch circuits, safety, or overcurrent protection, direct additional study toward that area instead of repeating material already understood.

How does the Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) exam work?

The Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
