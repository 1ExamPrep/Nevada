About this Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) package

Prepare for the Nevada Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) examination with an online exam prep program focused on residential electrical systems, National Electrical Code navigation, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors, overcurrent protection, raceways, enclosures, low-voltage systems, fire and intrusion alarm systems, electrical calculations, and construction safety.

The Nevada C-2F Residential Wiring classification is regulated by the Nevada State Contractors Board. The classification covers qualifying installation, alteration, and repair of electrical wires, fixtures, appliances, and apparatuses whose electrical current does not exceed 600 volts on new or existing structures that do not exceed more than three stories above the ground.

Residential electrical work requires more than memorizing code sections. Candidates should understand how electrical theory, loads, conductors, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, and equipment work together. The C-2F examination combines these subjects into a timed test that rewards both technical understanding and effective reference navigation.

The primary approved exam-room reference used for this preparation is NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 Edition. Candidates should become comfortable navigating the NEC throughout their study process. The open-book format can be valuable, but only when a candidate knows how the code is organized and can locate information without spending too much time searching.

Two additional study references are Ugly's Electrical References and Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA). These books support preparation involving electrical formulas, conductor information, calculations, practical electrical relationships, construction safety, and jobsite hazards. They are not allowed into the C-2F trade examination, so the knowledge developed from them should be reinforced before test day.

Online exam prep makes it possible to divide the C-2F examination into manageable study areas. Candidates can focus separately on services and feeders, branch circuits, grounding, conductor sizing, electrical theory, overcurrent protection, raceways, safety, low voltage, and alarm systems while spending additional time on topics that need more review.

Exam Details

The Nevada C-2F Residential Wiring trade examination is administered through PSI for qualifying candidates pursuing the classification through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-2F Residential Wiring
  • Number of Questions: 50
  • Time Allowed: 2 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 35 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination content is divided among the following electrical subjects:

  • General Electrical Knowledge: 9 questions
  • Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits: 10 questions
  • Grounding and Bonding: 7 questions
  • Conductors and Cables: 7 questions
  • Overcurrent Protection: 4 questions
  • Electrical Power: 2 questions
  • Low Voltage: 2 questions
  • Fire and Intrusion Detection and Alarm Systems: 2 questions
  • Safety Information: 4 questions
  • Raceways and Enclosures: 3 questions

Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits is the largest individual content area with 10 questions. Candidates should review residential service requirements, feeder sizing, branch-circuit requirements, electrical loads, required circuits, conductor protection, and the relationship between service equipment and downstream circuits.

General Electrical Knowledge contains nine questions. Preparation should include electrical theory, terminology, formulas, current, voltage, resistance, power, and common calculation methods. Strong general electrical knowledge supports nearly every other category on the exam.

Grounding and Bonding and Conductors and Cables each contain seven questions. Candidates should understand conductor sizing, ampacity, insulation, cable applications, grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding, bonding, grounded conductors, and effective fault-current paths.

Overcurrent Protection and Safety Information each contain four questions. Preparation should include circuit breakers, fuses, conductor protection, electrical hazards, ladders, personal protective equipment, tools, and other construction-safety topics.

Raceways and Enclosures, Electrical Power, Low Voltage, and Fire and Intrusion Detection and Alarm Systems make up smaller portions of the examination. These subjects should still be included in the study plan because every correct answer contributes toward the passing score.

Open Book Test

The Nevada C-2F Residential Wiring examination is an open-book test. The approved exam-room reference used for this preparation is:

  • NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 Edition

Candidates should use the NEC consistently during preparation rather than waiting until the final days before the exam to begin navigating it. Effective open-book testing depends on knowing how the code is structured and where different types of electrical requirements are generally located.

Useful NEC navigation practice includes services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor ampacity, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, boxes, raceways, enclosures, equipment, residential loads, and special electrical conditions.

Candidates should practice using the NEC index, Articles, definitions, tables, exceptions, notes, and cross-references. The goal is to recognize the subject of a question quickly, identify the likely location of the requirement, and confirm the answer efficiently.

The following study references are not allowed into the C-2F trade examination:

  • Ugly's Electrical References
  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)

Ugly's Electrical References should be used during preparation to reinforce formulas, conductor information, electrical calculations, and common electrical relationships. Because the book is not permitted during the trade exam, candidates should work toward understanding the calculation methods rather than depending on the reference for every formula.

OSHA should be used to strengthen safety knowledge involving electrical hazards, fall protection, ladders, personal protective equipment, tools, material handling, and other construction-site conditions. Safety concepts should be studied for recall because the OSHA reference cannot be used during the examination.

With 50 questions and two hours available, candidates have an average of approximately 2.4 minutes per question. That makes pacing an important part of preparation. Candidates should practice deciding when a code lookup is necessary and when a question can be answered directly from trade knowledge.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the C-2F Residential Wiring classification. Passing the trade examination is one part of the overall Nevada contractor licensing process.

  1. Confirm the C-2F classification. Determine that Residential Wiring matches the electrical work the contracting business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the Trade Qualified Individual. Designate the person who will establish the applicable experience and technical qualification.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare records demonstrating relevant residential electrical work in the requested classification.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor license application. Organize the required business, qualifier, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews qualification information before examination eligibility is established.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow the required fingerprint and background procedures associated with contractor licensing.
  7. Provide applicable financial information. Financial responsibility is evaluated as part of the Nevada licensing process.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Qualified applicants can proceed with required testing after authorization.
  9. Schedule the C-2F examination through PSI. Arrange the Residential Wiring trade exam after eligibility is established.
  10. Prepare for the technical examination. Study services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding, conductors, overcurrent protection, raceways, safety, low voltage, alarms, and electrical theory.
  11. Prepare for Nevada Business and Law. Nevada contractor applicants generally must also satisfy the applicable management qualification requirement unless an approved waiver applies.
  12. Complete applicable examinations. Meet the required trade and management examination requirements.
  13. Complete final licensing requirements. Address applicable financial responsibility, contractor bonding, and remaining Nevada State Contractors Board requirements.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-2F Residential Wiring subclassification covers qualifying installation, alteration, and repair of electrical wires, fixtures, appliances, and apparatuses whose electrical current does not exceed 600 volts on new or existing structures that do not exceed more than three stories above the ground.

Candidates should understand the authorized scope when deciding whether C-2F matches the projects they intend to perform. Nevada contractor licenses are classification-specific, and work should remain within the scope authorized by the license held.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual generally must establish four years of qualifying experience within the 15 years immediately preceding the application.

Qualifying experience can include applicable work performed as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor. Applicants should maintain organized documentation describing the residential electrical work performed and the period during which the experience was obtained.

Nevada contractor licensing also includes management qualification. The Management Qualified Individual generally satisfies the Contractor Management Survey and Nevada Business and Law requirement unless an approved waiver applies.

The licensing process can also include financial responsibility review, background procedures, contractor bonding, and other requirements separate from the technical examination. Passing the C-2F trade exam alone does not complete the contractor licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-2F study plan should reflect the weighting of the examination. Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits deserves substantial attention, followed closely by General Electrical Knowledge, Grounding and Bonding, and Conductors and Cables.

Prioritize services, feeders, and branch circuits. Review residential service requirements, feeder calculations, branch-circuit sizing, required circuits, electrical loads, conductor protection, and equipment relationships.

Strengthen general electrical knowledge. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, terminology, formulas, and calculation methods. Use Ugly's Electrical References during preparation to reinforce formulas and relationships.

Practice grounding and bonding. Review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding requirements, grounded conductors, and fault-current paths.

Review conductors and cables. Study conductor sizing, ampacity, insulation, cable applications, wiring methods, and applicable NEC requirements.

Prepare overcurrent protection. Review circuit breakers, fuses, conductor protection, and the relationship between loads, conductors, and protective devices.

Study safety for recall. Since OSHA is not allowed into the examination, candidates should become comfortable with the safety concepts before test day.

Review raceways and enclosures. Study raceways, boxes, enclosures, conductor fill, installation methods, and protection of wiring systems.

Review low-voltage and alarm systems. Although these are smaller categories, they remain part of the examination and should not be skipped.

Practice NEC navigation under timed conditions. Search for specific requirements with a time limit so reference navigation becomes part of normal exam preparation.

Use timed practice questions. With 50 questions in two hours, candidates should build a consistent pace and avoid spending excessive time on one lookup or calculation.

How does the Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) exam work?

The Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
