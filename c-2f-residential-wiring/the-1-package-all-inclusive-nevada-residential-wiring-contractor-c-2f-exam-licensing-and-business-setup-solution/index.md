## Exam Details

The Nevada C-2F Residential Wiring trade examination is administered through PSI for qualifying candidates pursuing the classification through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

- **Exam:** Nevada C-2F Residential Wiring
- **Number of Questions:** 50
- **Time Allowed:** 2 hours
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 35 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The examination is organized around the following electrical subjects:

- **General Electrical Knowledge:** 9 questions
- **Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits:** 10 questions
- **Grounding and Bonding:** 7 questions
- **Conductors and Cables:** 7 questions
- **Overcurrent Protection:** 4 questions
- **Electrical Power:** 2 questions
- **Low Voltage:** 2 questions
- **Fire and Intrusion Detection and Alarm Systems:** 2 questions
- **Safety Information:** 4 questions
- **Raceways and Enclosures:** 3 questions

**Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits** is the largest individual category with 10 questions. Candidates should devote substantial study time to residential service requirements, feeder sizing, branch-circuit requirements, loads, required circuits, conductors, panel relationships, and overcurrent protection.

**General Electrical Knowledge** contains nine questions. Preparation should include electrical theory, terminology, formulas, current, voltage, resistance, power, and common calculation methods. Strong general knowledge can make it easier to work through more specialized sections of the exam.

**Grounding and Bonding** and **Conductors and Cables** each contain seven questions. Candidates should understand grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding, bonding, conductor sizing, ampacity, cable applications, insulation, and fault-current paths.

**Overcurrent Protection** and **Safety Information** each contain four questions. Preparation should include circuit breakers, fuses, conductor protection, electrical hazards, personal protective equipment, ladders, tools, and other construction-safety concerns.

Raceways and Enclosures, Electrical Power, Low Voltage, and Fire and Intrusion Detection and Alarm Systems are smaller exam categories, but they should still be included in a complete study plan because every correct answer contributes toward the passing score.

Candidates should work with the NEC throughout preparation instead of waiting until the end of the study process to begin navigating it. Effective open-book testing depends on knowing how the code is organized and where different types of requirements are typically located.

Candidates should also practice using the NEC index, Article structure, definitions, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. The goal is to develop enough familiarity to locate a requirement efficiently when a question calls for a precise code answer.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-2F study plan should reflect the weighting of the examination. Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits deserves substantial attention, followed by General Electrical Knowledge, Grounding and Bonding, and Conductors and Cables.

**Prioritize services, feeders, and branch circuits.** Review residential service requirements, feeder calculations, branch-circuit sizing, required circuits, loads, conductor protection, and equipment relationships.

**Strengthen general electrical knowledge.** Review electrical theory, formulas, current, voltage, resistance, power, terminology, and common calculations. Ugly's Electrical References can support this stage of preparation.

**Practice grounding and bonding.** Review grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding requirements, grounded conductors, and fault-current paths.

**Review conductors and cables.** Study conductor sizing, ampacity, insulation types, cable applications, wiring methods, and applicable NEC requirements.

**Prepare overcurrent protection.** Review circuit breakers, fuses, conductor protection, and the relationship between loads, conductors, and protective devices.

**Study safety for recall.** OSHA is not allowed into the C-2F trade examination, so safety principles should become familiar before test day.

**Review raceways and enclosures.** Study raceways, boxes, enclosures, conductor fill, installation methods, and wiring-system protection.

**Practice NEC navigation under timed conditions.** Work through code questions with a clock so locating a specific requirement becomes part of normal preparation.

**Use timed practice questions.** With 50 questions in two hours, candidates should build a consistent pace and avoid spending too much time on one code lookup or calculation.

## How does the Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) exam work?

The Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
