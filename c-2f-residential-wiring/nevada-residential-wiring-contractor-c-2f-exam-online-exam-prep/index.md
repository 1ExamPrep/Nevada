## Exam Details

The Nevada C-2F Residential Wiring trade examination is administered through PSI for qualifying candidates pursuing the classification through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

- **Exam:** Nevada C-2F Residential Wiring
- **Number of Questions:** 50
- **Time Allowed:** 2 hours
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 35 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The examination content is divided among the following electrical subjects:

- **General Electrical Knowledge:** 9 questions
- **Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits:** 10 questions
- **Grounding and Bonding:** 7 questions
- **Conductors and Cables:** 7 questions
- **Overcurrent Protection:** 4 questions
- **Electrical Power:** 2 questions
- **Low Voltage:** 2 questions
- **Fire and Intrusion Detection and Alarm Systems:** 2 questions
- **Safety Information:** 4 questions
- **Raceways and Enclosures:** 3 questions

**Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits** is the largest individual content area with 10 questions. Candidates should review residential service requirements, feeder sizing, branch-circuit requirements, electrical loads, required circuits, conductor protection, and the relationship between service equipment and downstream circuits.

**General Electrical Knowledge** contains nine questions. Preparation should include electrical theory, terminology, formulas, current, voltage, resistance, power, and common calculation methods. Strong general electrical knowledge supports nearly every other category on the exam.

**Grounding and Bonding** and **Conductors and Cables** each contain seven questions. Candidates should understand conductor sizing, ampacity, insulation, cable applications, grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding, bonding, grounded conductors, and effective fault-current paths.

**Overcurrent Protection** and **Safety Information** each contain four questions. Preparation should include circuit breakers, fuses, conductor protection, electrical hazards, ladders, personal protective equipment, tools, and other construction-safety topics.

Raceways and Enclosures, Electrical Power, Low Voltage, and Fire and Intrusion Detection and Alarm Systems make up smaller portions of the examination. These subjects should still be included in the study plan because every correct answer contributes toward the passing score.

## Open Book Test

The Nevada C-2F Residential Wiring examination is an **open-book test**. The approved exam-room reference used for this preparation is:

- **NFPA 70 - National Electrical Code, 2017 Edition**

Candidates should use the NEC consistently during preparation rather than waiting until the final days before the exam to begin navigating it. Effective open-book testing depends on knowing how the code is structured and where different types of electrical requirements are generally located.

Useful NEC navigation practice includes services, feeders, branch circuits, conductor ampacity, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, boxes, raceways, enclosures, equipment, residential loads, and special electrical conditions.

Candidates should practice using the NEC index, Articles, definitions, tables, exceptions, notes, and cross-references. The goal is to recognize the subject of a question quickly, identify the likely location of the requirement, and confirm the answer efficiently.

The following study references are **not allowed into the C-2F trade examination:**

- **Ugly's Electrical References**
- **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)**

Ugly's Electrical References should be used during preparation to reinforce formulas, conductor information, electrical calculations, and common electrical relationships. Because the book is not permitted during the trade exam, candidates should work toward understanding the calculation methods rather than depending on the reference for every formula.

OSHA should be used to strengthen safety knowledge involving electrical hazards, fall protection, ladders, personal protective equipment, tools, material handling, and other construction-site conditions. Safety concepts should be studied for recall because the OSHA reference cannot be used during the examination.

With 50 questions and two hours available, candidates have an average of approximately 2.4 minutes per question. That makes pacing an important part of preparation. Candidates should practice deciding when a code lookup is necessary and when a question can be answered directly from trade knowledge.

**Practice NEC navigation under timed conditions.** Search for specific requirements with a time limit so reference navigation becomes part of normal exam preparation.

## How does the Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) exam work?

The Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
