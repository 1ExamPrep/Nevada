## State Requirements

Nevada recognizes **C-2F Residential Wiring** as a subclassification within the C-2 Electrical classification.

The C-2F scope covers the **installation, alteration, and repair of electrical wires, fixtures, appliances, and apparatuses whose electrical current does not exceed 600 volts on new or existing structures that do not exceed more than three stories above the ground**.

This makes C-2F a residential-focused electrical classification. Candidates should understand both the voltage limitation and the building-height limitation when evaluating the type of work authorized by the classification.

Residential wiring work can involve services, feeders, branch circuits, panelboards, grounding and bonding, conductors, raceways, receptacles, switches, lighting, appliances, overcurrent devices, and other electrical equipment within the authorized scope.

The C-2F trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-2F study plan should begin with **Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits**. Review service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, feeder arrangements, panelboards, branch circuits, load relationships, and common residential installations.

**General Electrical Knowledge** should include voltage, current, resistance, power, terminology, circuit relationships, and the basic principles needed to understand electrical code requirements.

**Grounding and Bonding** deserves substantial attention. Candidates should understand grounding electrode systems, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, grounded conductors, bonding, and the purpose of an effective fault-current path.

**Conductors and Cables** should include conductor materials, ampacity, insulation types, temperature ratings, cable assemblies, identification, and sizing considerations. Candidates should practice finding conductor tables and understanding when correction or adjustment factors affect conductor selection.

**Overcurrent Protection** should include circuit breakers, fuses, conductor protection, equipment protection, branch-circuit relationships, and the role of overcurrent devices in protecting residential electrical systems.

**Raceways and Enclosures** should include common raceway types, boxes, fittings, support, physical protection, and conductor-fill concepts.

**Electrical Power** preparation should include basic formulas and calculations. The included Ugly's Electrical References can support this study, but candidates should eventually practice common calculations without relying on the book because it is not available during the trade examination.

**Low Voltage** and **Fire and Intrusion Detection and Alarm Systems** should also be included in preparation. These are smaller exam categories, but every question can contribute toward the required passing score.

**Safety Information** should be studied carefully because OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 is not available in the trade exam room. Candidates should review electrical hazards, personal protective equipment, ladders, tools, fall protection, and general construction safety practices.

The **2017 National Electrical Code** should be used regularly during study. Practice navigating Article 100 definitions, Chapter 2 wiring and protection requirements, Chapter 3 wiring methods and materials, equipment articles, and tables that apply to residential installations.

The highlighted and tabbed format can help candidates move more efficiently through the NEC. Use the tabs as navigation tools while also learning the logic of the book so the correct section can still be located even when the exact article number is not immediately remembered.

Practice questions can be divided by topic at first. Work separately on services, grounding, conductors, overcurrent protection, raceways, calculations, and safety. Once those subjects become more familiar, move into mixed practice.

## How does the Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) exam work?

The Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
