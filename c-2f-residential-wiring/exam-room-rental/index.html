Exam Details

The Nevada C-2F Residential Wiring Contractor trade examination contains 50 questions. Candidates have 2 hours to complete the examination and must answer 35 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • General Electrical Knowledge: 9 questions
  • Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits: 10 questions
  • Grounding and Bonding: 7 questions
  • Conductors and Cables: 7 questions
  • Overcurrent Protection: 4 questions
  • Electrical Power: 2 questions
  • Low Voltage: 2 questions
  • Fire and Intrusion Detection and Alarm Systems: 2 questions
  • Safety Information: 4 questions
  • Raceways and Enclosures: 3 questions

Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits is the largest examination category with 10 questions. Candidates should be comfortable with residential service equipment, feeders, panelboards, branch circuits, disconnecting means, circuit relationships, and applicable National Electrical Code provisions.

General Electrical Knowledge accounts for 9 questions and provides a foundation for many of the other subjects. Preparation should include electrical terminology, voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, equipment, and practical residential wiring principles.

Grounding and Bonding and Conductors and Cables each account for 7 questions. Because these two categories represent a significant part of the examination, candidates should spend substantial study time on conductor selection, ampacity, grounding terminology, bonding requirements, and related NEC provisions.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-2F Residential Wiring as a subclassification within the C-2 Electrical classification.

The C-2F scope covers the installation, alteration, and repair of electrical wires, fixtures, appliances, and apparatuses whose electrical current does not exceed 600 volts on new or existing structures that do not exceed more than three stories above the ground.

The scope makes C-2F a residential-focused electrical classification. Candidates should understand both the voltage limitation and the building-height limitation when evaluating the work authorized under the classification.

Residential wiring work can involve services, feeders, branch circuits, panelboards, conductors, grounding and bonding, raceways, receptacles, switches, lighting, appliances, overcurrent protective devices, and other electrical equipment within the authorized scope.

Residential electrical systems require coordination between multiple code concepts. Conductor sizing must work with overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding must provide an effective fault-current path, and branch circuits must be properly matched to connected loads and equipment.

The C-2F trade examination is one part of Nevada contractor licensing. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, financial, and other licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-2F study plan should begin with Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits. Review service conductors, service equipment, disconnecting means, feeders, panelboards, branch circuits, load relationships, and common residential wiring arrangements.

General Electrical Knowledge should include voltage, current, resistance, power, terminology, circuit relationships, and the practical principles needed to understand residential electrical systems.

Grounding and Bonding deserves substantial attention. Candidates should understand grounding electrode systems, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, grounded conductors, bonding, and the purpose of an effective fault-current path.

The terminology used in grounding and bonding questions can be especially important. Candidates should practice distinguishing among similarly named conductors and understanding the function of each within the electrical system.

Conductors and Cables should include conductor materials, insulation, ampacity, temperature limitations, cable assemblies, conductor identification, and sizing considerations. Candidates should become comfortable using NEC tables and related notes.

Overcurrent Protection should include circuit breakers, fuses, conductor protection, equipment protection, and the relationship between overcurrent devices, branch circuits, feeders, and conductor ampacity.

Raceways and Enclosures should include raceway types, boxes, fittings, support, physical protection, and conductor-fill concepts. Candidates should understand how wiring methods and enclosures interact with conductor installations.

Electrical Power preparation should include common electrical relationships and basic calculations. Candidates should work through calculations repeatedly so mathematical steps become familiar under timed conditions.

Low Voltage and Fire and Intrusion Detection and Alarm Systems should also be included in preparation. Although these categories represent fewer questions, each correct answer contributes toward the passing score.

Safety Information should be treated as retained trade knowledge. Residential electrical work involves electrical hazards, tools, ladders, temporary power conditions, personal protective equipment, and other construction safety concerns.

The 2017 National Electrical Code should be used throughout the six-month course access period. Start with the structure of the book, then work through the articles and tables that relate most directly to the C-2F exam categories.

Article 100 definitions deserve regular review because the meaning of a code term can determine which requirement applies. Understanding terms such as service, feeder, branch circuit, grounded conductor, grounding electrode conductor, and equipment grounding conductor helps candidates navigate more effectively.

Repeat those drills until the process of moving from the question to the correct NEC section becomes more natural. The goal is not to memorize every section number, but to understand the organization of the code well enough to find information efficiently.

How does the Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) exam work?

The Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
