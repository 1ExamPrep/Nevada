## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-2F study plan should begin with **Service, Feeders, and Branch Circuits**. Review service conductors, service equipment, disconnects, feeder arrangements, branch circuits, panelboards, load relationships, and common residential applications.

**General Electrical Knowledge** should include voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, terminology, equipment, and the practical concepts needed to understand code requirements.

**Grounding and Bonding** deserves substantial attention. Candidates should understand grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, grounding electrode conductors, grounded conductors, bonding, and the purpose of an effective fault-current path.

Grounding terminology can be challenging because several terms sound similar but have different meanings. Use the highlighted NEC definitions and grounding sections to reinforce the distinctions during study.

**Conductors and Cables** should include conductor materials, insulation types, ampacity, temperature ratings, conductor identification, cable assemblies, and sizing considerations.

The highlighted and tabbed NEC can help candidates practice locating conductor tables, adjustment factors, correction factors, and notes that can affect conductor selection.

**Overcurrent Protection** should include circuit breakers, fuses, conductor protection, branch-circuit relationships, equipment protection, and the purpose of limiting current during overloads and fault conditions.

**Raceways and Enclosures** should include raceway types, boxes, fittings, protection from physical damage, support, conductor fill, and common installation requirements.

**Electrical Power** preparation should include common electrical formulas and calculations. Ugly's Electrical References can support this study by providing practical formula and conversion information.

Because Ugly's Electrical References is not allowed into the exam, candidates should eventually practice performing common calculations without relying on the book.

**Low Voltage** and **Fire and Intrusion Detection and Alarm Systems** should also be reviewed. Even though these categories contain fewer questions, they still contribute toward the required passing score.

**Safety Information** should be studied carefully because OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 is not available in the exam room. Candidates should review electrical hazards, personal protective equipment, ladders, tools, fall protection, temporary conditions, and general construction safety practices.

The **2017 National Electrical Code** should be used during every study session. The highlighted and tabbed preparation can help candidates return to important sections quickly while building familiarity with the organization of the book.

Start with the table of contents and major chapter structure. Then work through Article 100 definitions, Chapter 2 wiring and protection, Chapter 3 wiring methods and materials, and equipment articles that frequently apply to residential installations.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

The NEC index is also a valuable navigation tool. When the correct article is not immediately known, search for the technical term used in the question and follow the reference to the appropriate section.

Practice reading beyond the first matching sentence. NEC requirements can contain exceptions, related subsections, tables, notes, and conditions that affect how the rule applies.

A useful study exercise is to choose a subject such as service disconnects, grounding electrodes, conductor ampacity, receptacle circuits, overcurrent protection, or raceways and find the applicable code material under a time limit.

Repeat the same type of lookup until the search path becomes familiar. Highlighting and tabs can reduce navigation time, but repetition is what helps candidates recognize where to begin.

Practice questions should first be organized by subject. Work separately on services, grounding, conductors, overcurrent protection, raceways, calculations, and safety. Once those topics are comfortable, move into mixed practice.

Mixed practice is important because the examination moves between different electrical subjects. Candidates should become comfortable switching quickly from a service question to grounding, conductor sizing, raceways, or overcurrent protection.

Time management should also be part of preparation. With 50 questions in two hours, spending too long on one difficult lookup can reduce the time available for easier questions.

A balanced approach is to answer familiar electrical questions efficiently, use the NEC for targeted confirmation, mark difficult items, and return to them after completing the easier portion of the exam.

## How does the Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) exam work?

The Residential Wiring Contractor (C-2F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
