Exam Details

The Nevada C-21A Refrigeration Contractor examination contains 50 questions. Candidates are allowed 2 hours to complete the examination and must answer 35 questions correctly to pass.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Refrigeration and Refrigerants: 17 questions
  • Piping and Tubing: 8 questions
  • Condensers and Cooling Towers: 4 questions
  • Controls, Valves and Wiring: 7 questions
  • Walk-ins/Box Construction: 2 questions
  • Ducts and Ventilation: 2 questions
  • Test and Inspection: 3 questions
  • Gas: 3 questions
  • Safety: 4 questions

Refrigeration and Refrigerants is the largest category with 17 questions. Candidates should understand the refrigeration cycle, refrigerant behavior, compressors, evaporators, condensers, metering devices, system pressures, superheat, subcooling, refrigerant properties, and general refrigeration-system operation.

Piping and Tubing accounts for 8 questions. Preparation should include refrigeration piping materials, fittings, joining methods, line sizing concepts, suction and liquid lines, oil return, supports, insulation, and installation practices.

Controls, Valves and Wiring accounts for 7 questions. Candidates should understand thermostats, pressure controls, relays, contactors, solenoid valves, expansion devices, safety controls, electrical components, and the relationship between controls and refrigeration-system operation.

Condensers, cooling towers, testing, inspection, gas, safety, walk-in construction, ducts, and ventilation make up the remaining questions and should be included in a complete study plan.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-21A Refrigeration as a subclassification under C-21 Refrigeration and Air-Conditioning.

The C-21A scope covers the construction, erection, and installation of devices, machinery, and units used to control air temperatures below 50°F in refrigerators, refrigerated rooms, and insulated spaces.

The classification also includes installation of related insulation, ducts, blowers, registers, humidity controls, and thermostatic controls. This broad scope explains why the exam includes both refrigeration-system questions and topics involving ducts, ventilation, controls, valves, and wiring.

The scope includes lines supplying natural gas from refrigeration equipment to the point of connection with an existing gas line at the meter or at a point thereafter. It also includes specified electrical service from the unit's device protecting against excessive current to the main electrical panel, while excluding other gas or electrical services outside the scope of the classification.

Refrigeration contractors may work with equipment that requires careful coordination of piping, controls, electrical components, insulation, ventilation, and mechanical-system requirements. Candidates should understand how these different parts work together within a complete refrigeration installation.

The C-21A trade examination is one part of the Nevada contractor licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy applicable application, qualification, business and law, and other state licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-21A study plan should begin with Refrigeration and Refrigerants because this category represents 17 of the 50 examination questions.

Candidates should understand the basic refrigeration cycle, including compression, condensation, expansion, and evaporation. Study how refrigerant pressure and temperature change as the refrigerant moves through the system and how the compressor, condenser, metering device, and evaporator work together.

Refrigerants should be reviewed in relation to system operation, pressure-temperature relationships, charging concepts, recovery practices, system conditions, and safe handling. Strong refrigeration fundamentals can help candidates reason through technical questions rather than depending on memorization.

Compressors should be studied according to their purpose and operation. Candidates should understand common compressor types, how compressors affect refrigerant pressure and flow, and how operating conditions can indicate system problems.

Condensers and Cooling Towers account for 4 questions. Review heat rejection, air-cooled and water-cooled condenser operation, airflow, water flow, fan operation, maintenance, and system-performance considerations.

Piping and Tubing deserves significant preparation because it accounts for 8 questions. Study pipe and tubing materials, fittings, joining methods, supports, insulation, suction lines, liquid lines, discharge lines, oil return, and installation practices.

Candidates should understand how poor piping design or installation can affect refrigerant flow, oil return, pressure drop, system capacity, and equipment reliability.

Controls, Valves and Wiring account for 7 questions. Review thermostatic controls, pressure switches, contactors, relays, solenoid valves, expansion devices, safety controls, and basic refrigeration electrical circuits.

Modern refrigeration systems depend on controls to maintain temperature, regulate refrigerant flow, protect equipment, and respond to changes in operating conditions. Candidates should understand the purpose of each component rather than simply memorizing names.

Walk-ins and Box Construction should include insulation, panels, doors, vapor control, temperature maintenance, equipment coordination, and general refrigerated-space construction concepts.

Ducts and Ventilation should include airflow, blowers, registers, duct installation, ventilation requirements, and how air movement interacts with refrigeration equipment and conditioned spaces.

Testing and Inspection should include system checks, leak detection concepts, pressure testing, equipment inspection, commissioning, and recognition of conditions that may indicate improper installation or operation.

Gas preparation should include the gas-related work permitted within the C-21A scope and the relationship between refrigeration equipment and associated fuel connections.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Safety accounts for 4 questions, and OSHA is not allowed into the test. Candidates should therefore use the study reference to build safety knowledge before exam day. Review personal protective equipment, electrical hazards, ladders, tools, material handling, and general construction hazard recognition.

The Uniform Mechanical Code should be used differently from the study-only books because it is available during the exam. Practice finding applicable refrigeration, ventilation, equipment, and piping provisions under timed conditions.

A useful study method is to attempt a practice question from trade knowledge first. If the question involves a specific code requirement, use the UMC to confirm the answer. This helps candidates develop judgment about when a lookup is needed.

The Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd edition should be used to strengthen technical understanding. After studying a section, close the book and explain the system or component from memory. Active recall helps prepare for questions where the study book will not be available.

Timed practice is important. With two hours for 50 questions, candidates should avoid spending too much time on one difficult calculation or code search. Answer familiar questions efficiently and return to more difficult items later when possible.

How does the Refrigeration Contractor (C-21A) exam work?

The Refrigeration Contractor (C-21A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
