The remaining exam subjects include condensers and cooling towers, walk-in and box construction, ducts and ventilation, testing and inspection, gas, and safety. Candidates should include each of these areas in their study plan rather than focusing only on refrigeration theory.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-21A Refrigeration as a subclassification under C-21 Refrigeration and Air-Conditioning.

The classification covers the construction, erection, and installation of devices, machinery, and units used to control air temperatures below 50°F in refrigerators, refrigerated rooms, and insulated spaces.

The scope also includes related insulation, ducts, blowers, registers, humidity controls, and thermostatic controls. This explains why the examination includes refrigeration-system knowledge as well as ventilation, controls, wiring, and equipment-related subjects.

The classification includes certain natural gas connections associated with refrigeration equipment and specified electrical service related to the unit. Candidates should understand that refrigeration contracting can require coordination among mechanical piping, controls, electrical interfaces, ventilation, insulation, and equipment installation.

The C-21A trade examination is one part of the broader Nevada contractor licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy applicable qualification, application, business and law, and other state requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-21A preparation plan should combine refrigeration knowledge with repeated navigation of the Uniform Mechanical Code.

Refrigeration and Refrigerants should receive the largest share of study time because it represents 17 of the 50 exam questions. Candidates should understand the refrigeration cycle and how the compressor, condenser, expansion device, and evaporator work together.

Study refrigerant pressure and temperature relationships, heat absorption, heat rejection, system conditions, and the role of each major component. Understanding the system as a complete cycle can make technical questions easier to analyze.

Compressors should be reviewed in relation to refrigerant flow, pressure changes, lubrication, operating conditions, and system performance. Candidates should understand the purpose of the compressor within the refrigeration cycle and recognize common system relationships.

Condensers should be studied as heat-rejection devices. Candidates should understand the difference between common condenser arrangements and how airflow or water flow affects system performance.

Cooling Towers should be reviewed in connection with heat rejection, water circulation, maintenance, and mechanical-system operation.

Evaporators should be studied in relation to heat absorption, airflow, refrigerant boiling, temperature control, and the overall cooling process.

Piping and Tubing deserves significant attention because it accounts for 8 questions. Review piping materials, fittings, brazed or joined connections, supports, insulation, routing, pressure drop, and oil return.

Candidates should understand the basic differences among suction lines, liquid lines, and discharge lines and how improper piping can affect refrigeration-system operation.

Controls, Valves and Wiring should include thermostats, relays, contactors, pressure switches, solenoid valves, expansion devices, safety controls, and basic control circuits.

Refrigeration controls are used to regulate temperature, manage refrigerant flow, start and stop equipment, and protect the system from unsafe operating conditions. Candidates should understand the purpose of each control rather than memorizing terminology alone.

Walk-ins and Box Construction should include insulated panels, doors, vapor protection, temperature maintenance, equipment coordination, and the relationship between the enclosure and refrigeration system.

Ducts and Ventilation should include airflow, blowers, registers, ductwork, ventilation requirements, and how air movement relates to refrigeration equipment and conditioned spaces.

Testing and Inspection should include system checks, leak detection concepts, pressure testing, equipment inspection, and recognition of installation or operating conditions that may require correction.

Gas preparation should include the gas-related work permitted within the C-21A scope and the connection between refrigeration equipment and fuel supply.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Safety should also be studied as part of the complete trade. Refrigeration contractors may work around electrical equipment, pressurized systems, ladders, tools, piping, refrigerants, moving equipment, and mechanical rooms.

The Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018 should be used throughout preparation. Candidates should not wait until the final days before the exam to begin learning how the code is organized.

Start by reviewing the table of contents and major refrigeration sections. Then use the index to locate subjects involving refrigeration machinery, mechanical equipment, piping, ventilation, ducts, and other relevant topics.

Definitions should receive attention because code terminology can affect the meaning of a question. Knowing how the UMC defines a term can make both the question and the applicable requirement easier to understand.

A useful exercise is to create a list of common refrigeration subjects and practice finding each one in the code. Time each lookup and repeat the slower searches until navigation becomes more efficient.

Practice questions should be completed with the UMC nearby. First attempt the question from technical knowledge, then decide whether a code lookup is necessary. This helps candidates develop judgment about when to use the reference.

Candidates should also practice pacing. If a code search is taking too long, it may be better to move forward and return later rather than spend several minutes on one question.

A project-based study sequence can help organize the material. Start with system design and equipment selection, then move through piping, controls, installation, ventilation, testing, inspection, and system operation. This connects individual exam topics to the way refrigeration work occurs in the field.

How does the Refrigeration Contractor (C-21A) exam work?

The Refrigeration Contractor (C-21A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
