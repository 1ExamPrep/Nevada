The C-4A scope includes the application of **paints, varnishes, protective coatings, shellacs, stains, and other pigments** by brush, spray, or roller. It also includes the installation and application of polyurethane and similar products.

The scope means candidates should understand more than basic interior wall painting. Contractor-level painting work can involve a range of substrates, coating products, application methods, equipment, and protective finishes.

Applicants for a Nevada contractor license generally must document qualifying trade experience within the period required by the Nevada State Contractors Board. The qualifying individual must provide experience that supports the classification being requested.

Nevada also requires qualifying individuals to pass the applicable Business and Law examination and trade examination unless an authorized waiver applies. Application approval and examination eligibility occur through the Nevada State Contractors Board licensing process.

The C-4A examination evaluates trade knowledge, while the Business and Law examination addresses broader contractor responsibilities. Candidates should prepare for both portions of the licensing process separately.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-4A study plan should combine painting trade knowledge with safety preparation and active recall. Since the examination is closed book, candidates should focus on understanding concepts well enough to answer questions without reference assistance.

**Surface Preparation** should receive significant attention. The performance of a coating depends heavily on the condition of the surface underneath it. Candidates should understand cleaning, scraping, sanding, patching, repairing, removing loose material, addressing contamination, and applying the appropriate primer or sealer.

Different substrates can require different preparation methods. Wood, masonry, drywall, previously painted surfaces, and metal each present different conditions that may affect coating selection and application.

**Paint and Coating Materials** should include primers, topcoats, stains, varnishes, shellacs, sealers, protective coatings, and polyurethane products. Candidates should understand the purpose of different materials and how coating systems are selected for specific applications.

**Application Methods** should include brushing, rolling, and spraying. Each method requires knowledge of equipment selection, surface conditions, application technique, coverage, and finish quality.

**Brush and Roller Application** should include selection of appropriate tools, loading the applicator, maintaining consistent coverage, avoiding defects, and achieving the desired finish.

**Spray Application** should include equipment operation, spray pattern, pressure, material preparation, overspray control, masking, ventilation, and safe handling practices.

**Surface Defects and Coating Failures** should also be reviewed. Candidates should understand common causes of poor adhesion, peeling, blistering, cracking, uneven coverage, and other coating problems. Recognizing the cause of a failure can help determine the correct preparation or repair method.

**Estimating and Material Use** can require an understanding of surface area, coverage, material quantities, and practical job planning. Candidates should be comfortable with the basic calculations commonly encountered in painting work.

**Tools and Equipment** should include hand tools, brushes, rollers, spray equipment, ladders, scaffolds, sanding equipment, and other tools used in preparation and application. Candidates should understand both proper use and safe operating practices.

**Construction Safety** should be reviewed using OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Painting contractors frequently work at elevation, making ladder, scaffold, and fall-protection knowledge especially relevant.

**Personal Protective Equipment** should include appropriate protection for eyes, skin, respiratory exposure, and other hazards encountered during preparation and coating application.

## More on test Information and Study Materials

**Hazardous Materials and Solvents** should be approached with an understanding of safe handling, ventilation, storage, and exposure prevention. Contractors should recognize that coatings and related materials can create fire, health, and respiratory hazards if handled improperly.

**Ladders and Scaffolding** deserve dedicated review because painters frequently work above floor or ground level. Candidates should understand safe setup, access, support, use, and general construction safety requirements associated with elevated work.

**Jobsite Organization** can also affect safety and productivity. Proper placement of tools, materials, cords, hoses, ladders, and equipment can reduce hazards and help maintain an efficient work area.

Because the exam is closed book, candidates should turn reading into memory-based practice. After studying a chapter or subject, create questions from the material or use practice questions without looking at the answer first.

If the explanation cannot be completed without reopening the book, that subject may need additional review.

Flashcards can also support closed-book preparation for terminology, equipment, coating characteristics, safety rules, and application concepts. Repetition helps candidates move important information from short-term review into stronger recall.

Study sessions should rotate between painting trade material and OSHA safety content. This keeps preparation balanced and reduces the risk of concentrating only on application techniques while overlooking safety-related questions.

## How does the Painting Contractor (C-4A) exam work?

The Painting Contractor (C-4A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
