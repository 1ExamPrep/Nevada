Exam Details

The Nevada C-4A Painting Contractor examination contains 30 questions. Candidates are allowed 1 hour to complete the examination and must answer 21 questions correctly to pass.

The exam focuses on knowledge associated with painting contractor work, including the preparation of surfaces, use of paint and coating products, application methods, tools, equipment, protective finishes, and safe work practices.

Candidates should understand how different surfaces affect preparation and coating selection. Wood, masonry, drywall, metal, and previously painted surfaces may require different cleaning, patching, sanding, priming, sealing, or conditioning procedures before a finish coat is applied.

Painting materials should also receive careful review. Candidates should understand the purpose of primers, sealers, paints, stains, varnishes, shellacs, protective coatings, and polyurethane products, along with the situations in which different materials are commonly used.

Application methods are another important part of preparation. The work covered by the C-4A classification can involve brush, roller, and spray application, so candidates should understand the tools, techniques, and safety considerations associated with each method.

Jobsite safety is especially important for painting contractors because the work can involve elevated surfaces, ladders, scaffolding, spray equipment, solvents, coatings, power tools, and respiratory exposure. OSHA study should be integrated into the preparation plan rather than treated as a separate subject.

Closed Book Test

The Nevada C-4A Painting Contractor examination is a closed-book test.

The highlighted and tabbed books included with this package are intended for study before the examination. They are not allowed into the exam room.

Because candidates cannot perform reference lookups during the test, preparation should focus on active recall. After studying a section, candidates should close the book and try to explain the topic from memory or answer related practice questions without checking the text.

For example, after reviewing surface preparation, candidates should be able to explain the difference between cleaning, scraping, sanding, patching, priming, and sealing without reopening the book. The same approach can be used for coatings, application methods, tools, equipment, and safety procedures.

The highlighting and tabs in this package are designed to support this kind of study. Tabs help candidates find major subjects quickly during review sessions, while highlighting helps important material stand out when revisiting a topic several times.

Closed-book preparation also makes terminology important. Candidates should become comfortable with common painting terms, coating types, equipment names, surface conditions, and safety terminology so exam questions can be understood quickly.

Repeated review is especially useful when several similar materials or processes can be confused. Candidates should know the differences among primers, sealers, stains, varnishes, topcoats, and protective coatings and understand how each is used within a complete finishing system.

State Requirements

Nevada classifies C-4A Painting as a specialty painting subclassification.

The classification covers the application of paints, varnishes, protective coatings, shellacs, stains, and other pigments by brush, spray, or roller. It also includes the installation and application of polyurethane and similar products.

The scope means candidates should prepare for more than basic interior painting. Contractor-level knowledge can involve surface preparation, coating selection, equipment, application methods, protective finishes, and the relationship between the substrate and the coating system.

Surface condition has a major effect on final coating performance. Dirt, moisture, loose material, contamination, poor repairs, or incompatible coatings can lead to poor adhesion or premature failure. Candidates should understand why preparation is necessary and how different conditions affect the finished result.

Painting contractors must also work safely. Ladders, scaffolds, elevated work areas, power equipment, spraying operations, solvents, and coatings can create hazards that require proper planning and protective measures.

The Nevada licensing process includes requirements beyond the trade exam. Applicants must complete the applicable application, qualification, examination, and other state licensing requirements before receiving the contractor credential.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-4A study plan should combine painting trade review, safety preparation, active recall, and repeated practice. Because the exam is closed book, candidates should aim to understand the material well enough to use it without reference assistance.

Surface Preparation should receive significant attention. Candidates should understand cleaning, scraping, sanding, patching, repairing, removing loose material, treating contaminated surfaces, and preparing substrates before coatings are applied.

Primers and Sealers should be reviewed for their role in preparing surfaces and helping the final coating perform properly. Candidates should understand why certain surfaces require a primer or sealer and how improper preparation can affect adhesion and finish quality.

Paints and Coatings should include different types of finishing materials and their applications. Candidates should understand the general differences among paints, stains, varnishes, shellacs, protective coatings, and polyurethane products.

Brush Application should include brush selection, loading, coverage, finishing technique, and common application considerations.

Roller Application should include roller selection, surface texture, coating distribution, coverage, and maintaining a consistent finish.

Spray Application should include equipment operation, spray pattern, pressure, masking, overspray control, material preparation, ventilation, and safe work practices.

Coating Defects should also be reviewed. Candidates should understand common problems such as peeling, blistering, cracking, poor adhesion, uneven coverage, and other failures that may result from poor preparation or incorrect application.

Tools and Equipment should include brushes, rollers, spray equipment, scrapers, sanding tools, ladders, scaffolds, and other tools used during painting work. Proper selection and safe use are both important.

Estimating and Material Use may involve surface area, coverage, quantity calculations, and basic job planning. Candidates should be comfortable with the practical arithmetic associated with painting projects.

Safety should be studied using OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Painting contractors often work at height, so ladder, scaffold, and fall-protection concepts deserve special attention.

Personal Protective Equipment should include protection for eyes, skin, respiratory exposure, and other hazards associated with preparation, spraying, and coating materials.

Solvents and Coating Materials should be handled with an understanding of ventilation, storage, exposure prevention, and fire hazards. Candidates should recognize that some painting materials require special care even when the work itself appears routine.

More on test Information and Study Materials

The highlighted and tabbed format can help candidates build an efficient study routine. Tabs provide quick access to major subjects, while highlighting helps important concepts stand out during repeated review.

One useful study method is to read a highlighted section, close the book, and explain the main points aloud. If the explanation is incomplete, return to the book and review the material again.

Another method is to create short practice sessions focused on a single subject. One session might cover surface preparation, another coatings, another spray application, and another OSHA safety. Rotating subjects can help strengthen retention.

Flashcards can also help with terminology, coating types, equipment, safety requirements, and application concepts. Closed-book exams reward candidates who can recall key information without needing to search for it.

Practice questions are especially useful when they are followed by review. If a question is missed, candidates should identify the reason, return to the highlighted section, and review the underlying concept rather than memorizing only the correct answer.

Because the examination is short, candidates should also practice pacing. Thirty questions in one hour requires steady progress. Familiarity with terminology and strong recall can reduce time spent second-guessing basic trade concepts.

How does the Painting Contractor (C-4A) exam work?

The Painting Contractor (C-4A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
