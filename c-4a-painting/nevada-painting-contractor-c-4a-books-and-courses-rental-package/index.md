## State Requirements

Nevada classifies **C-4A Painting** as a specialty painting subclassification.

The classification covers the application of **paints, varnishes, protective coatings, shellacs, stains, and other pigments** by brush, spray, or roller. It also includes the installation and application of polyurethane and similar products.

The scope means candidates should prepare for more than basic wall painting. Contractor-level work can involve a variety of surfaces, coating systems, tools, equipment, application methods, and protective finishes.

Surface preparation is especially important because coating performance depends heavily on the condition of the substrate. Dirt, moisture, contamination, loose material, incompatible coatings, or poor repairs can lead to adhesion problems and premature failure.

The Nevada contractor licensing process also includes requirements beyond the trade examination. Applicants must complete the applicable application, qualification, examination, business and law, and other licensing requirements before a contractor credential is issued.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-4A study plan should combine painting trade review, safety preparation, business and law study, and active recall. The closed-book format makes retention especially important.

**Surface Preparation** should receive substantial attention. Candidates should understand cleaning, scraping, sanding, patching, repairing, removing loose material, treating contaminated areas, priming, and sealing.

Different substrates require different approaches. Wood, drywall, masonry, metal, and previously coated surfaces can each require different preparation methods and coating systems.

**Primers and Sealers** should be reviewed for their role in preparing surfaces, improving adhesion, sealing porous materials, and supporting the final coating system.

**Paints and Coatings** should include paints, varnishes, shellacs, stains, protective coatings, and polyurethane products. Candidates should understand their general purposes and common applications.

**Brush Application** should include brush selection, loading, coverage, technique, and maintaining a consistent finish.

**Roller Application** should include roller selection, surface texture, material distribution, coverage, and finish consistency.

**Spray Application** should include spray equipment, pressure, spray pattern, masking, overspray control, ventilation, material preparation, and safe operating practices.

**Coating Defects** should also be reviewed. Candidates should understand common problems such as blistering, peeling, cracking, poor adhesion, and uneven coverage and recognize how poor preparation or application can contribute to these conditions.

**Tools and Equipment** should include brushes, rollers, spray equipment, scrapers, sanding tools, ladders, scaffolds, and related jobsite equipment. Candidates should understand both proper use and safe operation.

**Estimating and Material Use** can involve basic calculations related to surface area, coverage, quantities, and job planning. Candidates should be comfortable with practical arithmetic associated with painting work.

**Safety** should be studied using OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Painting contractors frequently work at elevation, so ladder, scaffold, and fall-related topics deserve focused review.

**Personal Protective Equipment** should include appropriate protection for eyes, skin, respiratory exposure, and other hazards encountered during preparation and coating application.

**Solvents and Coating Materials** should be handled with an understanding of ventilation, storage, exposure prevention, and fire-related hazards. Candidates should recognize the importance of proper handling even when the material is commonly used.

## How does the Painting Contractor (C-4A) exam work?

The Painting Contractor (C-4A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
