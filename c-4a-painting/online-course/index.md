## Exam Details

The Nevada C-4A Painting Contractor trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who receive examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

- **Exam:** Nevada C-4A Painting Contractor
- **Number of Questions:** 30
- **Time Allowed:** 1 hour
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 21 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The C-4A examination content is divided into seven technical categories:

- **Materials:** 6 questions
- **Surface Preparation:** 6 questions
- **Application:** 6 questions
- **Tools:** 4 questions
- **Problems and Prevention:** 2 questions
- **Safety:** 4 questions
- **Color:** 2 questions

Materials, Surface Preparation, and Application are the three largest categories, with six questions each. Together they represent 18 of the 30 examination questions, so candidates should devote substantial preparation time to these subjects.

**Materials** preparation should include common paints, primers, stains, varnishes, shellacs, protective coatings, solvents, thinners, and related products. Candidates should understand how coating selection can depend on the surface, desired finish, application method, and working conditions.

**Surface Preparation** is a major part of both professional painting and the C-4A examination. Candidates should review cleaning, scraping, sanding, patching, priming, removal of loose material, treatment of existing coatings, and preparation procedures for different substrates.

**Application** preparation should cover brushing, rolling, spraying, coating sequence, coverage, film consistency, handling, and practical procedures used to apply paints, stains, varnishes, and protective coatings.

**Tools** contains four questions. Candidates should be familiar with brushes, rollers, spray equipment, scrapers, sanding equipment, ladders, preparation tools, and other equipment commonly used throughout painting projects.

**Safety** also contains four questions. Preparation should include ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, fall hazards, ventilation, respiratory concerns, tools, and the safe handling of coating products and related materials.

**Problems and Prevention** and **Color** contain two questions each. These are smaller categories but should still be included in a complete study plan because every correct answer contributes toward the passing score.

## Closed Book Test

Because the examination is closed book, candidates should prepare differently than they would for an open-book contractor exam. Instead of focusing on page locations or code-book navigation, preparation should emphasize comprehension, repetition, recall, and practical application.

Painting materials should be studied in relation to their intended uses. Candidates should understand the differences among primers, paints, varnishes, stains, shellacs, protective coatings, solvents, and related products and how those materials interact with different surfaces.

OSHA safety information must be learned before test day because the reference cannot be consulted during testing. Candidates should be able to recognize safe procedures involving ladders, scaffolding, personal protective equipment, ventilation, respiratory protection, fall hazards, tools, and hazardous materials.

Practice questions are especially useful for closed-book preparation. Rather than memorizing the wording of an answer, candidates should identify the trade principle behind each question. When an answer is missed, reviewing the underlying topic can help strengthen recall for similar questions.

## State Requirements

The Nevada C-4A Painting Contractor classification is a specialty contractor classification covering qualifying painting and coating work.

The classification includes the application of paints, varnishes, protective coatings, shellacs, stains, and other pigments using brush, spray, or roller methods. Candidates should understand multiple types of coatings and application procedures rather than focusing only on conventional wall painting.

Surface preparation is an important part of the trade because proper coating performance depends heavily on the condition of the substrate. Cleaning, sanding, scraping, patching, priming, and treatment of existing finishes can affect both appearance and durability.

Product selection is also an important part of professional painting. Different surfaces and working conditions may require different primers, coatings, application methods, and preparation procedures.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual generally must have at least four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the requested classification.

Applicants should maintain clear records showing the type of painting work performed and the period during which the qualifying experience was gained. Experience documentation is used as part of establishing the trade qualification for the requested contractor classification.

Nevada contractor licensing distinguishes between management and trade qualification. One individual may fulfill both roles when all applicable requirements are met, or separate qualified individuals may be used.

The Trade Qualified Individual establishes the required trade experience and qualification for C-4A. The Management Qualified Individual generally satisfies the Nevada Business and Law requirement unless an applicable waiver is approved.

Financial responsibility, background requirements, contractor bonding, and other licensing conditions are separate from the trade examination. Passing the C-4A exam does not by itself result in issuance of a Nevada contractor license.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-4A study plan should reflect the examination weighting and closed-book format. Candidates should spend the most time on Materials, Surface Preparation, and Application while still reviewing Tools, Safety, Problems and Prevention, and Color.

**Prioritize Materials.** Review primers, paints, stains, varnishes, shellacs, protective coatings, solvents, thinners, and related products. Focus on how coating selection changes depending on substrate, finish requirements, and application conditions.

**Study Surface Preparation thoroughly.** Review cleaning, scraping, sanding, patching, priming, treatment of existing coatings, and preparation of different substrates. Understand why each step affects coating performance.

**Review Application methods.** Study brushing, rolling, spraying, coating sequence, coverage, handling, and practical workmanship considerations.

**Know the Tools.** Become familiar with brushes, rollers, spray systems, scrapers, sanding tools, ladders, and other equipment used in preparation and coating application.

**Prepare Safety for recall.** Review ladders, scaffolds, PPE, fall protection, ventilation, respiratory protection, tools, and safe handling of paints, coatings, solvents, and related materials.

**Study Problems and Prevention.** Review common coating failures and the conditions that contribute to them. Understanding causes can make prevention and troubleshooting questions easier to approach.

**Review Color principles.** Although Color is a smaller section, candidates should understand basic color concepts relevant to painting and decorating work.

**Use active recall.** After studying a subject, put the book aside and explain the concept from memory. This directly supports preparation for a closed-book examination.

**Review missed questions by category.** Repeated mistakes in one subject should lead to additional focused study rather than simply repeating the same practice test without reviewing the underlying material.

**Practice under timed conditions.** Candidates have one hour for 30 questions. Timed practice can help establish a steady pace and improve comfort answering without access to reference books.

## How does the Painting Contractor (C-4A) exam work?

The Painting Contractor (C-4A) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
