Test Information and Study Materials

The Nevada Low Voltage Electrician (ICC - G21-N) Exam Book Package supports preparation by giving candidates the core references needed for code-based study. Low-voltage exam preparation should focus on the NEC articles and trade concepts most closely related to limited-energy systems. Candidates should study actively by locating rules in the code, answering practice questions, reviewing missed answers, and repeating the process until reference navigation becomes familiar.

General knowledge study should include NEC organization, definitions, electrical safety, basic electrical theory, conductor terminology, cable classifications, wiring method concepts, grounding and bonding basics, and the difference between power circuits and limited-energy circuits. Candidates should know how to use the NEC index and how to follow article structure from general rules to more specific requirements.

Remote-control and alarm system topics should include power-limited circuits, control wiring, signaling circuits, fire alarm wiring concepts, cable types, circuit classifications, installation methods, separation from other conductors, support, protection, and the use of listed equipment and materials. Candidates should understand that low-voltage circuits may still have strict installation requirements.

Communications and sound system study should include communications wiring, audio system wiring, cable routing, cable ratings, plenum and riser concepts, grounding and bonding where applicable, equipment connection concepts, and the rules that separate communications circuits from other types of electrical circuits. These topics are important because communications and sound systems make up a large portion of low-voltage work.

Control circuit preparation should include circuit classification, conductor selection, cable use, wiring methods, protection from physical damage, and safe installation practices. Candidates should be able to identify whether a question is asking about a control circuit, alarm circuit, communications circuit, or another limited-energy system, because different NEC rules may apply.

Fiber optic preparation should include optical fiber cable types, installation requirements, routing, support, protection, and separation from other conductors where required. Fiber optic systems have their own terminology, so candidates should spend time learning the language used in the NEC and related trade references.

Cable ratings are especially important for low-voltage candidates. Candidates should study where different cable types may be installed, how plenum and riser spaces affect cable selection, how cables must be supported and protected, and how cable substitution rules work. Using the wrong cable type can create code and safety problems.

How does the Low Voltage Electrician (ICC G21) exam work?

The Low Voltage Electrician (ICC G21) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
  • Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
  • ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
  • Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
