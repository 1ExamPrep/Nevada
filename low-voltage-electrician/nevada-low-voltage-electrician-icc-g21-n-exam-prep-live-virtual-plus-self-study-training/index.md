## Exam Details

The ICC G21-N National Standard Low-Voltage Electrician examination is based on NFPA 70, National Electrical Code, 2017 Edition. The examination contains 50 multiple-choice questions, and candidates are allowed three hours to complete it.

The examination focuses on code requirements and electrical knowledge associated with low-voltage wiring and equipment. Candidates should prepare for direct code questions, system-identification questions, installation scenarios, grounding and bonding requirements, cable-use questions, and basic electrical calculations.

Important areas of preparation include:

- General electrical knowledge and terminology
- Electrical safety and basic theory
- Low-voltage circuit classifications
- Remote-control and signaling circuits
- Class 1, Class 2, and Class 3 circuits
- Power-limited circuit requirements
- Fire alarm circuit concepts
- Communications circuits and equipment
- Optical fiber cables and raceways
- Cable markings and permitted uses
- Conductor and cable support
- Separation from electric light and power conductors
- Grounding and bonding requirements
- Building entrances and protection methods
- Raceways, boxes, fittings, and enclosures
- Environmental and physical-protection requirements
- Basic electrical formulas and calculations
- Testing, troubleshooting, and system verification concepts

Class 1, Class 2, and Class 3 circuits are central preparation areas. Candidates should understand how these circuits are classified, the wiring methods permitted for each category, where cables may be installed, and how low-voltage conductors must be separated from power and lighting conductors.

Remote-control and signaling questions may involve control circuits, signaling equipment, cable ratings, circuit power limitations, enclosures, raceways, support requirements, and permitted methods of installation. Candidates should practice distinguishing between a general wiring rule and a specialized power-limited circuit provision.

Communications questions may involve cable types, communications raceways, building entrances, grounding, bonding, equipment locations, abandoned cable, support, fire-resistance classifications, and separation from other electrical conductors.

Optical fiber questions may address conductive and nonconductive cable types, cable markings, raceways, support, routing, building spaces, separation, and the conditions under which optical fiber cables may share an enclosure or pathway with other conductors.

Grounding and bonding questions may require candidates to identify the correct conductor, connection point, routing method, or protection requirement. Preparation should include the general grounding concepts in Article 250 along with the specialized grounding provisions found in the applicable low-voltage and communications articles.

## State Requirements

Nevada electrical contractor licensing and individual electrical card requirements are separate regulatory matters. The Nevada State Contractors Board licenses contracting businesses and qualifying individuals, while local jurisdictions and recognized card providers may establish credentials for individuals performing electrical work.

Passing the ICC G21-N examination does not automatically issue a Nevada contractor license, journeyman card, low-voltage card, or municipal credential. The organization receiving the examination result determines whether the exam is accepted and what additional requirements apply.

Nevada contractor applicants generally must complete the Nevada State Contractors Board application process and pass the examinations required for the requested contractor classification unless a waiver or endorsement provision applies. That process is separate from an ICC low-voltage electrician examination.

Low-voltage contracting work may fall within a Nevada contractor classification based on the nature and scope of the systems being installed. Operating a contracting business, advertising contracting services, submitting bids, obtaining permits, or assuming responsibility for contracted work may require an appropriate Nevada contractor license.

Requirements for individual workers may vary by jurisdiction. A city or card provider may require an approved examination, identification card, apprenticeship status, documented training, employer supervision, continuing education, or periodic renewal.

The G21-N examination uses the 2017 National Electrical Code. Nevada electrical card administrators announced that 2017 NEC-based testing would be accepted only through March 31, 2026. Beginning April 1, 2026, only qualifying examinations based on the 2020 or 2023 NEC are accepted for Nevada Electrical Card issuance through that program.

Candidates preparing for the G21-N examination for an employer, an existing application, professional development, or another authority should confirm that the 2017 NEC-based examination remains valid for that specific purpose before scheduling the test.

Current Nevada jurisdictions may also enforce a newer NEC edition for field installations. Candidates should use the 2017 NEC when preparing for the G21-N examination and follow the code edition and local amendments currently adopted by the authority having jurisdiction when performing electrical work.

## Test Information and Study Materials

Begin preparation by learning how general NEC rules connect with specialized low-voltage articles. Review Article 300 wiring requirements and Article 250 grounding concepts before moving into power-limited, alarm, communications, and optical fiber systems.

Study circuit classifications carefully. Practice distinguishing Class 1, Class 2, and Class 3 circuits and identifying the wiring methods, cable types, equipment, and separation rules associated with each classification.

Review cable markings and permitted uses. Candidates should understand how cable ratings relate to plenums, risers, general-purpose spaces, raceways, environmental conditions, and building construction.

Give separation requirements focused attention. Practice identifying when low-voltage cables may occupy the same enclosure, raceway, cable tray, or pathway as power conductors and when barriers, separation, or different wiring methods are required.

Study communications grounding, building entrances, primary protectors, bonding connections, cable routing, and abandoned-cable requirements. Review both the applicable communications article and related general NEC provisions.

Practice optical fiber questions involving conductive and nonconductive cables, cable support, pathways, markings, building spaces, and separation from electric light and power conductors.

Use Ugly's Electrical References to reinforce Ohm’s law, power calculations, voltage drop, conductor properties, electrical units, and conversions. Calculation questions should be practiced until the correct formula and units can be identified quickly.

Complete timed practice using the 2017 NEC. Read the question, identify the system, locate the likely article, review the applicable provision and exceptions, and move forward. Mark difficult questions for later review instead of allowing one item to consume too much examination time.

## How does the Low Voltage Electrician (ICC G21) exam work?

The Low Voltage Electrician (ICC G21) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

- Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
- Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
- ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
- Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
