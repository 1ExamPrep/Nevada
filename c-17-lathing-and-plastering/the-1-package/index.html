Exam Details

The Nevada C-17 Lathing and Plastering trade examination is part of the contractor licensing qualification process. The Nevada State Contractors Board evaluates applicants and authorizes eligible qualifying individuals to proceed with the required examinations. PSI administers Nevada contractor licensing examinations.

C-17 preparation should address the broad scope of the classification. The Nevada C-17 license encompasses multiple types of interior and exterior finish work rather than a single plastering task. Candidates should be prepared to study the selection and installation of lath, plaster materials and coatings, gypsum systems, stucco and cement coatings, acoustical tile applications, nonstructural sheet-metal studs, surface preparation, installation methods, finishing, and construction safety.

This is particularly useful because the C-17 classification also includes work authorized under the drywall subclassification.

Closed Book Test

The Nevada C-17 Lathing and Plastering trade examination is a closed-book test. The included Plastering Skills, OSHA, and Gypsum Construction Handbook materials are used for preparation and are not available for reference during the trade examination.

Closed-book preparation requires a different study strategy from an open-book contractor exam. Candidates cannot rely on searching a reference during testing, so the goal should be to understand the material well enough to recognize correct procedures, materials, safety requirements, and construction concepts from memory.

Use the highlighted and tabbed books to organize study before examination day. Highlighting can draw attention to important concepts, while tabs can make repeated review more efficient. Although the books cannot be used during the trade test, organized preparation can help candidates revisit difficult topics and strengthen retention.

Plastering preparation should emphasize the relationship between substrates, bases, lath, plaster materials, mixing, application, curing, base coats, finish coats, and completed surfaces. Candidates should understand why specific methods are used, not simply memorize isolated terms.

Safety preparation should focus on recognizing proper jobsite procedures. Since OSHA information cannot be looked up during the examination, candidates should be familiar with common construction hazards and safe work practices associated with scaffolds, ladders, tools, personal protective equipment, material handling, and work at elevation.

Gypsum construction preparation should include wallboard and gypsum products, installation methods, finishing procedures, ceilings, partitions, assemblies, accessories, and other gypsum-related concepts connected to the C-17 classification.

Practice-oriented study is especially important for a closed-book exam. Candidates can use practice questions to test whether they truly understand a subject. Missed questions should be reviewed by topic so additional study can be directed toward plastering, lathing, gypsum construction, safety, or another weak area.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-17 classification authorizes a qualified license holder to perform the work included within the C-17 lathing and plastering subclassifications.

Lathing: The work includes selecting and applying wood and metal lath and other products designed to provide a base for plaster coatings. It can include channel iron used to support fireproof lath and solid plaster partitions within the authorized scope.

Plastering: The work includes coating surfaces with plaster made from materials such as sand, cement, quicklime, hydrated lime, and water, along with installation of lath or another suitable base for the plaster.

Drywall: The C-17 classification also includes the work authorized under the drywall subclassification, including installation and coating of gypsum wallboard and sheathing and installation of suspended ceiling systems made from gypsum wallboard.

Acoustical Tile: The classification includes installation of wood, mineral, fiber, and other types of acoustical tile by adhesives or other accepted methods, along with preparation of the installation surface.

Coatings of Stucco and Cement: The scope includes manual or mechanical application of stucco and cement coatings, preparation of surfaces, provision of the appropriate base for coatings, and related fireproofing work within the classification.

Studs of Sheet Metal: C-17 also includes erection and installation of sheet-metal studs used to frame ceilings and nonstructural partition walls.

For the standard Nevada licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual must generally document at least four years of experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the specific classification requested. Current Nevada licensing guidance states that this experience must generally have been obtained within the 10 years immediately preceding the filing of the application.

Nevada contractor applicants must also satisfy applicable management qualification requirements. A Business and Law examination is generally required in addition to the trade examination unless the qualifying individual qualifies for an approved waiver.

Financial responsibility, bonding, and background requirements are also part of Nevada contractor licensing. These requirements are separate from passing the trade examination and must be completed before the contractor license is issued.

How does the Lathing and Plastering Contractor (C-17) exam work?

The Lathing and Plastering Contractor (C-17) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
