Exam Details

The Nevada Lathing and Plastering Contractor (C-17) trade examination evaluates knowledge associated with lathing, plastering, gypsum-related construction, materials, tools, installation methods, finishing, and jobsite safety.

Candidates should understand the complete lathing and plastering system rather than concentrating only on the final finish. Proper plaster work begins with the supporting surface and includes substrate preparation, lath selection and attachment, plaster materials, mixing, application, curing, finishing, and protection of the completed work.

Plastering Skills, 1984 supports preparation involving plaster materials, lath, plaster bases, mixing, application methods, tools, coats, curing, and finishing. Candidates should understand the purpose of the different stages of plaster installation and how errors in preparation or application can affect the finished surface.

Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th edition supports preparation involving gypsum board, wall and ceiling systems, framing, fastening, joints, finishing, and assembly construction. Gypsum systems are an important part of modern interior construction and can overlap with the knowledge expected from contractors working in lathing and plastering.

OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports construction-safety study. Lathing and plastering work can involve scaffolds, ladders, elevated work, tools, material handling, overhead installation, mixing equipment, and other hazards that require candidates to understand safe jobsite practices.

The included Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada supports preparation for contractor business and law topics that are separate from the C-17 trade exam but still important to the overall Nevada contractor licensing process.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada C-17 trade examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable qualification and licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the exam.

  1. Confirm the C-17 classification. Make sure the work the business intends to perform falls within Nevada's Lathing and Plastering classification.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor license application must designate the person responsible for providing the applicable trade qualification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable work history showing experience related to lathing, plastering, gypsum systems, finishing, and associated trade responsibilities.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Submit the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. The application and qualifying experience are reviewed before examination eligibility is established.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. Once authorized, the qualifying individual can proceed with scheduling the required examinations.
  7. Prepare for the C-17 trade exam. Study lath, plaster, gypsum construction, tools, materials, installation methods, finishing, and safety.
  8. Prepare for Nevada business and law. Use the included Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada and course materials to study the applicable business and law topics.
  9. Complete applicable examinations. Satisfy the examination requirements associated with the contractor license application unless an authorized waiver applies.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish the other applicable requirements before the Nevada contractor license is issued.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-17 Lathing and Plastering as a specialty contractor classification. The classification covers work involving lath and plaster systems performed within the authorized scope of the license.

Lathing work involves installing materials that provide a base or key for plaster coatings. Candidates should understand the purpose of lath, how it is supported, how it is attached, and how improper installation can affect plaster performance.

Plastering work involves applying plaster materials over properly prepared bases. Candidates should understand plaster materials, mixing, coat sequence, application, leveling, floating, finishing, curing, and the conditions that can affect bond and finished quality.

The scope requires an understanding of the complete system rather than isolated finishing techniques. Substrates, supports, lath, plaster materials, gypsum systems, tools, jobsite conditions, and safety all contribute to successful work.

The Nevada contractor licensing process also includes requirements beyond the trade examination. Applicants must complete the applicable application, qualification, business, financial, examination, and licensing requirements before a contractor license is issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-17 preparation plan should combine highlighted and tabbed reference review, course study, active recall, trade questions, and closed-book practice.

Lathing should be studied for materials, attachment methods, support, substrates, and the role of lath in creating a suitable base for plaster. Candidates should understand how lath affects bond, stability, cracking, and overall performance.

Plaster Materials should include the ingredients used in plaster and the way those materials affect workability, strength, adhesion, application, and finishing. Understanding why a material is used is more valuable than memorizing isolated terms.

Surface Preparation should receive careful attention. Plaster can fail when applied over an unsuitable or poorly prepared base. Candidates should study substrate condition, cleanliness, suction, bonding, and the need for an appropriate plaster base.

Plaster Application should include coat sequence, application methods, leveling, floating, finishing, and the purpose of each stage. Candidates should be able to explain the process from preparation through final finish without depending on the book.

Curing should be understood as part of the overall plastering process. Conditions such as temperature, moisture, wind, and rapid drying can affect the performance of cement-based plaster.

Gypsum Systems should include gypsum board, framing, wall and ceiling systems, fastening, joints, finishing, and complete assembly concepts. Candidates should understand how different components work together rather than treating each material separately.

Tools and Equipment should be studied by function. Know which tools are used for mixing, applying, leveling, finishing, cutting, fastening, measuring, and installing plaster or gypsum systems.

Scaffolds and Ladders deserve focused safety review because lathing and plastering frequently takes place above floor level. Candidates should understand basic safe access, platform use, and fall-prevention concepts.

Personal Protective Equipment should be connected to realistic trade hazards. Dust, cutting, mixing, overhead work, hand tools, and material handling can all require appropriate protection.

Material Handling should include safe movement and storage of lath, gypsum products, plaster materials, tools, and equipment. Proper handling supports both jobsite safety and material quality.

How does the Lathing and Plastering Contractor (C-17) exam work?

The Lathing and Plastering Contractor (C-17) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
