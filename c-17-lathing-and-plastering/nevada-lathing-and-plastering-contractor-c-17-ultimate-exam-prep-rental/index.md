## Exam Details

The Nevada C-17 Lathing and Plastering trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who have received examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

- **Exam:** Nevada C-17 Lathing and Plastering
- **Number of Questions:** 45
- **Time Allowed:** 1.5 hours
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 32 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The C-17 examination content outline is divided into six technical subject areas:

- **Safety:** 5 questions
- **Surface Preparation:** 11 questions
- **Drywall:** 5 questions
- **Application:** 10 questions
- **Base and Finish Coat:** 9 questions
- **Sheet Metal:** 5 questions

Surface Preparation is the largest individual category with 11 questions. Candidates should understand how the condition of a substrate affects lath, plaster, stucco, cement coatings, gypsum systems, and the completed finish. Preparation should include cleaning, inspection, preparation of bases, moisture-related considerations, attachment surfaces, bonding conditions, and methods used to create an appropriate surface for subsequent work.

Application contains 10 questions and is another major part of the examination. Candidates should study practical application methods, material placement, hand and mechanical application, plaster and stucco procedures, work sequencing, tools, and methods used to produce a consistent finished system.

Base and Finish Coat contains nine questions. Candidates should understand the relationship between base materials, scratch and brown coat concepts, finish coats, thickness, curing, preparation between coats, and finishing techniques used in plaster and stucco work.

Safety contains five questions. OSHA-related preparation should include ladders, scaffolds, fall hazards, personal protective equipment, tools, material handling, respiratory concerns, and other construction hazards associated with lathing, plastering, drywall, and stucco operations.

Drywall contains five questions. Candidates should review gypsum wallboard and sheathing, installation concepts, finishing, suspended gypsum ceilings, related accessories, and other drywall work included within the broader C-17 classification.

Sheet Metal also contains five questions. Preparation should include the erection and installation of sheet-metal studs used to frame ceilings and nonstructural partition walls, along with related construction considerations.

## State Requirements

The Nevada C-17 classification covers multiple related areas of specialty construction. Understanding the classification scope is important because the examination reflects more than traditional plaster application alone.

The lathing portion includes selection and application of wood and metal lath and other materials manufactured to provide a suitable base for plaster coatings. This includes supporting materials associated with fireproof lath and solid plaster partitions within the authorized scope.

The plastering portion includes coating surfaces with plaster materials made from combinations such as sand, cement, quicklime, hydrated lime, and water, as well as installing lath or another suitable plaster base.

The classification also includes installation and coating of gypsum wallboard and sheathing, including suspended ceiling systems made of gypsum wallboard. This is why drywall appears as a separate examination category.

Acoustical tile work within the classification includes installation of wood, mineral, fiber, and other types of acoustical tile using adhesives or other accepted methods, including preparation of the installation surface.

The C-17 scope also includes applying stucco and cement coatings by manual or mechanical methods, preparing surfaces for those coatings, providing an appropriate base, and performing related fireproofing work within the classification.

Sheet-metal stud work includes erection and installation of sheet-metal studs used to frame ceilings and nonstructural partition walls. Candidates should understand how this work relates to lathing, drywall, and interior finish construction.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the Trade Qualified Individual generally must demonstrate at least four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the requested classification.

Nevada contractor licensing also requires the applicant to satisfy applicable financial responsibility, background, bond, trade qualification, and management qualification requirements before the license can be issued.

## How does the Lathing and Plastering Contractor (C-17) exam work?

The Lathing and Plastering Contractor (C-17) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
