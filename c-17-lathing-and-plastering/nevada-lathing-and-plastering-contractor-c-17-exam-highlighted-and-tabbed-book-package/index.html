Exam Details

The Nevada Lathing and Plastering Contractor (C-17) trade examination evaluates the technical knowledge associated with lath installation, plastering, gypsum-related systems, tools, materials, finishing, and construction safety.

Candidates should understand the relationship between the supporting base and the finished plaster system. Proper preparation begins before plaster is mixed or applied. The type of substrate, condition of the surface, method of lath attachment, and compatibility of the materials can all affect the performance of the finished work.

Lathing preparation should include wood lath, metal lath, and other products used to create a suitable base or key for plaster. Candidates should understand why lath is required in particular conditions, how it is supported and attached, and how installation quality affects cracking, bond, and overall system performance.

Plastering preparation should include materials, mixing, application, coat sequence, curing, finishing, tools, and jobsite procedures. Candidates should be able to recognize the correct sequence of work and understand why each step is necessary.

Gypsum construction is another important preparation area. Candidates should study wall and ceiling assemblies, framing, gypsum board attachment, finishing, joints, and related installation principles. The Gypsum Construction Handbook can help reinforce these topics as part of broader C-17 trade preparation.

Safety should be incorporated throughout study. Lathing and plastering work frequently involves scaffolds, ladders, overhead work, mixing equipment, tools, dust, material handling, and work at elevated locations. OSHA preparation should therefore be connected to realistic trade scenarios rather than treated as a separate subject.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada C-17 examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Candidates must complete the applicable licensing requirements in addition to preparing for the trade examination.

  1. Confirm the C-17 classification. Make sure the lathing and plastering work the business intends to perform falls within the Nevada C-17 scope.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor license application must identify the person providing the applicable trade qualification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize applicable work history related to lathing, plastering, gypsum systems, surface preparation, finishing, and associated trade responsibilities.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Submit the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. The licensing application and qualifying experience must be reviewed before examination eligibility is established.
  6. Receive examination eligibility. Once authorized, the qualifying individual can proceed with scheduling the applicable contractor examination.
  7. Prepare for the C-17 trade examination. Study plastering, lathing, gypsum construction, tools, materials, finishing, and safety.
  8. Use closed-book practice. Because the exam does not allow reference books, complete increasing amounts of practice without the study materials open.
  9. Complete applicable examinations. Satisfy the examination requirements associated with the contractor license application.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish all other applicable requirements before the Nevada contractor license is issued.

State Requirements

Nevada recognizes C-17 Lathing and Plastering as a specialty contractor classification. The scope includes lathing and plastering work performed within the authority of the classification.

Lathing work includes selecting and applying lath and related products used to create a suitable base for plaster coatings. Candidates should understand how the lath functions as part of the overall plaster system and why proper installation is essential.

Plastering work includes coating surfaces with plaster materials and installing the lath or other base necessary to support the plaster. This makes C-17 preparation broader than simply learning how to create a finished surface.

Candidates should understand the complete system from the supporting structure through the final finish. Substrates, lath, plaster materials, coats, curing, gypsum components, and jobsite conditions can all affect the quality of the finished work.

The Nevada contractor licensing process also includes requirements beyond the C-17 trade examination. Applicants must complete the applicable qualification, application, examination, financial, and licensing requirements before a contractor license can be issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-17 study plan should combine highlighted reference review with closed-book practice. The purpose of the markings is to help candidates organize preparation and return to important subjects more efficiently while studying.

Lathing should be studied for materials, attachment, support, spacing concepts, substrates, and the role of lath in providing a base for plaster. Candidates should understand what can go wrong when lath is improperly installed and how that affects the finished system.

Plaster Materials should include the basic ingredients used in plaster, their purpose, and how mixing and application affect the final result. Candidates should understand the relationship between workable material, proper proportions, surface preparation, and successful finishing.

Surface Preparation deserves focused review because even properly mixed plaster can fail when the base is unsuitable. Study cleanliness, suction, bonding, substrate condition, and the role of the plaster base.

Plaster Application should include the sequence of coats, application methods, leveling, floating, finishing, and the purpose of each stage. Candidates should know why one coat cannot simply be treated as interchangeable with another.

Curing should be reviewed as part of plaster performance. Candidates should understand how environmental conditions and moisture affect cement-based plaster and why improper curing can contribute to poor results.

Gypsum Systems should include gypsum board, wall and ceiling systems, framing, fastening, joints, finishing, and the basic construction principles used to create complete assemblies.

Fire-Resistance Concepts should be reviewed through the Gypsum Construction Handbook. Candidates should understand that complete assemblies rely on specified materials and construction methods working together.

Tools and Equipment should be studied by function. Learn which tools are used for mixing, applying, leveling, finishing, cutting, fastening, measuring, and installing lath or gypsum materials.

Scaffolds and Ladders should receive dedicated OSHA review. Lathing and plastering frequently involves elevated wall and ceiling work, so candidates should be able to recognize unsafe access and working conditions.

Personal Protective Equipment should be connected to the actual hazards of the trade. Cutting, mixing, dust, tools, overhead work, and material handling can all create situations requiring appropriate protection.

Material Handling should include safe movement and storage of plaster materials, gypsum products, lath, tools, and equipment. Candidates should understand how damaged materials or unsafe storage practices can affect both quality and safety.

How does the Lathing and Plastering Contractor (C-17) exam work?

The Lathing and Plastering Contractor (C-17) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
