Open Book Test

The Nevada C-1D Plumbing Contractor examination is an open-book test.

The following books in this package are approved for exam-room use:

  • Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018
  • NFPA 54 - Standard for National Fuel Gas Code, 2012
  • NFPA 58 - Standard for Liquid Petroleum Gas Code, 2014
  • Uniform Mechanical Code, 2012

Candidates should use these references throughout preparation rather than waiting until test day to become familiar with them. Knowing which book is most likely to contain the applicable requirement can save valuable time.

For example, questions involving drainage, water supply, traps, cleanouts, fixtures, and storm drainage will typically lead candidates toward the Uniform Plumbing Code. Natural gas and fuel-gas questions may require NFPA 54, while LP-Gas questions may require NFPA 58. Mechanical or hydronic questions may be supported by the Uniform Mechanical Code.

With 80 questions in three hours, candidates have a little over two minutes per question on average. Searching through multiple code books without a clear plan can consume time quickly. Effective open-book preparation means learning the trade concepts first, then using the references strategically.

Timed navigation drills can help candidates become faster. Choose a subject, identify the correct reference, locate the applicable section or table, and repeat the process until the book structure becomes familiar.

State Requirements

Nevada's C-1D Plumbing classification covers plumbing work involving sanitary conditions in buildings through a permanent supply of safe water and sanitary waste and vent systems.

The classification also includes roof drainage systems, landscape irrigation systems, systems used to heat potable water including solar equipment, and hydronic heating systems not exceeding 200,000 British thermal units.

C-1D work also includes applicable gas supply systems and piping for compressed air, vacuums, and gases used for medical purposes. The scope reflects why the examination covers a wide range of plumbing, gas, hydronic, and mechanical subjects.

For contractor qualification, Nevada generally requires the qualifying individual to have the applicable level of experience in the trade. The licensing process includes qualification, application, trade examination, business and law, and other requirements before the license is issued.

The breadth of the C-1D classification makes code navigation especially important. Candidates should understand which systems fall under plumbing, fuel gas, LP-Gas, or mechanical requirements and which reference is most likely to provide the needed rule.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada C-1D preparation plan should combine trade knowledge with deliberate reference-navigation practice.

Water Supply should include sizing, pressure, materials, valves, distribution, supports, and protection. Candidates should practice locating applicable tables and requirements in the Uniform Plumbing Code.

Drain, Waste, and Vent Systems should include drainage sizing, slope, venting, fixture units, cleanouts, traps, connections, and sanitary drainage principles. These topics are central to plumbing work and should be reviewed thoroughly.

Gas Systems should be studied with both NFPA 54 and NFPA 58 nearby. Candidates should understand the difference between natural gas and LP-Gas systems and learn which reference applies to each type of question.

Water Heaters and Venting should include appliance installation, venting, temperature and pressure protection, combustion-air provisions, clearances, and fuel-gas considerations.

Hydronics should include hot-water heating principles, piping, controls, circulators, expansion, heat emitters, and general system operation. Candidates should also know when the Uniform Mechanical Code is the most useful reference.

Backflow preparation should include protection of potable water systems, device selection concepts, cross-connection concerns, and applicable plumbing-code requirements.

Traps and Interceptors should include trap function, seal protection, installation, prohibited configurations, and interceptor applications.

Cleanouts should include access, location, spacing, sizing, and installation concepts. These questions often reward efficient code navigation.

Storm Drainage should include roof drainage, conductor sizing, drainage pathways, and general storm-water handling concepts.

Plumbing Mathematics and Isometric Analysis should be practiced repeatedly. Candidates should work through measurements, elevations, pipe sizing, areas, volumes, offsets, and related calculations until the steps become familiar.

Code books can support certain values and tables, but the arithmetic itself should be practiced enough that candidates can perform it efficiently during the examination.

A useful study method is to create separate practice sets for each allowed reference. One group can focus on UPC questions, another on NFPA 54, another on NFPA 58, and another on UMC topics.

This helps build the habit of identifying the correct reference before starting a lookup. Choosing the wrong book can waste time even when the candidate understands the subject.

Timed drills are also useful. Select a plumbing topic, identify the correct reference, and locate the applicable table or section as quickly as possible. Repeat difficult subjects until navigation becomes faster.

Definitions deserve attention because plumbing and fuel-gas terminology can affect the meaning of a question. If the wording is unclear, knowing how the code defines a term can help clarify the issue.

How does the Plumbing Contractor (C-1D) exam work?

The Plumbing Contractor (C-1D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
