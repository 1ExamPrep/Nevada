Open Book Test

The Nevada C-1D Plumbing Contractor examination is an open-book test.

The following references in this package are allowed into the examination:

  • Uniform Plumbing Code, 2018
  • NFPA 54 - Standard for National Fuel Gas Code, 2012
  • NFPA 58 - Standard for Liquid Petroleum Gas Code, 2014

The other books included in this package are study references and are not allowed into the examination.

With 80 questions in three hours, candidates should not plan to search multiple books for every answer. Effective open-book preparation means learning the subject first and then knowing which reference is most likely to contain the applicable requirement.

For example, drainage, water supply, fixtures, cleanouts, traps, backflow, and storm drainage questions will often involve the Uniform Plumbing Code. Fuel-gas questions may require NFPA 54, while LP-Gas topics may lead to NFPA 58. Mechanical or hydronic questions may involve the Uniform Mechanical Code.

Practice should include timed reference navigation. Candidates can select a subject, identify the appropriate book, locate the applicable section or table, and repeat the exercise until the process becomes more efficient.

State Requirements

Nevada's C-1D Plumbing classification authorizes installation and maintenance of material and fixtures used to provide sanitary conditions in buildings through a permanent supply of safe water and sanitary waste and vent systems required for proper waste removal.

The classification also includes roof drainage systems, landscape irrigation systems, systems used to heat potable water including solar equipment, and hydronic heating systems not exceeding 200,000 British thermal units.

C-1D work includes a permanent means of supplying gas for residential and industrial purposes, along with piping for compressed air, vacuums, and gases used for medical purposes. The classification also permits applicable water and gas piping between the edge of a building and the utility meter.

For contractor qualification, Nevada generally requires the qualifying individual to have at least four full years of experience at the journeyman, foreman, supervisor, or contractor level within the applicable period before filing the application.

The breadth of the C-1D scope explains why the examination covers plumbing code, fuel gas, LP-Gas, hydronic systems, solar water heating, mathematics, safety, water distribution, drainage, venting, and storm systems.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-1D preparation plan should start with the major plumbing systems. Water Supply study should include sizing, pressure, materials, valves, backflow prevention, distribution, supports, and system protection. Candidates should practice locating applicable tables and requirements in the Uniform Plumbing Code.

Drain, Waste, and Vent Systems should include drainage sizing, slope, vents, fixture units, cleanouts, connections, traps, interceptors, and general sanitary drainage requirements. Practice interpreting both written questions and plumbing diagrams.

Gas Systems should be studied with NFPA 54 and NFPA 58 nearby. Learn the difference between natural gas and LP-Gas systems and practice identifying which reference should be used for a particular question.

Water Heaters and Venting should include appliance installation, venting, temperature and pressure protection, combustion air, clearances, and fuel-gas considerations.

Hydronics should include basic hot-water heating principles, circulators, piping arrangements, controls, heat emitters, expansion, and system operation. The Modern Hydronic Heating reference provides additional study support for this subject.

Plumbing Mathematics and Isometric Analysis should be practiced repeatedly. Work through pipe sizing, elevations, measurements, areas, volumes, offsets, and other calculations until the process can be completed efficiently.

Safety should be studied before test day because OSHA is not allowed into the examination. Candidates should develop enough familiarity with construction safety principles to answer these questions from retained knowledge.

Solar preparation should include the major components and basic operating principles of solar water-heating systems. The solar design and installation manual can help strengthen this portion of the exam.

Code navigation should be part of every study session. Rather than reading all four allowed books from beginning to end, candidates should practice finding answers to targeted questions and learn where major subjects are located.

A useful exercise is to sort practice questions by reference. Create separate groups for UPC, NFPA 54, NFPA 58, and UMC questions. This helps build the habit of choosing the correct book before beginning the lookup.

Timed practice is also important. Three hours for 80 questions gives candidates a little over two minutes per question on average. Candidates should learn when to perform a code lookup and when to answer from trade knowledge and move forward.

How does the Plumbing Contractor (C-1D) exam work?

The Plumbing Contractor (C-1D) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
