## Exam Details

The Nevada A-23 Asbestos Contractor trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who have been approved to test through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

- **Exam:** Nevada A-23 Asbestos Contractor
- **Classification:** A-23 Removal of Asbestos
- **Number of Questions:** 60
- **Time Allowed:** 2 hours
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 42 correct answers
- **Passing Percentage:** 70%
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The current examination content outline is divided into four subject areas:

- **General:** 20 questions
- **Respirators:** 10 questions
- **Mini Enclosures/Glove Bag:** 5 questions
- **Methods and Means:** 25 questions

Methods and Means is the largest section of the examination, accounting for 25 of the 60 questions. Candidates should devote substantial preparation time to the actual processes used to control, contain, handle, remove, encapsulate, enclose, transport, and manage asbestos-containing materials.

The General category contains 20 questions and represents another major portion of the exam. General preparation should include asbestos terminology, basic hazard recognition, asbestos-containing materials, exposure considerations, project organization, regulatory concepts, worker protection, and the overall principles associated with asbestos abatement work.

Respirators accounts for 10 questions. Respiratory protection is especially important in asbestos work because airborne fibers can create serious health hazards. Candidates should understand the basic principles of respiratory protection, selection, use, limitations, fit, maintenance, and the relationship between respiratory protection and exposure-control programs.

Mini Enclosures/Glove Bag contains five questions. Although it is the smallest of the four examination categories, candidates should understand how specialized containment and glove-bag methods are used, the purpose of these procedures, and the basic principles associated with maintaining controlled work areas.

## Closed Book Test

The Nevada Asbestos Contractor (A-23) trade examination is a **closed-book test**. None of the technical references used to prepare the A-23 examination questions are permitted in the examination center.

This makes the study approach for A-23 different from an open-book contractor exam. Candidates cannot rely on finding a regulation, procedure, definition, or technical requirement during the test. Preparation should therefore emphasize comprehension, repetition, recall, and the practical application of asbestos-abatement principles.

The examination may contain questions based on the listed references as well as trade knowledge and general industry practices. Candidates should study the reference materials as a connected body of information rather than treating each book as an isolated source.

A useful closed-book strategy is to organize preparation around the four examination categories. Build a separate study plan for General knowledge, Respirators, Mini Enclosures/Glove Bag procedures, and Methods and Means. Because Methods and Means plus General account for 45 of the 60 questions, those two categories deserve the largest share of study time.

Candidates should also become comfortable with asbestos-specific vocabulary. Closed-book questions can become more difficult when similar technical terms are confused. Review definitions, abatement procedures, enclosure and encapsulation concepts, containment terminology, respiratory-protection terms, worker-protection concepts, and common asbestos-control methods until the language becomes familiar.

The testing center permits a silent, nonprinting, non-programmable calculator, although the A-23 examination is primarily focused on asbestos knowledge, procedures, and safe work practices rather than extensive mathematical calculation.

## State Requirements

The Nevada A-23 classification is officially identified as Removal of Asbestos. The permitted scope includes the cleaning, handling, repair, removal, encapsulation, enclosure, hauling, and disposal of materials containing asbestos, along with other work involving asbestos-containing materials.

For a standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual must establish the experience required for the requested classification. Qualifying experience is generally based on work as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the applicable trade.

Nevada's standard contractor experience requirement is four years of qualifying experience within the 15 years immediately preceding the filing of the application. Experience documentation must support the qualifier's ability to perform and supervise work within the classification being requested.

The contractor application also includes financial-responsibility requirements. The Nevada State Contractors Board uses financial information when establishing the monetary limit under which the contractor may operate. Applicants must provide the financial documentation applicable to the monetary limit requested.

Nevada contractors are also subject to bonding requirements. The Nevada State Contractors Board determines the required contractor bond amount when the license is approved. The amount is based on factors considered by the Board and is communicated before issuance of the license.

The A-23 contractor classification is specific to asbestos work. Contractors should remain within the scope and monetary limit of the license issued to the business. The A-23 classification addresses contractor licensing for asbestos removal and related work and does not replace other occupational, environmental, worker-training, project-notification, or asbestos-control obligations that apply to asbestos projects.

Nevada contractor applicants are generally required to pass both a trade examination for the requested classification and the Contractor Management Survey examination. The CMS examination evaluates contractor Business and Law subjects including licensing, estimating and bidding, lien law, financial management, tax laws, labor laws, project management, contracts, risk management, environmental requirements, and safety.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada A-23 exam-preparation plan should reflect the examination weighting. Candidates should not divide study time evenly across every subject because the content outline clearly places greater emphasis on Methods and Means and General knowledge.

**Prioritize Methods and Means.** The section contains 25 questions, more than 40% of the examination. Study asbestos-abatement procedures, engineering controls, containment, removal techniques, encapsulation, enclosure, cleanup, handling, waste procedures, and project practices.

**Build strong General knowledge.** The General category contains 20 questions. Review asbestos terminology, hazards, project planning, exposure concepts, materials, regulations, worker protection, and the fundamental principles that support safe asbestos work.

**Study respirators as a separate subject.** Ten questions are dedicated to Respirators. Review respiratory-protection terminology, selection concepts, limitations, proper use, fit, maintenance, and program requirements. Do not treat respiratory protection as a minor subsection of general safety.

**Review mini enclosures and glove-bag techniques.** Five questions focus specifically on these procedures. Understand their purpose, setup concepts, containment principles, work practices, and the precautions associated with specialized asbestos-removal methods.

**Use closed-book recall practice.** After studying a topic, close the reference and summarize the concept without looking at the text. Practice answering questions from memory. This approach more closely matches the conditions candidates face during the A-23 examination.

**Create topic-based review sessions.** Instead of reading one reference from beginning to end repeatedly, group information by examination category. For example, combine respiratory-protection information from OSHA materials with related concepts in the asbestos management references.

**Practice under time limits.** Candidates have two hours to complete 60 questions, which averages approximately two minutes per question. Timed practice can help develop a steady pace and reduce the amount of time spent on individual questions.

**Review terminology frequently.** Asbestos examinations can involve specialized language concerning containment, regulated areas, respirators, engineering controls, exposure, encapsulation, enclosure, glove bags, decontamination, and abatement. Frequent terminology review can improve recognition and recall.

## How does the Asbestos Contractor (A-23) exam work?

The Asbestos Contractor (A-23) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
