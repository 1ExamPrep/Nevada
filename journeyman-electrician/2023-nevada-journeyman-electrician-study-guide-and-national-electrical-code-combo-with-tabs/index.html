Open Book Test

Many journeyman electrician exams used for Nevada jurisdictions and national testing programs are open book. Open book does not mean easy—it means the exam rewards the candidate who can navigate efficiently.

An open-book exam rewards three habits:

  • Go to the right destination quickly (chapter → article → section → exception/table)
  • Confirm details accurately (especially exceptions, table notes, and conditions of use)
  • Keep moving so you don’t get stuck and lose time for the rest of the test

This is exactly why the NEC tabs are included. Tabs help you create consistent “routes” to the places you visit repeatedly while studying—services, grounding and bonding, branch circuits, wiring methods, motors, special occupancies, and more. The more consistent your routes become, the less mental energy you waste searching, and the more focus you have for solving the problem correctly.

Use your tabbed NEC like you’ll use it on exam day:

  1. Identify the keywords in the question (service, feeder, branch circuit, raceway, conductor, bonding, motor, etc.).
  2. Pick the code path before opening the book (don’t “hunt” your way to an answer).
  3. Check exceptions and tables every time the question smells like an exception trap.
  4. Verify the final step (table column, ambient temperature, conductor type, correction factors, or required protection).
  5. Answer and move—flag anything time-consuming and come back later.

Licensing Steps

Nevada electricians often follow a journeyman pathway that includes documented experience and passing a recognized exam used by their jurisdiction. Your exact steps can vary depending on where you’re working, but the overall progression is typically consistent:

  1. Choose your jurisdiction path. Determine whether you need a local journeyman card, a Nevada qualifications card, or another credential required by your employer or municipality.
  2. Build qualifying experience. Accumulate supervised work hours in electrical installation, alteration, repair, and code-compliant practices.
  3. Prepare documentation. Many programs require proof of training and work history before issuing a credential.
  4. Schedule and take the exam. Many Nevada-related journeyman exams are computer-based and administered through a testing provider.
  5. Submit passing results and complete the credential step. Depending on your jurisdiction, this may include an application, fee, and card issuance process.
  6. Maintain your credential. Some programs include renewal and continuing education expectations.

How does the Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • The ICC G17 Journeyman Electrician exam has 80 questions in 4 hours and is open book with the NEC.
  • The ID card is valid for 3 years and renews with continuing education from an approved provider.
  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
  • Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
  • ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
  • Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
