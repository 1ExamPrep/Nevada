Exam Details

Nevada journeyman electrician exam requirements may vary by local jurisdiction or qualification card program because individual journeyman-level credentials are not issued through one uniform statewide journeyman electrician license for every electrician in the state. Candidates should follow the instructions from the Nevada electrical qualification card program, local licensing authority, municipality, county, or authority having jurisdiction where they plan to work.

Journeyman-level electrical testing in Nevada is commonly NEC-based and may be administered through recognized testing organizations such as ICC or another approved testing provider, depending on the local or program requirement. A commonly used journeyman-level Nevada electrical exam format includes 80 questions with 4 hours allowed for testing. Published journeyman-level subject areas commonly include general knowledge, electrical theory, load calculations, code definitions, plan reading, services and service equipment, feeders, branch circuits and conductors, wiring methods and materials, raceways, boxes, cabinets, panelboards, equipment and devices, control devices, motors, generators, special occupancies, special equipment, emergency systems, signaling systems, and fire alarm systems.

Nevada statewide electrical contractor licensing is separate from many individual journeyman-level credentials. The Nevada State Contractors Board administers contractor licensing, and PSI administers contractor examinations for the Board. The Nevada C-2 Electrical Contractor exam is a contractor-level examination and should not be confused with a local journeyman card or individual journeyman qualification exam. Contractor candidates may need to pass both a trade examination and a business and law examination, depending on the license classification and application requirements.

For journeyman-level Nevada study, students should prepare broadly for NEC navigation, electrical calculations, installation requirements, grounding and bonding, services, feeders, branch circuits, wiring methods, overcurrent protection, conductors, raceways, boxes, motors, controls, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, emergency systems, fire alarm systems, safety, and general trade knowledge. Open Book Test

Nevada journeyman-level electrical qualification exams are commonly prepared for as open-book, NEC-based examinations, especially when candidates are testing through an ICC-style electrical exam or a local electrical qualification process. Candidates must follow the exact open-book rules issued by the electrical qualification card program, local Nevada jurisdiction, or testing provider administering their exam.

Open book does not mean easy. Electrical candidates still need to understand the material, know how questions are written, find code information quickly, apply rules correctly, and manage time during the exam. A student who depends only on searching through the code book for every answer may struggle to complete the test within the time allowed.

Open-book electrical preparation rewards reference familiarity. Students should practice using the table of contents, index, article structure, definitions, tables, exceptions, notes, and common code sections before exam day. Students should also practice calculations repeatedly so formulas, load relationships, conductor sizing concepts, grounding rules, bonding rules, voltage drop concepts, motor requirements, transformer concepts, fire alarm concepts, and overcurrent protection requirements become easier to recognize.

Reference rules for Nevada electrical testing may vary by testing provider and jurisdiction. Approved books may need to be clean, permanently bound, properly tabbed, and free of loose papers or unapproved notes. Candidates should not bring practice exams, study guides, removable notes, unauthorized tabs, loose papers, or unapproved materials into the testing room. The study guide, practice exams, final exams, and flash cards in this combo are preparation tools to use before test day.

A strong open-book strategy includes learning where major NEC rules are located, practicing article navigation, studying tables, reviewing definitions, and using practice questions to build familiarity with how code-based questions are written. The goal is not simply to look up answers, but to understand what the question is asking and know where to confirm the answer efficiently.

Licensing Steps

Nevada journeyman electrician licensing or qualification steps depend on the credential path. Because Nevada does not use one single statewide individual journeyman electrician license for every journeyman credential, candidates should begin by identifying the program, city, county, local authority, electrical qualification card process, or employer requirement that applies to the area where they plan to work.

A typical Nevada journeyman-level path may include completing apprenticeship training or supervised electrical work experience, documenting qualifying work hours, applying to the appropriate electrical qualification card program or local authority, receiving approval to test, scheduling the required examination, passing the examination, and completing any final card, licensing, registration, or credential steps required by that program or jurisdiction.

Nevada electrical qualification card program requirements may require documented electrical work experience or apprenticeship program hours along with proof of passing an approved examination. Local jurisdictions may also require identification, application forms, fees, proof of work experience, apprenticeship documentation, testing results, renewal paperwork, or continuing education. Candidates should use the current instructions from the authority issuing the card or credential.

Nevada contractor licensing is a separate path. Electrical contractors performing work that requires a contractor license apply through the Nevada State Contractors Board. A contractor license is not the same as a journeyman electrical card or individual journeyman-level qualification. Contractor applicants must meet Board requirements, satisfy experience and business requirements, and pass the required contractor examinations when applicable.

How does the Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • The ICC G17 Journeyman Electrician exam has 80 questions in 4 hours and is open book with the NEC.
  • The ID card is valid for 3 years and renews with continuing education from an approved provider.
  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
  • Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
  • ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
  • Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
