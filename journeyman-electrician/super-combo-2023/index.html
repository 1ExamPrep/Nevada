Exam Details

Nevada uses multiple pathways and programs connected to electrician credentials, depending on the jurisdiction and the specific credential you’re pursuing. One widely referenced pathway is the Nevada Electrical Qualifications Card Program, which publishes updates and application information through Electrical Safety Professionals. This program has announced that the International Code Council (ICC) offers National Electrician exams used to obtain Nevada Journeyman/Master electrical cards, with testing available through Pearson VUE testing centers and ICC’s online testing platform.

In an Electrical Industry Announcement for the program, the listed electrician exams include:

  • 2017 NEC electrical exams (including G17 Journeyman Electrician)
  • 2020 NEC exams (including T17 Journeyman Electrician)
  • 2023 NEC electrical exam testing availability announced as available starting November 2024

For candidates taking the ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam (commonly referenced as W17/F17/G17 in the ICC outline), the ICC exam outline describes a format of:

  • 80 multiple-choice questions
  • 4-hour time limit
  • Open-book testing
  • Content areas spanning general knowledge, services and service equipment, feeders, branch circuits and conductors, wiring methods and materials, equipment and devices, control devices, motors and generators, and special occupancies/equipment/conditions

Even when your exam path or jurisdiction differs, the core performance skills are the same: accurate code interpretation, fast reference navigation, and confident calculations. This Super Combo is built to train those skills with structure.

Open Book Test

For ICC’s National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam, the ICC outline lists the exam as open book. That means your results are often decided by how well you can do three things under time pressure:

  • Recognize the topic the question is testing (services, wiring methods, branch circuits, special occupancies, motors, etc.)
  • Find the exact rule—including exceptions and table notes—without wandering
  • Apply the rule correctly to the scenario in the question

That’s why this bundle includes the National Electrical Code 2023 paperback with tabs. Tabs aren’t just about convenience—they support better practice. When you reduce the time it takes to locate the right chapter or article, you can spend your study time doing what matters: reading the code language carefully, verifying the exception, and building a repeatable lookup routine.

Use your tabbed NEC during practice sets to develop habits that hold up on exam day:

  • Start with the index only when needed—then confirm the article quickly using tabs.
  • Read headings and scope language so you don’t land in the wrong section.
  • Check exceptions every time—especially when questions feel “too easy.”
  • Verify tables and notes before finalizing an answer on sizing, ratings, or conductor rules.

Open book doesn’t remove the difficulty—it shifts the skill. This combo helps you train the skill that matters most: speed with accuracy.

Licensing Steps

Journeyman-level credentials in Nevada can vary by program and jurisdiction. A commonly referenced journeyman pathway is through the Nevada Electrical Qualifications Card Program, which points candidates to ICC electrician exams and provides application and renewal processes through Electrical Safety Professionals.

While details vary based on your classification and local requirements, a practical journeyman preparation path often looks like this:

  1. Confirm the credential you need for the work you plan to perform
    Some jurisdictions and job types require a specific card or credential. Start by identifying your required classification (journeyman vs. residential, low voltage, etc.).
  2. Build qualifying field experience
    Journeyman testing and credentialing typically assumes you’ve developed real jobsite knowledge across wiring methods, services, branch circuits, equipment, and safe work practices.
  3. Apply through the correct Nevada program or jurisdiction
    Follow the application process for the credential you need, including any program-specific forms and documentation.
  4. Schedule your exam through the listed testing provider
    The Nevada Electrical Qualifications Card Program references ICC electrician exams with testing available through Pearson VUE and ICC online testing options.
  5. Prepare for code navigation and calculations performance
    Use structured study tools so your practice is consistent and measurable.
  6. Maintain your credential with renewals and continuing education
    Renewal and CE requirements depend on the credential, but the program publishes CEU expectations for renewals.

State Requirements

Nevada’s electrical landscape includes both credentialing and code adoption realities that can differ by jurisdiction. On the continuing education side, the Nevada Electrical Qualifications Card renewal requirements published through Electrical Safety Professionals state that to renew an Electrical Qualifications Card, journeyman-level cardholders must obtain 1.8 CEUs (18 hours of instruction) every three years related to electrical code and safety-related topics (with higher CEU totals listed for master-level renewals).

On the code side, building code adoption can vary by Nevada jurisdiction. Nevada Building Officials publishes a “Current Adopted Codes” listing showing that several jurisdictions have been moving from the 2017 NEC toward the 2023 NEC, and some jurisdictions list the 2023 NEC as currently adopted or in progress, with local amendments noted in certain areas.

Test Information and Study Materials

The Super Combo is most effective when you use it as a complete system: practice questions for coverage, calculations drills for accuracy and speed, flash cards for daily retention, and the tabbed NEC for navigation training. Here’s a practical way to structure your prep.

1) Use the tabbed NEC to build navigation speed

Your code book is only helpful if you can use it efficiently. During study sessions, train the same way you’ll perform during a timed exam:

  • Identify the topic category first (branch circuits, wiring methods, special occupancies, motors, etc.).
  • Jump to the likely chapter or article using tabs.
  • Read the full rule and exceptions—don’t stop at the first line that looks right.
  • Confirm tables and notes whenever a question involves sizing or ratings.

Do this consistently and you build a repeatable pattern that reduces wasted time and increases confidence.

2) Use the Journeyman Study Guide for broad, exam-style practice

The journeyman study guide is your main “coverage” tool. Use it to work across the NEC topics that show up repeatedly in journeyman testing—especially areas like wiring methods, services, grounding and bonding concepts, motors, and special conditions.

A routine that works well for many electricians:

  • 2–4 sessions per week doing timed question sets (even short ones).
  • Track missed questions by topic so you know exactly what to study next.
  • Verify every miss in the Code to learn both the rule and the location.
  • Repeat weak categories until your accuracy becomes reliable.

3) Use the Calculations Study Guide to strengthen accuracy and speed

Calculations are where many candidates lose easy points—not because they don’t understand electricity, but because they don’t practice the setup and process repeatedly. Your calculations study guide is designed to help you train:

  • Setup discipline (what’s given, what’s required, and which rule or concept applies)
  • Clean steps that you repeat the same way every time
  • Quick error checks to catch unit mistakes and unrealistic results

When the math becomes more automatic, you free up time and focus for code lookup and careful reading—exactly what improves performance on timed exams.

4) Use Flash Cards for daily recall and momentum

Flash cards are ideal for electricians with demanding schedules because they turn small pockets of time into real progress. Use them to:

  • Reinforce NEC language and commonly tested concepts
  • Review key terms and definition-style knowledge
  • Keep calculation reminders fresh between longer practice sessions
  • Warm up before timed question sets

The secret is consistency. Ten minutes a day can keep your momentum strong—especially when you’re balancing work and study.

How does the Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • The ICC G17 Journeyman Electrician exam has 80 questions in 4 hours and is open book with the NEC.
  • The ID card is valid for 3 years and renews with continuing education from an approved provider.
  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
  • Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
  • ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
  • Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
