Exam Details

Nevada does not currently maintain a single statewide Journeyman Electrician examination for individual electrical workers. Because there is no universal Nevada journeyman exam, there is no single Nevada-specific question count, time limit, passing score, approved book list, or testing provider that applies to every journeyman electrician in the state.

The Nevada State Contractors Board administers the state's contractor licensing program. Applicants for contractor classifications generally must meet Board qualification requirements and pass the applicable Construction Management Survey examination and the trade examination associated with the requested contractor classification unless an approved waiver applies.

Electrical contracting falls within Nevada's contractor licensing system. The state's regulations include C-2 Electrical Contracting and electrical subclassifications addressing specific scopes of electrical work. These examinations and licenses apply to contractors and qualifying individuals and are not a statewide Journeyman Electrician licensing exam.

Journeyman-level electricians who want a standardized examination benchmark may encounter the ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician examination. The current ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam contains 80 multiple-choice questions and allows four hours.

The current ICC content outline assigns approximately 6% to General Knowledge, 11% to Services and Service Equipment, 4% to Feeders, 19% to Branch Circuits and Conductors, 26% to Wiring Methods and Materials, 13% to Equipment and Devices, 4% to Control Devices, 6% to Motors and Generators, and 11% to Special Occupancies, Equipment, and Conditions.

The national-standard examination is useful as a model for journeyman-level electrical preparation, but it should not be presented as a Nevada statewide licensing requirement. Candidates should determine exactly which examination or qualification is required for their employment, apprenticeship completion, local credential, or future contractor licensing goal.

Open Book Test

The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician examination is an open-book test. The current examination allows four hours for 80 multiple-choice questions and is built around National Electrical Code knowledge and practical electrical trade subjects.

The open-book format makes NEC navigation an important study skill. Having access to a code book does not eliminate the need to understand electrical principles or recognize where requirements are located. Candidates who attempt to research every question from the beginning can spend too much time moving through unrelated code sections.

Nevada State Contractors Board trade examinations are also administered according to their own approved reference and testing rules. Electricians moving from journeyman-level work toward contractor licensing should prepare specifically for the Nevada contractor classification they intend to obtain rather than assuming a journeyman study reference automatically matches the contractor examination.

State Requirements

Nevada's primary statewide construction licensing authority is the Nevada State Contractors Board. The Board determines the qualifications of contractor license applicants and regulates contractor classifications, examinations, financial requirements, license limits, and professional conduct.

Electrical contractor applicants seeking statewide contracting authority must apply for the appropriate Nevada electrical contractor classification. The Nevada Administrative Code includes C-2 Electrical Contracting and electrical subclassifications such as electrical wiring and other specialized electrical work.

A contractor applicant's qualifying individual must demonstrate the experience required for the classification. After the application is filed and experience is verified, the Nevada State Contractors Board provides eligible candidates with examination information for the required tests.

Nevada contractor licensing typically requires both a trade examination and the Construction Management Survey examination unless an applicant qualifies for an approved examination waiver or licensure-by-endorsement pathway.

Nevada also maintains a formal registered apprenticeship system through the state's Office of the Labor Commissioner and State Apprenticeship Council. Electrical apprenticeship programs provide structured pathways for workers to gain the supervised on-the-job and classroom experience associated with becoming journey-level electricians.

For example, Nevada's registered apprenticeship system includes inside-wireman programs built around extensive supervised electrical work. Completing a recognized apprenticeship can establish journey-level trade proficiency even though Nevada does not issue one universal statewide individual Journeyman Electrician license.

The distinction between journey-level worker status and contractor licensing matters. A skilled journey-level electrician may be qualified to perform electrical installations while working for a properly licensed electrical contractor, but independently contracting for electrical construction is governed by Nevada contractor licensing law.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Nevada journeyman-level electrical study plan should focus on technical subjects that transfer across electrical examinations, apprenticeship completion requirements, and future advancement toward contractor licensing.

Begin with the NEC overview. Learn how the National Electrical Code is organized before attempting to memorize individual rules. Become familiar with chapters, articles, definitions, tables, informational notes, and the index.

Prioritize Wiring Methods and Materials. Prioritize Wiring Methods and Materials is the largest category on the current ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician content outline. Study conductors, raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, enclosures, supports, and installation requirements.

Study Branch Circuits and Conductors. Review conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, required branch circuits, receptacles, lighting outlets, and other common installation requirements.

Review Services and Service Equipment. Learn how service conductors, disconnecting means, service equipment, grounding, bonding, and load calculations relate to one another.

Practice feeder requirements. Feeders connect many parts of an electrical distribution system. Study conductor sizing, calculated loads, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and feeder installation requirements.

Spend time on grounding and bonding. Grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, conductor sizing, and related Article 250 requirements are important areas of journeyman-level electrical knowledge.

Study motors and generators. Motor circuits can combine electrical calculations with multiple NEC requirements involving conductor sizing, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, and controls.

Review equipment and devices. Become familiar with requirements applying to electrical equipment commonly encountered in residential, commercial, and industrial work.

Include control devices. Controls are a smaller portion of many examination outlines but remain important to practical electrical work and motor installations.

Study special occupancies, equipment, and conditions. Develop familiarity with the later NEC chapters so specialized installations are easier to navigate when they appear in practice questions.

Practice electrical calculations actively. Use the included Calculations Study Guide to solve problems step by step. Work through load calculations, conductor calculations, box fill, raceway fill, motors, and other electrical mathematics instead of simply reading completed examples.

Use the NEC tabs consistently. Tabs can help you learn where major code topics are located. Over time, work toward understanding the organization well enough that you can identify the likely article before reaching for a tab.

How does the Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • The ICC G17 Journeyman Electrician exam has 80 questions in 4 hours and is open book with the NEC.
  • The ID card is valid for 3 years and renews with continuing education from an approved provider.
  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
  • Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
  • ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
  • Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
