Exam Details

Nevada does not administer one statewide Journeyman Electrician examination for individual electricians. Journeyman-level competency testing may depend on the municipality, local authority, or certification program associated with the work being performed.

The International Code Council operates Contractor/Trades examinations that licensing agencies can use to evaluate electrical professionals. The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician examination is one recognized journeyman-level testing option available through the Contractor/Trades program.

Passing an ICC examination does not automatically issue a Nevada electrician credential. ICC provides the examination as an independent assessment of trade knowledge, while the licensing authority determines whether that examination satisfies its requirements and what additional steps are required before a credential is issued.

Journeyman electrical preparation should include general electrical knowledge, electrical theory, services, service equipment, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, equipment, control devices, motors, generators, and specialized electrical installations.

General electrical knowledge includes voltage, current, resistance, power, Ohm’s Law, Watt’s Law, alternating-current principles, series and parallel circuits, electrical terminology, and the mathematical relationships used in practical electrical work.

Branch-circuit preparation should include conductor ratings, required circuits, overcurrent protection, receptacles, lighting outlets, GFCI protection, AFCI protection, equipment connections, and applicable load calculations.

Services and feeders require an understanding of conductor sizing, load calculations, service equipment, disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and the distribution of electrical power throughout a building or structure.

Wiring methods and materials can include raceways, cables, conductors, boxes, cabinets, enclosures, fittings, underground wiring, flexible wiring methods, physical protection, supports, and the permitted uses and limitations associated with different wiring systems.

Motor and generator preparation should include branch-circuit conductors, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, motor controllers, multiple-motor installations, generators, and related calculations.

Special occupancies, special equipment, and special conditions may require candidates to recognize when specialized NEC requirements supplement or modify general electrical rules. These questions reward candidates who understand how the NEC is organized rather than relying only on memorized section numbers.

The simulated practice exams included with this package help candidates work through these subjects in an exam-oriented format. Practice testing can reveal whether additional study is needed in electrical theory, calculations, services, motors, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, or code navigation.

Open Book Test

The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician examination is administered as an open-book Contractor/Trades examination. ICC allows approved reference materials according to the rules established for the examination, and candidates are expected to understand how to use those references efficiently.

The National Electrical Code is the central reference for journeyman-level electrical testing. Candidates should prepare with the NEC edition identified for the exact examination selected by the licensing jurisdiction.

The 2023 NEC included with this package provides extensive preparation for reference-based electrical testing. Students can practice identifying the subject of a question and moving directly to the appropriate article, definition, table, section, exception, or cross-reference.

The included NEC tabs support this navigation process during study. Repeated use throughout the course, study guides, calculations, and simulated practice exams helps candidates become more familiar with where frequently tested subjects are located.

Open-book testing still requires substantial preparation. Candidates should not expect to search the entire code book from beginning to end for every answer. Strong performance requires a combination of direct electrical knowledge and efficient reference research.

Students should practice using the NEC table of contents, index, Article 100 definitions, chapter organization, article numbering, tables, exceptions, informational notes, and annexes. Reading complete code sections is important because an exception, condition, table note, or cross-reference may affect the answer.

ICC examination rules permit bound reference material under the Contractor/Trades testing policies, but the specific approved references associated with the selected exam remain the sources from which examination questions are developed. Candidates should prepare their books according to the current rules for their scheduled examination.

The Master Electrician Study Guide, Calculations Study Guide, flash cards, online course, videos, and simulated practice exams are preparation resources. Their inclusion in this package does not automatically make them approved examination-room references.

Licensing Steps

Begin by identifying the Nevada jurisdiction, employer requirement, or certification program connected with the journeyman electrician credential you plan to pursue. Nevada statewide contractor licensing and individual journeyman competency requirements are separate matters.

Determine the exact credential required for the electrical work you intend to perform. Depending on the jurisdiction or program, the qualification may involve a Journeyman Electrician examination, competency certificate, trade card, apprenticeship completion, or another recognized electrical credential.

Review the applicable experience requirements. Journeyman-level programs commonly require several years of supervised electrical experience or successful completion of an approved apprenticeship before examination eligibility.

Maintain detailed employment and training records. Documentation can include apprenticeship records, employer verification, work classifications, dates of employment, and other evidence needed to establish electrical experience.

Determine which examination is accepted. A jurisdiction may use an ICC Contractor/Trades examination or another electrical competency test. The testing program selected by the licensing authority determines the examination format and approved references.

Confirm the NEC edition required for testing. This package is based on the 2023 National Electrical Code, but candidates should prepare the specific edition identified for their scheduled examination.

Submit any required application, experience verification, identification, and supporting documentation to the authority administering or recognizing the credential. Complete examination registration only after satisfying the applicable eligibility requirements.

Use the preparation period to build a consistent study routine. Start with the NEC overview and organize the included 2023 NEC with the tabs. Learn the layout of the code before relying heavily on full-length practice tests.

Work through the Master Electrician Study Guide as an advanced supplemental resource while concentrating on journeyman-level subjects. Complete the Calculations Study Guide regularly and use flash cards for frequent active-recall review.

Attend live instructor-led NEC training and use the recorded videos to revisit difficult subjects. Complete simulated practice exams under timed conditions and review every missed question to determine the reason for the error.

After passing the required examination, complete the remaining licensing, certification, registration, or employment requirements established by the applicable authority before representing yourself as holding the credential.

State Requirements

Nevada’s statewide construction licensing program is administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board. The Board determines contractor qualifications, evaluates experience, administers licensing requirements, and regulates businesses performing work that falls within Nevada contractor licensing laws.

Electrical contracting is regulated through Nevada’s contractor classification system. Electrical contractors fall within the state’s specialty contractor classifications and must satisfy the licensing requirements associated with the applicable electrical classification.

State contractor licensing should not be confused with journeyman electrician qualification. An individual trade credential demonstrates electrical competency, while a Nevada contractor license authorizes a properly licensed contractor or business to offer and perform contracting work within the scope of the license.

Applicants for Nevada contractor licenses are subject to experience review and examination requirements. The Nevada State Contractors Board requires applicants to pass the applicable trade examination and the Contractor Management Survey, commonly identified as the business and law examination, unless a qualifying exemption applies.

The Board verifies an applicant’s qualifications before granting eligibility for contractor examinations. Contractor licensing also involves additional business, financial responsibility, background, and bonding requirements beyond an individual electrician’s technical competency.

Journeyman electricians should also understand that local permits, inspections, code adoption, and administrative requirements remain important even when the electrician holds an accepted trade credential. Electrical work must comply with the code enforced by the authority having jurisdiction over the project.

The NEC edition used by a local authority or licensing examination may differ from the 2023 NEC included in this package. Candidates should distinguish between the edition used for course instruction, the edition used for testing, and the edition enforced for actual construction work.

A journeyman-level credential does not automatically authorize someone to contract independently for electrical work. Individuals planning to operate an electrical contracting business should review Nevada State Contractors Board requirements separately from journeyman electrician exam preparation.

Test Information and Study Materials

An effective Nevada Journeyman Electrician study plan should combine NEC navigation, electrical theory, calculations, active recall, instructor guidance, and simulated testing.

Begin with the NEC overview. Learn how the 2023 National Electrical Code is organized and practice moving from a question topic to the appropriate chapter, article, definition, section, table, exception, or cross-reference.

Divide preparation into manageable categories. Create separate sessions for branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding and bonding, conductors, overcurrent protection, wiring methods, raceways, boxes, motors, generators, transformers, equipment, and special installations.

Use the Calculations Study Guide consistently. Identify the known values, select the appropriate formula, determine which NEC requirements affect the problem, complete the mathematical steps, and verify the final result.

Use flash cards for frequent active recall. Short sessions can reinforce formulas, electrical terminology, definitions, and code concepts between longer periods of study.

Live instructor-led NEC training provides structured guidance through important electrical topics. Recorded videos allow candidates to pause explanations, replay examples, and revisit difficult subjects after simulated testing identifies weaknesses.

Complete simulated practice exams under controlled conditions. Practice deciding when to answer directly from electrical knowledge and when to research an approved reference. Efficiently balancing these two methods is an important open-book testing skill.

After every practice exam, review incorrect answers and questions that required excessive time. Determine whether the difficulty resulted from weak theory knowledge, an incorrect calculation, inefficient NEC navigation, unfamiliar terminology, or overlooking an exception or table note.

How does the Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • The ICC G17 Journeyman Electrician exam has 80 questions in 4 hours and is open book with the NEC.
  • The ID card is valid for 3 years and renews with continuing education from an approved provider.
  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
  • Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
  • ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
  • Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
