Exam Details

The exam content covers a broad range of journeyman electrical topics. Major study areas include general knowledge, services and service equipment, feeders, branch circuits and conductors, wiring methods and materials, equipment and devices, control devices, motors and generators, and special occupancies, equipment, and conditions. Candidates should study across the full outline rather than focusing only on the topics they already use most often in the field.

Journeyman electrician questions may require candidates to use definitions, article rules, tables, notes, exceptions, and calculation methods. A question may appear simple at first but depend on a specific condition, such as conductor type, temperature rating, occupancy, voltage, continuous load, motor application, wiring method, or equipment location. Candidates should practice reading each question carefully before choosing a reference section or answer.

Electrical calculations are also an important part of preparation. Ugly’s Electrical References can help reinforce formulas and common trade information, while the NEC Handbook supports the code requirements that control the final answer.

Because the exam is timed, candidates should prepare for both accuracy and pacing. Four hours provides enough time for careful work, but not enough time to search through the books without a strategy. Candidates should practice identifying keywords in questions, using the NEC index, locating tables quickly, and deciding when to move on and return to a difficult question later.

Test Information and Study Materials

The Reno Nevada National Standard Journeyman Electrician (ICC - F17-N) Exam Book Package supports preparation by giving candidates the reference books needed for code-based study. The journeyman electrician exam requires candidates to understand both practical electrical work and the NEC rules that govern installations. The best study method combines reading, lookup practice, calculations, and review of missed questions.

General knowledge study should include NEC organization, definitions, units of measurement, electrical safety concepts, basic theory, plan-reading awareness, and common trade terms. Candidates should understand words such as service, feeder, branch circuit, conductor, raceway, equipment grounding conductor, grounding electrode conductor, bonding jumper, outlet, receptacle, overcurrent device, continuous load, and disconnecting means.

Services and service equipment should be studied carefully. Candidates should review service conductors, service disconnecting means, grounding electrode systems, bonding requirements, service load calculations, overcurrent protection, and related installation rules. Service questions can be detailed, and the correct answer may depend on the type of service, load, grounding method, or equipment involved.

Feeder study should include conductor sizing, feeder loads, overcurrent protection, grounding, voltage drop awareness, panelboard concepts, and how feeders differ from branch circuits. Candidates who can clearly distinguish a feeder from a branch circuit will have an easier time selecting the correct code rule.

Branch circuits and conductors are major study areas. Candidates should review conductor ampacity, overcurrent protection, required outlets, circuit ratings, continuous loads, multiwire branch circuits, small-appliance circuits, appliance circuits, lighting outlet requirements, GFCI protection, AFCI protection where applicable under the adopted code edition, and conductor identification.

Wiring methods and materials should include raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, support requirements, protection from physical damage, conduit fill, box fill, conductor fill, and installation rules. Candidates should learn the major wiring method articles and practice identifying which article applies to a given installation condition.

Equipment and devices may include receptacles, switches, luminaires, appliances, panelboards, disconnects, transformers, control devices, and other equipment for general use. Candidates should study installation requirements, listing and labeling concepts, working space requirements where applicable, and the relationship between equipment rules and branch circuit or feeder requirements.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Motors and generators should receive focused review. Candidates should study motor branch circuit conductors, motor overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, sizing rules, and table use. Motor questions often require careful reading because different parts of the motor circuit are sized by different rules.

Special occupancies, equipment, and conditions may include hazardous locations, health care facilities, assembly occupancies, signs, pools, generators, transformers, HVAC equipment, and similar subjects. Candidates should become familiar with where these topics are located so they can quickly move to the right article during the exam.

Calculations should be practiced regularly. Candidates should use Ugly’s Electrical References for formula review and the NEC Handbook for code-controlled calculation rules. A strong practice routine includes conductor ampacity, derating, box fill, raceway fill, service loads, feeder loads, motor calculations, overcurrent protection, and grounding or bonding conductor sizing.

The most effective preparation is active. Candidates should read a topic, locate it in the reference, answer questions, check missed answers, and repeat the process. Over time, the books become easier to navigate and the candidate becomes more confident applying code language under timed conditions.

How does the Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • The ICC G17 Journeyman Electrician exam has 80 questions in 4 hours and is open book with the NEC.

  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
