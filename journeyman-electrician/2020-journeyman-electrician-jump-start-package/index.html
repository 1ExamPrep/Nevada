Open Book Test

This is an Open Book Test. Open-book does not mean easy—it means your performance is strongly tied to your ability to navigate the NEC efficiently and interpret code language correctly under timed conditions.

Open-book candidates typically do best when they practice these habits:

  • Start with a “where is it?” instinct: quickly identify whether the answer is likely in a section, a table, or an exception.
  • Confirm with the exact NEC language: don’t rely on memory alone—verify the details directly in the code.
  • Use tabs as a shortcut, not a crutch: tabs speed up access to major areas; your skill is finding the exact line you need.
  • Watch for exceptions and notes: many questions are built around those details.
  • Manage time with a method: locate, confirm, apply, and move on—avoid getting stuck too long on one question.

The included National Electrical Code 2020 Paperback with Tabs supports this workflow by reducing time spent flipping blindly through the book. When combined with consistent practice, it can make your code lookups feel smoother and faster as test day approaches.

Licensing Steps

Licensing steps vary by location, but the journeyman path often follows a familiar progression. This overview helps you understand where exam prep fits into the broader process.

  1. Meet eligibility requirements (typically related to verified work experience, apprenticeship completion, and/or supervised hours).
  2. Apply to test through the licensing authority or exam administrator and submit required documentation.
  3. Receive approval and scheduling instructions once your application is accepted.
  4. Study using the correct NEC edition and build strong open-book code navigation habits.
  5. Take the journeyman exam and apply code, calculations, and trade knowledge under timed conditions.
  6. Complete final licensing steps after passing (fees, paperwork, or other jurisdiction requirements).

Test Information and Study Materials

The fastest way to improve for a journeyman exam is to study in a way that reflects how you’ll actually be tested. That means combining understanding with retrieval: learn the concept, then practice finding the supporting NEC language and applying it in realistic scenarios.

A simple weekly structure you can repeat with this Jump Start Package:

  • NEC navigation practice (daily): choose a topic area, then practice locating key sections and related tables quickly.
  • Study guide review (3–5 sessions per week): work through topics in focused blocks and summarize what you learned in your own words.
  • Calculations practice (2–4 sessions per week): complete a set of problems, track missed setups, then revisit weak areas with repetition.
  • Online kit reinforcement (as often as possible): use online prep to keep your study active and to reinforce concepts you just reviewed.

How to use your tabbed NEC effectively:

  1. Identify the topic the question is testing (wiring methods, grounding and bonding, load calculations, etc.).
  2. Use tabs to reach the correct major area quickly.
  3. Scan headings and section numbers to locate the exact requirement.
  4. Check tables, notes, and exceptions before committing to an answer.
  5. Confirm the wording and apply it precisely to the scenario described.

With practice, this workflow becomes a habit—one that can help you stay calm and consistent during timed testing.

How does the Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • The ICC G17 Journeyman Electrician exam has 80 questions in 4 hours and is open book with the NEC.
  • The ID card is valid for 3 years and renews with continuing education from an approved provider.
  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
  • Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
  • ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
  • Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
