Open Book Test

The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician exam is an open-book test. The ICC exam outline lists the exam as open book with an 80-question format and a 4-hour time limit. Approved references for the national journeyman exam include the National Electrical Code and Ugly’s Electrical References, with the NEC serving as the primary reference for most code-based preparation.

Open-book testing still requires serious preparation. The candidate must know how to use the NEC quickly and accurately. Many questions depend on precise wording, definitions, exceptions, table notes, wiring methods, conductor types, equipment ratings, or installation conditions. A candidate who has not practiced code navigation can lose valuable time searching through the book.

The National Electrical Code 2023 Paperback included in this combo should be used actively throughout preparation. When reviewing a topic, open the codebook and locate the related article. Read the surrounding section, check Article 100 definitions, review tables and notes, and make sure the rule is being applied in context. This helps candidates build the habits needed for an open-book electrical exam.

Strong open-book preparation includes learning the structure of the NEC. Chapter 1 covers general rules and definitions. Chapter 2 covers wiring and protection. Chapter 3 covers wiring methods and materials. Chapter 4 covers equipment for general use. Later chapters cover special occupancies, special equipment, special conditions, communications systems, and tables. Knowing where subjects are located helps reduce lookup time during a timed exam.

How does the Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

  • The ICC G17 Journeyman Electrician exam has 80 questions in 4 hours and is open book with the NEC.
  • The ID card is valid for 3 years and renews with continuing education from an approved provider.
  • Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
  • Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
  • ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
  • Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
