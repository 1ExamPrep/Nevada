## Exam Details

Nevada does not administer one statewide Journeyman Electrician examination for individual electricians. Journeyman-level competency testing may depend on the municipality, local authority, or certification program associated with the work being performed.

The International Code Council operates Contractor/Trades examinations that licensing agencies can use to evaluate electrical professionals. The ICC National Standard Journeyman Electrician examination is one recognized journeyman-level testing option available through the Contractor/Trades program.

Passing an ICC examination does not automatically issue a Nevada electrician credential. ICC provides the examination as an independent assessment of trade knowledge, while the licensing authority determines whether that examination satisfies its requirements and what additional steps are required before a credential is issued.

Journeyman electrical preparation should include general electrical knowledge, electrical theory, services, service equipment, feeders, branch circuits, conductors, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, equipment, control devices, motors, generators, and specialized electrical installations.

General electrical knowledge includes voltage, current, resistance, power, Ohm’s Law, Watt’s Law, alternating-current principles, series and parallel circuits, electrical terminology, and the mathematical relationships used in practical electrical work.

Branch-circuit preparation should include conductor ratings, required circuits, overcurrent protection, receptacles, lighting outlets, GFCI protection, AFCI protection, equipment connections, and applicable load calculations.

Services and feeders require an understanding of conductor sizing, load calculations, service equipment, disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, grounding, bonding, and the distribution of electrical power throughout a building or structure.

Wiring methods and materials can include raceways, cables, conductors, boxes, cabinets, enclosures, fittings, underground wiring, flexible wiring methods, physical protection, supports, and the permitted uses and limitations associated with different wiring systems.

Motor and generator preparation should include branch-circuit conductors, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, motor controllers, multiple-motor installations, generators, and related calculations.

Special occupancies, special equipment, and special conditions may require candidates to recognize when specialized NEC requirements supplement or modify general electrical rules. These questions reward candidates who understand how the NEC is organized rather than relying only on memorized section numbers.

The simulated practice exams included with this package help candidates work through these subjects in an exam-oriented format. Practice testing can reveal whether additional study is needed in electrical theory, calculations, services, motors, wiring methods, grounding and bonding, or code navigation.

## State Requirements

Nevada’s statewide construction licensing program is administered by the Nevada State Contractors Board. The Board determines contractor qualifications, evaluates experience, administers licensing requirements, and regulates businesses performing work that falls within Nevada contractor licensing laws.

Electrical contracting is regulated through Nevada’s contractor classification system. Electrical contractors fall within the state’s specialty contractor classifications and must satisfy the licensing requirements associated with the applicable electrical classification.

State contractor licensing should not be confused with journeyman electrician qualification. An individual trade credential demonstrates electrical competency, while a Nevada contractor license authorizes a properly licensed contractor or business to offer and perform contracting work within the scope of the license.

Applicants for Nevada contractor licenses are subject to experience review and examination requirements. The Nevada State Contractors Board requires applicants to pass the applicable trade examination and the Contractor Management Survey, commonly identified as the business and law examination, unless a qualifying exemption applies.

The Board verifies an applicant’s qualifications before granting eligibility for contractor examinations. Contractor licensing also involves additional business, financial responsibility, background, and bonding requirements beyond an individual electrician’s technical competency.

Journeyman electricians should also understand that local permits, inspections, code adoption, and administrative requirements remain important even when the electrician holds an accepted trade credential. Electrical work must comply with the code enforced by the authority having jurisdiction over the project.

The NEC edition used by a local authority or licensing examination may differ from the 2023 NEC included in this package. Candidates should distinguish between the edition used for course instruction, the edition used for testing, and the edition enforced for actual construction work.

A journeyman-level credential does not automatically authorize someone to contract independently for electrical work. Individuals planning to operate an electrical contracting business should review Nevada State Contractors Board requirements separately from journeyman electrician exam preparation.

## How does the Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician (ICC G17) card is part of the Electrical Qualifications ID card program run by Electrical Safety Professionals for Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno. You pass the ICC national exam, then apply to ESP with the score report.

- The ICC G17 Journeyman Electrician exam has 80 questions in 4 hours and is open book with the NEC.
- The ID card is valid for 3 years and renews with continuing education from an approved provider.
- Nevada has no state electrician license; Las Vegas, Clark County and Reno require an Electrical Qualifications ID card on every job site.
- Electrical Safety Professionals (ESP) issues the cards, after you pass an ICC national exam: G17 journeyman, G16 master, G18 residential or G21 low voltage.
- ICC electrician exams are open book with the NEC, and ICC offers them in more than one code edition, so match the edition to the exam you book.
- Cards are valid for 3 years; one expired for more than a year means taking the exam again.
