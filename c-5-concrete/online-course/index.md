## Exam Details

The Nevada C-5 Concrete Contractor trade examination is administered through PSI for applicants who have received examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

- **Exam:** Nevada C-5 Concrete Contractor
- **Number of Questions:** 60
- **Time Allowed:** 2 hours
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 42 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The C-5 examination content is divided into six major areas:

- **Safety:** 6 questions
- **Concrete-Earthwork:** 7 questions
- **Concrete:** 15 questions
- **Concrete-Reinforcement:** 10 questions
- **Concrete-Formwork:** 12 questions
- **Concrete Cutting:** 10 questions

Concrete is the largest individual examination category with 15 questions. Candidates should review concrete materials, placement, finishing, curing, joints, quality considerations, field procedures, and the principles that influence successful concrete construction.

Concrete-Formwork contains 12 questions and represents another major part of the examination. Candidates should understand form construction, form materials, bracing, shores, supports, form pressure, assembly, stripping, and safe work practices associated with concrete formwork.

Concrete-Reinforcement contains 10 questions. Preparation should include reinforcing steel, placement, spacing, support, concrete cover, embedded materials, structural requirements, and the use of ACI 318 when researching reinforcement-related code questions.

Concrete Cutting also contributes 10 questions. Candidates should prepare for sawcutting, joint cutting, concrete flatwork cutting, drilling, coring, cutting equipment, blade-related concepts, timing of sawcuts, and practical techniques used to cut hardened and newly placed concrete.

Concrete-Earthwork includes seven questions. This category can involve excavation, trenching, grading, compaction, subgrade preparation, soil conditions, and site preparation associated with concrete construction.

Safety accounts for six questions. Candidates should review construction safety involving personal protective equipment, excavation hazards, tools, equipment, material handling, fall hazards, and other OSHA-related conditions encountered on concrete projects.

## State Requirements

The classification also includes applying concrete sealants and waterproofing products. Surface preparation, durability, moisture control, and protection of completed concrete can therefore be relevant to the contractor's work.

Sawing, cutting, drilling, and coring concrete and asphaltic paving material are also included within the C-5 scope. The examination devotes 10 questions to Concrete Cutting, making this area an important component of preparation.

For the standard Nevada contractor licensing pathway, the qualifying individual generally must establish four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the requested classification within the applicable period preceding the application.

Nevada contractor applicants must also address financial responsibility, background requirements, the monetary limit associated with the license, and applicable bonding before final license issuance. The required trade and management qualification requirements must also be completed unless an approved waiver applies.

## How does the Concrete Contractor (C-5) exam work?

The Concrete Contractor (C-5) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
