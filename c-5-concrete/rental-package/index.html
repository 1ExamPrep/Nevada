Exam Details

The Nevada C-5 Concrete Contractor trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates authorized to test through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-5 Concrete Contractor
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 60
  • Time Allowed: 2 hours
  • Minimum Passing Score: 42 correct answers

The examination is divided into the following subject areas:

  • Safety: 6 questions
  • Concrete-Earthwork: 7 questions
  • Concrete: 15 questions
  • Concrete-Reinforcement: 10 questions
  • Concrete-Formwork: 12 questions
  • Concrete-Cutting: 10 questions

Concrete is the largest individual category with 15 questions. Candidates should review materials, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, joints, surface preparation, waterproofing, sealants, and general concrete construction practices.

Concrete-Formwork accounts for 12 questions. Preparation should include form materials, supports, bracing, layout, molds, pans, slipforms, stripping, alignment, loading considerations, and practical construction methods.

Concrete-Reinforcement accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should understand reinforcing steel, embedded materials, placement, cover, spacing, development concepts, and structural relationships covered by ACI 318-14.

Concrete-Cutting also accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should prepare for saw cutting, joint cutting, drilling, coring, cutting equipment, timing, and field techniques used with concrete and asphaltic paving materials.

Concrete-Earthwork includes seven questions involving excavation, grading, subgrade preparation, compaction, drainage relationships, and other site conditions that affect concrete construction.

Safety accounts for six questions and should include preparation involving reinforcing steel, formwork, excavation, concrete placement, cutting equipment, personal protective equipment, tools, and other common jobsite hazards.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board regulates the C-5 Concrete classification. Passing the C-5 trade examination is one component of the complete Nevada contractor licensing process.

  1. Confirm the C-5 classification. Determine that Concrete Contracting matches the type of work the business intends to perform.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. Designate the person who will satisfy the applicable experience and examination requirements.
  3. Document qualifying experience. Prepare records showing relevant work involving concrete placement, finishing, reinforcement, formwork, earthwork, cutting, drilling, coring, sealants, waterproofing, and related responsibilities.
  4. Prepare the Nevada contractor license application. Gather business information, qualifier information, experience documentation, financial information, and required supporting materials.
  5. Submit the application. The Nevada State Contractors Board reviews the applicant's qualifications before examination eligibility is established.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow required disclosure, fingerprint, and background procedures.
  7. Meet applicable financial responsibility requirements. Provide the financial information required during the licensing process.
  8. Receive examination eligibility. Candidates required to test can proceed with scheduling after authorization.
  9. Schedule the C-5 examination through PSI. Arrange the Concrete Contractor trade examination after receiving eligibility.
  10. Prepare for the trade examination. Study concrete, formwork, reinforcement, cutting, earthwork, safety, and approved-reference navigation.
  11. Prepare for contractor management requirements. Use the included Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada for Nevada contractor business-law and management preparation.
  12. Complete applicable examinations. Satisfy the trade and management testing requirements that apply to the license.
  13. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Address applicable bonding, financial responsibility, background, and other Nevada State Contractors Board requirements.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-5 Concrete classification covers preparation of surfaces and placement of reinforcing steel and other embedded materials essential to concrete construction.

The classification also includes pouring, placing, finishing, and installing concrete, along with constructing and assembling forms, molds, slipforms, and pans used during concrete construction.

Additional authorized work includes applying concrete sealants and other waterproofing products and sawing, cutting, drilling, and coring concrete and asphaltic paving material.

This means C-5 preparation should cover the complete concrete construction process rather than focusing only on placement and finishing. Candidates should understand site preparation, reinforcement, forms, placement, finishing, waterproofing, cutting, drilling, coring, and related safety practices.

Formwork and reinforcement are major parts of both the work and the examination. Together, these categories account for 22 of the 60 trade exam questions. Candidates should understand how forms support fresh concrete and how reinforcing steel and embedded materials become part of the completed concrete system.

Concrete cutting is another significant part of the classification. Saw cutting, drilling, coring, joint cutting, equipment use, and timing should receive dedicated preparation.

Passing the C-5 trade exam does not automatically issue a Nevada contractor license. Applicants must also satisfy applicable experience, management qualification, financial responsibility, bonding, background, and other Nevada State Contractors Board requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-5 study plan should reflect the examination weighting. Concrete, Formwork, Reinforcement, and Cutting make up most of the exam and should receive the greatest amount of preparation time.

Prioritize concrete fundamentals. Review concrete materials, mixing, placement, consolidation, curing, finishing, joints, surface preparation, waterproofing, sealants, and quality practices.

Study formwork thoroughly. Review form materials, bracing, supports, loads, alignment, stripping, molds, pans, slipforms, and practical field procedures.

Review reinforcement. Understand reinforcing steel, embedded materials, placement, cover, spacing, and structural relationships. Practice using ACI 318-14 to find applicable requirements efficiently.

Prepare for concrete cutting. Study saw cutting, joint timing, drilling, coring, flatwork cutting, equipment, and practical methods. Concrete-Cutting accounts for 10 questions, making it a major exam category.

Review earthwork. Study excavation, grading, subgrade preparation, compaction, drainage relationships, and site conditions that affect concrete work.

Prepare for safety. Use OSHA during study to reinforce safe practices involving reinforcing steel, formwork, excavation, concrete placement, cutting equipment, tools, and personal protective equipment.

Practice code navigation. Use the highlighted and tabbed International Building Code and ACI 318-14 regularly so the organization of both books becomes familiar before exam day.

Use timed practice. With 60 questions and two hours available, candidates should develop a steady pace and learn when a code lookup is worth the time.

How does the Concrete Contractor (C-5) exam work?

The Concrete Contractor (C-5) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
