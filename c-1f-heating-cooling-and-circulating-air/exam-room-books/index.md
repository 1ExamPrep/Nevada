## Exam Details

The Nevada Heating, Cooling, And Circulating Air Contractor (C-1F) trade examination contains **30 questions**. Candidates are allowed **1 hour** to complete the examination, and the minimum passing score is **21 correct answers**.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

- **Safety:** 3 questions
- **Piping:** 9 questions
- **Equipment:** 10 questions
- **Valves and Controls:** 3 questions
- **Ducts:** 5 questions

Equipment is the largest individual subject area, followed closely by piping. Together, these categories account for a substantial portion of the examination, making them important priorities in a C-1F study plan.

Equipment preparation should include familiarity with heating and air-circulation equipment, evaporative cooling equipment, installation considerations, mechanical components, clearances, operation, and applicable code requirements.

Piping preparation should include pipe materials, installation requirements, supports, connections, system components, testing concepts, and mechanical-code provisions associated with heating and circulating-air systems.

Duct preparation should address air distribution, duct construction and installation concepts, supports, clearances, exhaust-related requirements, and the relationship between duct systems and mechanical equipment.

Valves and controls should be studied for their purpose, placement, operation, shutoff functions, system regulation, and relationship to safe mechanical-system performance. Safety preparation should include both mechanical-system safety and general awareness of hazards associated with construction and equipment installation.

A better approach is to identify the subject first. If a question concerns ducts, go directly to the duct-related section. If it concerns equipment installation, begin in the applicable equipment provisions. If it involves piping, identify the piping subject before searching the code.

## State Requirements

The Nevada C-1F Heating, Cooling, And Circulating Air classification covers the fabrication and installation of systems used for **heating, evaporative cooling, and circulation of air**.

The classification excludes cooling systems that require refrigeration as an integral part of the system. This distinction is important because the C-1F license is not the same as a broad refrigeration and air-conditioning classification.

Candidates should understand the scope of the classification when preparing for the examination. The exam focuses on the mechanical systems and components associated with heating, evaporative cooling, and air circulation rather than refrigeration-based cooling equipment as an integral system function.

The Nevada contractor licensing process requires applicants to satisfy more than the trade examination. Application review, qualification requirements, and other applicable licensing requirements must also be completed before a contractor license is issued.

The trade exam should therefore be treated as a major preparation goal within the larger licensing process rather than as the only requirement for obtaining the C-1F contractor license.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-1F study plan should combine mechanical knowledge with repeated Uniform Mechanical Code navigation. The exam contains only 30 questions, so each question has meaningful weight toward the final score.

**Equipment** deserves significant preparation because it accounts for 10 questions. Study equipment installation, operating concepts, mechanical components, clearances, placement, and related code requirements. Candidates should be able to recognize what kind of equipment a question describes and identify where that subject is addressed in the code.

**Piping** accounts for 9 questions. Preparation should include piping materials, installation methods, supports, connections, valves, testing concepts, and requirements related to mechanical systems. Practice locating relevant piping sections instead of simply reading the code from beginning to end.

**Ducts** account for 5 questions. Review duct installation, supports, air movement, clearances, construction concepts, and related requirements. Learn which chapters or sections contain duct information and practice reaching those areas efficiently.

**Valves and Controls** account for 3 questions. Candidates should understand how valves and controls regulate mechanical systems, where shutoff or control devices may be required, and how these components relate to system operation and safety.

**Safety** accounts for 3 questions. Candidates should prepare for safe installation practices and common hazards associated with mechanical work, tools, equipment, piping, ducts, access, and construction activities.

A useful code-navigation method is to begin each practice question by identifying one or two keywords. Those keywords can then be used in the index to locate the applicable chapter or section. Over time, frequently tested subjects should become easier to locate without extensive searching.

Timed practice is especially useful. With 30 questions in 1 hour, candidates should become comfortable making quick decisions about whether to answer from knowledge or consult the code.

If a code lookup becomes lengthy, candidates should avoid spending excessive time on one question. Practice sessions can help establish a rhythm for answering familiar questions first and returning to more difficult searches afterward.

When reviewing a code section, candidates should read enough surrounding material to understand the complete rule. A nearby exception, definition, table, or referenced section may change how a requirement applies.

## How does the Heating, Cooling and Circulating Air Contractor (C-1F) exam work?

The Heating, Cooling and Circulating Air Contractor (C-1F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
