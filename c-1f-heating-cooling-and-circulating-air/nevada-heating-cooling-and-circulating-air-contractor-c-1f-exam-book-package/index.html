Exam Details

The Nevada Heating, Cooling, And Circulating Air Contractor (C-1F) trade examination contains 30 questions. Candidates are allowed 1 hour to complete the exam, and the minimum passing score is 21 correct answers.

The examination content is organized into the following subject areas:

  • Safety: 3 questions
  • Piping: 9 questions
  • Equipment: 10 questions
  • Valves and Controls: 3 questions
  • Ducts: 5 questions

Equipment and piping make up the largest portion of the examination, so candidates should dedicate significant preparation time to those subjects. Equipment questions can involve heating and air-circulation systems, installation requirements, clearances, components, operation, and mechanical-code provisions. Piping questions can involve materials, installation, supports, connections, testing, and related mechanical requirements.

Duct questions may involve duct construction, installation, clearances, air distribution, exhaust, and mechanical-system requirements. Valves and controls should be studied for their purpose, placement, operation, and relationship to mechanical equipment. Safety preparation should include both mechanical-system safety and general construction jobsite practices.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada C-1F examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable licensing requirements in addition to preparing for and passing the trade exam.

  1. Confirm the C-1F classification. Make sure the work the business intends to perform falls within Nevada's Heating, Cooling, and Circulating Air scope.
  2. Identify the qualifying individual. The contractor license application must designate the individual who provides the applicable trade qualification.
  3. Prepare qualifying experience documentation. Organize experience that demonstrates work related to heating, evaporative cooling, air circulation, ducts, piping, equipment, and related mechanical systems.
  4. Complete the Nevada contractor license application. Provide the required business, qualifying individual, experience, financial, and supporting information.
  5. Submit the application for review. Examination eligibility is established through the Nevada contractor licensing process.
  6. Prepare for the C-1F trade examination. Study the Uniform Mechanical Code along with supplemental materials covering mechanical systems and construction safety.
  7. Complete applicable examinations. Satisfy the examination requirements associated with the contractor license application unless an authorized waiver applies.
  8. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Finish all other applicable requirements before the contractor license is issued.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-1F Heating, Cooling, And Circulating Air classification authorizes the fabrication and installation of systems for heating, evaporative cooling, and circulation of air. The scope also includes installation of solar equipment used for those purposes.

The classification does not authorize the holder to fabricate or install cooling systems that use refrigeration as an integral part of the system. Candidates should understand this limitation because it defines the type of work covered by the C-1F license.

The scope places emphasis on mechanical systems that heat air, move air, and provide evaporative cooling rather than refrigeration-based air-conditioning systems. Typical examination preparation therefore focuses on mechanical equipment, piping, ducts, controls, installation requirements, and system safety.

The C-1F trade examination is only one portion of the licensing process. Applicants must also complete the other requirements that apply to Nevada contractor licensing before a license can be issued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Nevada C-1F preparation plan should begin with the examination content outline. Since equipment and piping account for most of the questions, those areas deserve the largest share of study time.

Equipment preparation should include heating equipment, evaporative cooling systems, air-moving equipment, mechanical appliances, installation requirements, clearances, combustion-related considerations where applicable, and general system operation. Use the Uniform Mechanical Code to become familiar with code requirements and Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning to strengthen practical understanding.

Piping should include pipe materials, installation methods, supports, connections, testing, system components, and mechanical-code requirements. Candidates should practice identifying where piping subjects are addressed in the Uniform Mechanical Code and become comfortable finding the applicable provisions quickly.

Ducts should be studied for installation, construction, air distribution, exhaust, clearances, supports, and related mechanical requirements. Duct questions may require a combination of practical knowledge and code navigation.

Valves and Controls should include control functions, system regulation, shutoff requirements, placement, equipment operation, and the relationship between controls and mechanical-system safety.

Safety should be studied from both the mechanical code and OSHA material. Candidates should understand safe equipment installation and common jobsite hazards associated with construction, tools, ladders, work areas, electrical exposure, material handling, and personal protective equipment.

The Uniform Mechanical Code, 2018 should be used repeatedly during preparation. Begin by learning the overall organization of the book. Then practice locating major topics without relying on memory alone. Use the index and table of contents to develop a repeatable search process.

A good code-navigation exercise is to read a practice question and identify its main keyword before opening the book. For example, a question involving duct clearance should direct the search toward duct requirements, while a question involving a piece of heating equipment should point toward the appropriate equipment chapter or section.

After finding a likely provision, read enough surrounding text to understand exceptions, definitions, tables, and conditions that may affect the answer. Avoid stopping at the first sentence that appears related to the question.

For Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd edition, concentrate on understanding system operation and components rather than trying to memorize isolated facts. Candidates should be able to recognize the purpose of major equipment, how air moves through a system, how controls affect operation, and how heating and cooling components work together.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Because this book is not available during the exam, use active recall. Read a section, close the book, and explain the concept from memory. Then work practice questions without using the reference and return to the book only after attempting an answer.

Use the same approach with OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Focus on common safety principles and jobsite practices that can apply to mechanical work. Candidates should be able to recognize unsafe conditions and understand basic construction safety responsibilities without needing the OSHA book open during the exam.

The examination contains only 30 questions and allows 1 hour, so time management is important. Candidates should avoid spending too long on one difficult lookup. A balanced strategy is to answer familiar questions first, use the Uniform Mechanical Code where necessary, and return to more difficult questions if time remains.

Practice under timed conditions before test day. Completing 30-question practice sessions within a 1-hour limit can help candidates develop a realistic pace and identify whether code lookups are taking too long.

How does the Heating, Cooling and Circulating Air Contractor (C-1F) exam work?

The Heating, Cooling and Circulating Air Contractor (C-1F) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
