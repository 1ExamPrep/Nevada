## State Requirements

The Nevada C-17C Drywall classification authorizes the **installation and coating of gypsum wallboard and sheathing**. The scope also includes the installation of suspended ceiling systems made of gypsum wallboard.

This means candidates should prepare for more than basic drywall attachment. The work can involve board selection, placement, fastening, joint treatment, coating, finishing, wall and ceiling systems, sheathing, support conditions, and applicable code requirements.

Suspended gypsum wallboard ceilings are specifically included in the C-17C scope. Candidates should understand the general relationship between the support system, board installation, fastening, finishing, and the completed ceiling assembly.

Fire-resistance is another important subject because gypsum wallboard is frequently used as part of rated walls and ceilings. Candidates should be able to recognize when a question involves a fire-resistance-rated assembly and know when the International Building Code should be consulted.

The C-17C classification is a specialty contractor classification, so candidates should focus their preparation on drywall and gypsum-related work rather than assuming the classification covers unrestricted framing, plastering, or other specialty work outside the C-17C scope.

Passing the C-17C trade examination does not automatically issue a Nevada contractor license. Applicants must also complete the other applicable Nevada State Contractors Board licensing requirements.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-17C study plan should combine practical drywall knowledge with repeated use of the highlighted and tabbed International Building Code.

**Study gypsum wallboard installation.** Review board types, placement, orientation, fastening, support, joints, edges, wall applications, and ceiling applications.

**Review sheathing.** The C-17C scope includes gypsum sheathing, so candidates should understand its role in wall systems and related construction.

**Study finishing and coating.** Review tape, joint compound, finishing sequences, joint treatment, surface preparation, and common methods used to complete gypsum wallboard surfaces.

**Review wall systems.** Understand how gypsum wallboard functions within complete wall assemblies and how support, framing, openings, fastening, and adjacent construction affect installation.

**Study suspended gypsum ceilings.** Candidates should understand the relationship between support systems, gypsum wallboard installation, fastening, finishing, and the completed suspended ceiling assembly.

**Review fire-resistance concepts.** Gypsum wallboard is commonly used in fire-resistance-rated construction. Candidates should practice recognizing when a question involves a rated wall, rated ceiling, separation, or another condition that may require an IBC lookup.

**Practice International Building Code navigation.** Use the highlighted and tabbed 2018 IBC regularly during preparation. Learn where major subjects are located and practice moving from a question to the correct chapter or section.

**Study fastening and support.** Candidates should understand proper attachment, support, spacing, and general installation relationships within drywall systems.

**Review interior construction concepts.** Drywall is part of broader interior building systems. Candidates should understand how gypsum wallboard interacts with walls, ceilings, materials, openings, and rated assemblies.

**Know when the IBC is needed.** Not every C-17C question requires a code lookup. Practical questions may be answered from trade knowledge, while code-based questions should be confirmed with the approved reference.

**Practice timed questions.** With one hour for 20 questions, candidates should maintain a steady pace and avoid spending excessive time on one difficult code lookup.

**Use the highlighted sections actively.** Highlighting is most useful when candidates understand the surrounding context and connect the marked language to practice questions and real drywall applications.

## How does the Drywall Contractor (C-17C) exam work?

The Drywall Contractor (C-17C) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
