## Exam Details

The Nevada C-17C Drywall trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates who receive examination eligibility through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

- **Exam:** Nevada C-17C Drywall
- **Number of Questions:** 20
- **Time Allowed:** 1 hour
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 14 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The C-17C examination content outline is divided into four technical areas:

- **Installation:** 6 questions
- **Taping and Finishing:** 6 questions
- **Materials:** 4 questions
- **Special Applications and Accessories:** 4 questions

Installation and Taping and Finishing are the two largest examination categories, with six questions each. Together they make up more than half of the trade exam. Candidates should understand gypsum wallboard handling, layout, installation practices, attachment methods, joints, corners, taping procedures, finishing materials, coats, and surface preparation.

The Materials category contains four questions. Candidates should become familiar with gypsum wallboard products, sheathing, compounds, fasteners, accessories, and other materials encountered during drywall installation and finishing.

Special Applications and Accessories also contains four questions. Preparation should include the components and procedures associated with specialized drywall applications, suspended gypsum wallboard ceilings, trims, corner treatments, control-related accessories, and other materials used to complete drywall systems.

Nevada contractor applicants generally must also satisfy the Contractor Management Survey examination unless an applicable waiver is approved. The CMS examination includes 60 scored questions with 120 minutes allowed, plus three non-scored items with six additional minutes. The minimum passing score is 45 correct answers, or 75%.

The Contractor Management Survey covers Licensing, Estimating and Bidding, Lien Law, Financial Management, Tax Laws, Labor Laws, Project Management, Contracts, Risk Management, and Environmental and Safety subjects. The included Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada supports preparation for this separate licensing examination.

## State Requirements

The Nevada C-17C Drywall subclassification covers the installation and coating of gypsum wallboard and sheathing. The authorized scope also includes installation of suspended ceiling systems made of gypsum wallboard.

Candidates should understand that contractor classifications define the type of work a licensed business is authorized to perform. The C-17C classification is specifically focused on drywall work rather than every activity associated with a complete interior construction project.

The qualifying individual for a Nevada contractor license must demonstrate appropriate trade experience. Under the standard experience pathway, the trade qualifier must have at least four years of experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the specific classification requested within the 10 years immediately preceding the application.

The qualifying party represents the business entity for purposes of the licensed classification and must be actively engaged in the classification of work for which that individual qualifies.

Nevada generally requires the qualifying individual to satisfy both the trade examination and the Business and Law, or Contractor Management Survey, examination unless the individual meets the requirements for an applicable waiver.

The Nevada contractor licensing process also includes financial responsibility and bonding requirements. The Board establishes licensing conditions and a monetary limit based on applicable information submitted during the licensing process.

## How does the Drywall Contractor (C-17C) exam work?

The Drywall Contractor (C-17C) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
