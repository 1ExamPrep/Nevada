## Exam Details

The Nevada C-17C Drywall trade examination contains **20 questions**. Candidates must answer at least **14 questions correctly** to achieve the listed minimum passing score. The examination allows **1 hour** for completion.

The official C-17C content outline divides the examination into four major subject areas:

- **Installation — 6 questions:** Preparation should include the knowledge and methods associated with installing drywall systems and related materials.
- **Taping and Finishing — 6 questions:** Candidates should be prepared for questions involving the procedures and principles associated with completing drywall surfaces.
- **Materials — 4 questions:** The portion evaluates familiarity with materials relevant to drywall work and their proper application.
- **Special Applications and Accessories — 4 questions:** The area addresses additional applications, components, and accessories encountered in drywall work.

The examination may include questions drawn from the listed reference material as well as trade knowledge and general industry practices. For code questions, the examination is based on the specific code edition identified by PSI, which for the C-17C examination is the **2018 International Building Code**.

Nevada also requires the appropriate qualifying individual to satisfy the Business and Law examination requirement unless a waiver applies. That examination focuses on the business and legal knowledge involved in operating a contracting business in Nevada and uses the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada as its approved exam-room reference.

## Open Book Test

The Nevada C-17C Drywall examination is officially listed as an **open-book examination**. For the C-17C trade exam, the **International Building Code, 2018** is the reference material approved for use in the examination center.

Open book does not mean that every study resource can be taken into the testing room. PSI specifically identifies which references are permitted. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved reference materials. References may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination session, but candidates may not write in or highlight their references during the examination.

PSI also states that additional papers, whether loose or attached, are not permitted with approved references. Permanent tabs may be used for indexing. Temporary tabs, including Post-It-style tabs, are not allowed and must be removed before testing begins.

The Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada is separately approved for the Nevada Business and Law examination. This makes organized preparation with each book important: the International Building Code supports the C-17C trade exam, while the Nevada manual supports the CMS Business and Law exam.

## State Requirements

Nevada contractor licenses are issued through the Nevada State Contractors Board. The trade qualifying individual generally must have at least **four full years of experience** as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the specific classification requested, with the experience obtained within the **15 years immediately preceding the application**.

Training from an accredited college, university, or equivalent program accepted by the Board may be used toward no more than three years of the experience requirement. Nevada does not impose a general educational degree requirement for contractor licensure.

The Board requires applicants to provide appropriate documentation supporting qualifying experience. Depending on the applicant's circumstances, accepted documentation may include Certification of Work Experience forms, a qualifying governmental master certification, transferable military experience and training, or applicable documentation for licensure by endorsement.

Applicants must also demonstrate financial responsibility as part of the licensing process. The contractor license belongs to the licensed business entity rather than personally to the qualifying individual. Nevada licenses may be issued to qualifying business structures including individuals, partnerships, corporations, limited liability companies, and joint ventures.

The qualifying individual is responsible for meeting the applicable experience and examination requirements. Nevada generally requires a Business and Law examination and the trade examination for the requested classification unless the Board determines that an applicant qualifies for an applicable waiver.
