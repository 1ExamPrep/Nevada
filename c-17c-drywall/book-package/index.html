Exam Details

The Nevada C-17C Drywall Contractor trade examination is administered through PSI for candidates authorized to test through the Nevada State Contractors Board.

  • Exam: Nevada C-17C Drywall Contractor
  • Testing Provider: PSI
  • Number of Questions: 20
  • Time Allowed: 1 hour
  • Minimum Passing Score: 14 correct answers

The examination is relatively short, but that makes accuracy and pacing especially important. Candidates have one hour to complete 20 questions, which provides enough time for careful reading and selective code reference use, but inefficient searching can still consume valuable testing time.

Preparation should focus on the core drywall trade subjects represented by the classification, including gypsum board installation, wall and ceiling systems, framing relationships, layout, materials, fastening, finishing, fire-resistance considerations, and applicable building-code provisions.

Candidates should also be prepared to identify when a question is best answered from practical trade knowledge and when the International Building Code should be used to confirm a requirement. Developing that judgment before the exam can make the open-book format more useful.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-17C classification is associated with drywall contracting. Candidates should understand the work involved in installing and finishing gypsum wallboard systems and how that work interacts with framing, walls, ceilings, fire-resistance requirements, and interior construction standards.

Drywall work requires more than simply attaching gypsum board. Proper installation involves layout, board selection, orientation, fastening, joint treatment, finishing, wall and ceiling coordination, and awareness of the building-code requirements that apply to the assembly.

The International Building Code is especially important where drywall is part of a rated wall or ceiling assembly, an interior finish system, or another regulated part of building construction. Candidates should become comfortable recognizing when a drywall question may involve a broader building-code requirement.

Framing knowledge also supports drywall preparation because gypsum board systems depend on the underlying wall or ceiling framing. The included Carpentry and Building Construction reference helps candidates review framing relationships, layout, support conditions, and other structural concepts that affect drywall installation.

The Gypsum Construction Handbook supports preparation involving gypsum products, installation methods, wall and ceiling systems, finishing, and common construction practices. Although it cannot be taken into the examination, it can help build the technical knowledge needed before test day.

Passing the C-17C trade exam does not automatically issue a Nevada contractor license. Applicants must also complete the other applicable Nevada State Contractors Board licensing requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-17C study plan should combine technical drywall preparation with repeated use of the International Building Code.

Study gypsum board installation. Review board types, placement, orientation, fastening, support, joints, edges, and general installation practices.

Review wall and ceiling systems. Candidates should understand how drywall is installed on different wall and ceiling configurations and how framing and support affect the finished system.

Study joint treatment and finishing. Review tape, joint compound, finishing sequences, surface preparation, and common procedures used to create completed gypsum board surfaces.

Understand framing relationships. Use the carpentry reference to review wall framing, ceiling framing, layout, spacing, openings, backing, and other conditions that affect drywall installation.

Review fire-resistance concepts. Drywall is frequently part of rated construction. Candidates should be prepared to recognize when fire-resistance, separation, or assembly requirements may require an IBC lookup.

Practice IBC navigation. Use the 2018 International Building Code during study so the organization of the book becomes familiar. Practice locating relevant sections rather than relying on broad searches during the exam.

Use the index effectively. Learn how to identify useful terms from a question and use them to locate the correct chapter or section more efficiently.

Know when the code is needed. Some questions may be practical drywall questions that can be answered directly from trade knowledge. Others may require a specific building-code provision. Recognizing the difference can improve pacing.

Review materials and fasteners. Candidates should understand commonly used drywall materials, board products, fastening methods, and how installation details affect the completed system.

Study interior finish concepts. Review the role drywall plays in interior wall and ceiling construction and how code requirements can affect finishes, assemblies, and related materials.

Practice timed questions. Although the exam allows one hour for 20 questions, candidates should avoid spending too much time on any single lookup. Use the available time to answer direct questions efficiently and reserve code searches for items that need confirmation.

Use the study-only books before test day. Because the Carpentry and Building Construction and Gypsum Construction Handbook references are not allowed into the exam room, candidates should study them thoroughly enough to apply the concepts without having the books available during testing.

How does the Drywall Contractor (C-17C) exam work?

The Drywall Contractor (C-17C) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
