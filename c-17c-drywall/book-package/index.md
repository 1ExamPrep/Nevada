## Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-17C study plan should combine technical drywall preparation with repeated use of the International Building Code.

**Study gypsum board installation.** Review board types, placement, orientation, fastening, support, joints, edges, and general installation practices.

**Review wall and ceiling systems.** Candidates should understand how drywall is installed on different wall and ceiling configurations and how framing and support affect the finished system.

**Study joint treatment and finishing.** Review tape, joint compound, finishing sequences, surface preparation, and common procedures used to create completed gypsum board surfaces.

**Understand framing relationships.** Use the carpentry reference to review wall framing, ceiling framing, layout, spacing, openings, backing, and other conditions that affect drywall installation.

**Review fire-resistance concepts.** Drywall is frequently part of rated construction. Candidates should be prepared to recognize when fire-resistance, separation, or assembly requirements may require an IBC lookup.

**Practice IBC navigation.** Use the 2018 International Building Code during study so the organization of the book becomes familiar. Practice locating relevant sections rather than relying on broad searches during the exam.

**Use the index effectively.** Learn how to identify useful terms from a question and use them to locate the correct chapter or section more efficiently.

**Know when the code is needed.** Some questions may be practical drywall questions that can be answered directly from trade knowledge. Others may require a specific building-code provision. Recognizing the difference can improve pacing.

**Review materials and fasteners.** Candidates should understand commonly used drywall materials, board products, fastening methods, and how installation details affect the completed system.

**Study interior finish concepts.** Review the role drywall plays in interior wall and ceiling construction and how code requirements can affect finishes, assemblies, and related materials.

**Practice timed questions.** Although the exam allows one hour for 20 questions, candidates should avoid spending too much time on any single lookup. Use the available time to answer direct questions efficiently and reserve code searches for items that need confirmation.

**Use the study-only books before test day.** Because the Carpentry and Building Construction and Gypsum Construction Handbook references are not allowed into the exam room, candidates should study them thoroughly enough to apply the concepts without having the books available during testing.

## How does the Drywall Contractor (C-17C) exam work?

The Drywall Contractor (C-17C) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
