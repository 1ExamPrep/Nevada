Repeated practice with the International Building Code can help candidates develop familiarity with the terminology used in code questions. Knowing the correct technical term can make the index much more useful during a timed examination.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-17C Drywall subclassification authorizes the installation and coating of gypsum wallboard and sheathing. The scope also includes the installation of a system of suspended ceilings made of gypsum wallboard.

This means C-17C preparation should cover more than attaching sheets of drywall to framing. Candidates should understand gypsum wallboard installation, sheathing, fastening, finishing, joint treatment, wall and ceiling systems, materials, and the relationship between drywall work and applicable building-code requirements.

Suspended gypsum wallboard ceilings are also within the classification scope. Candidates should understand the general construction relationships involved in ceiling systems, support, gypsum installation, finishing, and code considerations affecting the assembly.

Fire-resistance concepts are important because gypsum wallboard is frequently used as part of rated walls and ceilings. Candidates should be able to recognize when a question involves a fire-resistance-rated assembly or another condition that may require an International Building Code lookup.

The C-17C classification is a specialty contractor classification. Candidates should prepare specifically for drywall and gypsum-related work rather than assuming the license authorizes unrestricted framing, plastering, acoustical tile, or other specialty work outside the C-17C scope.

Passing the C-17C trade examination does not automatically issue a Nevada contractor license. Applicants must also complete the applicable Nevada State Contractors Board requirements associated with the licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive C-17C study plan should combine practical drywall knowledge with repeated International Building Code navigation. Candidates should understand the trade well enough to answer common installation questions directly while also knowing how to locate applicable code requirements efficiently.

Study gypsum wallboard installation. Review gypsum board types, placement, fastening, support, joints, edges, wall applications, ceiling applications, and general installation practices.

Review sheathing. The C-17C scope includes gypsum wallboard and sheathing. Candidates should understand the role of sheathing and how it relates to the construction systems covered by the classification.

Study coating and finishing. Review joint treatment, tape, compound, finishing procedures, surface preparation, and the steps involved in producing a completed drywall surface.

Review wall assemblies. Understand how gypsum wallboard functions within wall systems and how framing, support, fastening, openings, and adjacent construction can affect installation.

Study ceiling systems. The C-17C scope includes suspended ceiling systems made of gypsum wallboard. Candidates should understand the relationship between support systems, gypsum installation, fastening, finishing, and the completed ceiling.

Review fire-resistance requirements. Gypsum board is commonly used in fire-resistance-rated construction. Candidates should practice identifying relevant IBC provisions when a question involves rated walls, rated ceilings, separation requirements, or protected assemblies.

Practice International Building Code navigation. Use the 2018 IBC during preparation rather than waiting until exam day. Learn where major subjects are located and practice moving from a question to the relevant chapter or section.

Use the index effectively. Candidates should practice identifying technical terms in a question and using those terms to find applicable code provisions. The index can save time when the candidate understands the vocabulary of the subject.

Review materials. Understand the basic construction materials associated with drywall work and how code requirements can affect their use in walls, ceilings, and other assemblies.

Study fastening and support. Candidates should understand the purpose of proper fastening, attachment, support, and spacing in drywall systems and how those factors contribute to a completed installation.

Review interior construction concepts. Drywall is part of broader interior construction systems. Candidates should understand how gypsum wallboard interacts with walls, ceilings, openings, framing, and rated assemblies.

More on test Information and Study Materials

Know when a code lookup is appropriate. Not every C-17C question requires the International Building Code. Candidates who understand the trade can answer practical questions directly and reserve the code for requirements that need confirmation.

Practice timed questions. With 20 questions and one hour available, candidates should maintain a steady pace and avoid spending too much time searching for a single code provision.

Build familiarity instead of relying on page numbers. Learning the organization of the IBC is generally more useful than memorizing individual page numbers. Candidates should understand how topics are grouped and how the index directs them toward specific provisions.

Connect code requirements to field practice. Building-code provisions are easier to understand when candidates relate them to actual drywall installations, wall systems, ceiling assemblies, materials, fire-resistance conditions, and finishing work.

How does the Drywall Contractor (C-17C) exam work?

The Drywall Contractor (C-17C) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
