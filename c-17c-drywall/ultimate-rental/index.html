Exam Details

The Nevada C-17C Drywall trade examination is part of the qualification process for applicants pursuing this contractor classification. The Nevada State Contractors Board contracts with PSI to administer contractor licensing examinations after the applicant has submitted the appropriate license application and received examination eligibility.

Candidates should prepare for drywall-specific technical knowledge as well as the ability to locate applicable information in the approved International Building Code reference. The trade preparation should include gypsum wallboard and panel products, installation procedures, fastening, framing relationships, taping and finishing, materials, accessories, special applications, fire-resistance-related construction, and code requirements relevant to drywall systems.

Drywall preparation requires more than memorizing isolated terms. Candidates should understand how materials, framing, wallboard, fasteners, joints, finishing products, accessories, and building-code provisions work together in an installed system. Practical knowledge is particularly important because the Carpentry and Building Construction and Gypsum Construction Handbook references are study materials rather than exam-room references.

Candidates pursuing a Nevada contractor license are also generally required to satisfy the Business and Law, or Contractor Management Survey, examination unless an applicable waiver is approved. The included Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada provides the reference material for preparing for contractor management subjects.

Business and Law preparation should be treated separately from technical drywall preparation. Creating distinct study sessions for trade subjects and contractor management subjects can make it easier to identify weak areas and build a more organized licensing study plan.

Licensing Steps

The Nevada State Contractors Board administers the contractor licensing process. The C-17C applicant must complete the applicable licensing requirements before the contractor license can be issued.

  1. Identify the appropriate classification. Confirm that the Nevada C-17C Drywall classification matches the type of work the contracting business intends to perform.
  2. Designate the qualifying individual. The applicant identifies the person who will satisfy the applicable trade qualification requirements for the business.
  3. Document qualifying experience. The Trade Qualified Individual must provide appropriate evidence of relevant experience in the requested trade classification.
  4. Prepare the contractor license application. Gather the business, qualifying-party, experience, financial, and other required information for submission to the Nevada State Contractors Board.
  5. Submit the application. The licensing application is submitted for Board review before an applicant can proceed to required contractor examinations.
  6. Complete applicable background requirements. Follow the fingerprint and background procedures required as part of Nevada contractor licensing.
  7. Receive examination eligibility. Once the application and experience qualifications have been reviewed, eligible candidates receive the information needed to schedule required examinations.
  8. Schedule the applicable examination through PSI. PSI administers Nevada contractor licensing examinations.
  9. Prepare for the C-17C trade examination. Review drywall systems, installation, finishing, materials, special applications, accessories, and International Building Code navigation.
  10. Prepare for Business and Law. Use the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada to prepare for the Contractor Management Survey requirement.
  11. Complete applicable examinations. Satisfy the required trade and Business and Law examination requirements unless an approved waiver applies.
  12. Complete final licensing requirements. Address the applicable financial responsibility, bonding, and remaining Board requirements before license issuance.

State Requirements

The Nevada C-17C license is a specialty contractor classification. Candidates should pursue the classification that matches the specific work they intend to perform and should understand the limitations of the license before bidding or contracting for projects.

The qualifying individual plays an important role in the Nevada contractor licensing process. A Trade Qualified Individual must have a minimum of four years of qualifying experience as a journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor in the specific trade category requested.

The four years of qualifying trade experience must have been gained within the 15 years preceding the application. Applicants should organize experience documentation carefully so the Board can evaluate the type, duration, and relevance of the work performed.

Nevada allows one individual to serve as both the Management Qualified Individual and Trade Qualified Individual when that person satisfies the applicable requirements. A company may also use separate qualified individuals for the management and trade qualification roles.

The Management Qualified Individual is responsible for satisfying the Business and Law examination requirement unless an applicable waiver is approved. The Trade Qualified Individual establishes the required work experience and satisfies the applicable trade examination requirement.

Nevada contractor licensing also involves financial responsibility and bonding requirements. The Board evaluates the licensing application and supporting information before establishing the conditions applicable to the contractor license.

Separate trade preparation from Business and Law preparation. Use the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada to review contractor management subjects as a distinct part of the licensing study plan.

Use practice-oriented preparation. How does the Drywall Contractor (C-17C) exam work?

The Drywall Contractor (C-17C) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

  • Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
  • The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
  • PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
  • The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
