## State Requirements

The Nevada C-17C Drywall classification authorizes the **installation and coating of gypsum wallboard and sheathing**. The scope also includes installation of suspended ceiling systems made of gypsum wallboard.

This means candidates should prepare for more than basic drywall attachment. The work can involve material selection, support conditions, framing relationships, installation, fastening, joint treatment, coating, finishing, wall systems, ceilings, sheathing, and related code requirements.

Suspended gypsum wallboard ceilings are specifically included in the C-17C scope. Candidates should understand the general relationship between support systems, gypsum installation, fastening, finishing, and the completed ceiling assembly.

Fire-resistance is another important area because gypsum wallboard is frequently used as part of rated construction. Candidates should be able to recognize when a question involves a fire-resistance-rated wall or ceiling and know when an International Building Code lookup may be appropriate.

Framing knowledge is also valuable because drywall systems depend on the underlying structure and support. The included Carpentry and Building Construction reference helps candidates review framing, layout, wall and ceiling construction, openings, support, and other building relationships that affect drywall installation.

The Gypsum Construction Handbook provides additional trade-focused study involving gypsum products, finishing systems, board installation, joints, fastening, wall systems, ceilings, and practical construction methods.

## Test Information and Study Materials

A strong C-17C study plan should combine practical drywall preparation with repeated reference navigation using the International Building Code.

**Study gypsum wallboard installation.** Review board types, placement, orientation, fastening, support, joints, edges, wall applications, and ceiling applications.

**Review sheathing.** The C-17C scope includes gypsum sheathing, so candidates should understand its role and how it fits within wall systems and related construction.

**Study finishing and coating.** Review tape, joint compound, finishing sequences, surface preparation, joint treatment, and common methods used to complete gypsum wallboard surfaces.

**Review framing relationships.** Use Carpentry and Building Construction to strengthen understanding of framing, wall layout, ceiling framing, openings, backing, support conditions, and other construction elements that affect drywall installation.

**Study suspended gypsum ceilings.** Candidates should understand how suspended gypsum wallboard ceiling systems relate to support, attachment, board installation, and finishing.

**Review fire-resistance concepts.** Gypsum wallboard is commonly used in fire-resistance-rated construction. Practice recognizing when a question may involve rated walls, rated ceilings, separation requirements, or protected assemblies.

**Practice IBC navigation.** Use the highlighted and tabbed International Building Code regularly. Learn where major subjects are located and how to move from a question to the appropriate chapter or section efficiently.

**Use the index strategically.** Identify technical words from a question and use those terms to locate relevant code material. Familiarity with terminology can make searches much faster.

**Study materials and fastening.** Understand common drywall materials, board products, fasteners, attachment methods, and support considerations.

**Review wall and ceiling assemblies.** Understand how drywall functions within complete systems rather than treating each board as an isolated component.

**Know when the code is needed.** Not every C-17C question requires a code lookup. Candidates should answer practical questions from trade knowledge when possible and reserve the IBC for code-specific requirements.

**Practice timed questions.** The one-hour exam provides three minutes per question on average. Candidates should still avoid spending too long on one difficult lookup.

**Use the highlighted sections actively.** Highlighting is most valuable when candidates understand the meaning of the material and can connect it to practice questions or real drywall situations.

**Learn the tabs before exam day.** Tabs should become familiar navigational tools during preparation. Candidates should know what subjects they represent and how to use them efficiently.

## How does the Drywall Contractor (C-17C) exam work?

The Drywall Contractor (C-17C) license comes from the Nevada State Contractors Board. You apply to the NSCB with your experience and financial statement, the Board sends an exam eligibility letter, and you book the CMS and the trade exam with PSI. Scores come right after the exam.

- Every Nevada contractor license needs the Business and Law (CMS) exam plus a trade exam for the classification, both given by PSI.
- The CMS is open book with the Construction Business and Law Manual for Nevada; trade exams are closed book except for the code references their outline allows.
- PSI charges $95 for one exam portion, or $140 for a trade exam taken with the CMS, and you get three attempts before the application is void.
- The Board sets a bond from $1,000 to $500,000 and a monetary limit on every license, based on your experience and financial statements.
